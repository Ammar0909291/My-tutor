## Learning Objective
- The learner can solve a system of two linear equations in two unknowns using the substitution method, correctly substituting into the equation NOT used to derive the isolated expression.
- The learner can solve the same system using the elimination method, correctly determining whether to add or subtract equations (or first multiply by a scalar) based on the sign of the target variable's coefficients.
- The learner can classify a system's outcome — a unique solution, no solution, or infinitely many solutions — correctly interpreting the algebraic results 0 = k (k ≠ 0) as no solution and 0 = 0 as infinitely many, never confusing the two.

## Core Understanding
A system of two linear equations in two unknowns is, geometrically, two lines in the coordinate plane, and solving the system means finding every point lying on *both* lines simultaneously — which is why exactly three outcomes are possible, corresponding to the only three ways two lines can relate: they cross at exactly one point (a unique solution), they run parallel and never meet (no solution, an inconsistent system), or they are actually the same line traced twice (infinitely many solutions, a dependent system). Substitution and elimination are not two competing algorithms to memorise separately; both are mechanical routes to the same destination — reducing two equations in two unknowns to one equation in one unknown, which can then be solved by already-secured single-variable technique. Substitution does this by isolating one variable in one equation and replacing it, everywhere it appears, in the *other* equation — never the equation it was isolated from, since substituting back into the same equation merely reconstructs an identity and reveals nothing new about the second variable. Elimination does this by scaling one or both equations so that a target variable's coefficients become additive inverses, then adding the equations so that variable cancels — a step that requires either already-opposite signs or a preliminary multiplication to create them, and that produces the wrong result (or no cancellation at all) if the equations are added when the coefficients share a sign instead of being subtracted, or vice versa. The two degenerate outcomes are not failures of the method but genuine, informative results: eliminating a variable and arriving at a false numerical statement like 0 = 5 proves the two original equations describe parallel lines with no shared point, while arriving at a true statement like 0 = 0 proves they describe the identical line, every point of which is therefore a solution.

## Mental Models
1. **Beginner — two roads, one crossroads.** Each equation is a line; if the lines cross, that single crossing point is the system's one solution. *Upgrade trigger*: a system where the two lines happen to be parallel or identical, where "find the crossroads" has no direct procedure for detecting or handling either case. *Shelf life*: one to two sessions.
2. **Intermediate — substitution swaps a known expression into the equation that hasn't used it yet; elimination cancels a variable by matching opposite-signed coefficients.** Both are mechanical procedures for collapsing two unknowns into one. *Upgrade trigger*: a system where elimination requires a preliminary multiplication (coefficients not already opposite), or where the result after elimination is a numerical identity rather than a value for the remaining variable. *Shelf life*: durable and remains correct permanently, once both procedures are independently fluent.
3. **Advanced — a false numerical statement (0=k, k≠0) proves the lines are parallel; a true one (0=0) proves they're the same line — read the algebra as a direct report on the geometry, not a separate fact to memorise.** *Upgrade trigger*: needing to determine, WITHOUT fully solving, which of the three cases a given system falls into — comparing coefficient ratios directly, before any elimination is attempted.
4. **Expert — a linear system's solvability is fully determined by the relationship between its equations' coefficients: proportional-with-matching-constants (dependent), proportional-with-mismatched-constants (inconsistent), or non-proportional (unique solution) — the same structural question that generalises, via matrices and determinants, to systems of any size.** *Shelf life*: permanent, and it is the model this concept's own two unlocks (matrix-form linear systems, three-variable systems) exist specifically to generalise.

## Why Students Fail
The single most damaging failure in the substitution method is circular substitution: having isolated a variable in one equation (say, x = 3 − y from equation 1), the learner substitutes that expression back into the *same* equation it came from, producing a tautology or an already-known identity (3 = 3, or a restatement of the original equation) that reveals nothing about the value of y — and depending on how the resulting non-informative statement is read, the learner either concludes, wrongly, that infinitely many solutions exist, or simply becomes stuck with no path forward, because the one equation that could actually pin down y (the *other* equation) was never used. The second major failure occurs in elimination and is a sign-management error: to eliminate a variable whose coefficients in the two equations share the same sign (e.g. both +3x), the learner adds the equations directly rather than subtracting one from the other (or first negating one equation), producing a result in which the target variable has *not* actually been eliminated — the learner has performed valid algebra that simply fails to accomplish the intended goal, often without immediately noticing that the variable is still present. The third failure is interpretive rather than procedural: having correctly eliminated a variable and arrived at the statement 0 = 0, the learner concludes this means "no solution," reasoning that "nothing is left" implies "nothing works" — exactly inverting the correct interpretation, which is that a statement true for every value of the remaining variable signals a dependent system with infinitely many solutions, not an empty one.

## Misconceptions
Reused by reference from the Blueprint's Component 2 registry (MC-1..MC-3) and its Component 5 repair actions B01–B03, with birth-type classification added.

- **MC-1 — SUBSTITUTE-INTO-SAME-EQUATION** (FOUNDATIONAL)
  - **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralisation — the learner does not yet distinguish "the equation I solved FROM" from "the other equation, not yet used," treating substitution as a generic next step rather than a targeted move into the equation still carrying unused information.
  - **Characteristic phrase**: given {x+y=5, x=3−y}, substituting x=3−y back into the first equation (the one x was already isolated from), producing a confusing identity or a false "always true" conclusion.
  - **Detection probe** (verbatim, Blueprint P41): given {x+y=5, x=3}, ask where x=3 should be substituted — choosing to substitute back into the equation x was found from (rather than the other one) confirms MC-1.
  - **Repair**: Blueprint Repair Action B01 — reframe substitution's goal explicitly as "create an equation with only one unknown," which requires using an equation not yet fully exploited; codify as a rule: substitute only into the equation you did NOT use to derive the expression.
  - **Verification of death**: given a fresh two-equation system, the learner substitutes an isolated expression into the correct (other) equation unprompted, without needing the rule restated.

- **MC-2 — ELIMINATION-WRONG-SIGN** (moderate)
  - **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralisation of "combine the equations" without the specific constraint that the target variable's coefficients must be additive inverses before addition eliminates anything.
  - **Characteristic phrase**: given {3x+2y=13, 3x−y=7}, adding the equations directly (rather than subtracting, since both have +3x), producing 6x+y=20 — a valid equation, but one in which x has not been eliminated.
  - **Detection probe** (verbatim, Blueprint P41): given the same system, ask whether adding or subtracting eliminates x — choosing to add confirms MC-2.
  - **Repair**: Blueprint Repair Action B02 — establish the explicit check: write out the target variable's coefficients in both equations; if they already sum to zero, add; if not, either subtract or first multiply one equation by −1 (or another scalar) to create that condition.
  - **Verification of death**: given a system with same-signed target coefficients, the learner subtracts (or negates first) without being prompted, correctly eliminating the variable.

- **MC-3 — ZERO-EQUALS-ZERO-MEANS-NO-SOLUTION** (moderate)
  - **Birth type**: Type 3, language contamination — "nothing is left" (the variable has vanished from the equation) is read informally as "nothing works" (no value satisfies the system), conflating the disappearance of a variable from the algebra with the emptiness of the solution set, when in fact the opposite conclusion (every value works) is correct for this specific outcome.
  - **Characteristic phrase**: reaching 0 = 0 after elimination and concluding "no solution."
  - **Detection probe** (verbatim, Blueprint P41): present the outcome 0 = 0 directly and ask what it means — a "no solution" answer confirms MC-3.
  - **Repair**: Blueprint Repair Action B03 — contrast explicitly against 0 = k (k≠0): the false statement (0=5) is what genuinely means no solution, because it is never true; the true statement (0=0) holds for every value, meaning the two original equations described the same line all along.
  - **Verification of death**: given both a 0=0 and a 0=k(k≠0) outcome in the same session, the learner correctly and confidently distinguishes "infinitely many" from "none" for each, unprompted.

## Analogies
- **Best — two roads on a map.** Two straight roads meet at exactly one crossroads unless they run in exactly the same direction (parallel, never meeting) or are literally the same road under two different names (every point shared). This is the Blueprint's own P03 analogy and it grounds all three solution-case outcomes in a single, already-intuitive physical picture.
- **Alternative — using up information versus reusing it.** Each equation carries one "unit" of information about the system; substituting back into the equation it came from spends that same unit twice, learning nothing new, while substituting into the other equation spends the *second* unit, which is what actually pins down the second variable. This targets MC-1's circularity directly.
- **Story analogy** — balancing two see-saws simultaneously by canceling a shared, opposing weight: if the weights on the target side are already opposite (one pushes up, one pushes down), combining them directly cancels; if they push the same direction, combining them only adds to the imbalance, and one side must first be flipped.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — "just plug the answer back in anywhere to check it."** For substitution specifically, this obscures the critical distinction between the equation the expression came FROM and the one it must go INTO; "anywhere" licenses MC-1 directly.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — "zero equals zero means the equation cancelled out to nothing, so there's no answer."** This is exactly MC-3's mechanism stated as a plausible-sounding rule; the correct frame treats "cancelled to a true statement" and "cancelled to a false statement" as opposite, not equivalent, outcomes.

## Demonstrations
- **The circular-substitution collision.** Take {x+y=5, x=3}, substitute x=3 back into the equation it came from (x=3 → 3=3), showing this produces nothing useful — then substitute correctly into the other equation (x+y=5 → 3+y=5 → y=2), showing the contrast directly. *Predict, before substituting into the same equation, whether it will reveal y's value* — the empty result is the demonstration, operationalising Blueprint B01's repair.
- **The same-sign elimination failure, attempted live.** Take {3x+2y=13, 3x−y=7}, add the equations directly, showing x survives (6x+y=20, not eliminated) — then subtract instead, showing x correctly cancels. *Predict, before adding, whether x will cancel* — the failed prediction is the demonstration, operationalising Blueprint B02's repair.
- **The 0=0 versus 0=k side-by-side.** Work one system to 0=0 and a near-identical one (same coefficients, one constant changed) to 0=k(k≠0), directly juxtaposing the two outcomes and their opposite interpretations. This is the Blueprint's own TA-A03 contrast pair, restaged as a live side-by-side comparison rather than a static table.

## Discovery Questions
Direct instruction wins for the substitution and elimination *mechanics* themselves — both are procedural extensions of already-secured single-variable equation-solving, not principles to be discovered, and the risk of "discovering" a plausible-but-wrong shortcut (e.g. discovering that substituting back into the same equation "feels" like it should work) is real enough that the correct target-equation rule should be stated and grounded directly. What is genuinely discoverable, and central to this concept's own distinguishing content, is the three-case classification itself: (1) **Need** — "Without fully solving, can you tell how many solutions {3x+y=4, 6x+2y=8} has?" (2) **Playground** — compare several pairs of systems, some proportional with matching constants, some proportional with mismatched constants, some non-proportional, checking each by scaling one equation and comparing. (3) **Invention** — "What pattern in the coefficients predicts each of the three outcomes?" (4) **Collision** — offer a near-miss pair like {2x+y=5, 4x+2y=8} versus {2x+y=5, 4x+2y=10}, differing only in one constant, producing opposite outcomes (no solution versus infinitely many) despite near-identical appearance. (5) **Formalisation** — scale one equation to match the other's variable coefficients; if the constants also match, the system is dependent (infinitely many); if they don't match, it's inconsistent (no solution); if the coefficients were never proportional to begin with, there is a unique solution. (6) **Compression** — "Make the coefficients match. Do the constants match too? Yes → same line. No → parallel lines. Never matched at all → one crossing point."

## Teaching Sequence
Substitution (TA-A01/TA-A02, targeting MC-1) is taught before elimination's sign-management content (also TA-A02, targeting MC-2), following the Blueprint's own worked-example ordering, so that the "substitute into the OTHER equation" discipline — the single most consequential rule in this concept — is established on the simpler of the two methods before a second procedural rule (coefficient sign-matching) is introduced alongside it. The three-case classification (TA-A03, targeting MC-3) is deliberately sequenced last, after both solving methods are independently fluent, because correctly interpreting a 0=0 or 0=k(k≠0) outcome presupposes the learner has actually reached that outcome through a correctly-executed elimination — a learner still making sign errors cannot reliably distinguish a genuine degenerate case from a self-inflicted computational error. The Blueprint's own Component 4 sequence (TA-A01 geometric preview and case introduction via analogy bridge, TA-A02 substitution-and-elimination worked-example pair, TA-A03 three-case contrast pair, TA-A04 mastery gate) is reused by reference and not restated turn-by-turn here.

## Tutor Actions
- **DO: Demonstration** — the circular-substitution collision, run on the learner's own attempted substitution rather than a hypothetical one, so the "this revealed nothing" realisation is felt directly.
- **ORGANIZE: Physical marking habit** — the Blueprint's own Teaching Notes' circle-and-arrow technique (circle the equation an expression was solved FROM; draw an arrow to the equation it will be substituted INTO), adopted as a standing visual habit rather than a one-time instruction.
- **TEST-THINKING: Prediction** — "will this cancel if I add, or do I need to subtract?" asked before every elimination attempt, until coefficient-sign checking is automatic.
- **TEST-THINKING: Contrast pair** — the 0=0 versus 0=k side-by-side, run as a direct comparison rather than presented as two separate, temporally distant examples.
- **Does NOT fit: teaching elimination before substitution is fluent.** MC-1's target-equation discipline, once established, transfers directly to "which equation to substitute a value back into after elimination" — introducing elimination first forfeits this scaffolding.
- **Does NOT fit: presenting only systems with a unique solution before the degenerate cases are introduced.** This never gives MC-3 a chance to surface during instruction, producing false confidence that "solve and report a point" is a universally sufficient procedure.

## Voice Teaching Notes
The load-bearing sentence for substitution is "into the OTHER equation — not the one you just used." Slow down on "other," every time a substitution step is narrated. Listen for a learner who, having isolated a variable, immediately substitutes without stating which equation they're substituting into — the unstated destination predicts MC-1 more reliably than watching the algebra unfold, since a correct substitution performed by luck looks identical to an incorrect one performed by design until the result is examined. For elimination, listen for whether the learner states the target variable's coefficient signs aloud before choosing to add or subtract ("these are both positive, so I need to subtract") versus reaching for addition by default. A confident, unprompted "0=0 means these were the same line all along" is the strongest positive signal this concept produces. Channel reality is owned by `../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §7`.

## Assessment Signals
- **A substituted expression returned to the equation it was derived from, producing an uninformative identity** — MC-1; route to the circular-substitution collision, never to a bare restatement of "use the other equation."
- **Equations added when their target-variable coefficients share a sign (or vice versa), leaving the variable un-eliminated** — MC-2, cleanly diagnostic when the "still present" variable is pointed out directly; route to the same-sign elimination failure demonstration.
- **A 0=0 outcome interpreted as "no solution," or a 0=k(k≠0) outcome interpreted as "infinitely many"** — MC-3, the two directions of the same confusion; route to the 0=0 versus 0=k side-by-side, never treating either direction as a separate misconception.
- **A solution reported alongside an unprompted verification in BOTH original equations** — the intended, fully-formed response pattern, per the Blueprint's own Teaching Notes' "consistency check is mandatory" rule; this habit, not merely a correct final pair, is the strongest evidence of genuine mastery.
- **Mastery trigger**: the Blueprint's TA-A04 gate, MAMR ⌈0.85×5⌉ = 5/5, including its P76 transfer probe requiring a stated method choice, full working, and dual-equation verification — a gate passed without the explicit method-justification component does not certify the strategic (substitution-versus-elimination) judgment this concept is meant to build, only rote execution of one memorised method.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy
The likely utterance is "I substituted it back in and got a weird answer" — a precise, diagnostic description of MC-1 in the learner's own words. The concept-specific smaller question redirects to the unused equation directly: **"Which equation did you get that expression FROM? Now — is there a different equation you haven't used yet?"** — making the target-equation choice an explicit, answerable question rather than leaving the learner to re-derive the rule under pressure. If the freeze is specifically at deciding add-versus-subtract in elimination, shrink to the signs alone: **"Look at just the x's in both equations. Are the signs the same, or opposite?"** — isolating the one judgment (sign comparison) from the larger elimination procedure. Generic machinery is owned by `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`.

## Memory Hooks
- Concept type: **procedure**, two related methods (substitution, elimination) sharing the goal of reducing two unknowns to one, plus an embedded classification judgment for the two degenerate outcomes. Review by *application on mixed input* every cycle, including at least one system requiring a preliminary multiplication before elimination and one genuinely degenerate (0=0 or 0=k) system.
- Concept-specific deviation: keep at least one dependent (0=0) and one inconsistent (0=k≠0) system in permanent rotation, deliberately paired — MC-3 is a confusion *between* two outcomes, and reviewing only one of them at a time never re-exercises the distinguishing judgment.
