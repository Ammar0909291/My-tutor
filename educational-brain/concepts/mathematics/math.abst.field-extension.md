## Learning Objective
Define a FIELD EXTENSION $K/F$ as a field $K$ containing a subfield $F$, and the DEGREE $[K:F]=
\dim_F(K)$ (the dimension of $K$ as a vector space over $F$), computing this degree for simple,
familiar extensions; state and apply the TOWER LAW, $[K:F]=[K:E][E:F]$ for a chain $F\subseteq
E\subseteq K$; and distinguish SIMPLE extensions ($K=F(\alpha)$) from general extensions, and
ALGEBRAIC extensions (every element satisfies some polynomial over $F$) from TRANSCENDENTAL
extensions (some element satisfies none).

## Core Understanding
$K$ IS A FIELD EXTENSION of $F$ (written $K/F$) if $K$ is itself a field CONTAINING $F$ as a
subfield — every element of $F$ is also an element of $K$, and $F$'s field operations are just
$K$'s operations restricted to $F$. THE DEGREE $[K:F]$ is defined as $\dim_F(K)$ — the dimension
of $K$, viewed as a VECTOR SPACE over $F$ (scalars from $F$, vectors are elements of $K$ itself).
E.g. $\mathbb C$ over $\mathbb R$: every complex number $a+bi$ is uniquely a linear combination
$a(1)+b(i)$ of the basis $\{1,i\}$ with REAL coefficients, so $[\mathbb C:\mathbb R]=2$.

THE TOWER LAW: for a chain of fields $F\subseteq E\subseteq K$ (each an extension of the
previous), $[K:F]=[K:E]\cdot[E:F]$ — the degree of the full extension is the PRODUCT (not the
sum) of the degrees of each individual step. This mirrors how a basis for $K$ over $F$ can be
constructed by combining a basis for $K$ over $E$ with a basis for $E$ over $F$ (multiplying
corresponding basis elements together, giving $[K:E]\times[E:F]$ total combinations).

SIMPLE, ALGEBRAIC, AND TRANSCENDENTAL EXTENSIONS: a SIMPLE extension $K=F(\alpha)$ is generated
by adjoining a SINGLE element $\alpha$ to $F$ (the smallest field containing both $F$ and
$\alpha$) — "simple" refers to the GENERATOR COUNT, never to small degree or ease of
understanding; $F(\alpha)$ can still have arbitrarily large degree. An extension is ALGEBRAIC if
EVERY element of $K$ satisfies some polynomial equation with coefficients in $F$ (e.g. $i$
satisfies $x^2+1=0$ over $\mathbb R$). An extension is TRANSCENDENTAL if SOME element of $K$
satisfies NO such polynomial (e.g. $\pi$ over $\mathbb Q$ — a famous, hard-won result proven by
Lindemann in 1882, not merely an open question).

## Mental Models
- **"$K/F$ formalizes 'a bigger field built on top of a smaller one,' and the degree measures how
  much bigger, in a precise dimension-counting sense."**
- **"The Tower Law multiplies degrees across a chain — never adds them."**
- **"'Simple' means generated by ONE element — nothing about the degree being small."**

## Why Students Fail

### MC-1: EVERY-REAL-NUMBER-ASSUMED-ALGEBRAIC
- **Surface form**: believes every real number (including $\pi$, $e$) must satisfy some
  polynomial equation with rational coefficients, not recognizing transcendental numbers as a
  genuine, proven exception.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared FOUNDATIONAL severity —
  nearly every specific number a student names by formula (roots, ratios, familiar constants
  built from arithmetic) happens to be algebraic, making the EXISTENCE of transcendental numbers
  feel exotic rather than the measure-theoretic norm — in fact the reverse is true).
- **Repair**: re-anchor on the specific historical fact directly — Lindemann's 1882 proof that
  $\pi$ satisfies no polynomial over $\mathbb Q$.

### MC-2: TOWER-LAW-DEGREES-ADDED-RATHER-THAN-MULTIPLIED
- **Surface form**: believes the Tower Law's degree for a chain of extensions is the SUM of the
  individual step degrees, rather than their PRODUCT.
- **Birth type**: Type 6, analogy overextension (Blueprint's own declared FOUNDATIONAL severity —
  "stacking" extension steps evokes an everyday additive intuition of accumulating layers, when
  the correct structure is multiplicative basis-combination).
- **Repair**: re-attempt the specific basis-combination argument directly, confirming the total
  degree is the PRODUCT via counting all combined basis pairs.

### MC-3: SIMPLE-EXTENSION-CONFUSED-WITH-SMALL-DEGREE-EXTENSION
- **Surface form**: believes "simple" extension means "small degree" or "easy to understand,"
  rather than the precise technical meaning: generated by adjoining a SINGLE element $\alpha$.
- **Birth type**: Type 3, language contamination (Blueprint's own declared Moderate severity —
  the everyday English word "simple" strongly suggests smallness or ease, obscuring the term's
  precise technical meaning about generator count).
- **Repair**: re-anchor on the precise definition directly, noting a simple extension can still
  have arbitrarily large (even infinite) degree.

## Analogies
- **"The Tower Law is like combining independent choices — 2 shirt choices times 3 pants choices
  gives 6 outfits, never 5; extending a field by degree 2 then by degree 3 gives total degree 6,
  by the same multiplicative counting."**
- **Anti-analogy**: "simple" does NOT mean "small" — $F(\alpha)$ can have enormous or even
  infinite degree while still being generated by the single element $\alpha$.

## Demonstrations
- **Demonstration 1 (targets baseline degree computation)**: $[\mathbb C:\mathbb R]=2$ (basis
  $\{1,i\}$); $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]=2$ (basis $\{1,\sqrt2\}$) — both computed by
  identifying the minimal spanning set over the base field.
- **Demonstration 2 (targets MC-2)**: for $\mathbb Q\subseteq\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\subseteq\mathbb
  Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)$: given $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2):\mathbb Q]=2$ and $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):
  \mathbb Q(\sqrt2)]=2$, the Tower Law gives $[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb Q]=2\times2=4$ —
  MULTIPLICATION, confirmed against a naive "2+2=4" addition that would coincidentally match here
  but genuinely fails for other degree pairs (e.g. 2 and 3 giving 6, never 5).
- **Demonstration 3 (targets MC-1, algebraic vs. transcendental)**: $\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)/\mathbb Q$
  is ALGEBRAIC — $\sqrt2$ satisfies $x^2-2=0$ over $\mathbb Q$. Contrast: $\mathbb Q(\pi)/\mathbb
  Q$ is TRANSCENDENTAL — $\pi$ satisfies NO polynomial with rational coefficients, a deep theorem
  (Lindemann, 1882), not an unconfirmed gap.

## Discovery Questions
1. "Since $\pi$ is a perfectly ordinary real number, does it satisfy SOME polynomial equation with
   rational coefficients, the same way $\sqrt2$ does?"
2. "If you extend by degree 2, then extend again by degree 3, is the total degree 5 or 6?"
3. "Does 'simple' extension mean the extension must have small or easily-computed degree?"

## Teaching Sequence
1. **Anchor**: connect to `math.abst.field`'s own field axioms and the already-familiar
   $\mathbb Q\subset\mathbb R\subset\mathbb C$ chain, framing $K/F$ as "a bigger field built on a
   smaller one."
2. **Conflict evidence**: Demonstration 3's algebraic-vs-transcendental contrast, directly
   challenging MC-1 by establishing $\pi$'s transcendence as a proven fact.
3. **Contrast pair**: the multiplicative Tower Law (Demonstration 2) against a naive additive
   reading, isolating MC-2 directly, using a genuinely non-matching degree pair (2 and 3 giving 6,
   not 5) to make the distinction unambiguous.
4. **Mastery gate**: require a correct degree computation for a specific extension, a correct
   application of the Tower Law to a chain, a correct classification of algebraic vs.
   transcendental, and a correct explanation of why $\mathbb Q(\pi)$ contradicts the
   every-number-is-algebraic assumption, at the Blueprint's own stated MAMR of 4/5 (⌈0.8×5⌉).

## Tutor Actions
- Never accept an additive computation for a chained Tower Law application — require the product
  of individual degrees explicitly.
- Never accept "simple" glossed as "small degree" — require the generator-count definition to be
  stated explicitly.

- When "simple extension" is discussed, ask "simple in what SENSE — generator count, or size?"
