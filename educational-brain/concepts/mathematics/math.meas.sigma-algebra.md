## Learning Objective
Define a $\sigma$-ALGEBRA $\mathcal F$ on a set $X$ as a collection of subsets satisfying three
closure properties — $X\in\mathcal F$, closed under complement, closed under COUNTABLE union —
deriving that countable-intersection closure FOLLOWS from these via De Morgan's laws, never an
independent axiom; explain why this restriction becomes NECESSARY precisely when $X$ is
UNCOUNTABLE, directly resolving `math.prob.event`'s own deferred question; and recognize the
BOREL $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal B(\mathbb R)$ as generated by open sets, defining a MEASURABLE
SPACE $(X,\mathcal F)$.

## Core Understanding
COUNTABLE INTERSECTION CLOSURE IS A THEOREM, NEVER A FOURTH AXIOM: a $\sigma$-algebra satisfies
exactly three closure properties: (1) $X\in\mathcal F$; (2) $A\in\mathcal F\Rightarrow A^c\in
\mathcal F$; (3) $A_1,A_2,\dots\in\mathcal F\Rightarrow\bigcup_iA_i\in\mathcal F$ (COUNTABLE
union). By De Morgan's law, $\bigcap_iA_i=(\bigcup_iA_i^c)^c$ — each $A_i^c\in\mathcal F$ by (2),
their union is in $\mathcal F$ by (3), and its complement is in $\mathcal F$ by (2) again —
countable intersection closure is DERIVED from the other two, never independently assumed.

THE RESTRICTION IS FORCED BY MATHEMATICAL NECESSITY, ONLY ONCE $X$ IS UNCOUNTABLE: for a finite
or countably infinite $X$ (like `math.prob.event`'s own die-roll $\Omega=\{1,\dots,6\}$), EVERY
subset can safely be assigned a probability — the full power set trivially satisfies all three
axioms, exactly as `event` already established for finite sample spaces. For an UNCOUNTABLE $X$
(like $\mathbb R$), it is a genuine, deep theorem that no consistent measure can be defined on the
FULL power set while preserving basic properties like countable additivity — some subsets
(non-measurable, e.g. via the Vitali construction) must be excluded. The $\sigma$-algebra is
exactly the collection of "well-behaved enough to measure" subsets — never arbitrary extra
bookkeeping, but a genuine mathematical necessity that only bites for uncountable spaces.

THE BOREL $\sigma$-ALGEBRA IS THE STANDARD MEASURABLE STRUCTURE ON $\mathbb R$: $\mathcal
B(\mathbb R)$ is the SMALLEST $\sigma$-algebra containing all open sets of $\mathbb R$ — generated
by them, meaning the intersection of every $\sigma$-algebra containing those open sets. It
includes essentially every subset one would naturally construct (open sets, closed sets,
countable sets, all intervals), making $(X,\mathcal F)$ a genuine MEASURABLE SPACE — the exact
structure on which a measure or probability can later be defined.

## Mental Models
- **"Check the closure axioms for EVERY member of a candidate collection, never just the two
  obvious ones (X and the empty set) — a single failing member disqualifies the whole
  collection."**
- **"The σ-algebra restriction only bites once X is uncountable — for finite or countable spaces,
  the full power set was always safe, exactly as already seen for the die roll."**

## Why Students Fail

### MC-1: PARTIAL-AXIOM-CHECK
- **Surface form**: verifies only that $X$ (and perhaps $\emptyset$) belongs to a candidate
  collection, without checking complement/union closure for every member, concluding it's a
  $\sigma$-algebra prematurely.
- **Birth type**: Type 5, instruction-induced (Blueprint's own declared FOUNDATIONAL severity —
  $X$ and $\emptyset$ membership feels like the defining check, obscuring that EVERY member must
  satisfy closure).
- **Repair**: re-check complement and union closure for every single member of the candidate
  collection, not just the two most obvious ones.

### MC-2: SIGMA-ALGEBRA-SEEN-AS-UNNECESSARY-FORMALISM
- **Surface form**: believes the $\sigma$-algebra restriction is arbitrary extra bookkeeping
  rather than a genuine mathematical necessity for uncountable sets.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared FOUNDATIONAL severity —
  the restriction never mattered for the finite examples seen so far, so it can feel like
  unmotivated formalism).
- **Repair**: re-anchor on the genuine fact that some subsets of $\mathbb R$ cannot be
  consistently measured at all — the restriction is forced, never conventional.

### MC-3: COUNTABLE-UNION-MISTAKEN-FOR-ARBITRARY-UNION
- **Surface form**: believes $\sigma$-algebras must be closed under ARBITRARY (possibly
  uncountable) unions, not just countable ones.
- **Birth type**: Type 3, language contamination (Blueprint's own declared Moderate severity —
  "closed under union" without the qualifier "countable" suggests an unrestricted claim).
- **Repair**: re-anchor on the precise axiom wording — countable union only; arbitrary unions of
  measurable sets need not be measurable in general.

## Analogies
- **"A σ-algebra is a guest list where every name must independently pass the same background
  check — having the host's own name on the list doesn't exempt anyone else."**
- **Anti-analogy**: the restriction to a σ-algebra is NOT a convenience or a convention — for
  uncountable spaces, it is mathematically forced, since some subsets provably cannot be measured
  consistently at all.

## Demonstrations
- **Demonstration 1 (targets MC-1)**: $\mathcal F=\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2,3\},\{1,2,3\}\}$ on
  $X=\{1,2,3\}$ passes all axioms directly; $\mathcal G=\{\emptyset,\{1\},\{2\},X\}$ FAILS —
  $\{1\}^c=\{2,3\}\notin\mathcal G$ — despite containing both $\emptyset$ and $X$.
- **Demonstration 2 (targets MC-2)**: the die-roll $\Omega=\{1,\dots,6\}$'s full power set (64
  subsets) trivially satisfies all axioms; for $X=[0,1]$, it is a genuine theorem that the full
  power set CANNOT support a consistent measure — the Borel $\sigma$-algebra is used instead.
- **Demonstration 3 (targets MC-3)**: the axioms require closure under COUNTABLE union only —
  an arbitrary (uncountable) union of measurable sets is never guaranteed to remain measurable in
  general.

## Discovery Questions
1. "Does a collection containing $X$ and $\emptyset$ automatically qualify as a $\sigma$-algebra?"
2. "Why can't we just use the full power set as the $\sigma$-algebra for every set $X$, including
   $\mathbb R$?"
3. "Must a $\sigma$-algebra be closed under unions of any size, or only countable ones?"

## Teaching Sequence
1. **Representation shift**: state the three axioms directly, working Example 1 (a genuine
   $\sigma$-algebra) alongside Example 2 (a failing collection) as a dual concrete verification.
2. **Conceptual shift/conflict evidence**: Demonstration 1's failing-collection check, isolating
   MC-1 by requiring every member's closure verified, not just $X$ and $\emptyset$.
3. **Contrast pair**: Demonstration 2's finite-versus-uncountable contrast, isolating MC-2 by
   requiring the genuine necessity of the restriction acknowledged.
4. **Conceptual shift**: Demonstration 3's countable-versus-arbitrary union distinction, isolating
   MC-3 by requiring the precise axiom wording honored.
5. **Mastery gate**: require a correct full axiom verification for a new candidate collection, a
   correct derivation of countable-intersection closure via De Morgan's law, and a correct
   explanation of why uncountable $X$ needs the restriction, at the Blueprint's own stated MAMR
   of 5/5 (⌈0.85×5⌉).
