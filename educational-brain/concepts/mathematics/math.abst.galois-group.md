## Learning Objective
Apply $|\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)|=[K:F]$ — recognizing this equality holds SPECIFICALLY for GALOIS
extensions and genuinely FAILS otherwise; compute $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)\cong
\mathbb Z_n$ concretely, generated by the FROBENIUS AUTOMORPHISM $\sigma(x)=x^p$ (the group's
generator, not merely one example element); and, at orientation level, recognize that Galois
groups are not always small and abelian — the generic quintic's Galois group is the full,
non-abelian $S_5$.

## Core Understanding
THE DEGREE EQUALITY REQUIRES THE GALOIS HYPOTHESIS, NOT JUST ANY EXTENSION:
`math.abst.galois-theory` already verified $|\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\mathbb Q)|=4=
[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb Q]$ on a Galois extension. This is NOT automatic for every finite
extension — $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb Q$ has degree 3 but is NOT Galois (it isn't a splitting
field: $x^3-2$'s other two roots are genuinely complex, absent from this entirely-real field). Any
automorphism must send $\sqrt[3]2$ to another root of $x^3-2$ still inside $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)$
— but the only such root available is $\sqrt[3]2$ itself, forcing
$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb Q)=\{\mathrm{id}\}$, order $1\ne3$. The equality
genuinely fails: it is a property of Galois extensions specifically, never automatic.

THE FROBENIUS AUTOMORPHISM GENERATES THE ENTIRE FINITE-FIELD GALOIS GROUP: for
$\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p$, $\sigma(x)=x^p$ is a genuine field automorphism fixing
$\mathbb F_p$ pointwise (every element of $\mathbb F_p$ satisfies $x^p=x$, the finite-field
analogue of Fermat's Little Theorem — reusing that already-mastered fact directly). Its powers
$\sigma,\sigma^2,\dots,\sigma^n=\mathrm{id}$ produce EVERY element of
$\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)\cong\mathbb Z_n$ — $\sigma$ is not one example
automorphism among unrelated others; it IS the generator of the whole cyclic group.

NOT EVERY GALOIS GROUP IS SMALL AND ABELIAN: the two concrete examples studied so far
($\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/2$ and $\mathbb Z_n$) are both abelian and small. For a GENERIC
degree-5 polynomial, the Galois group of its splitting field is the full symmetric group $S_5$
(order 120), genuinely non-abelian — exactly the group `math.abst.galois-theory`'s own
orientation-level discussion already identified as non-solvable, underlying Abel–Ruffini.

## Mental Models
- **"The degree equality $|\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)|=[K:F]$ is a PRIVILEGE of Galois extensions — it can
  genuinely collapse for a non-Galois extension of the very same degree."**
- **"Frobenius doesn't just sit in the Galois group — its powers ARE the Galois group."**

## Why Students Fail

### MC-1: GALOIS-DEGREE-EQUALITY-ASSUMED-UNIVERSAL
- **Surface form**: believes $|\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)|=[K:F]$ holds for EVERY finite field extension,
  regardless of whether it is Galois.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared Foundational severity —
  the one worked example seen so far ($\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\mathbb Q$) happened to be Galois,
  so the equality looks unconditional until a non-Galois counterexample is checked).
- **Repair**: re-walk the $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb Q$ counterexample directly, confirming
  order $1\ne3$.

### MC-2: FROBENIUS-TREATED-AS-MERE-EXAMPLE
- **Surface form**: believes the Frobenius automorphism is just one convenient element of
  $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/\mathbb F_p)$ among other, unrelated automorphisms.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared Foundational severity —
  Frobenius is introduced as "an" automorphism satisfying the fixing property, obscuring that its
  powers exhaust the entire group).
- **Repair**: re-compute $\sigma,\sigma^2,\dots,\sigma^n=\mathrm{id}$ explicitly, confirming every
  group element is some power of $\sigma$.

### MC-3: GALOIS-GROUPS-ASSUMED-ALWAYS-SMALL-AND-ABELIAN
- **Surface form**: believes all Galois groups resemble the small, abelian examples studied first
  (Klein four-group, cyclic groups).
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared Moderate severity — both
  examples encountered so far happen to be small and abelian).
- **Repair**: re-anchor on the $S_5$ generic-quintic case directly, confirming genuine
  non-abelian-ness and large order.

## Analogies
- **"Frobenius is the crank that turns out every gear in the machine — not one gear sitting
  alongside unrelated others."**
- **Anti-analogy**: a non-Galois extension of degree $n$ does NOT guarantee a Galois group of
  order $n$ — $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb Q$ has degree 3 but Galois group of order 1.

## Demonstrations
- **Demonstration 1 (targets MC-1)**: $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3)/\mathbb Q)$ has order
  $4=[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb Q]$ (Galois, equality holds) versus
  $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb Q)=\{\mathrm{id}\}$, order $1\ne3=
  [\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2):\mathbb Q]$ (not Galois — the other two roots of $x^3-2$ are complex,
  outside this real field) — direct proof the equality is conditional.
- **Demonstration 2 (targets MC-2)**: for $\mathbb F_8/\mathbb F_2$ ($p=2,n=3$), $\sigma(x)=x^2$;
  $\sigma^2(x)=x^4$; $\sigma^3(x)=x^8=x$ (every element of $\mathbb F_8$ satisfies $x^8=x$) — so
  $\sigma$ has order exactly 3, and $\{\mathrm{id},\sigma,\sigma^2\}$ IS the entire Galois group:
  every element is a power of $\sigma$.
- **Demonstration 3 (targets MC-3)**: the generic degree-5 polynomial's Galois group is $S_5$
  (order 120, non-abelian — two specific permutations composed in different orders give different
  results), in sharp contrast to $\mathbb Z/2\times\mathbb Z/2$ (order 4) and $\mathbb Z_3$
  (order 3), both abelian.

## Discovery Questions
1. "Does $|\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)|=[K:F]$ hold for every finite field extension, regardless of whether
   it's Galois?"
2. "Is the Frobenius automorphism just one example element of $\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb F_{p^n}/
   \mathbb F_p)$, among possibly many other unrelated automorphisms?"
3. "Do all Galois groups resemble the small, abelian examples studied so far?"

## Teaching Sequence
1. **Anchor**: connect to `math.abst.galois-theory`'s own $|\mathrm{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,
   \sqrt3)/\mathbb Q)|=4=[\mathbb Q(\sqrt2,\sqrt3):\mathbb Q]$ result, framing this concept as
   examining when that equality holds versus fails.
2. **Conflict evidence**: Demonstration 1's non-Galois counterexample, isolating MC-1 by requiring
   the Galois hypothesis be checked explicitly, never assumed.
3. **Representation shift**: Demonstration 2's explicit Frobenius power computation, isolating
   MC-2 by showing every group element is literally a power of $\sigma$.
4. **Contrast pair**: Demonstration 3's $S_5$ case against the two small abelian examples,
   isolating MC-3 by exposing genuine size and non-commutativity.
5. **Mastery gate**: require a correct explanation of why $\mathbb Q(\sqrt[3]2)/\mathbb Q$ fails
   to be Galois, a correct Frobenius-order computation for a new finite field, and a correct
   explanation connecting non-solvability to $S_5$'s non-abelian structure, at the Blueprint's own
   stated MAMR of 3/5 (⌈0.6×5⌉).

## Tutor Actions
- Never accept $|\mathrm{Gal}(K/F)|=[K:F]$ as automatic — require the Galois hypothesis to be
  checked explicitly for the extension at hand.
- Never accept Frobenius described as "an" automorphism without confirming its powers generate
  the entire group.

## Voice Teaching Notes
- Say "is this extension actually Galois, or are we assuming the equality without checking?"
  whenever the degree equality is invoked.
- When Frobenius appears, ask "does this one automorphism generate everything, or is it just
  sitting among others?"
