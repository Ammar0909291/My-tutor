## Learning Objective
Define a CYCLIC GROUP as one generated by a single element, $G=\langle g\rangle=\{g^n:n\in
\mathbb Z\}$, with every element some power (or integer multiple, additively) of the generator;
classify finite cyclic groups as isomorphic to $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ and the infinite cyclic
group as isomorphic to $(\mathbb Z,+)$; apply the gcd criterion to identify which elements of
$\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ are generators; and prove that every cyclic group is abelian, while
recognizing the converse is false.

## Core Understanding
A CYCLIC GROUP is one GENERATED BY A SINGLE ELEMENT: $G=\langle g\rangle=\{g^n:n\in\mathbb Z\}$
— every element of $G$ is SOME power of the generator $g$ (including negative powers $g^{-1},
g^{-2},\ldots$, and $g^0=e$). Two classification cases: a FINITE cyclic group of order $n$ is
isomorphic to $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$ (reusing `math.abst.group-theory`'s own inverse/identity
axioms, applied to the additive structure); an INFINITE cyclic group is isomorphic to
$(\mathbb Z,+)$ itself. The ORDER of an element is the smallest positive integer $k$ with
$g^k=e$ — if no such $k$ exists, the element has infinite order.

In $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, NOT EVERY ELEMENT IS A GENERATOR: an element $a$ generates the whole
group EXACTLY WHEN $\gcd(a,n)=1$ (verified as an empirical pattern across worked examples, not
formally proved here — the proof via Euler's $\varphi$-function belongs to `math.nt` territory).
In $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, $\gcd(1,6)=1$ and $\gcd(5,6)=1$ give the two generators; $\gcd(2,6)=2$
means $\langle2\rangle=\{0,2,4\}$, a proper subgroup, not the whole group.

EVERY CYCLIC GROUP IS ABELIAN: for $G=\langle g\rangle$, any two elements are $g^n$ and $g^m$ for
some integers $n,m$, and $g^n\cdot g^m=g^{n+m}=g^{m+n}=g^m\cdot g^n$ — commutativity of INTEGER
ADDITION forces commutativity of the group operation. The CONVERSE IS FALSE: the Klein four-group
$V_4=\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z\times\mathbb Z/2\mathbb Z$ is abelian (every element has order at most 2,
so all products commute trivially) but NOT cyclic — no single element has order 4, so no element
generates all 4 elements.

## Mental Models
- **"A cyclic group is completely described by one element and its powers — 'generated,' not
  'built from scratch.'"**
- **"Cyclic doesn't mean finite — $(\mathbb Z,+)=\langle1\rangle$ is cyclic and infinite."**
- **"Cyclic $\Rightarrow$ abelian always; abelian $\Rightarrow$ cyclic never guaranteed — $V_4$ is
  the standard counterexample."**

## Why Students Fail

### MC-1: CYCLIC-MEANS-FINITE
- **Surface form**: claims "cyclic group means a group that wraps around, so it must be finite,"
  missing that $(\mathbb Z,+)$ is cyclic and infinite.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared FOUNDATIONAL severity — the
  visual/verbal cue "cyclic" evokes a finite loop that returns to its start, and this everyday
  image is applied unmodified to the formal definition, which places no finiteness requirement).
- **Repair**: re-attempt the specific $(\mathbb Z,+)=\langle1\rangle$ example directly, confirming
  every integer is a power (multiple) of 1 despite the group being infinite.

### MC-2: EVERY-ELEMENT-IS-A-GENERATOR
- **Surface form**: claims any element of a cyclic group generates the whole group, missing that
  only elements coprime to $n$ generate $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared Secondary severity — the
  fact that a cyclic group HAS a generator is over-extended to "every element IS a generator,"
  conflating existence with universality).
- **Repair**: re-attempt the specific element's generated-subset computation directly (e.g. $\langle
  2\rangle$ in $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$ giving only $\{0,2,4\}$), confirming it falls short of the
  whole group.

### MC-3: CYCLIC-IMPLIES-NONABELIAN-POSSIBLE
- **Surface form**: thinks some cyclic groups might be non-abelian.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared Secondary severity — the
  general expectation that "most groups can be non-abelian" is carried forward into the cyclic
  case without checking whether the proof of commutativity genuinely holds there).
- **Repair**: re-attempt the specific commutativity proof directly, writing two arbitrary elements
  as $g^n,g^m$ and computing both products.

## Analogies
- **"A cyclic group is like a single gear tooth stamped into a die — every other tooth (element)
  is produced by repeating the SAME stamp (the generator), over and over."**
- **Anti-analogy**: "cyclic" does NOT mean "returns to where it started in finitely many steps" —
  $(\mathbb Z,+)$ never returns to 0 under repeated application of $+1$, yet is genuinely cyclic.

## Demonstrations
- **Demonstration 1 (targets MC-2, orders table)**: in $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$, computing the order
  of every element — $0$ (order 1), $1$ (order 6, generator), $2$ (order 3, generates $\{0,2,4\}$),
  $3$ (order 2, generates $\{0,3\}$), $4$ (order 3, generates $\{0,2,4\}$), $5$ (order 6, generator)
  — confirming generators are EXACTLY $\{1,5\}$, the elements coprime to 6.
- **Demonstration 2 (targets MC-1)**: $\mathbb Z=\langle1\rangle$ — every positive integer $n$ is
  $1+1+\cdots+1$ ($n$ times), every negative integer is $(-1)+(-1)+\cdots$; the group is genuinely
  cyclic despite being infinite.
- **Demonstration 3 (targets MC-3, the proof)**: for $G=\langle g\rangle$, taking arbitrary
  $a=g^n$, $b=g^m$: $a\cdot b=g^n\cdot g^m=g^{n+m}=g^{m+n}=g^m\cdot g^n=b\cdot a$ — commutativity
  of integer addition forces the group operation to commute.
- **Demonstration 4 (contrast pair, cyclic vs. non-cyclic abelian)**: $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$
  (cyclic, generator 1) against $V_4=\{e,a,b,c\}$ with every non-identity element of order 2 —
  no element of $V_4$ has order 4, so no single element generates all 4 elements; $V_4$ is abelian
  but genuinely not cyclic.

## Discovery Questions
1. "Does a cyclic group have to be finite, or could it have infinitely many elements?"
2. "If $G=\langle g\rangle$, does every element of $G$ generate the whole group, or only some?"
3. "Could a cyclic group fail to be abelian?"

## Teaching Sequence
1. **Anchor**: connect to `math.abst.group-theory`'s own axioms, framing a cyclic group as the
   SPECIAL case where a single element and its powers exhaust the entire group.
2. **Conflict evidence**: the $(\mathbb Z,+)=\langle1\rangle$ example, directly challenging MC-1 by
   showing an infinite cyclic group.
3. **Contrast pair**: the generator $1$ (order 6, generates all of $\mathbb Z/6\mathbb Z$) against
   the non-generator $2$ (order 3, generates only $\{0,2,4\}$), isolating MC-2 directly; $\mathbb
   Z/6\mathbb Z$ (cyclic) against $V_4$ (abelian but not cyclic), isolating MC-3 by proving cyclic
   $\Rightarrow$ abelian while showing the converse fails.
4. **Mastery gate**: require a correct proof that every cyclic group is abelian, a correct gcd-based
   identification of all generators of a specific $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$, and a correct application
   of the cyclic-group concept to a new setting (the $n$th roots of unity), at the Blueprint's own
   stated MAMR of 5/5 (⌈0.9×5⌉).

## Tutor Actions
- Never accept "cyclic groups are finite" as a general claim — require a specific check of whether
  the group's own generator produces finitely or infinitely many distinct powers.
- Never accept "element $a$ generates the group" without the gcd (or direct enumeration) check —
  require the learner to list the generated subset explicitly.

## Voice Teaching Notes
- Say "does that generator's powers ever stop producing NEW elements, or do they run forever?"
  whenever MC-1 is suspected.
- When a generator is claimed, ask "does that element's own subgroup include every element of $G$,
  or just some?"

## Assessment Signals
- **Rung 1 (recognition)**: learner correctly computes the order of every element in a specific
  finite cyclic group and identifies which are generators.
- **Rung 2 (application)**: learner correctly proves that every cyclic group is abelian using
  arbitrary elements $g^n,g^m$.
- **Rung 3 (transfer)**: learner correctly applies the cyclic-group definition to a NEW setting (the
  $n$th roots of unity under multiplication), verifying closure, identity, inverse, and generation
  by a single element, and identifies the resulting isomorphism to $\mathbb Z/n\mathbb Z$.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy
- If MC-1 recurs, re-attempt the specific $(\mathbb Z,+)=\langle1\rangle$ example directly.
- If MC-2 recurs, re-attempt the specific element's generated-subset computation directly.
- If MC-3 recurs, re-attempt the specific commutativity proof directly.

## Memory Hooks
- "$\langle g\rangle$ means every element is a power of $g$ — finite or infinite, both count."
- "Generator means gcd with the group's order equals 1 — not every element qualifies."
- "Cyclic always gives abelian — abelian never guarantees cyclic ($V_4$ is the counterexample)."
