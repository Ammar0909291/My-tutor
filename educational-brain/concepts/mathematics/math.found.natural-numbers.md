## Learning Objective

The learner can: state both standard conventions for ℕ (NCERT:
{1,2,3,…}; ISO 80000-2: {0,1,2,3,…}) and correctly declare which
convention is in use rather than treating either as universally
correct; state the five Peano axioms and explain what each rules out
(cycles, branching, "starting" the successor chain twice); derive the
canonical total order on ℕ from the successor function and correctly
apply the well-ordering principle to distinguish ℕ from ℤ and ℚ; and
compute simple sums via the Peano recursive definition of addition,
correctly terminating at the base case.

## Core Understanding

`math.found.set-theory` already supplies set membership and
set-builder notation; `math.found.total-order` already supplies the
RATT (reflexive/antisymmetric/transitive/total) axioms and "least
element" terminology. ℕ is the set generated axiomatically by the five
**Peano axioms**: (P1) 0∈ℕ; (P2) ∀n∈ℕ, S(n)∈ℕ (successor closure); (P3)
∀n∈ℕ, S(n)≠0 (0 is not a successor); (P4) ∀m,n∈ℕ, S(m)=S(n)⟹m=n
(injectivity — rules out cycles and branching); (P5) if P(0) holds and
P(n)⟹P(S(n)) for all n, then P(n) holds for all n∈ℕ (induction — the
direct prerequisite for `math.found.proof-by-induction`). Every natural
number is a definite, finite number of applications of S to 0: 1:=S(0),
2:=S(S(0)), 3:=S(S(S(0))), and so on. The canonical **total order** on
ℕ (reusing `math.found.total-order`'s own RATT framework) is derived
from succession: m<n iff n=S^k(m) for some k≥1. This order satisfies
the **well-ordering principle**: every non-empty subset S⊆ℕ has a least
element — the property that most sharply distinguishes ℕ from ℤ (where
{n∈ℤ:n<0} has no minimum) and from ℚ (where no positive rational has a
smallest value above it). Arithmetic is DERIVED from the Peano
structure rather than assumed: addition is defined recursively by
a+0=a (base) and a+S(b)=S(a+b) (step), so a+n=S^n(a); multiplication is
then defined from addition by a×0=0 and a×S(b)=(a×b)+a. These
recursive definitions are what makes `math.found.proof-by-induction`
possible at all — P5 is not a redundant restatement of "you can count
forever," but the specific axiom that licenses proving a claim for
EVERY natural number from a base case and a single inductive step,
rather than only verifying it for however many specific cases one has
patience to check.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "natural numbers are just the counting numbers
  1, 2, 3, … (or 0, 1, 2, … — whichever I was taught), and that's a
  complete description"**: the learner has a working operational sense
  of ℕ from years of counting practice, with no awareness that the
  zero-inclusion question is a genuine, unresolved-by-convention choice,
  nor that anything beyond the informal list is needed. Shelf-life
  warning: this model gives no way to prove a claim about ALL natural
  numbers, only to check finitely many cases.
- **Intermediate model — "ℕ is generated by starting at a base element
  and repeatedly applying a successor function, with rules preventing
  cycles or repeats — this is what informal counting was really doing
  all along"**: the learner correctly restates counting in Peano terms
  and knows to declare a zero-convention explicitly, but may not yet see
  why the induction axiom (P5) specifically, rather than the other four,
  is what licenses inductive proof. Upgrade trigger: being asked to
  prove a claim true for the first million natural numbers by
  computation and asked whether that constitutes a proof for ALL of ℕ.
- **Advanced model — "the five Peano axioms, especially P5, are what
  make ℕ a rigorously defined mathematical object rather than an
  informal list, well-ordering distinguishes ℕ from ℤ and ℚ precisely,
  and all of arithmetic is recursively derivable from succession
  alone"**: the learner fluently moves between the axiomatic
  characterization, the well-ordering property, and the recursive
  arithmetic definitions, and can explain why each is necessary rather
  than merely restating informal counting facts. Upgrade trigger: being
  asked to state one mathematical consequence of well-ordering that
  holds for ℕ but fails for ℤ.
- **Do not upgrade early**: a learner still treating the zero-convention
  as a fixed universal fact (beginner model) should not be pushed into
  reasoning about well-ordering as ℕ's distinguishing property (advanced
  model) before the convention-is-a-choice point (MC-1) is fully
  resolved — MC-1 is FOUNDATIONAL per the Blueprint's own MAMR and must
  clear before MC-2 or MC-3 in all repair routing.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant, FOUNDATIONAL failure treats one zero-inclusion convention
(most often NCERT's {1,2,3,…}, from Indian school curricula) as the
single correct, universal definition of ℕ, rather than recognizing that
0's membership is a declared CONVENTION CHOICE that varies by context
(ISO 80000-2 and most university mathematics include 0) — this becomes
a genuine source of proof errors once a learner works across sources
using different conventions without noticing the mismatch. A second
failure dismisses the Peano axioms as adding nothing beyond informal
counting experience, missing specifically that P5 (induction) is the
formal license for proving claims about ALL natural numbers — without
it, a claim verified for a million specific cases remains, strictly,
unproven for the rest of ℕ. A third failure conflates "well-ordered"
(every non-empty subset has a MINIMUM) with "finite" or "has a
maximum," missing that ℕ is simultaneously infinite AND well-ordered —
well-ordering says nothing whatsoever about a set having a greatest
element.

## Misconceptions

Reusing the Blueprint's Misconception Registry, birth-type
classification added per this program's diagnostic procedure — not
re-derived:

### MC-1: ZERO-MEMBERSHIP (Foundational; Type 3 — language contamination)
**Trigger**: "Zero is not a natural number" or "ℕ has only one
definition," asserted as a universal fact rather than a convention
choice.
**Diagnostic note**: classified Type 3 (language/notation
contamination) rather than Type 1 overgeneralization — the learner is
not overgeneralizing from limited examples but importing one
authoritative-sounding source's terminology (their school curriculum's
"ℕ = {1,2,3,…}") as though it were the unique meaning of the symbol
ℕ itself, exactly the mechanism Type 3 names.
**Repair**: present the contrast table directly — NCERT: ℕ={1,2,3,…}
(Indian school curriculum); ISO 80000-2: ℕ={0,1,2,…} (international
standard, most university texts); ℕ⁺/ℤ⁺ always excludes 0 explicitly;
ℕ₀ always includes 0 explicitly. "0's membership in ℕ is a convention
choice, not a mathematical truth — always declare which one you're
using." This course adopts ISO: 0∈ℕ.
**MAMR**: FOUNDATIONAL — must be cleared before MC-2 or MC-3 in all
repair routing, per the Blueprint's own explicit MAMR declaration.

### MC-2: PEANO-INFORMAL (Moderate; Type 1 — overgeneralization)
**Trigger**: "Natural numbers are just what you count — Peano axioms
add nothing," dismissing the formal axioms as redundant restatement.
**Repair**: without P3 (0 is not a successor) and P4 (injectivity), a
"number system" {a,b,c} with S(a)=b, S(b)=c, S(c)=a would satisfy P1-P2
(base and successor-closure) while being a CYCLE — informal counting
never explicitly rules this out, but P3-P4 do. Separately: "0+n=n for
all n∈ℕ" can be verified by computation for n=0,1,2,…,1000, but proving
it for ALL n requires P5 specifically — base 0+0=0 holds directly; step
"if 0+n=n then 0+S(n)=S(0+n)=S(n)" holds by the recursive definition of
addition; P5 then licenses concluding the claim for every n∈ℕ, not
merely the checked cases.

### MC-3: WELL-ORDER-FINITE (Moderate; Type 1 — overgeneralization)
**Trigger**: "Well-ordered means finite" or "ℕ must end somewhere
because it has a minimum."
**Repair**: well-ordering requires only that every non-empty subset has
a MINIMUM — it says nothing about a maximum. ℕ={0,1,2,…} is well-ordered
(every non-empty S⊆ℕ has a least element) AND infinite simultaneously.
Contrast: ℤ is infinite but NOT well-ordered ({n∈ℤ:n<0} has no
minimum); {1,…,1000} is finite and (trivially) well-ordered; ℚ⁺ is
infinite and not well-ordered (no smallest positive rational). A
minimum is not a maximum, and well-ordering is compatible with
infinitude.

## Analogies

**Primary — an infinite staircase with a first step**: the Peano
axioms define ℕ like a staircase with a definite first step (0), where
from every step there is exactly one "next" step (its successor), no
two steps share the same next step (ruling out merges), and the
staircase is the smallest such structure reachable by climbing from the
first step — capturing P1 through P5 concretely, including P5's
guarantee that climbing from the first step, one step at a time,
eventually reaches every step (the basis of induction).

**Anti-analogy to retire**: "The Peano axioms are just a fancy way of
writing down what counting already does, so they're optional
formalism." This directly invites MC-2 by suggesting the axioms add
nothing beyond informal counting practice, missing specifically that
P5 is the load-bearing axiom that makes inductive proof valid at all —
without a formal statement of P5, "verified for the first million
cases" and "true for all n∈ℕ" remain logically distinct claims.

## Demonstrations

**Nested successor notation**: 1:=S(0); 2:=S(1)=S(S(0)); 3:=S(2)=
S(S(S(0))) — every natural number is a definite, finite chain of S
applications from 0.

**Injectivity rules out cycles**: without P4, {a,b,c} with S(a)=b,
S(b)=c, S(c)=a satisfies P1-P3 but is a 3-cycle, not ℕ; P4 (S(m)=S(n)
⟹m=n) rules this out because if two elements shared a successor, they
would have to be equal.

**Well-ordering contrast table**: ℕ well-ordered (every non-empty
subset has a minimum) and infinite; ℤ infinite but NOT well-ordered
({n∈ℤ:n<0} has no minimum); (0,1)∩ℚ not well-ordered (no smallest
rational strictly above 0); {1,…,100} finite and well-ordered.

**Peano recursive addition**: 3+2 = 3+S(1) = S(3+1) = S(3+S(0)) =
S(S(3+0)) = S(S(3)) = S(4) = 5, terminating at the base case a+0=a at
each nested step.

## Discovery Questions

Present the informal counting sequence 1,2,3,… and ask the learner what
rule would prevent someone from claiming the sequence "wraps around" and
repeats after some point — the learner discovers, largely unaided, that
"no two counts can lead to the same next count" (P4, injectivity) and
"the count never returns to the start" (P3) are exactly the guarantees
needed, before these are named formally. Recommendation: guided
discovery for the injectivity/no-cycles insight (directly experiential
from the counting sequence itself); direct instruction for the
zero-convention contrast (MC-1) and the formal well-ordering-vs-ℤ
distinction (MC-3), since both require declared external authorities
(ISO vs. NCERT; the ℤ counterexample) rather than being independently
rediscoverable.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Likeliest utterance: "wait, is zero a natural number or not — my
teacher said no" — the concept-specific smaller question: "which
textbook or standard is your teacher's definition from, and have you
seen a different one anywhere else (a university text, this course)?"
reframes the confusion from "there is one right answer I'm missing"
(treating MC-1 as a factual gap) to "there are two standard, both
internally consistent conventions, and the skill is declaring which one
you're using," directly isolating MC-1's language-contamination root
cause without contradicting the learner's teacher.
