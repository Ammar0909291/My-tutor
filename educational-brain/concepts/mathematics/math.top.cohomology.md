## Learning Objective
Define $H^n(X)=\mathrm{Hom}(H_n(X),\mathbb{Z})$ as the DUAL construction — NEVER just another
name or notation for $H_n(X)$; recognize the cup product as GENUINELY NEW ring structure — NEVER
assume cohomology's groups alone carry the same information as homology; and recognize de Rham
cohomology and singular cohomology as ISOMORPHIC via de Rham's theorem — NEVER unrelated
invariants that merely share a name.

## Core Understanding
$H^n(X)$ IS THE DUAL GROUP $\mathrm{Hom}(H_n(X),\mathbb{Z})$ — NEVER JUST ANOTHER NAME FOR
$H_n(X)$: for $X=S^1$: $H_0(S^1)=\mathbb{Z}$, $H_1(S^1)=\mathbb{Z}$ (torsion-free). The universal
coefficient theorem gives $H^0(S^1)=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}$ and
$H^1(S^1)=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{Z})=\mathbb{Z}$ — numerically the SAME groups in this
torsion-free case, but CONCEPTUALLY different objects: $H^1(S^1)$ consists of homomorphisms FROM
$H_1(S^1)$ TO $\mathbb{Z}$ (ways of "measuring" the loop), never the loop-classes themselves.
Believing $H^n(X)$ is just another notation for $H_n(X)$, computed the same way, is WRONG — it is
defined as the DUAL group, a conceptually different construction, even when the resulting groups
happen to look numerically identical.

THE CUP PRODUCT IS GENUINELY NEW RING STRUCTURE — NEVER JUST THE SAME GROUPS PRESENTED
DIFFERENTLY: for $X=T^2$ (torus): $H^1(T^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}^2$, generated by $\alpha,\beta$. The
cup product $\alpha\smile\beta\in H^2(T^2)\cong\mathbb{Z}$ is NONZERO (a generator), while
$\alpha\smile\alpha=0$ and $\beta\smile\beta=0$ (forced by anticommutativity on odd degree:
$\alpha\smile\beta=-\beta\smile\alpha$, so $2(\alpha\smile\alpha)=0$, and torsion-freeness gives
$\alpha\smile\alpha=0$). This multiplicative DISTINCTION has NO counterpart in bare homology
groups. Believing cohomology's groups alone carry the same information as homology, just
presented differently, is WRONG — the cup product makes $H^*(X)=\bigoplus_nH^n(X)$ a genuine
RING, additional algebraic structure with no natural homology-side analogue.

DE RHAM COHOMOLOGY AND SINGULAR COHOMOLOGY ARE ISOMORPHIC — NEVER UNRELATED INVARIANTS SHARING A
NAME: on $S^1$, the $1$-form $d\theta$ is CLOSED ($d(d\theta)=0$ trivially) but NOT EXACT (no
globally-defined smooth $\theta$ exists on $S^1$ itself — going around, $\theta$ increases by
$2\pi$) — so $[d\theta]$ is a NONZERO class in $H^1_{\mathrm{dR}}(S^1)$, matching the nonzero
singular class $H^1(S^1;\mathbb{R})\cong\mathbb{R}$ found combinatorially. Believing de Rham
cohomology (differential forms) and singular cohomology (chain complexes) are fundamentally
different invariants that merely happen to share a name is WRONG — de Rham's theorem PROVES
$H^n_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\cong H^n(X;\mathbb{R})$: two utterly different-looking constructions
(analytic versus combinatorial) computing the IDENTICAL invariant.

## Mental Models
- **"Cohomology isn't a rebranding of homology — H^n(X) is the group of homomorphisms FROM H_n(X)
  TO ℤ, a genuinely dual construction, even when the numbers match."**
- **"If cohomology were just homology with a different name, there'd be nothing new to compute —
  the cup product reveals structure homology alone simply cannot see."**
- **"De Rham and singular cohomology aren't competing definitions to pick between — de Rham's
  theorem guarantees they compute the identical invariant by completely different machinery."**

## Why Students Fail

### MC-1: COHOMOLOGY-ASSUMED-SAME-AS-HOMOLOGY
- **Surface form**: believes $H^n(X)$ is just another name or notation for $H_n(X)$, missing that
  it is the dual group $\mathrm{Hom}(H_n(X),\mathbb{Z})$, a conceptually different construction.
- **Birth type**: overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared foundational severity — in
  torsion-free cases the groups look numerically identical, inviting the conflation).
- **Repair**: re-walk Example 1's dualization of $S^1$'s homology, re-anchoring on "$H^n(X)$ is
  the dual group, a conceptually different construction."

### MC-2: COHOMOLOGY-ASSUMED-NO-EXTRA-INFORMATION
- **Surface form**: believes cohomology's groups alone carry the same information as homology,
  presented differently, missing that the cup product gives genuinely new ring structure.
- **Birth type**: instruction-induced (Blueprint's own declared high severity — cohomology is
  often introduced group-by-group before the cup product is emphasized).
- **Repair**: re-walk Example 2's cup-product distinction on the torus, re-anchoring on "the cup
  product is genuinely new structure homology alone cannot see."

### MC-3: DE-RHAM-ASSUMED-UNRELATED-INVARIANT
- **Surface form**: believes de Rham cohomology and singular cohomology are fundamentally
  different invariants that merely share a name, missing that de Rham's theorem proves them
  isomorphic.
- **Birth type**: perceptual intuition (Blueprint's own declared moderate severity — one
  construction is analytic and one is combinatorial, looking entirely unrelated on the surface).
- **Repair**: re-walk Example 3's analytic-versus-combinatorial computation of $H^1(S^1)$,
  re-anchoring on "de Rham's theorem proves these are isomorphic, the same invariant by different
  methods."

## Analogies
- **"Cohomology is homology's dual mirror — related, but a genuinely different vantage point,
  like a shadow versus the object casting it."**
- **Anti-analogy**: de Rham cohomology and singular cohomology aren't rival theories where you
  pick a favorite — they're two roads proven to arrive at the exact same destination.

## Demonstrations
- **Demonstration 1 (targets MC-1)**: the $S^1$ homology-to-cohomology dualization via the
  universal coefficient theorem.
- **Demonstration 2 (targets MC-2)**: the torus's cup-product computation, $\alpha\smile\beta\neq0$
  versus $\alpha\smile\alpha=0$.
- **Demonstration 3 (targets MC-3)**: the $S^1$ de Rham-versus-singular $H^1$ computation via
  $d\theta$.

## Discovery Questions
1. "Is H^n(X) just another name or notation for H_n(X), computed the same way?"
2. "Does cohomology, as a collection of groups H^n(X), carry exactly the same information as
   homology, just presented differently?"
3. "Are de Rham cohomology and singular cohomology fundamentally different invariants that merely
   happen to have similar names?"

## Teaching Sequence
1. **Representation shift**: work the $S^1$ dualization example, isolating MC-1.
2. **Conflict evidence**: work the torus cup-product example, isolating MC-2.
3. **Contrast pair**: work the $S^1$ de Rham-versus-singular contrast, isolating MC-3.
4. **Mastery gate**: require a correct statement of the torsion-free universal coefficient
   theorem, a correct computation of $H^0,H^1,H^2$ for $S^2$, a correct explanation of why the cup
   product carries extra information, and a correct explanation of de Rham's theorem's content, at
   the Blueprint's own stated MAMR of 3/5.

## Tutor Actions
- Never accept $H^n(X)$ treated as merely a relabeling of $H_n(X)$.
- Never accept cohomology's information content assessed from its groups alone, without
  mentioning the cup product.
- Never accept de Rham and singular cohomology treated as unrelated invariants.

## Voice Teaching Notes
- Say "is that H_n itself, or the group of homomorphisms out of it?" whenever $H^n(X)$ is
  introduced.
- Ask "does the cup product distinguish anything the bare groups can't?" whenever cohomology's
  informational content is discussed.

## Assessment Signals
- **Rung 1 (recognition)**: learner correctly computes $H^n(X)$ from $H_n(X)$ via the

  spaces with the same cohomology group ranks.
- **Rung 3 (transfer)**: learner correctly explains why cohomology-group ranks alone wouldn't
