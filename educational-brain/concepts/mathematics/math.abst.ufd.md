## Learning Objective
Define a UNIQUE FACTORIZATION DOMAIN (UFD) as an integral domain where every nonzero, non-unit
element factors into irreducibles UNIQUELY (up to order and units), recognizing this as EXACTLY
`math.nt.fundamental-theorem-arithmetic`'s own statement restated in general ring-theoretic
language; state and apply "every PID is a UFD," reusing `math.abst.pid`'s own structural facts
directly as the proof mechanism; and recognize UFD as a STRICTLY BROADER class than PID, using
$\mathbb Z[x]$ (a UFD, by Gauss's Lemma, but NOT a PID) as the separating example.

## Core Understanding
A UFD IS DEFINED BY THE UNIQUE-FACTORIZATION PROPERTY ITSELF: an integral domain $R$ is a Unique
Factorization Domain if every nonzero, non-unit element factors into irreducibles, and this
factorization is UNIQUE up to reordering the factors and multiplying by units. This directly
generalizes the familiar integer case — $\mathbb Z$'s elements factor into primes uniquely up to
order and sign ($\pm1$ being $\mathbb Z$'s units) — which is EXACTLY
`math.nt.fundamental-theorem-arithmetic`'s own statement, restated in this concept's general
ring-theoretic language: $\mathbb Z$ is an integral domain whose irreducibles are exactly the
primes, and factorization is unique up to order and units.

EVERY PID IS A UFD — `math.abst.pid`'s OWN STRUCTURAL FACTS ARE THE PROOF MECHANISM:
`math.abst.pid` already establishes that in a PID, every prime ideal is maximal and irreducibles
equal primes. These facts are PRECISELY what makes the unique-factorization argument work —
existence of a factorization follows from a PID's ascending-chain condition on ideals, and
UNIQUENESS follows from irreducibles being prime (a prime element dividing a product must divide
one of the factors). "Every PID is a UFD" is therefore NOT an independent new fact requiring
separate machinery — it is a direct consequence of structure `math.abst.pid` already proved.

UFD IS A STRICTLY BROADER CLASS — $\mathbb Z[x]$ IS THE STANDARD SEPARATING EXAMPLE: the
converse of "every PID is a UFD" is FALSE. $\mathbb Z[x]$ IS a UFD (a consequence of Gauss's
Lemma) but is NOT a PID — the ideal $(2,x)$ (all polynomials with even constant term) cannot be
generated by a single element, so it fails to be principal. This single example shows the UFD
property is strictly WEAKER than (implied by, but not equivalent to) the PID property.

## Mental Models
- **"UFD is the GENERAL name for 'unique factorization' — the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic
  is simply that property applied to $\mathbb Z$ specifically, not a separate, merely similar
  fact."**
- **"PID status IMMEDIATELY hands you UFD status — no separate factorization-uniqueness proof is
  ever needed once PID structure is already established."**

## Why Students Fail

### MC-1: FTA-TREATED-AS-MERELY-ANALOGOUS-TO-UFD
- **Surface form**: believes the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic is merely SIMILAR to the UFD
  definition, rather than LITERALLY an instance of it applied to $\mathbb Z$.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared Foundational severity —
  the two statements are encountered in different courses/contexts with different vocabulary
  ("primes" vs. "irreducibles"), so a learner treats the surface-level dissimilarity as
  indicating a genuinely different, merely parallel fact rather than a direct restatement).
- **Repair**: re-walk the direct term-by-term restatement of FTA using UFD language (irreducibles,
  uniqueness up to order and units) explicitly.

### MC-2: PID-TO-UFD-ASSUMED-TO-NEED-SEPARATE-VERIFICATION
- **Surface form**: believes confirming UFD status for an already-known PID requires a SEPARATE,
  independent factorization-uniqueness check, missing that PID status ALONE already guarantees
  it.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared High severity — most new
  ring-theoretic properties genuinely DO require independent verification, so this general
  pattern is over-applied to a case where the implication is already proven).
- **Repair**: re-invoke the "every PID is a UFD" theorem directly for the specific ring in
  question, citing its already-established PID structural facts as sufficient.

### MC-3: PID-AND-UFD-ASSUMED-EQUIVALENT-CLASSES
- **Surface form**: believes PID and UFD are ESSENTIALLY THE SAME class of rings, missing the
  genuine strict separation $\mathbb Z[x]$ demonstrates.
- **Birth type**: Type 1, overgeneralization (Blueprint's own declared Moderate severity — since
  "every PID is a UFD" is proven and every concrete example encountered so far happens to be
  both, the one-directional implication is mistaken for a two-directional equivalence).
- **Repair**: re-verify the $(2,x)$ non-principal-ideal proof directly in $\mathbb Z[x]$,
  re-anchoring on the genuine strict separation.

## Analogies
- **"UFD is to `math.nt.fundamental-theorem-arithmetic` as 'vehicle' is to 'car' — the general
  ring-theoretic concept, of which the integer case is one specific, already-familiar
  instance."**
- **Anti-analogy**: UFD does NOT mean "has a norm function like a Euclidean Domain" — UFD is a
  strictly broader property than either PID or Euclidean Domain, reachable even without any
  Euclidean structure.

## Demonstrations
- **Demonstration 1 (targets LO1/MC-1)**: `math.nt.fundamental-theorem-arithmetic`'s own
  statement, restated directly — $\mathbb Z$'s irreducibles are exactly the primes (and their
  negatives); every nonzero, non-unit integer factors into these uniquely up to reordering and
  unit multiplication, e.g. $12=2\times2\times3=(-2)\times(-2)\times3$ — confirming $\mathbb Z$
  IS a UFD, exactly the Fundamental Theorem's own content.
- **Demonstration 2 (targets LO2/MC-2)**: `math.abst.pid`'s own already-established PID structure
  for $\mathbb Z$ (irreducibles equal primes, prime ideals maximal) IMMEDIATELY implies UFD
  status via "every PID is a UFD" — no separate factorization-uniqueness proof needed; the PID
  structure already verified IS the proof.
- **Demonstration 3 (targets LO3/MC-3)**: in $\mathbb Z[x]$, the ideal $I=(2,x)$ (polynomials
  with even constant term) is NOT principal — any candidate generator $g$ dividing both $2$ and
  $x$ is forced to $g=\pm1$, giving $I=\mathbb Z[x]$, a contradiction since $1\notin I$. So
  $\mathbb Z[x]$ is NOT a PID, yet it IS a UFD (via Gauss's Lemma) — confirming the strict
  separation.

## Discovery Questions
1. "Is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic a separate fact merely analogous to the UFD
   definition, or literally an instance of it applied to $\mathbb Z$?"
2. "If a ring is already known to be a PID, does confirming it is also a UFD require a separate,
   independent factorization-uniqueness verification?"
3. "Does 'every PID is a UFD' mean the two classes of rings are essentially the same, or can a
   ring be a UFD without being a PID?"
