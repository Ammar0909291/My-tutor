# Sentence Stress — `eng.phonetics.sentence-stress`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.phonetics.sentence-stress` (canonical English
  KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / phonetics / "Sentence Stress" —
  the direct extension of word-level stress up to the level of the
  whole sentence, teaching that WHICH WORDS receive prominence, not
  merely which SYLLABLES, follows a predictable, learnable pattern.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.phonetics.syllable-stress` — specifically
  the multi-cue (length/pitch/vowel-quality) understanding of stress
  and schwa-reduction recognition; load-bearing because sentence
  stress is the identical acoustic phenomenon operating one level up —
  a learner who cannot yet reliably perceive or produce word-level
  stress and schwa reduction has no foundation for perceiving or
  producing the analogous pattern across a whole sentence.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.phonetics.intonation-patterns`. Pitch
  contour across a sentence (the subject of intonation) is built on top
  of, and closely interacts with, the stress pattern this concept
  establishes — stressed words are typically also where pitch
  movement is concentrated, so intonation cannot be taught in isolation
  from an already-secure sense of which words carry stress.
- **Difficulty**: proficient · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.75 · **Est. hours**: 2

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify content words (nouns, main verbs, adjectives, adverbs) and
   function words (articles, prepositions, auxiliaries, pronouns,
   conjunctions) within a sentence.
2. Produce a sentence with natural stress, stressing content words and
   reducing function words, including schwa reduction on common
   function words.
3. Contrast an unnaturally flat, equal-stress reading of a sentence
   against its naturally-stressed counterpart and identify which
   sounds like genuine spoken English.
4. Correctly shift stress for a word whose grammatical role changes
   between sentences (e.g., "can" as auxiliary vs. noun).
5. Produce and explain contrastive or emphatic stress on a normally-
   unstressed function word.

## Core Understanding

English sentences are not stressed evenly across their words — content
words (the words carrying the sentence's core semantic meaning) receive
stress, while function words (the grammatical "glue" connecting them)
are typically unstressed and frequently reduced in vowel quality (e.g.,
"to" becomes /tə/, "and" becomes /ən/). This is the direct sentence-
level extension of the syllable-stress concept's own core insight,
scaled up from within-word to within-sentence prominence. A second,
equally load-bearing fact is that the content/function classification
is a strong DEFAULT pattern, not a fixed, unchangeable label attached
permanently to each word — the same word can shift category depending
on its grammatical role in a given sentence (auxiliary "can" versus
noun "can," meaning a container), and a speaker can deliberately stress
a normally-unstressed function word for contrastive or emphatic
purposes ("I did NOT say that"). This flexibility is not an exception
that breaks the pattern — it is a genuine, rule-governed communicative
tool operating ON TOP of the default pattern, and recognizing it is
part of full mastery, not a caveat to memorize separately.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "every word in a sentence should get roughly the
  same emphasis, like careful word-by-word reading"**: the arriving,
  pre-instructional model this concept exists to correct — a natural
  outcome of careful, deliberate reading habits (or early L2 production)
  that has not yet been recalibrated toward the uneven rhythm of fluent
  spoken English.
- **Intermediate model — "I can sort a sentence's words into content
  (stressed) and function (reduced) categories, and produce the
  resulting uneven rhythm"**: installed by TA-1's identification task
  and TA-2's production task, using the Component 3 stress-beat
  sentence walk to physically embody the uneven rhythm. Upgrade
  trigger: producing a sentence with genuinely reduced (schwa-shifted)
  function words, not merely quieter versions of the full vowel.
- **Advanced model — "flat, equal stress sounds unnatural; the content/
  function pattern is what real spoken English actually relies on"**:
  installed by TA-3's direct side-by-side contrast, resolving
  MC-EVERY-WORD-GETS-EQUAL-STRESS through felt, audible comparison
  rather than an abstract rule statement. Upgrade trigger: correctly
  and spontaneously identifying a flat, equal-stress rendition as
  sounding unnatural, without being told which version to prefer.
- **Expert model — "the content/function split is a flexible default —
  grammatical role and speaker intent (emphasis, contrast) can both
  shift which words are stressed in a specific sentence"**: installed
  by TA-4 (grammatical-role shift) and TA-5 (contrastive/emphatic
  stress), resolving MC-CONTENT-FUNCTION-SPLIT-IS-FIXED-PER-WORD. This
  is the concept's genuine mastery bar — recognizing stress
  flexibility as a rule-governed tool, not treating any deviation from
  the default as an unexplained exception.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own explicit Adaptive
  Flag, the content/function default (TA-1/TA-2) is "the single most
  important pattern to establish first," before ANY of the contextual
  nuance (TA-4/TA-5) is introduced — a learner not yet reliably
  applying the basic default pattern should not be given grammatical-
  role-shift or emphatic-stress examples, which would layer genuine
  flexibility on top of a foundation not yet secure.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here. The equal-stress
failure exists because careful, word-by-word speech (common in formal
reading, early language instruction, or deliberate L2 production) is a
genuinely reasonable strategy for maximizing clarity and correctness
per word, and nothing in that strategy signals it is producing
unnatural rhythm — the learner is not applying a wrong rule, they are
extending an entirely sensible clarity-maximizing habit into a context
(natural conversational speech) where a different, uneven rhythm is
actually expected. The fixed-category failure exists because the
content/function distinction, once correctly learned as a default, is a
genuinely useful and largely reliable heuristic — a learner reasonably
treats a heuristic that has worked consistently so far as an
unconditional rule, with no signal (until this concept's own TA-4/TA-5)
that grammatical role or communicative intent can override the default
in specific, principled ways.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.phonetics.sentence-stress.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-EVERY-WORD-GETS-EQUAL-STRESS (Blueprint Component 1) — Type 2,
perceptual intuition**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: this is a habit-transfer effect rather
  than a wrongly-taught rule — careful, deliberate speech (the register
  most learners default to when consciously monitoring their own
  pronunciation, as in early phonics/phonetics work) genuinely does
  tend toward more even word-by-word emphasis, so a learner's most
  familiar production mode is itself the source of the flat pattern,
  not an incorrect belief about how English works.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, on hearing the
  natural-stress version contrasted with their own flat rendition,
  says something like "oh, that sounds more like how people actually
  talk" (rather than needing this explained) is showing the conflict-
  evidence probe working exactly as intended — this spontaneous,
  register-aware observation is a stronger signal than a flat
  acceptance of the same fact.

**MC-CONTENT-FUNCTION-SPLIT-IS-FIXED-PER-WORD (Blueprint Component 1) —
Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — the content/
  function default is genuinely reliable across the large majority of
  ordinary sentences the learner meets in TA-1/TA-2, and this
  reliability is extended into an unconditional claim (every instance of
  a given word always falls in the same category) rather than
  recognized as a strong but context-dependent default, with nothing in
  the earlier TAs marking that grammatical role or emphasis could ever
  override it.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, on meeting the
  "I did NOT say that" emphatic example, spontaneously generates their
  own additional example of stressing a normally-unstressed word for
  contrast (rather than only recognizing the given example) is showing
  the advanced model has generalized into a genuinely flexible,
  productive skill, not merely accepted as a single memorized
  exception.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the stress-beat sentence walk itself is the
  analogy, not a supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): stomping
  on content-word cards and tiptoeing on function-word cards while
  walking a physical row directly embodies the uneven, "stress-timed"
  rhythm English relies on. Breaking point: a literal walk implies a
  fixed physical pace, which may understate that English's stress-
  timing property (roughly equal time BETWEEN stressed beats,
  regardless of how many unstressed syllables fall between them) is a
  more subtle timing phenomenon than a simple walking cadence — this
  entry treats the walk as the introductory physical anchor, not a
  complete model of stress-timed rhythm.
- **Alternative — a drumbeat with fill notes in between, not every
  note equally emphasized**: the strong drumbeats (content words) carry
  the rhythm; the quieter fill notes (function words) connect them
  without competing for attention. Breaking point: musical fill notes
  can vary widely in number without disrupting the beat, which usefully
  parallels English's stress-timing property more directly than a
  fixed-pace walk, but should not be over-extended to suggest function
  words are optional or omittable — they remain grammatically
  necessary, merely unstressed.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe function-word reduction as
  "sloppy" or "lazy" speech**: framing schwa reduction and de-emphasis
  as careless risks the learner resisting the natural pattern as
  something to avoid, reinforcing MC-EVERY-WORD-GETS-EQUAL-STRESS by
  implying careful speech means equal stress. Function-word reduction
  should always be framed as the correct, natural, expected pattern of
  fluent English, never as a shortcut or a lowering of standards.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/auditory (stress-beat sentence walk)**: the Blueprint's
  Component 3 anchor — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: the walk can be performed with index cards, a
  written sentence on paper with a finger tracing each word, or purely
  vocally with a felt physical stomp/tiptoe gesture.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-4's grammatical-role contrast
  and TA-5's emphatic-stress production are both hands-on, generative
  activities requiring the learner to produce contrasting versions of
  the same words.
- **Teacher-demo (flat vs. natural contrast)**: the tutor models the
  Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstration — an all-equal-stress
  reading versus a natural one of the same sentence — making the
  difference audible and concrete.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming
  content/function status (TA-1) or a stress shift (TA-4/TA-5), ask the
  learner to predict from the sentence's meaning and grammar before the
  tutor confirms — Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** The
inadequacy of equal stress and the flexibility of the content/function
default are both genuine, directly-testable phenomena well-suited to
felt discovery: **need** (the learner, having just secured word-level
stress, is asked to say a full sentence with equal emphasis and compare
it to natural speech); **playground/invention** (TA-1's content/
function labeling across several sentences lets the learner notice the
pattern applies broadly); **collision** (TA-3's direct flat-vs-natural
contrast; TA-4/TA-5's grammatical-role and emphatic-stress examples
directly break an over-rigid, per-word-fixed assumption); **formaliz-
ation** (MP-5's own-words statement of both the default pattern and its
legitimate flexibility); **compression** (fluent, natural sentence
production without needing to consciously label every word's category
each time). The specific schwa-reduction FORMS for common function
words ("to" → /tə/, "and" → /ən/) are convention-bound phonetic facts
best introduced by direct instruction, since there is no regularity to
discover in the exact reduced vowel quality — only a pairing to
demonstrate and rehearse.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (content/function identification) must precede TA-2 (producing the
pattern) because the learner must first reliably classify a sentence's
words before attempting to apply differential stress and reduction to
them — production without secure classification would risk arbitrary
or inconsistent stress placement. TA-3 (equal-vs-natural contrast) is
placed immediately after production practice, directly targeting
MC-EVERY-WORD-GETS-EQUAL-STRESS once the learner has a natural-stress
production to compare against a flat one. TA-4 (grammatical-role shift)
and TA-5 (contrastive/emphatic stress) are placed last and in that
order — per the Blueprint's own explicit Adaptive Flag naming the
content/function default as the essential foundation to establish
FIRST — because both TAs introduce genuine flexibility layered on top
of the default pattern, which is only meaningful and correctly
interpreted once the default itself is secure; TA-4's grammatical-role
shift is placed before TA-5's emphatic/contrastive stress because a
role-based shift (a change in the word's actual grammatical function)
is a more categorical, rule-bound case than the more discretionary,
speaker-intent-driven emphatic shift. This entry does not restate the
Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see
Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner's own sentence production
  shows genuine schwa reduction and de-emphasis on function words
  (audibly different vowel quality, not merely lower volume) — this is
  the primary diagnostic signal for whether the multi-cue stress model
  from the prerequisite concept has correctly scaled up to the sentence
  level.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a pause before producing an emphatic-
  stress example (TA-5), followed by a correctly contrastive
  production, is a good sign — it suggests the learner is consciously
  applying the flexibility rule rather than defaulting reflexively; a
  fast, confident production using the SAME stress pattern regardless
  of the intended emphasis is the concerning pattern.
- **Pronunciation stakes**: real and central — schwa reduction on
  function words is a genuine, expected feature of fluent English, not
  a dialect variant to correct away, so a learner who reduces "to" to
  /tə/ should be affirmed, not corrected toward a full vowel; the
  structural skill being assessed is APPROPRIATE reduction and stress
  placement, not uniform "clear" pronunciation of every word.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "which words carry the meaning, and
  which are just grammatical glue?" is this concept's load-bearing
  sentence for the content/function default, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3`. Per the
  foundations' own channel-reality note, this concept — like its
  prerequisite — depends heavily on live, attentive listening for
  pitch/length/reduction cues that the platform's text-channel
  instrumentation does not fully capture.

## Assessment Signals

- **Diagnostic (golden probe)**: the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence
  probe (Component 1) — producing a sentence with fully equal stress
  and comparing it to a natural rendition — cleanly separates a
  learner still defaulting to even emphasis from one beginning to
  notice the uneven, natural pattern.
- **Guided practice → independent practice**: the Blueprint's WE-1 → WE-2
  → WE-3 → MP-1 through MP-5 fading ladder is reused directly
  (Components 5–6) — not re-authored here.
- **Mastery gate set** (`../../assessment/05-mastery-verification.md
  §3`): production (fresh content/function identification, MP-1);
  natural production (MP-2, a fresh sentence stressed and reduced
  correctly); naturalness judgment (MP-3); grammatical-role shift
  (MP-4 — a strong diagnostic item, since correct stress for BOTH roles
  of the same word demonstrates genuine grammatical sensitivity, not a
  memorized default); explanation (MP-5, requiring articulation of both
  the default pattern and its legitimate flexibility).
- **Interpretation of response pattern**: fast-and-correct natural
  production of a genuinely novel sentence (with audible schwa
  reduction and content-word emphasis) confirms the pattern is
  internalized. Fast-and-flat production (every word given roughly
  equal weight, with no reduction) is the dangerous quadrant for
  MC-EVERY-WORD-GETS-EQUAL-STRESS, warranting a return to TA-3's
  direct contrast rather than more identification-only practice. A
  response that applies the SAME stress regardless of grammatical role
  or clearly signaled emphasis is the dangerous pattern for
  MC-CONTENT-FUNCTION-SPLIT-IS-FIXED-PER-WORD.
- **Transfer items**: near (a fresh sentence's content/function stress
  produced); far (a sentence containing a genuine grammatical-role-
  shift or emphatic-stress opportunity not used during teaching,
  testing whether the flexibility rule generalizes); real-world
  (noticing content/function stress and reduction in the learner's own
  natural conversational speech, or in media the learner already
  consumes).

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know"** on a sentence-stress production task shrinks to
  the Component 3 stress-beat sentence walk using the most familiar,
  simplest sentence available (typically the first taught, "I want to
  go to the store"), with the tutor modeling the stomp/tiptoe pattern
  first.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: a flat, equally-
  stressed production (rather than "I don't know") is more likely, per
  MC-EVERY-WORD-GETS-EQUAL-STRESS's mechanism — route directly to that
  misconception's recovery (the flat-vs-natural side-by-side contrast)
  rather than treating it as a generic pronunciation gap. On a
  grammatical-role or emphatic-stress task, applying the SAME default
  stress regardless of context is more likely than hesitation —
  redirect explicitly to the relevant conflict-evidence probe.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who physically
  gestures (a subtle stomp, a hand tap) on content words while speaking,
  even without being asked to, is showing the stress-beat rhythm has
  already generalized beyond the anchor exercise itself — this should
  be named and praised explicitly.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedure — a rhythm-production skill (applying
content/function stress and reduction fluently to novel sentences)
rather than a fixed fact set, following a skill-fluency pattern:
correctness first (accurate content/function classification, even
slowly produced), then natural, unconscious rhythm at speed, with
ongoing attention to the contextual-flexibility skill (grammatical-role
and emphatic shifts) as a standing review category, since these are
more discretionary and situation-dependent than the base default and
therefore more prone to being forgotten or under-practiced.
Interleaving partners: mix straightforward-default sentences with
grammatical-role-shift and emphatic-stress examples within ongoing
review, so the learner continues applying judgment about WHEN the
default applies versus when it shifts, rather than only rehearsing the
default pattern.
