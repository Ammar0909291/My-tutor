## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Reject a topically-true but unconnected detail and explain why it doesn't support the specific claim.
2. Choose fewer, stronger details over more, weaker ones.
3. Elaborate a bare, underdeveloped detail with a specific explanation or example.
4. Order supporting details effectively with justification.
5. Compose an original paragraph with a specific topic sentence and 3 well-chosen, well-developed, effectively-ordered supporting details.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept, extending `eng.writing.topic-sentences`' specificity discipline to detail selection. First, **being true and about the same general topic is not sufficient for a detail to count as genuine support** — a supporting detail must directly develop or provide evidence for the topic sentence's SPECIFIC claim, not merely share its general subject; a true historical fact about dog breeding doesn't support a claim about temperament, even though both are "about dogs." Second, **more supporting details does not automatically make a paragraph stronger** — quantity is not the goal; quality and development are. A paragraph with 3 well-chosen, well-explained details is usually more convincing than one with 6 thin, underdeveloped ones, since cramming in more details typically means each gets less elaboration, weakening the paragraph overall. Both corrections replace an intuitive, surface-level heuristic (any true fact; more is better) with the same claim-specific, quality-focused discipline this program has established for related writing concepts.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "any true fact about the topic counts as a supporting detail; more details always make a paragraph stronger."**
The simulation: include any topically-related true statement regardless of whether it develops the specific claim, and pile on as many details as possible.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a historical breeding fact versus a temperament-relevant detail, asked which actually develops the specific claim; and a 6-thin-detail paragraph versus a 3-developed-detail paragraph, asked which is actually more convincing.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Check the specific claim, not just the general topic. Fewer, well-developed details beat many thin ones."

**Intermediate — "I check for claim-relevance now, but I still tend to include as many relevant details as I can find rather than selecting the strongest few."**
The simulation: correctly filters out topically-true-but-unconnected details but hasn't yet adopted the quality-over-quantity selection discipline.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-3's direct practice selecting and developing only the 3 strongest of 6 candidate details.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Don't include every relevant detail you find — choose your strongest few and develop each one fully."

**Advanced — "I select claim-specific details, elaborate each with explanation or example, order them effectively, and compose a fully developed original paragraph."**
The simulation: integrates claim-matching, elaboration, and ordering into one coherent composition. Matches TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: the direct unlock `eng.writing.transitions-and-cohesion`, which teaches the connective language linking the well-selected details this concept establishes.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you strong, well-chosen pillars. The next step is the mortar connecting them smoothly."

**Expert — "at fluency, claim-matching and quality-over-quantity selection happen as an integrated part of ordinary drafting."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit claim-match test and pillar-selection routine is a scaffold for a compositional judgment that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated part of drafting.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **"is this true and about my topic?" is a genuinely easier check than "does this develop my specific claim?"**, and the two frequently coincide in simple writing tasks, so the shortcut goes uncorrected until the topic sentence's claim is specific enough to exclude some topically-true facts.

The second mechanism is that **learners often equate length or detail-count with effort and thus quality**, a natural but incorrect proxy — more details can feel like "doing more work," when in fact the quality of each detail's development is what actually strengthens the paragraph.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-ANY-TRUE-STATEMENT-ABOUT-THE-TOPIC-COUNTS-AS-A-SUPPORTING-DETAIL
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry — the breeding-fact/temperament-detail discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely useful topical-relevance check, extended into treating it as sufficient when the stricter claim-match test is actually required.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **add the claim-match test as a further filter, not discard topical relevance as irrelevant**, since topical relevance is a reasonable first check the stricter test then narrows.
- **Verification of death**: given a topic sentence and a topically-true-but-unconnected detail, the learner correctly rejects it and explains why.

### MC-MORE-SUPPORTING-DETAILS-ALWAYS-MAKES-A-PARAGRAPH-STRONGER
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — the 6-thin-details/3-developed-details discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from detail-count functioning as a visible, easily-counted proxy for effort and thoroughness, mistaken for the harder-to-perceive quality of each detail's development.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct comparison of a thin-many-details paragraph against a developed-few-details paragraph**, since the counterintuitive claim (fewer can be stronger) is more convincing demonstrated than asserted.
- **Verification of death**: given 6 candidate details of varying strength, the learner selects and develops the 3 strongest rather than including all 6 thinly.

## Analogies

**Best — the Three Strong Pillars, Not Six Weak Sticks** (Blueprint Component 3): 3 sturdy pillars actually holding up a roof versus 6 thin, wobbling sticks, directly seeding the quality-over-quantity fix; a "roof label" (the topic sentence's specific claim) checked against a candidate pillar for genuine connection, not just generic topical relatedness, directly seeding the claim-match fix.
*Breaking point*: a physical pillar's load-bearing capacity is an objective engineering fact, while a supporting detail's persuasive strength is a matter of rhetorical judgment — useful for the fewer-but-stronger intuition, less precise for implying detail strength is as measurable as structural load.

**Alternative — a court case with three strong pieces of evidence versus ten weak, tangential ones.** A prosecutor with three directly relevant, well-explained pieces of evidence usually makes a more convincing case than one presenting ten loosely-related facts thinly.
*Breaking point*: legal evidence has formal relevance rules a jury applies, while a paragraph's supporting-detail relevance is judged by the writer's own claim, a less externally verifiable standard.

**Visual anchor — the Three Strong Pillars, Not Six Weak Sticks**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"As long as it's true and about the topic, include it."** Directly installs the topical-sufficiency misconception as a stated rule.
- **"The more details you include, the stronger your paragraph."** Directly installs the quantity-as-strength misconception as a stated rule.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Three Strong Pillars, Not Six Weak Sticks (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Which will actually hold up the roof — 3 sturdy pillars or 6 thin sticks?" Then connect to detail quality over quantity.
2. **The breeding-fact/temperament-detail collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Does this true fact actually support the SPECIFIC claim in the topic sentence?"
3. **The 6-thin/3-developed collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Which paragraph is actually more convincing?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both structural facts are directly checkable by testing candidate details against a topic sentence's specific claim.

1. **Need**: "If a detail is true and about the same subject, does that automatically mean it supports THIS paragraph's specific point?"
2. **Playground**: the learner sorts several candidate details into supports/doesn't-support freely before being told the official claim-match test.
3. **Invention**: "Can you pick your 3 strongest details from this list of 6 and explain why they beat the other 3?"
4. **Collision**: present a 6-thin-detail paragraph and a 3-developed-detail paragraph; ask which is more convincing.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — claim-match, not topical truth, determines genuine support; quality and development, not quantity, determine strength — from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Match the claim, not just the topic — and develop a few well, rather than listing many thinly."

## Teaching Sequence

- **The claim-match test (TA-1) comes first**, directly confronting the topical-sufficiency misconception before elaboration or quantity content is introduced.
- **Elaborating a detail (TA-2) comes second**, teaching the development skill that TA-3's quality-over-quantity selection depends on.
- **Choosing strongest details over maximum quantity (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the quantity misconception once elaboration is understood.
- **Ordering details (TA-4) and composing a fully developed paragraph (TA-5) come last**, integrating selection, elaboration, and structural sequencing — matching this program's established sequencing principle of reserving the most integrative task for the culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for including, excluding, or ordering a detail**, since a correctly-selected detail set with no articulated claim-match or strength reasoning cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated claim-match check** ("does this actually support what my topic sentence claimed, or is it just related to the topic?") applied to a new candidate detail — direct evidence the claim-match fix has taken hold.
- **Voluntary exclusion of a topically-relevant but thin detail** in favor of developing fewer, stronger ones — direct evidence the quality-over-quantity fix is internalized.
- **A drafted paragraph listing many one-line, undeveloped details** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-MORE-SUPPORTING-DETAILS-ALWAYS-MAKES-A-PARAGRAPH-STRONGER still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Match the claim, not just the topic — and develop a few details well, rather than listing many thinly."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But this is true, and it's about the same topic — why doesn't it count?"** — take this as an accurate observation about topical truth, not a mistake; respond by adding the stricter test: "It's true, that's right — now check: does it support what your topic sentence specifically claimed, or a different point?"
- **"Shouldn't I add more details to make my paragraph stronger?"** — a reasonable, effort-signaling instinct; respond by naming the actual cost: "More details usually means less development for each one. Pick your 2-3 strongest and really explain them."
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full paragraph composition down to **rejecting a single, clearly off-claim detail with no elaboration or ordering task demanded.** Isolates the most basic claim-match test before returning to fuller composition.
- **Never shrink to "just list a few facts about the topic."** This directly licenses the topical-sufficiency pattern, undermining the claim-match discipline this concept specifically builds.
