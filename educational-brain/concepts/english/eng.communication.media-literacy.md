## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify the specific production choices (word choice, emphasis, selection) that shape different framings of the same event.
2. Identify the likely primary purpose (inform, persuade, entertain, generate engagement/revenue) behind a media message.
3. Evaluate two media examples with different emotional tones but the same actual accuracy level, using accuracy/sourcing criteria alone.
4. Correctly identify which of a plain-but-inaccurate and a dramatic-but-accurate example is actually more trustworthy.
5. Fully evaluate a new media message's production choices, likely purpose, and trustworthiness based on accuracy and sourcing.

## Core Understanding

This concept extends `eng.reading.evaluating-sources`' single-source credibility assessment to the broader media landscape's production techniques. Two corrections define it. First, **news and media coverage does not simply show what happened, with no constructed choices involved** — every media message involves deliberate PRODUCTION CHOICES (what footage or facts to include, how to frame the story, which quotes to select, what headline to use) that shape what the audience experiences, even when the underlying facts reported are accurate; "Protesters Clash with Police" and "Community Members Demand Accountability" both describe the identical event but create different impressions through framing alone. Second, **emotionally engaging or attention-grabbing content is not automatically less trustworthy than plain content, and plain content is not automatically more trustworthy** — trustworthiness depends on the ACCURACY, sourcing, and transparency of the underlying content, a question entirely separate from its emotional tone or presentation style, which can vary independently in either direction.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "news simply shows what happened, with no constructed choices; emotionally engaging content is automatically less trustworthy than plain content."**
The simulation: treat media coverage as a neutral, unmediated window onto reality, and judge trustworthiness by how dramatic or plain a piece feels.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — two headlines on the identical protest event with different framing, asked whether they create the same overall impression; and an emotionally engaging, well-sourced piece versus a dry, poorly-sourced one, asked whether the dramatic piece is automatically less trustworthy.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Every media message involves deliberate production choices. Trustworthiness depends on accuracy and sourcing, not tone."

**Intermediate — "I notice framing choices now, but I still tend to trust plain, unemotional presentation more than dramatic presentation by default."**
The simulation: correctly identifies constructed framing but hasn't yet fully separated the tone/trustworthiness judgment.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-3's direct evaluation of four media examples varying in emotional tone and accuracy independently.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Check the substance — accuracy and sourcing — separately from the tone. They vary independently."

**Advanced — "I identify production choices, recognize a message's likely purpose and incentive, evaluate trustworthiness by substance rather than tone, and analyze a new media message integrating all three."**
The simulation: integrates constructed-choice recognition, purpose/incentive identification, and substance-based trustworthiness evaluation into one coherent media analysis. Matches TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: none within this concept — confirmed terminal in the current curriculum sequence.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the full media-literacy framework for evaluating any media message you encounter."

**Expert — "at fluency, constructed-choice recognition and substance-based trustworthiness evaluation happen as an integrated part of ordinary media consumption."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit construct-purpose-evaluate routine is a scaffold for a consumption habit that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated part of informed media literacy.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **media that reports accurate underlying facts feels, to a casual consumer, indistinguishable from an unmediated view of reality**, since the constructed choices (framing, selection, word choice) are invisible unless deliberately compared against an alternative framing of the identical event.

The second mechanism is that **emotional restraint and plain presentation are culturally associated with objectivity and seriousness** (a common stylistic convention in some traditional journalism), creating a heuristic that conflates presentation STYLE with evidentiary SUBSTANCE, when the two vary independently — a polished, professional style was already identified as an unreliable credibility proxy in `eng.reading.evaluating-sources`, and this concept extends that same insight to emotional tone specifically.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-NEWS-AND-MEDIA-COVERAGE-SIMPLY-SHOWS-WHAT-HAPPENED-WITH-NO-CONSTRUCTED-CHOICES-INVOLVED
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the "Clash with Police"/"Demand Accountability" discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from accurately-reported media feeling indistinguishable from unmediated reality, since the constructed choices shaping it are invisible without a direct comparison to an alternative framing.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct side-by-side comparison of two framings of the identical event**, since the fix requires making the invisible construction visible through contrast, not merely asserting that construction occurs.
- **Verification of death**: given two different framings of the same event, the learner identifies the specific production choices that shape each impression.

### MC-B-EMOTIONALLY-ENGAGING-OR-ATTENTION-GRABBING-CONTENT-IS-AUTOMATICALLY-LESS-TRUSTWORTHY-THAN-PLAIN-CONTENT
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — includes the dramatic-accurate/plain-inaccurate discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from plain, unemotional presentation carrying a cultural association with objectivity, mistaken for actual evidentiary substance — the identical mechanism identified for professional-appearance credibility in `eng.reading.evaluating-sources`, now applied to emotional tone specifically.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct comparison of a dramatic-but-accurate piece against a plain-but-inaccurate one**, since the counterintuitive claim (tone doesn't predict accuracy) is more convincing demonstrated than asserted.
- **Verification of death**: given two media examples with different emotional tones but the same actual accuracy level, the learner evaluates both using accuracy/sourcing criteria alone.

## Analogies

**Best — the Photograph-Framing-Choice and Website-Design-Polish Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): a photograph's deliberate angle, framing, and cropping shaping what's shown without any technical "faking" directly seeds the constructed-choices fix; a professionally designed website that could still be a scam alongside a plain website that could still be legitimate directly seeds the tone-versus-trustworthiness fix.
*Breaking point*: a photograph's framing choices are made by a single photographer with a bounded, identifiable set of decisions, while a news production involves many contributing choices (editors, headline writers, footage selectors) across an organization — useful for the deliberate-choice intuition, less precise for conveying the more distributed, organizational nature of media production.

**Alternative — a courtroom's opening statement versus its physical evidence.** A prosecutor's dramatic, emotionally compelling opening statement doesn't itself prove guilt, and a defense attorney's dry, understated presentation doesn't itself prove innocence — the actual evidence, evaluated on its own merits, determines the outcome regardless of either side's presentation style.
*Breaking point*: a courtroom has a formal, rule-governed evidence-evaluation process (rules of evidence, a judge, cross-examination) that everyday media consumption lacks, making the analogy looser for conveying HOW an individual consumer should actually verify accuracy without such formal structures.

**Visual anchor — the Photograph-Framing-Choice and Website-Design-Polish Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"The news just reports the facts, no interpretation involved."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated claim.
- **"If it's trying to make you feel something, it's probably not trustworthy."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated rule.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Photograph-Framing-Choice and Website-Design-Polish Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Does a photograph's framing and cropping shape what you see, even without any faking?" Then connect to media production choices.
2. **The "Clash with Police"/"Demand Accountability" collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "Do these two headlines describe the same actual event? Do they create the same impression?"
3. **The dramatic-accurate/plain-inaccurate collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-B. *Predict first*: "Does the emotionally engaging piece's style make it automatically less trustworthy?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both media-literacy facts are directly checkable by comparing paired framings of identical events and by testing tone against actual accuracy.

1. **Need**: "If two headlines describe the exact same event but use different words, are you getting the 'same' story from both?"
2. **Playground**: the learner compares several framing pairs freely, guessing which choices shape impression, before being told the official production-choices framework.
3. **Invention**: "Can you find a dramatic, emotionally engaging piece that's actually very well-sourced and accurate?"
4. **Collision**: present a plain-toned but inaccurate example and a dramatic but accurate one; ask which is actually more trustworthy.
5. **Formalization**: name the two media-literacy facts — media is always constructed through deliberate choices; trustworthiness depends on substance, not tone — from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Every message is built by choices — and check the substance, not the tone."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Media is constructed through deliberate choices (TA-1) comes first**, directly confronting MC-A before purpose-identification or tone-evaluation content is introduced.
- **Identifying a media message's purpose and incentive model (TA-2) comes second**, extending TA-1's construction-awareness to the underlying motivations shaping those choices.
- **Separating trustworthiness from emotional tone or presentation style (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting MC-B once construction-awareness from TA-1/TA-2 is secure.
- **Full application (TA-4) comes last**, integrating constructed-choice identification, purpose analysis, and substance-based evaluation into one synthesis task — matching this program's established sequencing principle of reserving the most integrative task for the culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for identifying a specific production choice or for a trustworthiness judgment based on substance rather than tone**, since a correct-looking judgment with no cited framing choice or accuracy/sourcing reasoning cannot be distinguished from an unexamined impression.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A specific word choice or framing detail cited** ("'clash' emphasizes conflict, 'demand accountability' emphasizes the cause") when comparing two accounts of the same event — the direct behavioral signature of the constructed-choices fix taking hold.
- **A stated substance check independent of tone** ("this sounds dramatic, but is it actually well-sourced?") applied to a new emotionally engaging piece — direct evidence the tone/trustworthiness-separation fix is internalized.
- **A media message accepted as "just the facts" with no identified framing choice, or judged trustworthy/untrustworthy purely by its emotional register** — the direct behavioral signature of either misconception still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Every message is built by choices — check the substance, not the tone, to know if you can trust it."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"This is just a straightforward news report, no spin involved"** — take this as a reasonable default for accurately-reported coverage; respond by finding the specific choices: "Even accurate reporting makes choices — what did they choose to include, and how did they word the headline?"
- **"This piece feels really dramatic, so it's probably exaggerating or unreliable"** — a reasonable-sounding tone-based heuristic; respond by checking the sourcing directly: "Let's set the drama aside for a second — is the actual information here well-sourced and accurate?"
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full new-message evaluation down to **identifying one specific production choice in a single framing example, with no purpose-identification or tone/trustworthiness task demanded.** Isolates the most basic constructed-choice awareness before returning to fuller evaluation.
- **Never shrink to "just say if it seems trustworthy to you."** This licenses an unexamined, tone-based impression without the substance-checking discipline this concept specifically builds.
