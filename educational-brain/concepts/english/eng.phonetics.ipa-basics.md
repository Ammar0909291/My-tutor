# IPA Basics for English — `eng.phonetics.ipa-basics`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.phonetics.ipa-basics` (canonical English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / phonetics / "IPA Basics for
  English" — the convergence point of the phonetics domain's two
  parallel classification branches, giving the already-known
  consonant and vowel sound inventories a formal, one-symbol-one-sound
  written notation.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.phonetics.consonant-sounds` and
  `eng.phonetics.vowel-sounds` — jointly load-bearing, since IPA
  symbols are organized by exactly the place/manner/voicing and height/
  backness/rounding dimensions those two concepts install; a learner
  cannot meaningfully attach a symbol to a sound they have not already
  learned to identify and classify by ear and feel.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.phonetics.minimal-pairs` and
  `eng.phonetics.syllable-stress`, both of which use IPA notation as
  their working representation for contrasting and marking sounds —
  neither concept is teachable without a learner already able to read
  and write basic IPA transcriptions.
- **Difficulty**: developing · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.75 · **Est. hours**: 3

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Produce the correct sound for an IPA symbol whose shape resembles
   its corresponding Latin letter (/p/, /t/, /k/, /m/, etc.).
2. Produce the correct sound for an IPA symbol with no direct Latin-
   letter resemblance (/ʃ/, /θ/, /ŋ/, vowel and diphthong symbols).
3. Transcribe a phonetically regular spoken word into IPA, representing
   sounds rather than letters.
4. Transcribe a word whose spelling and pronunciation genuinely
   diverge (silent letters, digraphs, unexpected vowel sounds),
   transcribing only what is actually heard.
5. Decode an IPA transcription back into a spoken word, demonstrating
   the skill works bidirectionally.

## Core Understanding

IPA (the International Phonetic Alphabet) is a NOTATION system, not a
new set of sounds to learn — every symbol in it labels exactly one
phoneme the learner has already learned to produce, identify, and
classify through the two prerequisite concepts' place/manner/voicing
and height/backness/rounding systems. The entire skill this concept
teaches is matching an unfamiliar written SYMBOL to already-familiar
SOUND knowledge, never learning a new sound from scratch. A second,
equally load-bearing fact is that IPA transcribes sound and only sound
— it is deliberately and completely blind to English spelling, silent
letters, and any mismatch between how a word is written and how it is
said. A transcription's symbol count need not match its word's letter
count in either direction (a five-letter word can transcribe to four
symbols, or fewer, or occasionally more), and the correct procedure is
always to say the word aloud first and transcribe only what is
genuinely heard, never to transcribe letter-by-letter from the
spelling.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "familiar-looking symbols are easy; unfamiliar
  ones feel like a whole new alphabet"**: a natural first response to
  IPA's visual novelty, addressed directly by TA-1's deliberate
  sequencing of Latin-letter-resembling symbols first, building
  confidence in the underlying "symbol names a known sound" principle
  before the visually unfamiliar symbols are introduced.
- **Intermediate model — "every symbol, familiar or not, labels a sound
  I already know how to produce and classify"**: installed by TA-2,
  which extends the beginner model to the genuinely novel-shaped
  symbols, resolving MC-IPA-SYMBOLS-ARE-JUST-FANCY-LETTERS — the
  learner stops trying to sound out a symbol using English spelling
  habits (reading /ʃ/ as "s" then "h") and instead retrieves the
  already-classified sound directly. Upgrade trigger: correctly
  producing the sound for a genuinely novel-shaped symbol without
  attempting to decompose it into familiar letters.
- **Advanced model — "transcription represents only what is heard,
  completely independent of spelling"**: installed by TA-3 (regular
  words, where sound and spelling happen to align reasonably well) and
  then TA-4 (words where they diverge sharply), resolving
  MC-IPA-MATCHES-ENGLISH-SPELLING. Upgrade trigger: correctly
  transcribing a word with a silent letter or unexpected digraph
  sound, omitting or adjusting for what the spelling would otherwise
  suggest.
- **Expert model — "the skill runs in both directions: I can encode
  sound into symbols, and decode symbols back into sound"**: installed
  by TA-5, which reverses the direction entirely — given a
  transcription rather than a spelled word, the learner must produce
  the spoken word. This model also surfaces a genuine, non-error
  property of IPA (transcription cannot distinguish homophones by
  spelling, since it represents sound alone), which the concept's own
  MP-5 treats as a legitimate outcome to recognize, not a failure to
  resolve.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own explicit chunking
  guidance, novel (non-Latin-resembling) symbols should be introduced
  in small batches rather than the full inventory at once — a learner
  overwhelmed by too many new symbols simultaneously risks reverting to
  guessing rather than genuine sound-to-symbol matching, undermining
  the very principle TA-2 exists to establish.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here. The letter-reading-
habit failure exists because the learner's entire prior reading
experience (years of English print literacy) has trained an automatic,
deeply overlearned habit of decoding written symbols using English
letter-sound rules — IPA symbols are visually presented in the same
medium (written text) as English letters, so the same overlearned
decoding reflex fires automatically unless explicitly redirected, even
though the underlying skill required (retrieve an already-classified
sound) is entirely different from ordinary reading. The spelling-
matching failure exists for a related but distinct reason: a learner's
entire prior experience of "writing down a word" has been English
spelling, where letter count and pronunciation are loosely but
persistently correlated — the expectation that a transcription should
resemble spelling in structure (roughly one symbol per letter) is a
reasonable extrapolation from that entire prior experience, not a
specific error introduced by this concept's own instruction.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.phonetics.ipa-basics.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-IPA-SYMBOLS-ARE-JUST-FANCY-LETTERS (Blueprint Component 1) — Type
3, language contamination**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: this is contamination from an entirely
  different, correctly-functioning skill (ordinary English letter-
  sound decoding) intruding into a domain where a different procedure
  (symbol-to-known-sound retrieval) applies — the learner is not
  applying an overextended IPA-internal rule, they are applying the
  wrong, well-established system (regular reading) to material that
  merely looks superficially similar (written symbols) but functions
  completely differently.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, on seeing /ʃ/,
  spontaneously says "oh, that's the 'ship' sound, right?" (retrieving
  from prior classification knowledge rather than attempting to sound
  out the symbol) is showing the misconception has already resolved —
  this retrieval-based response is qualitatively different from, and a
  stronger sign than, a learner who eventually produces the correct
  sound after visibly attempting a letter-by-letter decode first.

**MC-IPA-MATCHES-ENGLISH-SPELLING (Blueprint Component 1) — Type 1,
overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 1 signature — "a written
  representation of a word roughly tracks its spelling" is a rule
  genuinely valid and universally reinforced across the learner's
  entire literacy history (every prior written representation of a
  word HAS been its spelling), extended here into a domain (IPA
  transcription) specifically designed to break that correlation
  entirely.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, mid-transcription
  of a word like "knife," pauses and asks "wait, do I need a symbol for
  the 'k'?" (rather than automatically including one) is showing
  productive, self-monitoring doubt — a more advanced state than a
  learner who includes a k-symbol with full confidence and no
  hesitation.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — a universal name tag for a person you already
  know, written in an unfamiliar script**: the person (the sound) is
  already known; only the WRITTEN LABEL is new, and reading that new
  label correctly means recognizing the already-known referent, not
  learning a new person. Breaking point: name tags for people are
  typically unique and singular, whereas the analogy should not be
  extended to suggest IPA symbols are arbitrary labels with no internal
  systematic logic (they are, in fact, organized by exactly the place/
  manner/voicing and height/backness/rounding dimensions already
  taught) — the analogy targets "known referent, new label," not
  arbitrariness.
- **Alternative — a universal currency exchange, converting between
  systems without changing the underlying value**: IPA transcription
  converts a word's SOUND into a standard notation, the way currency
  exchange converts a value into a different denomination without
  changing what is actually being represented. Breaking point: currency
  exchange rates can be approximate or fluctuate, whereas a correct IPA
  transcription of a given pronunciation is exact, not approximate —
  the analogy should not suggest transcription accuracy is negotiable.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe IPA as "a more accurate spelling
  system" or "the real spelling"**: framing IPA as a SPELLING (even an
  improved one) directly installs MC-IPA-MATCHES-ENGLISH-SPELLING by
  keeping the learner's mental model anchored to spelling-based
  thinking rather than sound-based thinking. IPA should always be
  introduced as a sound-transcription system, categorically distinct
  from spelling, not a corrected version of it.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/auditory-visual (symbol-to-sound matching game)**: the
  Blueprint's Component 3 anchor, starting with familiar-looking
  symbols before introducing novel ones — canonical, referenced not
  restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any written IPA symbol (drawn by hand or
  printed) paired with the learner's own spoken production substitutes
  for matching cards.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-3/TA-4's transcription tasks and
  TA-5's reverse-direction decoding are all hands-on, generative
  activities requiring the learner to produce a transcription or a
  spoken word, not merely recognize one.
- **Teacher-demo (transcribe-by-ear contrast)**: the tutor models
  transcribing "knife" by first saying the word aloud slowly, then
  writing only the sounds heard, explicitly narrating the dropped
  silent "k" — directly instantiating the Blueprint's own conflict-
  evidence probe.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  symbol's sound (TA-1/TA-2) or a transcription (TA-3/TA-4), ask the
  learner to predict from the symbol or the spoken word before the
  tutor confirms — Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**Direct instruction wins here — argued, not defaulted to.** There is
no regularity for a learner to discover in WHICH IPA symbol represents
which sound — the symbol-to-sound mapping is a fixed, internationally
standardized convention to be demonstrated and matched, not a pattern
to be induced from examples, matching this domain's treatment of
similarly convention-bound content (base consonant sounds, articulator
names) elsewhere. The genuinely inducible insight — that transcription
tracks sound, not spelling — is well-suited to a brief, felt discovery
moment built into TA-4's own design: **need** (the learner, having
transcribed regular words successfully in TA-3, meets "knife" and must
decide whether to include a symbol for the silent "k"); **playground/
invention** (saying the word aloud slowly and noticing the "k" is
genuinely not pronounced); **collision** (the Blueprint's own conflict-
evidence probe, directly counting letters vs. symbols); **formalization**
(the replacement rule: say it aloud first, transcribe only what is
heard); **compression** (TA-5's fluent bidirectional application
without needing to re-derive the spelling-independence principle each
time).

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (familiar-looking symbols) must precede TA-2 (novel symbols)
because building confidence in the underlying "new symbol, known sound"
principle on the visually easiest cases first reduces the cognitive
load of simultaneously learning both a new principle and a large batch
of unfamiliar shapes. TA-3 (transcribing regular words) is placed after
both symbol-recognition TAs, since transcription requires the full
symbol inventory (or at least a working subset) to already be
recognizable. TA-4 (spelling-sound mismatch words) is placed
deliberately after TA-3's regular-word success, not alongside it — 
introducing the harder, spelling-diverging cases only once the basic
transcription mechanic is secure isolates the genuinely new challenge
(resisting the spelling-matching habit) from the more basic
transcription skill itself. TA-5 (reverse-direction decoding) is placed
last as the full bidirectional integration check, requiring the
learner to run the entire skill in reverse, which is only meaningful
once forward transcription is reliable. This entry does not restate the
Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see
Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner says a word ALOUD before
  attempting to transcribe it, versus attempting to transcribe directly
  from the written spelling without vocalizing first — this behavior
  itself is the primary diagnostic signal for MC-IPA-MATCHES-ENGLISH-
  SPELLING, independent of whether the resulting transcription happens
  to be correct.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a pause before transcribing a
  spelling-sound-mismatch word, especially one accompanied by "wait, do
  I need a symbol for that letter?", is a strong positive sign of
  self-monitoring against the spelling-matching habit.
- **Pronunciation stakes**: central to this concept in a specific way —
  since IPA transcribes what is actually said, dialect variation in the
  LEARNER's own pronunciation should be transcribed as such (a
  dialect-consistent vowel realization is not a transcription error);
  the skill being assessed is accurately capturing whatever is
  genuinely produced, not matching a single reference accent.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "say it out loud first, then write
  down only what you actually hear" is this concept's load-bearing
  sentence and should be repeated before every transcription task, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3`.

## Assessment Signals

- **Diagnostic (golden probe)**: the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence
  probe (Component 1) — reading /ʃ/ as "s" then "h" and being asked
  whether it is a new sound or one already known from "ship" — cleanly
  separates a learner still applying letter-reading habits from one who
  is retrieving from prior sound classification.
- **Guided practice → independent practice**: the Blueprint's WE-1 → WE-2
  → WE-3 → MP-1 through MP-5 fading ladder is reused directly
  (Components 5–6) — not re-authored here.
- **Mastery gate set** (`../../assessment/05-mastery-verification.md
  §3`): production (fresh familiar-symbol recognition, MP-1); novel-
  symbol recognition (MP-2); regular-word transcription (MP-3);
  mismatch-word transcription (MP-4 — the concept's single most
  diagnostic item for the spelling-independence principle, since it
  directly requires resisting the letter-count expectation); reverse
  decoding with homophone-ambiguity recognition (MP-5, a genuinely
  distinctive mastery signal, since correctly recognizing that a single
  transcription can map to more than one valid spelling demonstrates a
  deep, not merely mechanical, grasp of sound-vs-spelling
  independence).
- **Interpretation of response pattern**: fast-and-correct transcription
  of a mismatch word (correctly dropping silent letters without
  hesitation) confirms the spelling-independence principle is fully
  internalized. Fast-and-WRONG transcription that mirrors the letter
  count of the spelling (e.g., including a symbol for every letter in
  "knife") is the dangerous quadrant — it signals the spelling-matching
  habit is still dominant and unexamined, warranting a return to TA-4's
  say-it-aloud-first discipline rather than more regular-word practice.
- **Transfer items**: near (a fresh phonetically regular word
  transcribed); far (a word with a genuinely unusual spelling-sound
  relationship not used during teaching, testing whether the
  transcribe-by-ear habit generalizes); real-world (transcribing the
  learner's own name, or a word from a dictionary entry the learner has
  encountered).

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know"** on a symbol-recognition task shrinks to the
  Component 3 matching game using the most familiar-looking symbol
  available (typically /p/ or /m/), with the tutor modeling the "this
  symbol names a sound you already know" retrieval explicitly before
  returning to the target symbol.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: an attempted letter-
  by-letter sound-out of an IPA symbol (rather than "I don't know") is
  more likely, per MC-IPA-SYMBOLS-ARE-JUST-FANCY-LETTERS's mechanism —
  route directly to that misconception's recovery (the Blueprint's own
  "is this a new sound or one you already know" reframe) rather than
  treating it as a generic notation-recall gap. On a transcription
  task, a letter-count-matched (spelling-mirroring) attempt is more
  likely than genuine uncertainty — redirect explicitly to saying the
  word aloud first.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who audibly
  says a word aloud, pauses, and then begins writing symbols (rather
  than writing immediately from the spelling) is showing the correct
  transcribe-by-ear behavior even on items where the final
  transcription needs correction — this sequence should be praised
  explicitly as exactly the target habit.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: fact/procedure hybrid — the symbol-to-sound mapping
for each IPA character is a fact set (fixed pairings, standard spaced
fact-review, with novel/unfamiliar-shaped symbols requiring more
repetition than familiar-looking ones per the Blueprint's own chunking
guidance), while transcribe-by-ear (as opposed to by-spelling) is a
procedure reviewed through continued application to novel words,
particularly spelling-sound-mismatch words, which should remain a
standing review category since the spelling-matching habit is
overlearned and prone to resurfacing under time pressure even after
initial correction. Interleaving partners: mix regular and mismatch
words within the same practice set so the learner cannot predict which
type is being asked from context alone, and periodically mix
transcription (encoding) tasks with decoding tasks to keep the
bidirectional skill genuinely exercised in both directions.

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: recognizing fresh IPA symbols and transcribing fresh,
  previously-unpracticed words.
- **Far**: this concept's notation system transfers directly to
  `eng.phonetics.minimal-pairs` (contrasting sounds using IPA
  representations) and `eng.phonetics.syllable-stress` (marking stress
  on IPA-transcribed syllables), both of which assume fluent IPA
  reading and writing as a working tool rather than teaching it afresh.
- **Real-world**: dictionary pronunciation guides, many of which use
  IPA or IPA-adjacent notation, become directly readable once this
  concept is secure — an immediate, practical real-world application.
- **Expert-transfer**: eventual cross-linguistic phonetic comparison and
  formal linguistic study, both well downstream, depend on IPA fluency
  established here as a foundational tool.
