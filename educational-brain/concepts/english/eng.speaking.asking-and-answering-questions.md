## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Match an answer's shape to a question's type — a short confirming answer for yes/no questions, a full explanation for open wh-questions.
2. Identify the "bullseye" answer among candidates — the one that directly addresses the specific question, as distinct from topically-related but non-answering responses.
3. Formulate a clear, specific question likely to elicit a precise, useful answer, rather than a vague one.
4. Conduct a full question-answer exchange, both asking clear questions and giving bullseye-accurate, type-matched answers.
5. Recognize when a draft answer has drifted to a topic-adjacent non-answer and revise it back to directly address the question asked.

## Core Understanding

Different question types require **differently-shaped answers, matched to what's actually being asked** — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact: a question starting with "did," "is," "can," or "will" is typically seeking a yes/no confirmation, where a short answer can be genuinely complete, while a question starting with "why," "how," or "what" is asking for explanation or description, where a bare "yes" or "no" doesn't even make sense as a response. The reliable practice is checking the question's type before answering, not defaulting to the shortest possible response regardless of what was asked. The concept's second, equally load-bearing fact is that **being topically related is not the same as actually answering the question** — a response can be "about the same subject" while still failing to address the specific information requested, and only a response that directly hits that specific target (the "bullseye") genuinely satisfies the question; a near-miss, topic-adjacent response, however reasonable-sounding, has not actually answered anything.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "any short answer works for any question, and any response related to the topic counts as answering it."**
The runnable simulation: default to minimal, often yes/no-style responses regardless of question type, and accept or give any response loosely connected to the general subject as if it satisfies the specific question asked. This is a reasonable default, since brevity often feels efficient, and staying "on topic" feels like a reasonable, cooperative conversational contribution.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — being shown "Why were you late today?" answered with a nonsensical "Yes," alongside "Because my bus was delayed"; and being shown "What time does the movie start?" answered with "The movie theater is really nice" versus "It starts at 7:30," and asked which actually satisfies the question.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Check the question's type before answering — and check that your answer hits the specific thing asked, not just the general topic."

**Intermediate — "I match answer length to question type now, but I still sometimes give or accept responses that are related to the topic without actually addressing the specific question."**
The simulation: correctly distinguishes yes/no questions from open questions and shapes responses accordingly, but hasn't yet built the habit of checking whether a topically-related response actually hits the specific target the question asked for.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing "It's a really popular show, lots of people go" against "It's $15" for the question "How much does the ticket cost?", and confirming only the second is a true answer despite the first being topically reasonable.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Being related to the topic isn't the same as answering the question — check that your response hits the exact thing that was asked."

**Advanced — "I match answer type to question type, identify the bullseye answer among near-miss alternatives, formulate clear questions, and can catch and revise my own drifted answers."**
The simulation: conducts a full exchange with precisely-typed, bullseye-accurate answers, and formulates specific questions likely to elicit equally precise responses. This is the target model, matching TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: the direct unlock `eng.speaking.discussion-skills`, where this concept's bullseye-answer standard extends to multi-participant group exchanges where staying on-question matters even more.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the precision standard for one-to-one exchanges. The next step applies the same standard across multi-participant discussions."

**Expert — "at full conversational fluency, question-type recognition and bullseye-accurate answering happen largely automatically in real time, with explicit self-checking reserved for high-stakes or genuinely ambiguous exchanges (interviews, negotiations, precise information-gathering)."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit type-sorting and dartboard-testing routine is a scaffold for a judgment that, at true fluency, operates largely below conscious awareness during ordinary conversation — named here so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is fluent, automatic precision, not a permanently maintained conscious checklist.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **brevity genuinely feels efficient and low-risk**, so a learner's instinct to default to short answers is a reasonable (if sometimes mismatched) strategy — the mismatch surfaces specifically on open wh-questions, where the same brevity instinct that works fine for yes/no questions produces a response that doesn't even make grammatical or logical sense.

The second mechanism is that **staying "on topic" is a genuinely valued, reinforced conversational norm** (a response wildly off the general subject would clearly be a failure), so a learner reasonably extends "stay connected to the topic" into "any topic-connected response is acceptable," missing the further, more demanding requirement that a genuine answer must hit the specific informational target the question requested, not merely its general neighborhood.

The third mechanism, specific to this concept's terminal position in the curriculum, is that **without further scaffolded unlocks to reinforce the bullseye-precision standard specifically**, a learner exiting with only partial mastery of that half of this concept (per the Blueprint's own S9 failure-routing, requiring both MP-1 and MP-2 for certification) has no built-in downstream concept designed to catch and repair the gap, even though `eng.speaking.discussion-skills` explicitly assumes this precision is already solid.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-YES-OR-NO-ANSWER-IS-ALWAYS-A-COMPLETE-ANSWER
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A-YES-OR-NO-ANSWER-IS-ALWAYS-A-COMPLETE-ANSWER — includes the "why were you late"/"did you finish" discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable fact that short answers genuinely are complete for some question types, extended into treating all questions as if they were that type.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because short answers aren't wrong for yes/no questions, only mismatched for open ones, the repair must **add a type-check step, not discard brevity as a strategy** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this correctly, framing the goal as matching answer shape to question type, not always elaborating.
- **Verification of death**: given one yes/no question and one open wh-question, the learner produces an appropriately-shaped answer for each — short for the first, explanatory for the second.

### MC-A-GOOD-QUESTION-CAN-BE-ANSWERED-WITH-ANYTHING-RELATED-TO-THE-TOPIC
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A-GOOD-QUESTION-CAN-BE-ANSWERED-WITH-ANYTHING-RELATED-TO-THE-TOPIC — includes the movie-time bullseye/near-miss discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable, valued norm of topical relevance, extended into treating topical relevance as sufficient for actually answering a specific question.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **a concrete, checkable test** (does this response state the specific thing asked, or just something nearby?) rather than an abstract caution against "vagueness," since a specific test is more actionable than a general warning.
- **Verification of death**: given a specific question and both a bullseye and a near-miss candidate answer, the learner correctly identifies the bullseye as the only true answer, explaining why the near-miss fails despite being topically reasonable.

## Analogies

**Best — the Question Dartboard** (Blueprint Component 3): a dartboard with a bullseye labeled "exactly what was asked" and outer rings labeled "same general topic" (a near miss) and "off topic" (a total miss) directly seeds the bullseye-precision fix; a separate basket-sort of question cards into "yes/no" and "explain" baskets directly seeds the answer-type-matching fix.
*Breaking point*: a dartboard has a single, objectively correct bullseye location, while some real questions genuinely admit more than one equally valid, fully-answering response (several correct ways to state the same fact) — useful for the precision-over-topical-relatedness intuition, less precise for implying every question has exactly one uniquely correct bullseye phrasing.

**Alternative — a customer service request versus a vague complaint.** A specific request ("I'd like a refund for order #4521") gets a specific, actionable response, while a vague complaint ("your service is bad") tends to receive an equally vague, unsatisfying reply — the specificity of the question and the specificity of the answer are directly linked, and a specific question deserves (and should receive) an equally specific answer.
*Breaking point*: customer service interactions often have institutional scripts guiding response format regardless of the question's specificity, while ordinary conversational question-answering has no such external script — useful for the specific-question-deserves-specific-answer intuition, less precise for implying real conversations follow as structured a format as customer service exchanges.

**Visual anchor — the Question Dartboard** as already described above, doubling as both the primary teaching metaphor and the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"Shorter answers are always better — get to the point."** Directly installs MC-A-YES-OR-NO-ANSWER-IS-ALWAYS-A-COMPLETE-ANSWER by implying brevity is universally the right target, regardless of question type.
- **"As long as your answer is about the right subject, you've answered the question."** Directly installs MC-A-GOOD-QUESTION-CAN-BE-ANSWERED-WITH-ANYTHING-RELATED-TO-THE-TOPIC as a stated standard, exactly the topical-sufficiency error this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Question Dartboard (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Which ring do you think each of these three candidate answers lands in?" Then throw the marker and confirm.
2. **The "why were you late"/"did you finish" collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Does 'Yes' make sense as an answer to both of these questions?" Then resolve.
3. **The movie-time bullseye/near-miss collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Does the near-miss answer actually tell you what time the movie starts?" Then resolve.
4. **The full question-answer exchange (learner activity, TA-4).** *Predict first*: "Do you expect all your answers in this exchange to hit the bullseye?" Then conduct the exchange and confirm together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (type-matched answering; bullseye precision) is directly checkable through the learner's own sorting and ranking of real question-answer pairs.

1. **Need**: "Does 'Yes' or 'No' make sense as an answer to every question, or only some?"
2. **Playground**: the learner sorts a mixed batch of questions into "yes/no" and "explain" baskets freely, before being told the "official" categorization rule.
3. **Invention**: "For 'How much does the ticket cost?', which of these two answers actually tells you the cost?" The learner discovers the bullseye distinction directly.
4. **Collision**: present a near-miss, topic-adjacent answer and ask whether it genuinely answers the question that was asked.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — match answer shape to question type, and check for bullseye precision, not just topical relevance — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Match the shape to the question type — and hit the bullseye, not just the general area."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Question-type sorting (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting MC-A-YES-OR-NO-ANSWER-IS-ALWAYS-A-COMPLETE-ANSWER before the bullseye-precision content is introduced, since matching answer shape to question type is the more foundational discrimination.
- **The bullseye test for answer relevance (TA-2) comes second**, directly confronting the second misconception once the learner already correctly shapes answers by type, refining that shaped answer's actual content precision.
- **Formulating clear questions (TA-3) comes third**, extending the bullseye standard from evaluating answers to constructing questions that are themselves precise enough to elicit a genuine bullseye response.
- **The full exchange (TA-4) comes last**, requiring the learner to integrate type-matching (TA-1), bullseye-precision (TA-2), and clear-question-formulation (TA-3) simultaneously across a genuine, multi-turn exchange — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Matching answer type to question type (MP-1) and identifying the bullseye answer (MP-2).** Per the Blueprint's own S9 protocol routing, mastery must NOT be certified unless both pass — a learner who formulates clear questions (MP-3) but still mismatches answer types or accepts vague answers has not internalized the precision this concept requires.
- **Formulating a clear question (MP-3).** Listen for a genuine narrowing from vague to specific (not merely rephrasing the vague question in different words while remaining equally unanswerable).
- **Full exchange demonstration (MP-4).** This concept's synthesis item; a strong performance here with weak MP-1/MP-2 scores suggests scripted-practice success without full internalization.
- **Self-correcting a drifted answer (MP-5).** This is the concept's most metacognitive item — watch for the learner independently noticing the drift, not merely producing a bullseye answer when directly told the first attempt was wrong.

**Mastery certification trigger**: correctly type-matched answers on a novel yes/no and open-question pair; correct bullseye identification with stated reasoning on a novel three-candidate set; a genuinely narrowed, specific question rewritten from a vague one; a full 4-question exchange with both clear questions and bullseye-accurate, type-matched answers throughout; and independent self-correction of a drifted answer.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But my answer was about the right thing, wasn't it enough?"** — take this as a genuine, reasonable defense of a topically-connected response; respond by naming the actual test directly: "It's definitely on the right topic — now let's check specifically: does it state the exact thing that was asked, or something related but different?"
- **"I didn't know what else to say to that question"** — a genuine difficulty formulating an explanatory response to an open question; respond with a concrete scaffold: "Start with 'Because...' — that word alone will pull you toward an actual explanation instead of a one-word answer."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full 4-question exchange, down to **a single question-answer pair, either just a type-matching task or just a bullseye-ranking task, not both at once.** This removes the multi-skill integration entirely, isolating one basic skill before returning to the harder, combined exchange task.
- **Never shrink to "just say something related if you're not sure of the exact answer."** This directly licenses MC-A-GOOD-QUESTION-CAN-BE-ANSWERED-WITH-ANYTHING-RELATED-TO-THE-TOPIC's near-miss pattern, undermining the bullseye-precision habit this concept specifically builds.
