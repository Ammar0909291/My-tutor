## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Select and emphasize the key events of a story for audience engagement, rather than reciting every detail in flat, undifferentiated sequence.
2. Sequence selected key events (buildup, turning point, resolution) for maximum narrative engagement.
3. Mark where vocal pacing, pauses, and emphasis should fall to match a story's emotional shape.
4. Explain why a flat, monotone delivery undermines an otherwise exciting story despite accurate content.
5. Tell a complete, original oral story: select and sequence key events, and deliver with matched vocal expression.

## Core Understanding

A good oral story **selects and emphasizes the events that matter, rather than including every detail in exact chronological order** — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact: a retelling that lists every minor detail with equal weight produces a flat, exhausting info-dump, while a retelling that identifies 2-3 genuine turning points and gives them proportionally more time and emphasis produces an engaging, shaped narrative — exactly as a tour guide who reads every line of a building's blueprint aloud fails to engage visitors, while one who points out what's actually interesting succeeds. The concept's second, equally load-bearing fact is that **vocal expression — tone, pacing, emphasis — is an integral part of how a story is actually received, not an optional decoration separate from its "real" content**. A story's exciting events told in a flat, unvarying monotone contain the identical facts as the same events told with quickened pacing during tension and a pause before resolution, but the flat version simply doesn't land as exciting; how a story is said is genuinely part of what is being communicated, not a separate layer on top of accurate content.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "a good oral story includes every detail in exact order, and how I say it doesn't matter as long as the facts are accurate."**
The runnable simulation: recount events exhaustively and chronologically with equal weight given to all details, delivered in a flat, unvarying tone, believing thoroughness and factual accuracy are the whole job. This is a reasonable default, since accuracy and completeness genuinely feel like responsible standards for recounting something that actually happened, and vocal delivery can feel like a separate performance skill unrelated to getting the facts right.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing an exhaustive, unweighted retelling against a selective, emphasized one and asking which holds a listener's attention; and comparing an exciting chase scene told in flat monotone against the same content with pacing and pause changes, asking whether the flat version actually feels exciting.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Select and emphasize what actually matters — and match your voice to what's happening, because how you say it is part of what you're communicating."

**Intermediate — "I select and emphasize key events now, but I still deliver even the exciting parts in roughly the same flat tone throughout."**
The simulation: correctly trims and structures the story's content, but hasn't yet connected vocal delivery to that content's emotional shape, treating the two as separate tasks (get the story right, then just say it).
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — the flat-song-lyrics parallel, recognizing that a song's melody genuinely matters, not just its lyrics, and a flat delivery of exciting content simply doesn't land as exciting.
*Shelf-life warning*: "How you say something is part of what you're actually communicating — match your pacing, tone, and emphasis to your story's emotional shape, don't leave your voice flat regardless of content."

**Advanced — "I select and sequence key events for engagement, and I deliver them with vocal pacing, pauses, and emphasis matched to the story's emotional shape."**
The simulation: tells a complete, original story with both structural selection and integrated vocal expression, producing a genuinely engaging oral narrative. This is the target model, matching TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: the direct cross-link `eng.literature.dramatic-structure`, where this concept's event-selection-and-sequencing skill extends into formal literary structural analysis.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the practical skill of shaping and delivering a spoken story. The next step analyzes dramatic structure more formally, as a literary framework."

**Expert — "at full storytelling fluency, event selection and vocal expression happen as an integrated, largely intuitive part of narration, with deliberate planning reserved for high-stakes or unfamiliar storytelling contexts (public speaking, performance)."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit select-sequence-deliver routine is a scaffold for a skill that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated, largely automatic part of natural storytelling — named here so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is fluent, intuitive narrative shaping, not a permanently maintained conscious checklist.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **thoroughness and accuracy genuinely are valued standards elsewhere** (in factual reporting, in following instructions, in academic honesty), so a learner's instinct to recount everything in exact order is a reasonable extension of those otherwise-correct standards into a context (oral storytelling) where a different standard — selective emphasis for engagement — actually applies.

The second mechanism is that **vocal delivery and factual content feel like genuinely separate channels**, since a learner can consciously control word choice and content decisions much more deliberately than they consciously monitor their own tone and pacing — this makes content feel like "the real story" and delivery feel like an optional add-on, even though a listener experiences both simultaneously as one integrated communication.

The third mechanism, specific to this concept's terminal position in the curriculum, is that **without further scaffolded unlocks to reinforce vocal-expression integration specifically**, a learner exiting with only partial mastery of that half of this concept (per the Blueprint's own S9 failure-routing, where MP-3/MP-4 both diagnose the same underlying misconception) has no built-in downstream concept designed to catch and repair the gap, even though `eng.literature.dramatic-structure` explicitly builds on this concept's overall skill.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-A-GOOD-ORAL-STORY-INCLUDES-EVERY-DETAIL-IN-EXACT-ORDER
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the exhaustive-retelling/selective-retelling discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely valued standard of thoroughness and accuracy, extended into a storytelling context that specifically calls for selective emphasis instead.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because thoroughness isn't a wrong value in general, only mismatched to this specific task, the repair must **redirect the value toward selecting what matters, not discard accuracy or care** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this correctly, framing selection as a skill applied on top of, not instead of, caring about the story.
- **Verification of death**: given an exhaustive retelling, the learner cuts it down to the 2-3 most important moments, explaining why each one matters to the story's impact.

### MC-B-VOCAL-EXPRESSION-IS-AN-OPTIONAL-EXTRA-SEPARATE-FROM-THE-REAL-CONTENT-OF-THE-STORY
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-B — includes the flat-monotone/expressive-delivery discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), reinforced by the learner's own asymmetric conscious access to content decisions (deliberate, felt) versus vocal delivery (largely automatic, less consciously monitored) — content feels like "the real story" specifically because it's what the speaker is aware of choosing.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **a vivid, immediately recognizable parallel** (a song's melody mattering, not just its lyrics) that the learner already intuitively accepts, rather than an abstract claim that "delivery matters," since the learner already has the relevant intuition in a different domain — it just hasn't been connected to their own spoken storytelling yet.
- **Verification of death**: given a short story passage, the learner marks specific points where pacing should quicken, where a pause belongs, and where emphasis should land, rather than treating the passage as tonally uniform throughout.

## Analogies

**Best — the Blueprint-Reading-Tour-Guide and Flat-Song-Lyrics Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): a tour guide reading every line of a building's blueprint aloud instead of pointing out what's actually interesting directly seeds the selective-emphasis fix; a song whose melody supposedly doesn't matter as long as the lyrics are correct, delivered in flat monotone, directly seeds the vocal-expression-is-integral fix.
*Breaking point*: a blueprint has one single, objectively correct "interesting" set of features a good guide would highlight, while a story's most engaging moments can be genuinely, reasonably debatable between different tellers — useful for the selective-not-exhaustive intuition, less precise for implying there's always one uncontestable "correct" selection of key moments.

**Alternative — a photographer's edited highlight reel versus every single unedited photo from an event.** A skilled photographer doesn't show every single shot taken at an event — they select and sequence the images that actually tell the story of what happened, cutting the redundant or unremarkable ones, exactly as a skilled oral storyteller selects and sequences events rather than reciting everything.
*Breaking point*: photo editing happens after the fact, with time to deliberate over every choice, while oral storytelling often happens in real time with much less opportunity for extended reflection — useful for the selection-over-inclusion intuition, less precise for the deliberateness gap between edited-after-the-fact and live, in-the-moment storytelling.

**Visual anchor — the Blueprint-Reading-Tour-Guide and Flat-Song-Lyrics Anchor** as already described above, doubling as both the primary teaching metaphor and the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"A good storyteller never leaves anything out — tell it exactly how it happened."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated standard, precisely the exhaustive-inclusion error this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"As long as you get the facts right, how you say it doesn't really matter."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated assumption, exactly the delivery-is-separate error this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Blueprint-Reading-Tour-Guide and Flat-Song-Lyrics Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Which tour guide do you think visitors would enjoy more?" Then resolve, and extend to the song-lyrics parallel.
2. **The exhaustive-retelling/selective-retelling collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "Which retelling would hold a listener's attention?" Then compare and resolve.
3. **The flat-monotone/expressive-delivery collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-B. *Predict first*: "Does the flat version actually feel exciting, even with identical content?" Then compare and resolve.
4. **The full original oral storytelling task (learner activity, TA-4).** *Predict first*: "Which 2-3 moments from this new event do you think will matter most?" Then tell the full story and confirm together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (selective-emphasis engagement; vocal-expression integration) is directly, personally felt through the learner's own comparison of retellings and deliveries.

1. **Need**: "If you told me every single detail of your morning in exact order, would that actually be an interesting story?"
2. **Playground**: the learner drafts both an exhaustive and a selective retelling of the same event freely, comparing which feels more engaging.
3. **Invention**: "Which 2-3 moments in your exhaustive draft actually matter most?" The learner trims their own draft down.
4. **Collision**: present the flat-monotone chase scene and ask whether it actually feels exciting despite accurate content.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — select and emphasize what matters, and match vocal delivery to content — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Pick the moments that matter, and say them like they matter."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Selecting key events (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting MC-A before sequencing or delivery content is introduced, since the core skill of identifying what matters must be secure before refining how to order or deliver it.
- **Sequencing selected events for engagement (TA-2) comes second**, extending TA-1's selection skill into an ordering judgment (buildup, turning point, resolution), building the story's actual shape.
- **Matching vocal expression to content (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting MC-B once the story's content and shape (TA-1/TA-2) are already secure, since matching delivery to content presupposes the content itself is already selected and sequenced.
- **The full application (TA-4) comes last**, requiring the learner to integrate selection (TA-1), sequencing (TA-2), and vocal expression (TA-3) simultaneously on a genuinely new, original story — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's most integrative, generative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the mandatory "pick your 3 moments first" planning step for learners who revert to exhaustive retelling under a new prompt, the pacing/pause/emphasis annotation scaffold, and the transfer parallel with `eng.speaking.conversation-skills`'s own listener-awareness).

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **inherently spoken and performance-based** — the learner's actual delivered story, including both its structural shape and its vocal expression, observed directly, making this among the most directly performance-dependent, creation-oriented concepts in this program (Bloom level: create).

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A genuine pacing shift (faster during tension, a pause before a key moment, emphasis on a critical word) occurring at a point that actually corresponds to the story's emotional content** — the direct target evidence for the second misconception's repair.
- **A retelling that visibly compresses or skips minor details while dwelling on 2-3 clearly emphasized moments** — the direct target evidence for the first misconception's repair.
- **A flat, evenly-paced delivery with no vocal variation across a story containing genuinely varied emotional content (tension, surprise, relief)** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-B still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Pick the moments that matter — and say them like they matter."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I want to tell it exactly how it happened, isn't that more honest?"** — take this as a genuine, values-driven concern, not a mistake to be dismissed; respond by naming the actual distinction directly: "Selecting the key moments doesn't mean changing what happened — it means choosing which true parts to spend the most time on, so the listener actually feels what mattered, instead of getting lost in every minor detail."
- **"I don't really change how I talk depending on what's happening in the story"** — a genuine, honest self-report; respond with a concrete, low-stakes practice step: "Let's try the exact same short story twice — once completely flat, once where you speed up during the exciting part and pause right before the ending. Notice how differently each version actually feels, even with the same words."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full original oral story with selection, sequencing, and delivery, down to **trimming a single, already-provided exhaustive account down to just its 2-3 key moments, with no sequencing or delivery demand at all.** This removes the sequencing and delivery components entirely, isolating the most basic selection skill before returning to the harder, fuller storytelling task.
- **Never shrink to "just tell the story any way you want, it's fine."** This abandons both the selective-structuring and vocal-expression skills this concept specifically builds, producing no genuine progress toward mastery.
