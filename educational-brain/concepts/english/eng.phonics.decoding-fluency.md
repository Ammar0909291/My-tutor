## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Demonstrate increased speed and ease on repeated readings of the same short, decodable text.
2. Apply appropriate phrasing and pausing at punctuation marks, connecting written punctuation to natural spoken rhythm.
3. Match spoken expression to sentence type (flat/falling for statements, rising for yes/no questions, energetic for exclamations).
4. Self-regulate reading pace to preserve both accuracy and comprehension, rather than maximising either speed or word-by-word correctness alone.
5. Read a genuinely novel, decodable passage with accuracy, automaticity, and appropriate expression together, and summarise its content afterward.

## Core Understanding

Fluent reading is **accuracy, automaticity, and prosody/expression operating together** — not any one of the three alone, and specifically not accuracy alone, which is the single most common and most consequential oversimplification of "good reading" a learner brings to this concept. **Accuracy** means reading words correctly; **automaticity** means doing so quickly and effortlessly, without the conscious, letter-by-letter or syllable-by-syllable sounding-out that earlier decoding concepts in this program deliberately built and required; **prosody/expression** means reading with the natural rhythm, stress, and intonation of actual speech — pausing at punctuation, rising for questions, matching energy to exclamations — rather than in a flat, uninflected monotone. The concept's central, structurally important claim is that accuracy without the other two **still blocks comprehension**: a learner who decodes every word correctly but so slowly and effortfully that no mental capacity remains for holding the sentence's meaning in mind has not yet achieved fluency, regardless of how technically correct each individual word was — decoding accuracy is a necessary foundation, not a sufficient endpoint. The evidence-based instructional method this concept is built around is **repeated reading**: reading the same short, already-decodable text multiple times in immediate succession reliably produces a felt, measurable increase in speed and naturalness on each subsequent pass, which is how automaticity is actually built — not through decoding ever-more-difficult new material, but through sufficient repetition on material that is already within the learner's decoding reach.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "reading well means getting every word right; once I can decode a word correctly, the job is done."**
The runnable simulation: apply careful, effortful, letter-by-letter or syllable-by-syllable decoding to every word, treating a fully correct reading — however slow, however flat — as the complete goal. This is not tutor-installed — it is the direct, natural, and entirely reasonable residue of every prior phonics concept in this curriculum, which has correctly and necessarily emphasised getting the decoding right above all else, since accuracy genuinely must come first.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — reading every word of a short passage correctly, taking nearly a minute for two sentences, and then being asked to explain what the sentence meant.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Every word right is the foundation, not the finish line. Speed and expression are two further things fluency actually needs."

**Intermediate — "I understand fluency needs more than accuracy, and I try to read as fast as possible to demonstrate it."**
The simulation: correctly move beyond pure accuracy-as-sufficient, and interpret "more than accuracy" as primarily meaning speed, pushing pace upward even at the cost of occasionally skipping or mumbling an unfamiliar word or ignoring a punctuation-signalled pause. This model has correctly rejected the first misconception and has swung toward an equally incomplete overcorrection.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — reading quickly, skipping "because" and ignoring a period, then being unable to clearly explain what happened in the sentence.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Speed only helps if accuracy and expression come with it. Rushing past a word or a punctuation mark isn't fluency — it's just fast, and it breaks understanding just as much as reading too slowly does."

**Advanced — "fluency is accuracy, automaticity, and expression working together, built through repeated reading of already-decodable text, and the actual test of whether it's working is comprehension, not speed for its own sake."**
The simulation: given any decodable text, familiar or novel, the learner reads it accurately, at a natural pace, with appropriate expression, and can immediately summarise what was read — treating the ability to explain the content, not raw words-per-minute, as the actual evidence fluency has been achieved. This is the target model, matching TA-4 and TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: `eng.vocab.word-recognition` and `eng.reading.print-to-meaning` (the two direct unlocks), where this concept's fluent, automatic decoding becomes the foundation for meaning-focused reading work that assumes decoding itself is no longer the bottleneck.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gets decoding out of the way so meaning can be the focus. The next steps assume you can already do this, and build reading comprehension on top of it."

**Expert — "fluency functions as a bridge skill precisely because working memory has a fixed capacity, and effortful decoding consumes exactly the capacity that comprehension monitoring, inference-making, and meaning-construction require — making fluency not merely a nice-to-have polish on top of decoding but a genuine precondition for comprehension at any text complexity beyond the simplest sentences."**
Named here to mark the arc; this is the theoretical account (the "simple view of reading" and working-memory-capacity research) underlying why this concept exists at all, and while not required for mastery, it explains precisely why the Blueprint's own comprehension-check items (MP-1) are the correct, non-arbitrary way to verify fluency rather than merely measuring speed.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **every single prior phonics concept in this program has correctly and necessarily emphasised getting the decoding right**, with speed and expression genuinely secondary or entirely absent from instruction up to this point — a learner who has just spent the preceding several concepts mastering syllable types and sight words specifically to get individual words correct has excellent reason to treat "correct" as the complete and final goal, since that has been, quite reasonably, the goal of everything preceding this concept.

The second mechanism, once accuracy-as-sufficient is corrected, is a **natural overcorrection toward speed as the new, most salient, most easily-measured proxy for fluency** — words-per-minute is concrete, countable, and easy to praise, while automaticity's actual defining feature (effortlessness that *preserves* accuracy and comprehension) is harder to observe directly, making it tempting for both learner and observer to substitute the easily-measured proxy (speed) for the genuinely intended target (effortless, comprehension-supporting reading), exactly the substitution the Blueprint's own second misconception describes.

The third mechanism, specific to expression and prosody, is that **matching intonation and rhythm to sentence type and punctuation is itself a genuinely separate skill from decoding**, drawing directly on this program's own prior phonetics-strand work (sentence stress, intonation patterns) rather than on anything the phonics strand alone has built — a learner whose decoding is strong but who has not yet secured or transferred that separate prosodic skill into the reading-aloud context will predictably read accurately and even automatically while still sounding flat, and this is a distinct, separately-addressable gap from either of the two named misconceptions, worth diagnosing independently.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-ACCURATE-EQUALS-FLUENT
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-ACCURATE-EQUALS-FLUENT — includes the slow-flat-versus-natural-pace and flat-question-versus-rising-question discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the entirely correct and necessary prior emphasis on decoding accuracy across every preceding phonics concept in this program, extended past its actual scope to cover the whole of what "good reading" means.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because the over-extended prior knowledge (accuracy matters enormously) remains completely correct and should not be undermined, the repair must **add two further, genuinely new requirements without diminishing accuracy's continued importance** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this precisely, naming accuracy as one of three necessary parts rather than replacing it, and the comprehension-check instrument (asking the learner to explain what was just read) is the correct evidence source because it makes the *cost* of accuracy-without-the-rest concrete and felt, rather than merely asserted.
- **Verification of death**: given a novel decodable passage, the learner reads it at a natural pace with appropriate expression and can immediately, successfully summarise its content — the summarisation, not merely the reading itself, is the actual target evidence, since a learner could in principle read fluently-sounding text while still not comprehending it, and the reverse failure (accurate-but-uncomprehended reading) is exactly what this misconception predicts.

### MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL — includes the skipped-word-versus-included-word and honoured-comma-versus-ignored-comma discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization) again, but of a different, more recently-acquired piece of knowledge — the just-taught insight that fluency requires more than accuracy, over-extended specifically toward speed as the most obvious, most measurable candidate for "the something more."
- **Teaching consequence**: because this misconception is a direct, predictable overcorrection from the first, the repair must be sequenced to arrive *after* the first misconception has been addressed, not before or simultaneously — exactly as the Blueprint's own TA-3/TA-4 ordering does, introducing expression-matching (TA-3, which corrects the first misconception) before the speed-accuracy balance check (TA-4, which corrects the second) — and the repair itself requires reframing speed as a *consequence* of genuine fluency rather than a *goal* to chase directly, which the Blueprint's own replacement text states explicitly.
- **Verification of death**: given a passage read quickly, the learner can still answer simple comprehension questions about it correctly and completely — and, separately, when asked to self-assess their own pace, the learner can identify whether they skipped or mumbled anything, rather than only reporting how fast they felt they went.

## Analogies

**Best — learning to drive: knowing the rules of the road (accuracy) is necessary but not sufficient; a new driver who checks every mirror deliberately and consciously before every single manoeuvre (effortful, non-automatic) cannot yet safely merge into fast-moving traffic, and a driver who goes fast without properly checking mirrors or signalling (speed without care) is equally unsafe.** Fluent, safe driving requires the rules to become automatic enough that attention is free for the actual road conditions — exactly paralleling fluent reading freeing attention for comprehension.
*Breaking point*: driving safety failures have immediate, visible physical consequences that make the two failure modes easy to recognise; reading fluency failures (comprehension loss) are less immediately visible, requiring the explicit comprehension-check instrument this concept uses specifically because the failure isn't otherwise obvious.

**Alternative — a musician sight-reading a new piece versus performing a well-rehearsed one.** Sight-reading (like effortful, accurate-but-slow decoding) requires full conscious attention to just getting the notes right, leaving little room for musical expression. A well-rehearsed performance (like fluent reading) frees that attention, allowing genuine expression and interpretation, precisely because the note-reading itself has become automatic through repeated practice.
*Breaking point*: musicians typically rehearse the *same* piece repeatedly to reach performance fluency on it specifically; reading fluency, once established as a general skill (via repeated practice on several texts), is meant to transfer to genuinely novel material — useful for the repeated-practice-builds-automaticity mechanism, not for implying fluency is piece-specific the way a rehearsed musical performance is.

**Story analogy — a tour guide reciting facts from memory in a flat monotone versus a tour guide who has told the same story so many times it now comes with natural, engaged, appropriately-paced delivery.** The first guide is accurate but exhausting to listen to and hard to actually absorb information from; the second guide's fluency (built through repetition) makes the same facts genuinely land with the audience.
*Breaking point*: none serious; a reasonably close mapping to the accuracy-versus-fluent-delivery distinction, including the repetition-builds-fluency mechanism.

**Visual analogy — the repeated-reading race** (Blueprint Component 3): reading the same short sentence two or three times in immediate succession and noticing the felt increase in speed and ease each time. Not a metaphor but the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching method is organised around, directly demonstrating the repetition-builds-automaticity mechanism rather than merely asserting it.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"Fluent reading means reading like a professional news anchor."** Sets an intimidatingly polished, performative standard that risks discouraging a learner whose genuinely fluent, natural reading doesn't resemble broadcast delivery — the target is natural, comprehension-supporting expression, not professional performance polish.
- **"The faster you read, the more fluent you are."** Directly installs MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL as a definition rather than correcting it, conflating the (real, but secondary) speed component with the whole of what fluency means.
- **"Once you can decode, fluency comes automatically with no extra work."** Understates the genuine, separate practice this concept requires — automaticity and expression are real skills built through the specific technique (repeated reading) this concept teaches, not an automatic side effect of decoding competence alone, and several learners with strong decoding accuracy genuinely need this concept's dedicated practice to become fluent.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The repeated-reading race (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*, before the second and third readings: "Do you think this will feel any different the second time through?"
2. **The accurate-but-slow collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence. *Predict first*: "If every word is read correctly, do you think that guarantees you understood what it meant?" Then read slowly and correctly, and test comprehension directly.
3. **The rushed-and-skipped collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "If you read as fast as possible, do you think you'll definitely understand it better than reading at a natural pace?" Then read quickly, skipping a word and a punctuation pause, and test comprehension directly.
4. **The expression-matching task (learner activity, TA-3).** *Predict first*: "Do you think a question and a statement should sound the same when read aloud, or different?" Then read both and compare.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for the repeated-reading mechanism itself**, since the evidence (felt, immediate improvement across repeated attempts) is directly available to the learner through their own experience, but the **specific three-part definition of fluency (accuracy, automaticity, expression) is best delivered directly**, since it is a definitional claim about what the word "fluency" means in this technical sense, not something to be independently derived.

1. **Need**: "If you read the exact same sentence three times in a row, do you think anything would change?"
2. **Playground**: the learner reads a short, already-decodable sentence two or three times in immediate succession, without being told in advance what to expect.
3. **Invention**: "What did you notice changing across the three readings?" The learner identifies, from their own felt experience, that the later readings came faster and more naturally.
4. **Collision**: contrast this felt improvement against attempting a *different*, equally difficult but unpractised sentence immediately afterward — confirming the improvement was specific to repetition on that particular text, not a general warm-up effect alone.
5. **Formalization**: name the mechanism — repeated reading of already-decodable text is how automaticity is actually built, which is why this concept's core technique works.
6. **Compression**: "Read it again. And again. That's how fast, natural reading gets built — not by trying to read fast the first time."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Repeated reading for automaticity (TA-1) comes first**, establishing the core mechanism and instrument this whole concept is built around before either misconception is directly confronted — the learner needs firsthand, felt experience of what automaticity-building actually feels like before either "accuracy alone is enough" or "speed above all else" can be meaningfully contrasted against it.
- **Punctuation-signalled phrasing (TA-2) and expression matching (TA-3) are introduced before the speed-accuracy balance check (TA-4)**, and TA-3's expression work is deliberately the vehicle for confronting the first misconception (accurate-equals-fluent) — establishing that fluency includes expression, and confronting the too-narrow accuracy-only view, happens before the second misconception (the too-fast overcorrection) is even introduced, matching the Blueprint's own explicit ordering and the reasoning given under Misconceptions above (the second misconception is a predictable overcorrection from the first, and must be addressed after it, not before).
- **The speed-accuracy balance check (TA-4) is deliberately structured around a comprehension-check consequence** (per the Blueprint's own design, verified via the learner's ability to answer questions after reading), rather than around a simple, self-reported pace judgement — this instrumentation choice is what makes the overcorrection's cost concrete and felt rather than asserted, exactly paralleling the comprehension-check instrument already used for the first misconception.
- **Full, novel-passage fluency (TA-5) comes last**, requiring accuracy, automaticity, and expression together on genuinely unseen material — matching the general principle established throughout this program that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating, certifying stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the repeated-reading-as-core-technique designation, the three-components-assessed-together mandate, the decodable-text prerequisite for fluency practice, and prosody-transfer-difficulty anticipation for S9 learners).

## Tutor Actions

In recommended order, from `../teaching-actions/`:

1. **Demonstration** (SHOW) — the repeated-reading race and the accurate-but-slow and rushed-and-skipped collisions, all benefiting from being heard aloud and, for the race specifically, learner-performed across multiple passes.
2. **Retrieval-Schedule Prompt** (TEST-THINKING) — the repeated-reading technique is, at its core, a spaced/repeated-exposure retrieval task applied to whole-text fluency rather than to individual facts, and functions as the load-bearing action for this entire concept.
3. **Error Analysis** (TEST-THINKING) — the speed-accuracy balance check (TA-4) is fundamentally an error-analysis exercise: identifying, via comprehension-question failure or direct observation, whether words were skipped or mumbled to gain speed, and correcting the specific behaviour.
4. **Matching** (DO) — sentence type or punctuation mark to correct expressive delivery (statement to flat/falling; question to rising; exclamation to energetic), directly building the target prosody-in-reading skill.
5. **Prediction** (TEST-THINKING) — predicting whether a given reading pace preserved comprehension before checking, directly building the self-regulation habit this concept's second misconception's repair depends on.

**Does not fit**: **Concept Map / Organize** as a primary action — this concept's content (a three-part fluency definition, applied through repeated practice) is better built through direct, repeated performance practice than through a static, categorised artefact. **Worked Example** in the multi-step-derivation sense — there is no calculation to model, only a performance skill better served by Demonstration and repeated practice.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **audio**, encompassing accuracy (correct word production), automaticity (speed and effortlessness, inferred from timing and the absence of effortful sounding-out), and prosody (stress, rhythm, and intonation matching sentence type) — making it a genuine integration point for evidence types this program's phonics and phonetics strands have separately built, and correspondingly affected by the plain-text STT capture gap (`../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §7`) specifically on its automaticity and expression dimensions, even though its accuracy dimension (which words were said) remains fully capturable in text.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Slow, effortful, letter-by-letter or syllable-by-syllable sounding-out of words the learner has previously decoded correctly and even fluently in an earlier session.** The direct signature of automaticity not yet having transferred to this specific text or context, distinguishable from a genuine new-word decoding difficulty by the fact that the word itself has been correctly read before.
- **Flat, unvarying intonation and stress across a passage containing genuine sentence-type variety (statements, questions, exclamations).** The direct signature of the expression gap identified under Why Students Fail's third mechanism — decoding accuracy and even reasonable speed can co-occur with this flatness, and it should be diagnosed as a distinct, separately-addressable skill gap, not folded into a general "not fluent yet" assessment.
- **Rushed production with audible word-skipping, mumbling through an unfamiliar word rather than attempting it, or no audible pause at a comma or period.** The direct signature of MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL, and it is often accompanied by the learner's own subsequent inability to answer a simple comprehension question, which should be used as the concrete, felt evidence for the repair rather than only naming the rushed pace itself.
- **A felt, audible increase in ease and pace across repeated readings of the same short text, with expression emerging or improving on later passes even without explicit instruction to add it.** The target automaticity-building trajectory this concept's core technique is designed to produce, and it should be named and reinforced explicitly when observed.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Read it again, at a pace where you could still tell me what it meant — speed that costs you the meaning isn't fluency."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Comprehension check after fluent reading (MP-1).** This is this concept's single most diagnostic item type, precisely because it is the one instrument that can distinguish genuine fluency from either failure mode — a reasonable-paced, expressive reading followed by successful summarisation is the full target state; a reasonable-paced, expressive-*sounding* reading followed by failed summarisation would indicate the fluency is more superficial (performed) than genuinely comprehension-supporting, a distinction worth actively checking for rather than assuming away.
- **Punctuation-signalled expression (MP-2).** *Correct differentiation between an exclamatory and a flat-statement reading of near-identical content* → straightforward positive evidence for the expression component specifically, largely independent of the accuracy or speed components.
- **Speed-accuracy self-check (MP-3).** *Self-regulates to a pace that is neither over-slow nor over-fast, without being told the exact target pace* → strong evidence of genuine self-monitoring, since the item explicitly does not specify a pace and requires the learner to judge for themselves.
- **Novel passage fluency (MP-4).** The concept's actual mastery bar, mirroring the equivalent explicit designation already established at `eng.phonics.syllable-types` — a learner who succeeds on every prior, more isolated item but has not yet been tested on genuinely unseen connected text has not yet met this concept's real target.
- **Explanation (MP-5).** Listen for whether the explanation names all three components (accuracy, automaticity/speed, expression/prosody) and, ideally, articulates why all three matter for comprehension specifically — a response naming only accuracy, or only speed, has reverted to one of the two misconceptions even if prior items were answered correctly by other means.

**Mastery certification trigger**: fluent (accurate, natural-paced, appropriately expressive) reading of a novel decodable passage, immediately followed by successful, correct summarisation of its content; correct expression-matching for at least one statement, one question, and one exclamation; and self-regulated pacing that avoids both excessive slowness and word-skipping on a novel passage, verified by subsequent comprehension questions. The comprehension-verification requirement, present in multiple items, is essential and non-negotiable — per the Core Understanding above, comprehension support is the actual reason fluency matters, and any certification that omits it risks certifying a merely performance-level fluency that doesn't yet serve its real purpose.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I got every word right!"** — a genuine, understandable protest given everything this program's prior phonics concepts have correctly emphasised; respond by validating the accomplishment while adding, not subtracting: "You really did — that's the hard part, and you've got it. Now let's work on the part where it also sounds natural and you can tell me what it meant right after." Never frame this as the accuracy having been insufficient or wrong to focus on.
- **"I thought reading fast was the goal, why am I being slowed down?"** — the characteristic protest during the second misconception's correction, and it should be met by returning to the concrete comprehension evidence rather than only asserting a slower pace is better: "Let's check — after reading that fast, can you tell me what happened in the story? If not, that's the real cost of going too fast, not a rule I'm making up."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from full novel-passage fluency, down to **a single, very short, already-practised sentence, read once more, with no comprehension check and no expression demand** — isolating the most basic repeated-reading-produces-ease phenomenon before returning to the harder, combined, comprehension-verified tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just read it however feels easiest, speed doesn't matter at all."** This risks reinforcing the original accuracy-without-automaticity default (the first misconception) precisely by removing any pressure toward the pace and expression components this concept genuinely requires.

## Memory Hooks

- **Concept type**: integrated performance skill (accuracy plus automaticity plus expression, applied together to connected text) built through a specific, evidence-based **practice technique** (repeated reading), verified by a **comprehension-based certification standard** rather than a speed- or accuracy-only one.
- **Review form**: brief, frequent repeated-reading sessions on short, genuinely decodable texts — never on text containing decoding challenges beyond the learner's current secure range, per the Blueprint's own explicit prerequisite ("text must be decodable before fluency practice begins") — always paired with a comprehension check, never assessed on reading performance alone.
- **Concept-specific deviation**: unlike most concepts in this program, this skill's natural maintenance mechanism is largely **built into its own downstream use**: `eng.vocab.word-recognition` and `eng.reading.print-to-meaning` both depend on and continuously re-exercise fluent decoding, meaning dedicated stand-alone review can reasonably taper once initial fluency is established, provided the learner's ongoing reading material remains genuinely decodable and is not exclusively far beyond their current fluency range.
- **Interleaving partners**: `eng.phonetics.sentence-stress` and `eng.phonetics.intonation-patterns` items should be periodically revisited alongside fluency practice specifically for learners showing the expression-gap pattern identified under Why Students Fail's third mechanism, since that gap traces directly to those concepts' skills not yet transferring into the reading-aloud context.
