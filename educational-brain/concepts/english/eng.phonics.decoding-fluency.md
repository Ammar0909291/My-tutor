# Decoding Fluency — `eng.phonics.decoding-fluency`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.phonics.decoding-fluency` (canonical English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / phonics / "Decoding Fluency" — the
  final and capstone concept of the phonics domain, shifting the goal
  from CAN the learner decode accurately to DOES the learner read with
  the automaticity and expression that frees attention for
  comprehension.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.phonics.syllable-types` — specifically the
  systematic multisyllabic decoding procedure; load-bearing because
  fluency practice requires text the learner can already decode
  accurately, and multisyllabic words are common enough in connected
  text that fluency work would otherwise be blocked by unresolved
  decoding gaps. `eng.phonics.sight-words` — specifically instant,
  automatic recognition of high-frequency words; load-bearing because
  connected text is dense with sight words, and sounding out every one
  of them (rather than recognizing them instantly) is itself a major
  source of the choppy, non-fluent reading this concept exists to
  resolve.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): this concept has no `unlocks` entry within
  the phonics domain in the raw KG data available — it functions as the
  domain's terminal concept, with its skills (accuracy, automaticity,
  prosody) consumed implicitly by every subsequent reading, listening,
  and comprehension concept across the English curriculum rather than
  gating one specific next node.
- **Difficulty**: developing · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.80 · **Est. hours**: 3

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Read a short, already-decodable text with increasing speed and ease
   across repeated readings of the same passage.
2. Pause and phrase appropriately at punctuation marks (periods,
   commas, question marks, exclamation points), connecting print
   punctuation to natural spoken rhythm.
3. Match vocal expression to sentence type (falling intonation for
   statements, rising for yes/no questions, energetic delivery for
   exclamations).
4. Self-regulate reading pace to preserve both accuracy and
   comprehension, neither reading so slowly that meaning is lost nor so
   quickly that words are skipped or mumbled.
5. Read a genuinely unseen, decodable passage with reasonable accuracy,
   automaticity, and expression, and then accurately summarize its
   content.

## Core Understanding

Fluent reading is the simultaneous presence of three components —
ACCURACY (correct words), AUTOMATICITY (effortless, rapid word
recognition rather than deliberate sounding-out), and PROSODY (natural
speech rhythm, stress, and intonation) — and fluency is only genuinely
achieved when all three are present TOGETHER, not when any one is
maximized at the expense of the others. This is the single most
consequential fact the concept must install: accuracy alone, however
perfect, is not fluency if it consumes so much conscious effort that no
capacity remains for understanding meaning; and speed alone, however
impressive in words-per-minute, is not fluency if it comes at the cost
of skipped words or ignored punctuation. The functional PURPOSE of
fluency — the reason it matters beyond sounding polished — is that
decoding effort and comprehension effort draw on the same limited
cognitive resource; a reader who has automated word recognition has
freed that resource for understanding, while a reader still consciously
sounding out every word has not, regardless of how accurate each
individual word turns out to be.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "reading the same text again makes it easier and
  faster"**: installed directly by the Component 3 repeated-reading-
  race anchor, giving the learner direct, felt evidence that
  automaticity develops through REPETITION on already-accessible text,
  not through attempting harder, unfamiliar text. This is a foundational
  insight the entire concept builds on — fluency is a product of
  practice on decodable text, not a separate decoding challenge.
- **Intermediate model — "punctuation tells me how to pace and phrase
  my reading"**: installed by TA-2, connecting print conventions
  (periods, commas, question marks) to spoken-language conventions
  (pauses, phrase boundaries, intonation contours) the learner already
  possesses from ordinary speech. Upgrade trigger: correct, unprompted
  pausing at a comma in an unfamiliar sentence.
- **Advanced model — "my expression should match the sentence's actual
  type and meaning, not remain flat regardless of content"**: installed
  by TA-3, resolving MC-ACCURATE-EQUALS-FLUENT directly — a learner who
  has secured accuracy and basic phrasing but still reads everything in
  a flat monotone has not yet reached fluency, since prosody is one of
  its three required components, not an optional polish. Upgrade
  trigger: correctly differentiated intonation on a genuinely novel
  question versus a genuinely novel statement.
- **Expert model — "I self-regulate pace to preserve BOTH accuracy and
  comprehension, checking myself rather than chasing raw speed"**:
  installed by TA-4, resolving MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL
  — the learner monitors their own reading against a comprehension
  check rather than treating speed as the sole optimization target.
  Upgrade trigger: successful self-correction (slowing down
  voluntarily) after noticing a comprehension check would fail at the
  current pace.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own explicit Adaptive
  Flag, fluency practice text must already be fully decodable using
  already-mastered phonics patterns and sight words — introducing new,
  not-yet-secure decoding challenges DURING fluency practice conflates
  two independent skills and undermines the automaticity this concept
  is specifically trying to build.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, corresponding to the
two ways a reader can fail to hold all three fluency components
together. The accurate-but-flat failure exists because accuracy has,
by this point in the curriculum, been the SOLE, exclusive success
criterion across every prior phonics concept — a learner who has just
spent the entire domain being praised for correct decoding reasonably
carries that same singular criterion forward, with no prior signal that
speed and expression are ALSO required, since they were never assessed
before this concept. The rushed-and-inaccurate failure exists for a
related but distinct reason: once a learner senses that speed is now
valued (correctly, as part of fluency), an easy and tempting shortcut
is to maximize speed alone by skipping unfamiliar words or ignoring
punctuation-signaled pauses — this is an overcorrection toward the
newly-introduced criterion (speed) at the expense of the two the
learner already had secure (accuracy, and implicitly meaning), rather
than a failure to understand what fluency requires.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.phonics.decoding-fluency.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-ACCURATE-EQUALS-FLUENT (Blueprint Component 1) — Type 1,
overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — "get every word
  right" has been the correct, sole, and exhaustively reinforced success
  criterion across the entire phonics domain up to this point, and the
  learner extends that criterion's SUFFICIENCY into this concept, where
  it is necessary but no longer sufficient, with nothing in prior
  instruction marking that the bar was about to change.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, after reading
  accurately but slowly and flatly, cannot answer a simple comprehension
  question about what they just read is showing the misconception's
  actual cost directly and concretely — this comprehension-failure
  evidence (not merely the slow/flat delivery itself) is the strongest
  and most convincing conflict-evidence probe available, since it
  demonstrates the functional consequence rather than only describing
  an abstract standard.

**MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL (Blueprint Component 1) —
Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: also Type 1, but overgeneralizing from
  the OPPOSITE direction — the learner has correctly absorbed that
  speed now matters (a new, correct addition to their success
  criteria), and extends that new criterion's IMPORTANCE past its
  proper bound, treating it as the dominant or sole goal rather than
  one of three co-equal components, mirroring the first misconception's
  mechanism in reverse.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when asked to slow
  down slightly, resists ("but I read it fast!") is showing the
  misconception as an entrenched belief that speed alone is the metric
  being optimized — this resistance itself is diagnostic and should be
  met with the Blueprint's own explicit reframing (slowing down to
  preserve all three components is a sign of skill, not a step
  backward), not merely a repeated instruction to slow down.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — a musician who plays every note correctly but with
  no rhythm or dynamics, versus one who plays fast and sloppy, versus
  one who plays with both accuracy and musicality**: fluent reading is
  like a musical performance — hitting every note (accuracy) is
  necessary but not sufficient without tempo and expression working
  together; rushing through notes without care produces speed without
  music. Breaking point: music has an aesthetic dimension reading
  fluency does not require (a "beautiful" reading is not the goal) —
  the analogy targets the THREE-COMPONENTS-TOGETHER structure, not an
  aesthetic standard.
- **Alternative — a car driven at the right speed for the road
  conditions, not the fastest possible speed regardless of safety**:
  reinforces that appropriate pace is calibrated to a purpose
  (comprehension, safety) rather than maximized in isolation. Breaking
  point: driving safety is externally imposed by law/danger, while
  reading pace is self-regulated by the reader's own comprehension
  check — the analogy should not suggest an external authority sets the
  correct speed.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT frame fluency practice as "reading faster
  each time" without qualification**: presenting repeated reading
  (TA-1/Component 3) purely as a speed-increase exercise, without the
  accompanying accuracy-and-expression framing, directly risks
  installing MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL. The repeated-
  reading anchor's actual purpose is building automaticity that FREES
  capacity for expression and comprehension, not raw speed as an end in
  itself — this framing must accompany every use of the technique.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/auditory (repeated-reading race, against yourself)**: the
  Blueprint's Component 3 anchor — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any short, already-familiar text (a favorite
  book passage, a simple sentence) substitutes for a prepared passage;
  repeated reading requires no materials beyond the text itself.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-3's expression-matching task and
  TA-4's speed-accuracy self-check are both hands-on, self-monitored
  activities requiring the learner to produce and evaluate their own
  reading.
- **Teacher-demo (three-component contrast)**: the tutor models the
  same short passage three ways — accurate-but-flat-and-slow, fast-but-
  inaccurate-and-choppy, and genuinely fluent (all three components
  together) — making the distinction audible and concrete rather than
  only described.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before reading a sentence
  with a specific punctuation mark or sentence type (TA-2/TA-3), ask
  the learner to predict how it should sound before reading it aloud —
  Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**Direct instruction wins for the three-component DEFINITION of
fluency; a brief, felt discovery step suits the repeated-reading
technique itself.** The three-component structure (accuracy +
automaticity + prosody) is not a regularity the learner can induce from
examples — it is a definitional framework that must be stated and then
demonstrated via the three-way contrast above, matching this domain's
pattern for pure-convention content. The VALUE of repeated reading,
however, is well-suited to a brief, self-evident discovery moment
built directly into Component 3's own design: **need** (the learner's
first read of a passage is effortful and slow); **playground/invention**
(re-reading the SAME passage 2-3 times, noticing for themselves that it
becomes faster and easier — a directly felt experience requiring no
external explanation); **formalization** (naming, after the felt
experience, that repetition on ALREADY-decodable text is what builds
automaticity — this is where TA-1 explicitly states the principle the
learner has just experienced); **compression** (fluent reading of
genuinely novel text in TA-5, generalizing the automaticity gained on
practiced text to text never repeated). This is a narrower, single-
session discovery moment rather than a full multi-step arc, appropriate
to a felt experience rather than an inducible rule.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (repeated reading) must come first because it establishes the
foundational mechanism (automaticity through repetition on accessible
text) that every subsequent TA depends on — expression and self-
regulation are meaningless additions on top of a decoding process still
effortful enough to consume all available attention. TA-2 (punctuation-
signaled phrasing) is placed before TA-3 (expression matching sentence
type) because phrasing at punctuation marks is a narrower, more
mechanical skill (pause at this mark) than matching intonation to
sentence TYPE, which requires the learner to interpret the sentence's
communicative function, not merely its surface punctuation — the
easier mechanical skill is secured first. TA-4 (speed-accuracy balance)
is placed after expression work, not before, because it requires the
learner to already be attempting genuine, expressive reading in order
to have a meaningful self-check to apply — checking pace against
comprehension is only informative once the reading being checked
already includes real attention to expression, not merely mechanical
decoding. TA-5 (novel-text integration) is placed last as the full
capstone, requiring all four prior skills operating together on
genuinely unseen material. This entry does not restate the Blueprint's
own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see Blueprint
References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: this concept is, more than any other in the
  phonics domain, ENTIRELY dependent on genuine audio evidence —
  accuracy, automaticity, and prosody are all properties of the
  learner's actual spoken delivery, not merely their eventual correct
  answer. Listen specifically for pace (word-by-word vs. natural flow),
  intonation contour (flat vs. matched to sentence type), and pause
  placement (at punctuation vs. arbitrary or absent).
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause at a comma or period is
  correct, expected phrasing, not struggle; a long, effortful pause
  mid-word or mid-phrase (not aligned with any punctuation) signals
  decoding effort still consuming attention, distinct from appropriate
  prosodic pausing.
- **Pronunciation stakes**: secondary here — this concept is centrally
  about PACE and EXPRESSION rather than individual phoneme accuracy,
  which prior phonics concepts already secured; dialect-consistent
  pronunciation of individual sounds should never be marked as a
  fluency problem, only genuine pace/expression/accuracy issues as
  defined by the three-component model.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "read it again — notice how much
  easier that felt" is this concept's load-bearing sentence for the
  repeated-reading technique specifically, and should be delivered with
  genuine warmth and attention after each re-read, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3`. Per this
  concept's foundations §7 citation, the voice channel's real
  instrumentation gap (captured text/timing but not always full
  prosodic signal) makes this concept's own assessment MORE dependent
  than most on live, attentive human/tutor listening rather than
  automated capture.

## Assessment Signals

- **Diagnostic (golden probe)**: the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence
  probe (Component 1) — reading accurately but slowly, then being asked
  to explain what the passage meant — cleanly surfaces
  MC-ACCURATE-EQUALS-FLUENT by testing the functional CONSEQUENCE of
  the imbalance (lost comprehension capacity), not merely the delivery
  style in isolation.
- **Guided practice → independent practice**: the Blueprint's WE-1 → WE-2
  → WE-3 → MP-1 through MP-5 fading ladder is reused directly
  (Components 5–6) — not re-authored here.
- **Mastery gate set** (`../../assessment/05-mastery-verification.md
  §3`): comprehension-linked (fluent reading followed by successful
  summary, MP-1 — this concept's single most important mastery
  signal, since it directly verifies the functional PURPOSE of fluency,
  not just its surface form); expression-differentiation (MP-2,
  distinguishing exclamatory from flat statement delivery);
  self-regulation (MP-3, pacing that preserves comprehension rather
  than maximizing raw speed); full-integration (MP-4, a genuinely novel
  passage read with all three components); explanation (MP-5).
- **Interpretation of response pattern**: this concept inverts the
  usual speed-correctness diagnostic logic used elsewhere in this
  domain — FAST reading alone is not automatically a good sign here,
  since MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL is specifically about
  speed pursued at the cost of accuracy or comprehension. The genuinely
  positive signal is reading at a natural, moderate pace THAT STILL
  SUPPORTS a correct comprehension answer immediately after — this
  comprehension-check pairing, not raw words-per-minute, is the
  concept's real diagnostic instrument. A fast, accurate, but flat-toned
  read that fails a follow-up comprehension question is the dangerous
  quadrant for MC-ACCURATE-EQUALS-FLUENT even though every word was
  correct.
- **Transfer items**: near (a fresh short decodable passage practiced
  through repeated reading); far (a novel passage never previously
  seen, read cold with reasonable fluency, MP-4-class); real-world (any
  book, sign, or text the learner encounters and chooses to read aloud
  expressively outside the lesson).

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know" / visible frustration on a fluency task** shrinks to
  the Component 3 repeated-reading anchor using the shortest, most
  familiar sentence available, read once by the tutor first with full
  expression modeled, then echoed by the learner, rather than asking
  for independent fluent reading of anything unfamiliar.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: rather than an
  explicit "I don't know," the two likeliest behaviors are exactly the
  two misconceptions in action — a slow, flat, word-by-word read
  (MC-ACCURATE-EQUALS-FLUENT) or a rushed, word-skipping read
  (MC-FLUENCY-MEANS-READING-FAST-ABOVE-ALL). Route directly to whichever
  misconception's specific recovery framing applies (the three-
  component reminder, or the comprehension-check reframe,
  respectively) rather than a generic "read it again" instruction.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who
  self-corrects mid-passage — slowing down after noticing they rushed
  past a word, or adding expression after an initial flat pass — is
  showing the advanced self-regulation model actively at work, even
  when the overall reading is imperfect; this self-correction should be
  named and praised explicitly as the target skill itself, not merely
  as a nice extra.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedure/tool-skill — automaticity and prosody are
skills requiring ongoing practice for genuine fluency, not facts to be
recalled once; per the Standard's own note on tool-skill review
patterns, this concept benefits from continued speeded practice
(repeated reading on increasingly varied text) even after initial
mastery, since automaticity, unlike factual recall, decays without
ongoing use and re-emerges as effortful decoding if practice lapses.
Interleaving partners: rotate fluency practice across texts of varying
sentence types (statements, questions, exclamations) and punctuation
density, rather than practicing only one sentence type repeatedly, to
keep the expression-matching skill genuinely flexible rather than
rehearsed for one narrow case.

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: fluent reading of fresh, short, already-decodable passages.
- **Far**: this concept's automaticity and prosody skills are the
  direct enabling foundation for all subsequent English reading
  comprehension, oral reading, and public-speaking-adjacent work across
  the curriculum — comprehension work downstream assumes decoding no
  longer consumes the reader's full attention.
- **Real-world**: reading aloud to a family member, reading a favorite
  book with expression, or reading any text encountered outside the
  lesson are all immediate, high-engagement fluency practice
  opportunities.
- **Expert-transfer**: silent reading fluency (the internalized,
  unvoiced version of the same three components), and eventual
  connection to oral presentation and public speaking skills much later
  in the curriculum (`eng.speaking.*`), where audible prosody control
  becomes directly relevant again in a new context.
