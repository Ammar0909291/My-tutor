# Main Idea and Supporting Details — `eng.reading.main-idea-and-details`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.reading.main-idea-and-details` (canonical
  English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / reading / "Main Idea and
  Supporting Details" — identifying a text's central idea and the
  specific details that develop or support it.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.reading.literal-comprehension` — load-bearing
  because main-idea identification is fundamentally a SYNTHESIS task
  built on top of accurately extracted facts; a student who cannot
  reliably extract literal details (who, what, where, when) will
  fabricate main ideas from misremembered or incomplete details rather
  than genuinely synthesizing across what the text actually states, per
  the Blueprint's own Protocol Routing (S0).
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.reading.inference-in-reading`,
  `eng.reading.summarizing`, `eng.reading.text-structure`. Per the
  Blueprint's own Component 8 Adaptive Flags, `eng.reading.summarizing`
  in particular depends directly on this concept's passage-level
  synthesis skill (TA-4), and reuses this concept's umbrella/exclusion-
  test vocabulary as the identical coverage-check operation applied to
  a full passage rather than a single paragraph.
- **Difficulty**: developing · **Bloom**: analyze · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.75 · **Est. hours**: 2

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify supporting details as specific, narrower pieces of evidence
   for a given main idea, distinguishing them from restatements of the
   main idea itself.
2. Distinguish STATED main ideas (explicitly given in one sentence)
   from IMPLIED main ideas (synthesized by the reader from a pattern
   across details, not quotable verbatim anywhere in the text).
3. Apply the umbrella/exclusion test — checking whether a candidate main
   idea genuinely covers every detail, and identifying any detail that
   does not belong — rather than relying on a sentence's position (e.g.,
   assuming the first sentence is always the main idea).
4. Identify main ideas at both the paragraph level and the whole-
   passage level, synthesizing multiple paragraph-level main ideas into
   one overall passage main idea.
5. Construct an original main-idea sentence from a set of detail
   sentences alone, with no candidate main idea provided, and self-
   verify it against the umbrella/exclusion test.

## Core Understanding

A paragraph's main idea is not identified by WHERE a sentence sits — it
is identified by whether that idea is broad enough to COVER every
sentence in the paragraph. The first sentence often grabs attention,
but position doesn't tell you the job a sentence is doing: a vivid,
narrow detail can legitimately open a paragraph while the real
controlling idea only becomes clear once several sentences have been
read. The reliable test is coverage, not position: "what is the one
idea that ALL of these sentences are supporting?" A second, equally
load-bearing insight concerns HOW a main idea appears in a text. Main
ideas are not always stated word-for-word somewhere for a reader to
find and copy. When a writer explicitly names the controlling idea in
one sentence, that's a STATED main idea. But often a writer instead
presents several pieces of evidence and expects the reader to notice
the pattern and articulate it independently — that's an IMPLIED main
idea, and it is just as real and just as valid as a stated one, even
though it will never appear verbatim anywhere in the text. Treating
main-idea identification as a find-and-copy task (searching for a
sentence that already says it) fails completely on implied-idea
paragraphs, where synthesis — not location — is the actual required
skill. This synthesis operates at more than one level: a multi-
paragraph passage has both individual paragraph-level main ideas and
one overall passage-level main idea that those paragraph-level ideas
themselves support — the identical umbrella-covering relationship,
applied one level up.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "the main idea is whatever the first sentence
  says"**: the arriving, pre-instructional model this concept exists to
  correct for MC-A — a natural default because the first sentence
  genuinely IS often the main idea in simple, well-behaved paragraphs
  (especially ones deliberately written that way for early practice),
  making position a reasonable-seeming shortcut until a paragraph
  breaks the pattern.
- **Intermediate model — "I check whether a candidate main idea covers
  every sentence in the paragraph, regardless of where it sits"**:
  installed by TA-1 (identifying supporting details) and TA-3 (the
  umbrella/exclusion test) using the Umbrella Test anchor, resolving
  MC-A-FIRST-SENTENCE-IS-ALWAYS-THE-MAIN-IDEA. Upgrade trigger:
  correctly identifying a main idea in sentence 3 of 4 while explaining
  why sentence 1 is merely a detail.
- **Advanced model — "I recognize that a main idea can be stated
  directly or implied through a pattern of details, and I construct
  implied main ideas in my own words rather than searching for a
  quote"**: installed by TA-2 (stated vs. implied), resolving MC-MAIN-
  IDEA-MUST-BE-STATED-WORD-FOR-WORD-SOMEWHERE.
- **Expert model — "I identify main ideas at both the paragraph and
  whole-passage level, and I can construct an original main-idea
  sentence from detail sentences alone, with nothing given to me"**:
  installed by TA-4 (paragraph vs. passage level) and TA-5 (constructing
  a main idea from scratch), the concept's genuine mastery bar — moving
  from recognizing a given main idea to independently generating one,
  and from single-paragraph synthesis to multi-paragraph synthesis.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own Protocol Routing
  (S9), mastery must NOT be certified unless MP-2 (implied main idea)
  and MP-5 (constructive) both pass independently — a learner who only
  succeeds on stated-main-idea items (MP-1) has not yet demonstrated the
  actual synthesis skill this concept is built around.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, each traceable to a
distinct, reasonable-seeming shortcut. The first-sentence-is-the-main-
idea failure (MC-A) exists because position and controlling-idea status
genuinely DO coincide often in simple paragraphs — many early or
deliberately simplified texts are structured with the main idea
literally in sentence one, giving the learner real, partial evidence
that position is a reliable signal; the shortcut breaks down
specifically on paragraphs that open with a hook, a vivid detail, or a
narrower supporting point before the controlling idea becomes clear.
The find-the-quote failure (MC-MAIN-IDEA-MUST-BE-STATED-WORD-FOR-WORD-
SOMEWHERE) exists because SOME main ideas genuinely are stated directly
and quotably — a learner who has successfully "found" a stated main
idea in earlier, easier examples reasonably generalizes that the main
idea should always be locatable as an exact sentence, freezing or
forcing an awkward quote-only answer when a paragraph instead requires
synthesizing a pattern across details into original wording.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.reading.main-idea-and-details.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-A-FIRST-SENTENCE-IS-ALWAYS-THE-MAIN-IDEA (Blueprint Component 1)
— Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 1 — the first-sentence-is-the-main-
  idea pattern genuinely IS correct for a large, real subset of
  paragraphs (particularly simpler or deliberately structured ones), so
  treating it as a universal rule is a reasonable extension of real,
  partial evidence; the overgeneralization surfaces specifically on
  paragraphs where the opening sentence is a hook, scene-setter, or
  narrow detail rather than the controlling idea — exactly the case the
  Blueprint's own conflict-evidence probe (the roaring-lion opener for
  a paragraph really about pride hierarchy) is designed to surface.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, given a paragraph
  with the main idea in sentence 3, spontaneously reads the whole
  paragraph before proposing a main idea (rather than answering from
  sentence 1 alone) is showing the coverage-based, not position-based,
  test has been internalized.

**MC-MAIN-IDEA-MUST-BE-STATED-WORD-FOR-WORD-SOMEWHERE (Blueprint
Component 1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 1 — stated main ideas genuinely DO
  exist and are a valid, common paragraph structure, so a learner who
  has correctly located several stated main ideas reasonably extends
  "the main idea is a sentence I find" to every paragraph; the
  overgeneralization surfaces on implied-main-idea paragraphs, where no
  single sentence can be found because none was written to directly
  state it — the true skill required (synthesis) diverges from the
  learner's established find-and-copy strategy exactly there.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, unable to find a
  single sentence stating the main idea, spontaneously asks "what do
  all these details have in common?" rather than freezing or forcing an
  awkward quote is showing the stated-vs-implied distinction has
  generalized past the find-and-copy default.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the Umbrella Test itself is the analogy, not a
  supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): detail cards physically
  arranged UNDER a blank umbrella-shaped card makes the covering
  relationship directly visible — the main idea is the idea broad
  enough to shelter every detail, and every detail is a specific
  instance sheltered by it; adding one detail that doesn't fit under a
  proposed umbrella (the "she likes chocolate ice cream" card) makes
  the exclusion test — a detail that DOESN'T belong — equally concrete.
  This single anchor directly seeds the fix for MC-A (coverage, not
  position, determines the main idea) and indirectly supports MC-MAIN-
  IDEA-MUST-BE-STATED-WORD-FOR-WORD-SOMEWHERE's fix (an implied main
  idea is simply an umbrella the learner must construct rather than one
  already labeled). Breaking point: the physical umbrella metaphor
  works cleanly for single-paragraph coverage, but needs explicit
  extension (TA-4) to make the paragraph-vs-passage two-level
  distinction visible — a single umbrella card doesn't on its own show
  that paragraph-level umbrellas can themselves be gathered under one
  larger, passage-level umbrella.
- **Alternative — a lawyer's closing argument vs. individual pieces of
  evidence**: the main idea is like a lawyer's one-sentence closing
  claim, and the details are like the individual pieces of evidence
  presented — the claim must be broad enough to be supported by every
  piece of evidence, and evidence that doesn't support the claim
  doesn't belong in that argument. Breaking point: strong for the
  coverage/exclusion insight, but doesn't naturally extend to the
  stated-vs-implied distinction (a legal claim is always explicitly
  stated, unlike an implied main idea).
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT teach "look for the topic sentence, which is
  usually first or last"**: this framing directly reinforces MC-A by
  making POSITION the primary search strategy, with coverage-checking
  treated as a fallback only when position fails; instead, per the
  Blueprint's own replacement text, the coverage test ("what is the one
  idea that ALL of these sentences are supporting?") should be the
  FIRST and only reliable strategy taught, with position noted only as
  a frequent (not universal) correlation.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/visual (the Umbrella Test)**: the Blueprint's Component 3
  anchor — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any short paragraph from a real article,
  recipe, or story the learner has access to supplies genuine TA-1/TA-3
  material — list its individual sentences as "detail cards" on paper
  and test candidate main ideas against them using the coverage
  question, without needing physical props.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-5's generative capstone task
  (constructing an original main-idea sentence from detail sentences
  alone, with nothing given) is inherently hands-on, requiring the
  learner to produce and self-verify rather than merely recognize a
  presented main idea.
- **Teacher-demo (the roaring-lion contrast)**: the tutor models the
  Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstration directly — presenting
  a paragraph whose vivid first sentence ("The lion's roar shook the
  grass") is not the main idea, then reading the whole paragraph aloud
  to reveal the real controlling idea (pride hierarchy) only becomes
  clear across sentences 2-5, making the position-vs-coverage gap
  directly audible.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  detail-identification, stated-vs-implied classification, umbrella-
  test, or main-idea-construction attempt (TA-1 through TA-5), ask the
  learner to state their own attempt before the tutor confirms —
  Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** Both the
coverage-not-position insight and the stated-vs-implied insight are
genuinely felt, directly testable phenomena well-suited to guided
discovery: **need** (the learner, given a paragraph whose main idea is
NOT in sentence 1, is asked to state the main idea using only sentence
1, then to read the whole paragraph and check whether that answer
covers sentences 2-5, discovering the position-based shortcut failed);
**playground/invention** (TA-1's supporting-details practice and TA-3's
umbrella/exclusion test build initial coverage-checking confidence
before the implied-main-idea complication is introduced); **collision**
(the Blueprint's own flood-recovery paragraph — three sentences each
describing a different way townspeople helped, with no sentence stating
"the town came together" — directly breaks the find-the-quote
assumption when the learner searches in vain for a stating sentence);
**formalization** (the umbrella-coverage framing for MC-A, and the
stated-vs-implied framing for MC-MAIN-IDEA-MUST-BE-STATED-WORD-FOR-
WORD-SOMEWHERE); **compression** (TA-5's fluent original-construction
task, generating a main idea directly from details without a candidate
to check first).

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (identifying supporting details) is placed first, building the
detail-vs-main-idea distinction on paragraphs where the main idea is
already given, before either misconception's correcting insight is
introduced — establishing the vocabulary and basic recognition skill
the rest of the sequence depends on. TA-2 (stated vs. implied main
idea) directly targets MC-MAIN-IDEA-MUST-BE-STATED-WORD-FOR-WORD-
SOMEWHERE, introduced early because the stated/implied distinction
reframes what "finding" a main idea even means, before the coverage
test (TA-3) is introduced as the tool for verifying either kind. TA-3
(the umbrella/exclusion test) directly targets MC-A-FIRST-SENTENCE-IS-
ALWAYS-THE-MAIN-IDEA, per the Blueprint's own framing — deliberately
placed after TA-2 so the coverage test is understood as applying to
BOTH stated and implied main ideas, not just as a check on where a
sentence sits. TA-4 (paragraph vs. whole-text level) extends the same
umbrella-coverage logic one level up, introducing the idea that main-
idea identification operates at multiple levels of a text — directly
preparing for `eng.reading.summarizing`'s dependency on passage-level
synthesis. TA-5 (constructing a main idea from scratch) is placed last
as the concept's genuine generative capstone, requiring the learner to
produce an original main-idea sentence with no candidate given at all,
then self-verify it against the same umbrella/exclusion test built in
TA-3. This entry does not restate the Blueprint's own turn-by-turn
session script (Component 7) — see Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner reads a full paragraph
  before proposing a main idea (rather than answering from the first
  sentence alone), and whether the learner spontaneously distinguishes
  "is this stated or do I need to figure it out myself?" before
  attempting to answer — these are the primary diagnostic signals for
  whether TA-2 and TA-3's disciplines have genuinely generalized.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause after reading a
  paragraph's first sentence, followed by continued reading before
  answering, suggests the learner is consciously withholding judgment
  until the whole paragraph is checked — a good sign. Instant,
  confident quoting of sentence 1 as the main idea, without reading
  further, is the concerning pattern for MC-A-FIRST-SENTENCE-IS-ALWAYS-
  THE-MAIN-IDEA.
- **Native-intuition-gap stakes**: not specifically flagged for L1-
  transfer in this Blueprint — both misconceptions here stem from
  genuine, reasonable overgeneralizations from real, partial evidence
  (correct-often patterns) rather than L1-specific structural transfer,
  so no S9-specific routing beyond standard practice depth is called
  for.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "what is the one idea that ALL of
  these sentences are supporting?" is this concept's load-bearing
  sentence for MC-A-FIRST-SENTENCE-IS-ALWAYS-THE-MAIN-IDEA, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` — asked
  whenever a fresh paragraph is presented for main-idea identification,
  making the coverage check a routinely-invited response rather than an
  afterthought.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know"** on a main-idea-identification or construction task
  shrinks to the Component 3 Umbrella Test anchor using the simplest,
  most concrete detail set available (the original snacks/weather-map/
  raincoat/early-departure/boots set), with the tutor modeling the
  coverage check before the learner attempts a fresh stated, implied,
  or constructive main-idea task independently.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: for MC-A, confidently
  quoting the first sentence as the main idea is more likely than "I
  don't know" — route directly to the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence
  probe (reading the whole paragraph and checking coverage) rather than
  treating it as a generic identification gap. For MC-MAIN-IDEA-MUST-
  BE-STATED-WORD-FOR-WORD-SOMEWHERE, freezing or searching repeatedly
  for a stating sentence that doesn't exist is more likely than a
  direct "I don't know" — route directly to TA-2's stated-vs-implied
  framing, asking what the details have in common.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who
  spontaneously re-reads a full paragraph after an initial first-
  sentence-based guess, then revises their answer without being told
  it was wrong, is showing the coverage-check discipline has
  generalized — this should be named and praised explicitly, consistent
  with the Blueprint's own Component 8 Adaptive Flags emphasis on
  weighting remediation toward the umbrella/exclusion test whenever
  first-sentence defaulting is observed.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedural/framework — a coverage-based synthesis
practice (checking whether a candidate idea shelters every detail,
distinguishing stated from implied main ideas, operating at both
paragraph and passage level) applied to genuinely novel paragraphs and
passages; mastery is demonstrated by correctly identifying and
constructing main ideas on fresh, previously-unseen text — including
counterexample-rejection cases (MP-1, MP-3) and fully generative
construction (MP-5) — rather than by recognizing a previously-practiced
paragraph. Interleaving partners: mix stated-main-idea, implied-main-
idea, exclusion-detection, and constructive tasks together in ongoing
review sets (rather than blocking practice by task type), converging
toward TA-4/TA-5's multi-paragraph synthesis and from-scratch
construction only once single-paragraph coverage-checking is
independently secure.
