## Learning Objective
The learner will choose an appropriate communication channel for a professional scenario based on urgency, need for a documented record, and sensitivity; ensure consistency between a written document and an accompanying spoken presentation of the same content; and adapt the same underlying message appropriately across both channels.

## Core Understanding
Professional communication is the INTEGRATION of written and spoken skill, not their separate application. Channel choice (email, phone call, in-person conversation, formal report) is a FUNCTIONAL decision — driven by urgency, need for a documented record, and sensitivity/privacy — not an arbitrary or cosmetic one; even perfectly accurate content fails if delivered through the wrong channel (an urgent deadline change by email the recipient won't see for hours; sensitive feedback by public group email instead of a private conversation). Separately, when a spoken presentation accompanies a written document, consistency means matching KEY POINTS and overall tone, NOT reading the written text verbatim — spoken delivery has different natural conventions (shorter sentences, verbal signposts, strategic repetition) suited to a listening audience that gets only one pass and can't pause to re-read.

## Mental Models
- **Shout-vs-Letter Anchor**: an urgent warning delivered by shouting directly at someone reaches them immediately; the identical warning mailed as a letter arrives too late. Channel choice functionally affects whether a message achieves its purpose, even with identical content.
- **Map-Description Anchor**: describing a route to someone who can't see a map, adapted for real-time listening, versus simply holding up the map and reading its labels verbatim — a listener gets one pass and needs the structure adapted (shorter units, verbal signposts, strategic repetition), unlike a reader who can pause and re-read.

## Why Students Fail
Learners who've mastered content accuracy assume that's the whole job, missing that HOW and WHEN a message is delivered is itself a communication decision with real functional consequences — channel choice feels secondary to "getting the words right." Separately, when asked to present written content aloud, reading it verbatim feels safest (it's already "correct"), but this ignores that spoken and written comprehension work through fundamentally different real-time constraints.

## Misconceptions
- **MC-A (Type 3, language contamination from "the message is what matters, not the medium")**: "The channel (written vs. spoken) doesn't matter as long as the message content is right." Diagnostic: present an urgent deadline change sent only by email (missed for hours) against the same message delivered by immediate phone call, and ask whether channel choice affects whether the message achieves its purpose. Repair: shout-vs-letter anchor — ask how urgent this is, whether it needs a documented record, and how sensitive it is, BEFORE choosing a channel.
- **MC-B (Type 1, overgeneralization from "consistency means identical wording")**: "A spoken presentation should be a word-for-word reading of the written document." Diagnostic: present a dense written sentence read aloud verbatim against the same content adapted for listening (shorter, signposted, with strategic repetition), and ask whether a listening audience would follow the verbatim version as easily as a reader follows the page. Repair: map-description anchor — keep key points and tone consistent, but adapt sentence-level phrasing for spoken convention.

## Analogies
- Shout-vs-letter anchor (above) for channel choice as a functional decision.
- Map-description anchor (above) for spoken adaptation vs. verbatim reading.
- Anti-analogy: spoken adaptation is not license to change the actual message — adaptation means changing phrasing and pacing, not the underlying key points or overall message, which must remain consistent across both channels.

## Demonstrations
- Compare an urgent same-day deadline change sent only by email against the same message via immediate phone call/instant message.
- Compare sensitive critical feedback delivered via a public group email against the same feedback in a private conversation.
- Full worked example: a project status update delivered both as a written summary email (documented record, details) and a spoken meeting update (adapted, signposted phrasing: "So, quick update — we're on track, but there's one blocker I want to flag...").

## Discovery Questions
- "Does the channel choice actually affect whether this message achieves its purpose, even with identical content?"
- "If you read the written version aloud exactly as written, would a listening audience follow it as easily as a reader could follow it on the page?"
- "What are the 2-3 key points that must appear in BOTH the written and spoken versions of this message?"

## Teaching Sequence
1. Diagnostic: business-writing check and presentation-skills check.
2. Anchor: shout-vs-letter and map-description framing together.
3. TA-1: select and justify the most appropriate channel for five scenarios based on urgency/record-need/sensitivity (targets MC-A).
4. TA-2: identify the 2-3 key points that must stay consistent across written and spoken versions.
5. TA-3: orally paraphrase a dense written sentence into spoken-friendly phrasing (targets MC-B).
6. TA-4: full coordinated communication — channel selection, consistent key points, adapted spoken delivery.

## Tutor Actions
- SHOW: urgent-email-missed vs. immediate-phone-call pair; verbatim-reading vs. adapted-spoken-delivery pair.
- TEST-THINKING: select and justify the most appropriate channel for a given scenario.
- DO: orally paraphrase one dense sentence from a written draft, confirming the core point stays identical.
- ORGANIZE: a select-channel → list-key-points → draft-each-version checklist as scaffolding.

## Voice Teaching Notes
Watch for a comfort-zone bias toward written channels even in scenarios requiring urgency or sensitivity — if a learner defaults to email regardless of scenario, explicitly prompt with the three-criteria checklist until channel selection becomes balanced.

## Assessment Signals
- Probe: given three professional scenarios, "select and justify the most appropriate channel." A default-to-email answer regardless of urgency/sensitivity signals MC-A.
- Probe: given a spoken script that reads the written document verbatim, "identify at least one place needing adaptation." Inability to find any adaptation point signals residual MC-B.
- Confident-wrong signature: a well-organized written-and-spoken pair where the spoken version is a fluent but word-for-word recitation of the written text — flag MC-B regardless of how well-delivered the recitation is.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy
If a learner's spoken adaptations drift so far from the written content that consistency is lost (overcorrecting MC-B into an unrelated spoken version), clarify that adaptation means changing phrasing and pacing, not the actual key points or overall message.

## Memory Hooks
"Shouting reaches them now; a letter arrives too late" for channel choice; "a listener gets one pass, a reader can re-read" for spoken adaptation.
