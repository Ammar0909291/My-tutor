## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Produce common multisyllabic conversational words with correct stress placement.
2. Judge, when comparing two pronunciation errors, that a wrong-stress error is more likely to impede a listener's understanding than a minor individual-sound imperfection.
3. Apply appropriate intonation patterns (question versus statement, emphasis) in unscripted, spontaneous conversational exchanges.
4. Articulate that intelligibility (being clearly understood), not elimination of accent, is the realistic, appropriate pronunciation goal.
5. Self-monitor during real conversation for high-impact pronunciation features, prioritizing stress/intonation over segmental perfectionism.

## Core Understanding

Word stress and intonation carry **disproportionate weight for being understood in conversation, often outweighing individual sound (segmental) precision** — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact, directly grounded in research on spoken intelligibility: a slightly imprecise vowel sound is usually still understandable in context, but misplaced word stress (saying "comPUter" instead of "COMputer") can make an otherwise correctly-pronounced word genuinely hard for a listener to recognize, even when every individual sound is technically accurate. This means pronunciation practice that prioritizes perfecting individual sounds above all else is misallocating effort relative to what actually determines whether a listener understands a spoken word. The concept's second load-bearing fact is that the realistic, appropriate goal of pronunciation instruction is **intelligibility — being clearly understood — not eliminating every trace of accent to sound "native-like."** A speaker can retain a noticeable, entirely legitimate accent while being highly, even perfectly, intelligible; accent itself is not a communication problem, and pursuing "sound completely native" as a goal is neither realistic nor, according to current pronunciation-teaching research, actually necessary — targeted practice belongs on specific features that genuinely impede understanding (like the wrong-stress errors this concept centers on), not on erasing every marker of a speaker's natural accent.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "if I get every individual sound exactly right, I'll be understood, and my ultimate goal should be to sound exactly like a native speaker."**
The runnable simulation: treat segmental (individual-sound) precision as the primary, sufficient target for both immediate intelligibility and long-term pronunciation improvement, with native-like accent elimination as the implicit end goal. This is a reasonable default, since individual sounds are the most concrete, checkable, drillable unit of pronunciation, and "sound native" is a common, culturally reinforced aspiration.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing "COMputer" (correct stress, slightly imprecise vowel) against "comPUter" (wrong stress, perfect individual sounds) and discovering the second is harder to recognize despite its segmental accuracy.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Perfect individual sounds don't guarantee you'll be understood — stress and intonation matter more than most learners expect."

**Intermediate — "I understand stress/intonation matter a lot, but I still think eliminating my accent entirely is the real, ultimate goal to work toward."**
The simulation: correctly reprioritizes stress and intonation practice, but continues to treat any remaining accent as an unfinished problem rather than recognizing intelligibility, not native-likeness, as the actual functional target.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — being asked whether someone can have a noticeable accent and still be completely, clearly understood, and recognizing the answer is yes.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Being understood clearly is the real goal — a noticeable accent alongside clear, correct stress and intonation is already a genuine communicative success, not a problem still to be fixed."

**Advanced — "I prioritize stress and intonation accuracy in real conversation because they carry outsized intelligibility weight, and I hold intelligibility, not accent elimination, as my actual pronunciation goal."**
The simulation: applies both reframes together during genuine spontaneous conversation — actively monitoring for stress/intonation accuracy as the higher-priority target, while explicitly not treating a remaining accent as a deficiency needing correction. This is the target model, matching TA-4/TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: the direct unlock `eng.speaking.conversation-skills`, where this concept's intelligibility-focused pronunciation foundation becomes one component of the broader skillset for effective, natural conversational interaction.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the right pronunciation priorities and the right goal. The next step builds the fuller conversational skillset — turn-taking, repair, and interactive strategies — on top of this pronunciation foundation."

**Expert — "stress/intonation self-monitoring, at full conversational fluency, happens largely automatically and in real time during spontaneous speech, with deliberate attention reserved for genuinely high-stakes or unfamiliar vocabulary."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit self-monitoring routine is a scaffold for a skill that, at true conversational fluency, operates largely below conscious awareness during ordinary talk — named here so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is automatic, real-time monitoring, not a permanently maintained deliberate checklist.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **individual sounds are the most concrete, controllable, checkable unit of pronunciation**, making segmental precision feel like the natural, primary target for deliberate practice — this is a reasonable allocation of effort given how pronunciation instruction is often structured (drilling individual phonemes), and the failure surfaces specifically when this reasonable-feeling priority turns out to be misaligned with what actually determines a listener's ability to recognize a word.

The second mechanism is that **cultural messaging around accent commonly frames "sounding native" as the implicit success marker for language learning**, giving a learner well-earned reason to internalize accent elimination as the goal — this is not a reasoning error the learner invented independently but a widely reinforced external framing that this concept must explicitly, and often repeatedly, counter.

The third mechanism, specific to learners from instructional traditions that heavily emphasize individual-sound perfection and accent reduction (per the Blueprint's own S9 routing), is that **both misconceptions may be strongly held, deeply practiced beliefs reinforced by substantial prior instruction**, not first-encounter assumptions — requiring sustained, evidence-based counter-instruction across repeated exposures rather than a single correction, since the prior instruction's authority and volume both work against a quick reframe.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-PERFECT-INDIVIDUAL-SOUNDS-MORE-IMPORTANT-THAN-STRESS-AND-INTONATION
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-PERFECT-INDIVIDUAL-SOUNDS-MORE-IMPORTANT-THAN-STRESS-AND-INTONATION — includes the "COMputer"/"comPUter" and imprecise-segment-correct-stress/perfect-segment-wrong-stress discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable instructional priority (individual sounds are the most controllable, checkable target) extended into an assumption that segmental precision is the *primary* determinant of intelligibility, which research does not support.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because segmental practice isn't wrong, only mis-prioritized, the repair must **reorder priorities rather than dismiss segmental work entirely** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this precisely, framing stress/intonation as mattering "alongside (not instead of, but often ahead of)" segmental accuracy.
- **Verification of death**: given a novel comparison between a segmental-imperfect/stress-correct word and a segmental-perfect/stress-wrong word, the learner correctly judges the stress error as more likely to impede recognition.

### MC-NATIVE-LIKE-ACCENT-IS-THE-GOAL
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-NATIVE-LIKE-ACCENT-IS-THE-GOAL — includes the noticeable-accent-intelligible/accent-elimination-striving and targeted-stress-practice/broad-anxiety-driven-practice discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 3 (language contamination), where a genuine social/cultural narrative (sounding native is desirable, prestigious) gets absorbed and re-encoded as though it were a linguistic or communicative necessity (sounding native is required for successful communication), rather than the separate, more limited claim that intelligibility is what actually matters functionally.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct, credible evidence that intelligibility and accent are separable**, since an abstract reassurance ("your accent is fine") is less convincing than concretely confirming that a specific, noticeable-accent speaker is nonetheless completely understood.
- **Verification of death**: given a scenario describing a speaker with a noticeable accent and correct stress/clear articulation, the learner correctly identifies that speaker as having achieved genuine communicative success, without treating the remaining accent as an unresolved problem.

## Analogies

**Best — a GPS giving turn-by-turn directions in a functional but slightly synthetic-sounding voice, versus a GPS with wrong street names.** A slightly artificial-sounding voice (an imprecise individual sound) is a minor cosmetic issue you barely notice once you're following the directions successfully; a wrong street name (misplaced stress) sends you the wrong way entirely, regardless of how natural the voice sounds otherwise — the functional content (getting the stress/turn right) matters far more than the surface polish (the voice's naturalness).
*Breaking point*: GPS directions have a single, objectively correct route, while conversational intelligibility is a matter of degree and listener-dependent tolerance rather than a strict right/wrong outcome — useful for the functional-content-over-cosmetic-polish intuition, less precise for implying a single, universally correct pronunciation target.

**Alternative — a handwritten letter with beautiful, elegant penmanship but a misspelled key word, versus plain, slightly messy handwriting with every word spelled correctly.** The elegant penmanship (perfect individual sounds) doesn't rescue a misspelled key word (wrong stress) from causing genuine confusion, while plain but accurate handwriting (correct stress, imperfect sounds) communicates the intended message successfully despite lacking polish.
*Breaking point*: spelling has one single, objectively correct answer per word, while spoken stress placement, though largely fixed per word, interacts with listener familiarity and context in ways spelling correctness does not — useful for the content-over-polish intuition, less precise for implying stress errors are as binary as spelling errors.

**Visual/auditory anchor — the intelligibility-priority sort** (Blueprint Component 3): not a metaphor but the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around, using genuine recorded/modeled comparisons to make the stress-versus-segmental priority concrete and convincing.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"As long as you say each sound perfectly, people will understand you."** Directly installs MC-PERFECT-INDIVIDUAL-SOUNDS-MORE-IMPORTANT-THAN-STRESS-AND-INTONATION as a stated rule, precisely the segmental-sufficiency claim this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"Your goal should be to sound exactly like a native speaker, with no trace of an accent."** Directly installs MC-NATIVE-LIKE-ACCENT-IS-THE-GOAL as explicit, stated instruction, exactly the accent-elimination framing this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The intelligibility-priority sort (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Which do you think is easier to understand: a slightly imperfect sound with correct stress, or a perfect sound with wrong stress?" Then sort real examples and confirm.
2. **The "COMputer"/"comPUter" collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "If every individual sound is perfect but the stress is wrong, will the listener still recognize the word easily?" Then compare and resolve.
3. **The noticeable-accent-intelligibility collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Can someone have a noticeable accent and still be completely understood?" Then confirm and discuss.
4. **The self-monitoring spontaneous conversation task (learner activity, TA-5).** *Predict first*: "During this conversation, what pronunciation features do you think you should be watching for most closely?" Then converse and reflect together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for the stress-priority fact (via direct comparison, per TA-2)**; the intelligibility-versus-native-likeness fact benefits from more **direct instruction with an explicit, reassuring reframe**, since the relevant research finding and the affective reassurance both need to be stated clearly rather than discovered independently.

1. **Need**: "If you had to choose, would you rather perfect every individual sound, or make sure your stress is always right?"
2. **Playground**: the learner compares several word pairs (one segmental-imperfect/stress-correct, one segmental-perfect/stress-wrong) freely, judging intelligibility before being told which factor "should" matter more.
3. **Invention**: "Which of the pairs you judged were actually harder to understand?" The learner reviews their own judgments against the pattern.
4. **Collision**: present the noticeable-accent-intelligible scenario and ask whether that speaker has failed to communicate successfully.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — stress/intonation outweigh segmental precision for intelligibility, and intelligibility (not accent elimination) is the real goal — from the learner's own evidence plus the taught reframe.
6. **Compression**: "Get the stress right, and aim to be understood — not to erase your accent."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Word stress accuracy in conversational words (TA-1) comes first**, building on the learner's prior word-stress knowledge (from the prosody cross-link) in a spontaneous-speech-relevant context, before the stress-versus-segmental priority judgment is introduced.
- **Prioritizing stress/intonation over segmental perfection (TA-2) comes second**, directly confronting the first misconception once basic stress production (TA-1) is secure, since the priority judgment presupposes the learner can already produce correct stress to compare against segmental precision.
- **Applying intonation patterns in spontaneous conversation (TA-3) comes third**, extending the stress-priority skill (TA-2) into the harder, less controlled conversational context, before the goal-reframing content (TA-4) is introduced.
- **Setting intelligibility as the goal (TA-4) comes fourth**, directly confronting the second misconception once the learner has direct, positive experience prioritizing stress/intonation (TA-1-3) — this ordering matters because the intelligibility-goal reframe lands more convincingly once the learner has already seen concrete evidence that specific, targeted practice (not blanket accent elimination) is what actually improves understanding.
- **Self-monitoring in real conversation (TA-5) comes last**, requiring the learner to apply both reframes (stress/intonation priority, intelligibility goal) simultaneously during genuine spontaneous speech — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the evidence-based stress-priority teaching, the intelligibility-goal reframe as an affective/motivational correction, the prosody-to-spontaneous-context transfer emphasis, and the sustained counter-instruction priority for S9 learners from segmental-perfection-focused traditions).

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **inherently spoken and audio-based** — the learner's actual stress placement and intonation in real or near-real conversational production, observed directly, rather than inferred from a written or purely declarative response, making this among the most directly performance-dependent concepts in this program.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Correct stress placement in spontaneously produced multisyllabic words, offered fluently within a spoken sentence** — the direct target evidence for TA-1/MP-1, distinguishable from correct stress recalled only when a word is asked about in isolation.
- **A stated priority judgment favoring stress/intonation correctness over segmental precision when comparing two example pronunciations** — the direct behavioral signature of the first misconception's repair, and the specific target evidence for MP-2.
- **Visible anxiety or self-consciousness about a persisting accent, expressed during or after a conversational task, despite otherwise strong stress/intonation performance** — per the Blueprint's own S6 routing, this should trigger explicit, repeated reassurance (Component 1's s6_path) rather than being treated as a performance problem to fix.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Get the stress right — that matters more than perfect sounds — and aim to be understood, not to sound like someone else."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Word stress in conversation (MP-1).** *Correct stress produced fluently within a spoken sentence* → target evidence; correct stress only when the word is asked about in isolation, without embedding it in speech, is weaker evidence of genuine conversational-context mastery.
- **Stress vs. segmental priority (MP-2).** This concept's most direct test of the first misconception; listen for whether the stated judgment cites the actual mechanism (wrong stress impedes word recognition) rather than a vague "stress is more important."
- **Intonation in spontaneous conversation (MP-3).** Listen for a correctly described (or, ideally, correctly demonstrated) rising-versus-falling intonation contrast, not merely a correct verbal description with no accompanying production evidence.
- **Intelligibility as the goal (MP-4).** This concept's most direct test of the second misconception; a response that concedes intelligibility is the goal "in theory" while still expressing a personal desire to sound native has not fully resolved the misconception's affective dimension, even if the stated answer is technically correct.
- **Explanation (MP-5).** Listen for whether the explanation names both required elements — that stress/intonation often outweigh segmental precision for intelligibility, and that intelligibility, not native-like accent elimination, is the appropriate goal.

**Mastery certification trigger**: correct stress production embedded fluently in spontaneous speech; a correct, mechanism-citing priority judgment on a novel segmental-versus-stress comparison; correctly demonstrated or described intonation contrast in a spontaneous exchange; a confident, non-conflicted affirmation of intelligibility as the goal; and an explanation naming both required elements. Because this concept's second misconception carries a real affective dimension, full mastery includes the *absence* of expressed native-likeness anxiety during the self-monitoring task, not merely a technically correct verbal answer.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I want every sound to be perfect, that feels like the real goal"** — take this as an accurate statement about a genuine, well-earned prior priority, not a mistake to be dismissed; respond by adding rather than contradicting: "Wanting your sounds to be accurate is a good instinct — and it's worth keeping. What the research shows is that stress and intonation matter even more for being understood, so let's make sure those come first."
- **"I really want to sound like a native speaker, not just be understood"** — a genuine, emotionally significant aspiration, per the Blueprint's own S6/S9 routing; respond with direct, repeated reassurance, not a one-time correction: "That's a completely understandable goal to have — and it's worth knowing that many highly successful, clearly understood speakers keep a noticeable accent their whole lives. Being clearly understood is the goal that actually matters for real communication, and that's fully achievable without erasing your accent."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full spontaneous self-monitoring conversation, down to **a single, pre-selected multisyllabic word, produced once with a deliberate focus on stress alone, with no live conversational pressure at all.** This removes both the spontaneity demand and the goal-reframe discussion, isolating the most basic stress-production skill before returning to harder, real-time application.
- **Never shrink to "just try to sound as native as possible."** This directly licenses MC-NATIVE-LIKE-ACCENT-IS-THE-GOAL's accent-elimination framing, undermining the intelligibility-focused goal this concept specifically builds.
