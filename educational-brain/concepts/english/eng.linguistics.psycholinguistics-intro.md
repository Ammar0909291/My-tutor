## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify which claims about mental language processing are supported by controlled experimental evidence versus merely introspective assumptions.
2. Identify the systematic pattern in documented speech-error examples (same sound category, same grammatical category being swapped).
3. Explain what a reaction-time or eye-tracking experiment's timing pattern reveals about incremental comprehension.
4. Identify which stage of language production a speech error likely reveals information about.
5. Fully evaluate a new speech error and experimental description for evidence type, systematic pattern, and incremental-processing implications.

## Core Understanding

This concept examines the real-time mental machinery underlying language use, extending `eng.linguistics.language-acquisition-intro`'s developmental focus into moment-by-moment cognitive processing. Two corrections define it. First, **introspection — simply thinking about how language feels to use — is not a reliable way to study mental language processes** — psycholinguists rely on controlled EXPERIMENTAL evidence (reaction times, speech-error analysis) specifically because introspection is notoriously unreliable at revealing what happens beneath conscious awareness; reading comprehension feels instantaneous, but reaction-time experiments reveal it actually happens incrementally, word by word, in measurable time. Second, **speech errors ("slips of the tongue") are not random noise** — they follow systematic patterns (swapped sounds are almost always the same TYPE — consonants swap with consonants, not vowels), revealing that speech is planned in structured stages, not produced as an undifferentiated stream; this systematicity is precisely what makes speech errors genuine data, not meaningless mistakes.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "thinking carefully about how language feels to use is a reliable way to study mental processes; speech errors are just random noise or carelessness."**
The simulation: trust introspective impressions about how comprehension or production "feels," and dismiss slips of the tongue as meaningless accidents.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — reading comprehension feeling instantaneous while reaction-time experiments reveal incremental, word-by-word processing; and documented speech errors showing a consistent pattern (consonants swap only with consonants), asked whether this looks like random chaos or a systematic rule.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Introspection can be wildly inaccurate about automatic mental processes. Speech errors reveal real, systematic planning stages."

**Intermediate — "I trust experimental evidence over introspection now, but I still tend to view individual speech errors as isolated mistakes rather than looking for their systematic pattern."**
The simulation: correctly favors experimental evidence in the abstract but hasn't yet reliably applied pattern-detection to newly encountered speech errors.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-2's direct practice identifying the systematic pattern (sound category, grammatical category) across four documented speech-error examples.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Don't treat one error as a random accident — ask what TYPE of element got swapped, and whether that type recurs."

**Advanced — "I evaluate claims by evidence type, identify systematic speech-error patterns, interpret incremental-comprehension data, connect errors to production stages, and evaluate new evidence integrating all of this."**
The simulation: integrates evidence-type evaluation, error-pattern identification, and incremental-processing interpretation into one coherent psycholinguistic analysis. Matches TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: none within this concept — confirmed terminal, completing the psychological/cognitive branch of the linguistics sequence.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the complete methodological and evidentiary toolkit for studying the mind's real-time language processes."

**Expert — "at fluency, experimental-evidence evaluation and systematic-pattern detection happen as an integrated part of any psycholinguistic reasoning."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit evaluate-identify-interpret routine is a scaffold for a habit that, at true fluency, is inseparable from doing psycholinguistic research or informed cognitive-science reasoning.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **introspection is the only form of "evidence" most people have direct, effortless access to about their own mental processes**, so it feels naturally authoritative, when in fact many automatic cognitive processes (including most of language comprehension and production) occur below the threshold of accurate conscious report.

The second mechanism is that **an individual speech error, encountered in isolation, genuinely looks like a one-off accident** — the systematic pattern only becomes visible when multiple error instances are compared side by side, a comparison most casual observers of their own or others' speech errors never perform.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-INTROSPECTION-SIMPLY-THINKING-ABOUT-HOW-LANGUAGE-FEELS-TO-USE-IS-A-RELIABLE-WAY-TO-STUDY-MENTAL-LANGUAGE-PROCESSES
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the instantaneous-feeling/incremental-reaction-time discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from introspection being the only immediately accessible form of self-knowledge about mental processes, mistaken for a reliable window into processes that actually occur automatically, beneath conscious awareness.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **present a case where introspection and experimental evidence directly diverge**, since the fix requires demonstrating introspection's specific failure mode, not merely asserting experiments are "more scientific."
- **Verification of death**: given three claims about mental language processing, the learner identifies which are supported by controlled experimental evidence and which are merely introspective assumptions.

### MC-B-SPEECH-ERRORS-SLIPS-OF-THE-TONGUE-ARE-JUST-RANDOM-NOISE-WITH-NO-SYSTEMATIC-PATTERN
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — includes the consonant-swap-pattern discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from a single speech error, viewed in isolation, genuinely looking like random noise, with the systematic pattern only visible upon comparing multiple instances.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct comparison of multiple documented speech errors showing the same pattern type**, since the fix requires the systematicity to be empirically demonstrated across examples, not asserted about a single instance.
- **Verification of death**: given three documented speech-error examples, the learner identifies the systematic pattern in each (same sound category, same grammatical category being swapped).

## Analogies

**Best — the Heart-Rate-Monitor and Printing-Press-Misprint-Pattern Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): a heart-rate monitor's objective measurement contradicting subjective impressions directly seeds the experimental-evidence-over-introspection fix; a printing press's misprints following a consistent mechanical pattern rather than pure randomness directly seeds the systematic-speech-error fix.
*Breaking point*: a heart-rate monitor measures a purely physiological signal with no interpretive ambiguity, while psycholinguistic experiments (reaction times, eye-tracking) require more interpretive inference from the measured behavior to the underlying mental process — useful for the objective-measurement-beats-subjective-impression intuition, less precise for implying psycholinguistic measurement is as direct as a heart-rate reading.
*Breaking point (printing press)*: a printing press's mechanical fault is a purely physical, deterministic cause, while speech production's "mechanism" is a cognitive process inferred indirectly from error patterns, not directly observed — useful for the systematic-not-random intuition, less precise for the different epistemic status of mechanical versus cognitive causation.

**Alternative — a poker player's "tells" analyzed statistically versus trusting a player's self-report of their own behavior.** A poker player might sincerely believe they show no signs of bluffing, but statistical analysis of recorded footage can reveal a systematic, below-awareness pattern (a specific tell) the player themselves cannot introspectively access or accurately report.
*Breaking point*: a poker tell is a voluntary behavioral pattern potentially under some strategic control, while automatic language processing is an involuntary cognitive process with no equivalent strategic dimension.

**Visual anchor — the Heart-Rate-Monitor and Printing-Press-Misprint-Pattern Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"If you think carefully about how you understand a sentence, you can accurately describe what your mind is doing."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated claim.
- **"Slips of the tongue are just random mistakes, nothing more."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated claim.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Heart-Rate-Monitor and Printing-Press-Misprint-Pattern Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Can a heart-rate monitor contradict what you FEEL your heart rate is?" Then connect to introspection versus experimental measurement.
2. **The instantaneous-feeling/incremental-reaction-time collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "Does introspection alone reveal that comprehension happens word by word?"
3. **The consonant-swap-pattern collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-B. *Predict first*: "If these errors were truly random, would we expect this consistent pattern across many speakers?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both methodological facts are directly checkable by comparing introspective claims against experimental findings and by examining multiple documented speech errors for shared patterns.

1. **Need**: "Does reading a sentence FEEL like it happens all at once, or word by word? How would you actually find out which is true?"
2. **Playground**: the learner examines several documented speech-error examples freely, looking for patterns, before being told the official systematicity finding.
3. **Invention**: "Can you find a claim about how your mind processes language that you can't actually verify just by thinking about it?"
4. **Collision**: present several consonant-swap speech errors; ask whether this looks like random chaos or a systematic rule.
5. **Formalization**: name the two methodological facts — experimental evidence, not introspection, reveals real mental processes; speech errors are systematic data, not noise — from the learner's own comparisons.
6. **Compression**: "Measure, don't just introspect — and errors reveal real structure, not randomness."

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for classifying a claim as experimentally-supported versus introspective, or for identifying a speech error's systematic pattern**, since a correct-looking classification with no stated evidentiary or pattern-based reasoning cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated evidence-type check** ("is this based on actually measuring something, or just on how it feels?") applied to a new claim — the direct behavioral signature of the MC-A fix taking hold.
- **A stated pattern search across multiple errors** ("do other examples show the same TYPE of swap?") applied to a newly encountered speech error — direct evidence the MC-B fix is internalized.
- **A claim about mental processing accepted purely because it "feels right,"** or a speech error dismissed as a one-off accident with no pattern search — the direct behavioral signature of either misconception still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Measure, don't just introspect — and every slip of the tongue reveals real structure, not randomness."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I can just tell how my mind processes language when I think about it"** — take this as a genuine, reasonable trust in introspection, which works well for many purposes; respond by naming the specific limitation: "For automatic processes below conscious awareness, introspection can be wildly off — that's exactly why psycholinguists measure things like reaction time instead."
- **"This slip of the tongue was just a random accident"** — a reasonable-sounding default for a single isolated instance; respond by widening the sample: "Let's check — does this SAME kind of error (sound type, word category) show up in other examples too?"
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full new-evidence evaluation down to **classifying a single claim as experimental or introspective, with no speech-error-pattern or incremental-processing task demanded.** Isolates the most basic methodological distinction before returning to fuller evaluation.
- **Never shrink to "just say what feels true about how your mind works."** This directly re-licenses the introspection-as-reliable-evidence misconception this concept specifically works to dismantle.
