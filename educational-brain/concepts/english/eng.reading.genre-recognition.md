## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify a text's genre using its overall purpose and multiple structural conventions rather than a single surface feature.
2. Match texts to the major genres (fiction, nonfiction, poetry, drama) and state the confirming convention.
3. Identify specific subtypes within a genre.
4. Recognize a hybrid or blended text that combines conventions from more than one genre, naming which conventions come from which.
5. Correctly classify genre or hybrid status across a novel set of unlabeled texts.

## Core Understanding

A text's genre cannot be reliably identified from **one surface feature alone** — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact: line breaks don't automatically mean poetry (a nonfiction informational text for young readers can use line breaks purely for pacing), and dialogue in quotation marks doesn't automatically mean fiction (a factual biography can quote real, verified speech). The reliable test is the text's overall **purpose** — to inform with verified facts, to tell an invented story, to persuade, to evoke feeling through condensed language — checked together with its **full set of structural conventions**, not any single visible trait that could plausibly appear across multiple genres. The concept's second, equally load-bearing fact is that **not every text belongs to exactly one fixed, non-overlapping genre box** — some texts deliberately blend conventions from more than one genre, such as narrative nonfiction (a memoir using story-shaped structure — rising action, climax, resolution — to tell true, factual events) or a graphic novel that can be fiction or nonfiction depending on content. Forcing such a hybrid text into a single ill-fitting category misrepresents it; the accurate response is to name it explicitly as a hybrid and identify which conventions come from which genre.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "genre is identified by one obvious visible trait — line breaks mean poetry, quotation marks mean fiction, and every text fits into exactly one fixed category."**
The runnable simulation: scan for a single, familiar surface cue and classify accordingly, then assume that classification is complete and exclusive. This is a reasonable default, since surface cues genuinely do correlate with genre most of the time, and early, simple practice texts rarely present the misleading edge cases that reveal the shortcut's limits.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a nonfiction ocean-animals text written with poetic line breaks, and a fictionalized biography that "sounds serious," both of which mislead the single-surface-feature test.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "One visible trait can appear in more than one genre — check the text's overall purpose and its full set of conventions together, not just one feature."

**Intermediate — "I check purpose and multiple conventions rather than one surface feature, but I still assume every text fits cleanly into exactly one genre box."**
The simulation: correctly resists the single-feature shortcut, but forces a hybrid text into a single ill-fitting category rather than recognizing its genuinely blended nature.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a memoir using clear narrative-arc structure to tell a true, factual story, and being asked whether a text can be both factual and story-shaped at once.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Not every text fits one box. Some genuinely combine conventions from more than one genre — name those as hybrids, like narrative nonfiction, rather than forcing a single label."

**Advanced — "I identify genre from overall purpose and full structural convention, correctly identify major genres and subtypes, and recognize and name genuine hybrid texts, specifying which conventions come from which genre."**
The simulation: analyzes any text using purpose and convention together, correctly distinguishing single-genre texts from genuine hybrids and naming the specific blend when one exists. This is the target model, matching TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: none within the current curriculum — this concept is terminal, with no further KG unlocks.
*Shelf-life warning*: none required; this concept's genre-and-hybrid-recognition skill is intended to generalize directly to independent reading without a further formal teaching step.

**Expert — "at full literary-analytical fluency, genre boundaries are recognized as historically contingent, culturally variable, and continuously evolving through new hybrid forms, with the five-category framework (fiction, nonfiction, poetry, drama, plus hybrids) serving as a practical, teachable entry point rather than a fixed, universal taxonomy."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's clean genre-and-hybrid framework is a deliberately simplified scaffold for a much more fluid, historically evolving reality — named here so the tutor understands the simplification is a teaching tool, not literary scholarship's actual final word on genre.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **surface features genuinely do correlate strongly with genre in the majority of everyday texts a learner encounters**, so relying on them is a reasonably efficient, usually-successful shortcut — the failure surfaces specifically in the minority of texts deliberately constructed to use an atypical surface feature (a nonfiction text choosing poetic formatting for young readers, a serious-toned fictionalized account), which are rare enough that the shortcut's weakness is rarely exposed without direct, deliberate demonstration.

The second mechanism is that **introductory genre instruction typically presents fiction/nonfiction/poetry/drama as a clean, mutually exclusive taxonomy using clearly single-genre example texts**, so a learner's assumption that every text fits exactly one box is a reasonable generalization from genuinely representative early practice material — the assumption breaks down specifically on more sophisticated texts (narrative nonfiction, graphic novels, docudramas) the learner hasn't yet had reason to classify as deliberately blended.

The third mechanism, specific to this concept's terminal position in the curriculum, is that **without further scaffolded unlocks to reinforce hybrid-recognition specifically**, a learner exiting with only partial mastery of the hybrid half of this concept (the harder, less intuitive half per the Blueprint's own S9 failure-routing) has no built-in downstream concept designed to catch and repair the gap.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-A-TEXTS-GENRE-CAN-BE-IDENTIFIED-FROM-ONE-SURFACE-FEATURE-ALONE
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the poetic-nonfiction/actual-poem and fictionalized-biography/factual-biography discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of a genuinely correlated pattern (surface features usually do predict genre) extended into a universal, always-sufficient test.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because surface features aren't a wrong signal, only an insufficient one on their own, the repair must **add purpose-and-full-convention checking as a supplement, not discard surface features as useless clues** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this correctly by treating surface features as one input among several, not as irrelevant.
- **Verification of death**: given a text with one misleading surface feature, the learner identifies the actual genre using purpose and full structural convention, rather than being misled by the single feature.

### MC-B-EVERY-TEXT-BELONGS-TO-EXACTLY-ONE-FIXED-GENRE-WITH-NO-OVERLAP
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-B — includes the narrative-nonfiction-memoir/pure-nonfiction discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of a correct rule for the great majority of single-genre texts, extended past its scope to texts genuinely designed to blend genres.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **naming the hybrid explicitly as its own recognized category** (narrative nonfiction, graphic memoir), rather than merely saying "sometimes texts don't fit neatly" — a concrete, namable alternative is more usable than an abstract caveat.
- **Verification of death**: given a genuinely hybrid text, the learner names it as a specific, recognized hybrid and identifies which conventions come from which genre, rather than forcing it into a single category.

## Analogies

**Best — the Pointed-Roof-House and Sweet-and-Savory-Dish Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): a house with a pointed roof mistaken for a church based on one surface feature alone directly seeds the multi-convention-checking fix; a dish that is deliberately both sweet and savory at once, resisting a single fixed category, directly seeds the hybrid-recognition fix.
*Breaking point*: a building's actual function (church versus house) is ultimately unambiguous once fully investigated, while some texts' genre classification remains genuinely, legitimately debatable even after full investigation (is a fictionalized biography closer to fiction or nonfiction? reasonable readers can differ) — useful for the multi-feature-checking and dual-nature intuitions, less precise for implying every genre question resolves to one clean, uncontested answer.

**Alternative — a costume that combines elements from two different historical periods.** Just as some costumes are deliberately designed to blend two eras rather than representing either one purely, some texts are deliberately designed to blend two genres' conventions rather than belonging cleanly to either.
*Breaking point*: a costume's blended design is usually an intentional, visible artistic choice the wearer can explain, while a text's genre-blending is sometimes a more organic, less deliberately "announced" feature of how the author chose to tell the story — useful for the deliberate-blend intuition, less precise for implying every hybrid text advertises its own hybridity as obviously as a costume would.

**Visual anchor — the Pointed-Roof-House and Sweet-and-Savory-Dish Anchor** as already described above, doubling as both the primary teaching metaphor and the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"If it has line breaks, it's a poem."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated rule, precisely the single-surface-feature shortcut this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"Every book is either fiction or nonfiction — pick one."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated procedure, exactly the forced-single-category error this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Pointed-Roof-House and Sweet-and-Savory-Dish Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Does a pointed roof always mean a church?" Then resolve, and extend to the sweet-and-savory dish for hybrid texts.
2. **The poetic-nonfiction/actual-poem collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "Does having line breaks automatically make this a poem?" Then check purpose and resolve.
3. **The narrative-nonfiction-memoir collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-B. *Predict first*: "Is this memoir telling a true story or using a story's structure — can it be both?" Then resolve as a hybrid.
4. **The full genre identification across a new text set (learner activity, TA-4).** *Predict first*: "Do you expect any of these texts to be a hybrid?" Then classify and confirm together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (purpose-over-surface-feature; genuine hybrid existence) is directly checkable through the learner's own classification of real, carefully-chosen text examples.

1. **Need**: "Does having line breaks automatically make a text a poem?"
2. **Playground**: the learner sorts a mixed set of texts by genre freely, including at least one with a misleading surface feature, before being told which classifications are correct.
3. **Invention**: "For the text that surprised you, what was misleading about it, and what actually told you its real genre?" The learner names the purpose/convention evidence.
4. **Collision**: present the narrative-nonfiction memoir and ask whether it must be either purely fiction or purely nonfiction.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — genre requires checking purpose and full convention, and hybrid texts genuinely exist — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Check purpose and convention together — and expect some texts to genuinely blend genres."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Purpose and convention over one surface feature (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting MC-A before any genre-content vocabulary is introduced, since the correct identification *method* must be secure before the specific genre categories themselves are taught.
- **The major genres and their conventions (TA-2) comes second**, building the concrete content (what fiction, nonfiction, poetry, and drama actually look like) that TA-1's method is meant to be applied to.
- **Genre subtypes (TA-3) comes third**, extending TA-2's major-genre vocabulary into finer-grained categories, building analytical precision before the harder hybrid-recognition content.
- **Hybrid and blended texts (TA-4) comes last**, directly confronting MC-B only once the learner has a secure grasp of the major genres and subtypes (TA-2/TA-3) to recognize as the *components* being blended in a hybrid — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's hardest, most integrative content is reserved for its culminating stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the mandatory purpose-first checklist for learners who revert to surface-feature judgments, the subtype-reference-list scaffold, and the transfer parallel with `eng.reading.text-structure`'s own pattern-recognition-over-surface-detail skill).

## Tutor Actions

In recommended order, from `../teaching-actions/`:

1. **Demonstration** (SHOW) — the Pointed-Roof-House and Sweet-and-Savory-Dish Anchor and both collision scenarios.
2. **Error Analysis** (TEST-THINKING) — both collisions function as guided error analysis against the learner's own plausible-but-incorrect classification (surface-feature-based, or forced-single-category).
3. **Matching** (DO) — matching texts to major genres and subtypes (TA-2/TA-3) is fundamentally a matching/classification action.
4. **Concept Map** (ORGANIZE) — a simple hybrid-decomposition map (which conventions come from which genre) built during TA-4, giving a compact, reusable artifact for analyzing any future hybrid text.

**Does not fit**: **Game** — the genuine analytical care this concept requires (checking multiple conventions rather than one salient cue) could be undermined by speed-pressured competition, which would reward quick surface-level guessing. **Role-Play** — no natural social/dialogic dimension to a genre-classification skill.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **spoken classification with cited purpose-and-convention evidence** — the learner's genre judgment plus the specific reasoning (purpose and confirming convention, or the specific conventions combined in a hybrid), since a correct-sounding classification with no stated reasoning cannot be distinguished from a lucky surface-feature guess that happened to land correctly.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated purpose-and-convention check overriding an initially misleading surface feature** ("it has line breaks, but its actual purpose is to inform with real facts, so it's nonfiction") — the direct behavioral signature of MC-A's repair having been internalized.
- **Spontaneous naming of a text as a hybrid, with specific conventions attributed to each contributing genre** ("this uses true events, that's nonfiction, but it's shaped like a story with a climax, that's from fiction") — the direct target evidence for MC-B's repair.
- **Confident, immediate single-genre classification of a genuinely hybrid text, with visible resistance or confusion when asked whether it could be both** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-B still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Check the purpose and the full set of conventions — and expect some texts to genuinely blend more than one genre."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But it has line breaks, doesn't that just mean it's a poem?"** — take this as an accurate observation about a real surface feature, not a mistake to be dismissed; respond by adding rather than contradicting: "Line breaks are a real clue that often points to poetry — now let's check the actual purpose. Is this text trying to inform with real facts, or evoke feeling through condensed, imagery-rich language?"
- **"I don't get how something can be both fiction and nonfiction at once"** — a genuine, reasonable difficulty given the typically-taught mutually-exclusive framing; respond by naming the actual pattern directly: "Most texts really do fit one box — but some are deliberately built to combine two, like a memoir that tells a completely true story using the same shape a fictional story would use. That's not a contradiction, it's its own real category."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full genre-and-hybrid classification across a new text set, down to **a single, clearly single-genre text with an obviously matching surface feature and no misleading trait or hybrid complication at all.** This removes both discrimination demands entirely, isolating the most basic genre-matching skill before returning to harder, misleading or blended cases.
- **Never shrink to "just go with whatever the text looks like at a glance."** This directly licenses MC-A's surface-feature shortcut, undermining the purpose-and-convention discipline this concept specifically builds.

## Memory Hooks

- **Concept type**: categorical classification skill (genre and subtype identification) with a discrimination component (surface feature vs. genuine convention; single-genre vs. hybrid) requiring genuine multi-feature analysis, not a single-cue lookup.
- **Review form**: spaced items always mixing straightforward single-genre texts with misleading-surface-feature and genuine-hybrid texts in the same review cycle, since this concept's own mandatory dual-pass gate treats surface-reliance and hybrid-recognition as separately diagnosable, and reviewing only the easier cases would understate the actual skill being maintained.
- **Concept-specific deviation**: because this concept is terminal (no further KG unlocks), review should track long-term retention of the harder hybrid-recognition half specifically, rather than relying on a downstream concept to naturally re-exercise it, as most non-terminal concepts in this program can.

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: none — this concept has no direct unlocks in the current curriculum.
- **Real-world**: recognizing genre-blending in contemporary media — a "based on a true story" film that fictionalizes real events, a documentary that uses dramatized reenactments, or a memoir written with novelistic techniques — and correctly identifying which conventions come from which source genre rather than dismissing the blend as a genre "error."
- **Expert transfer**: the durable skill is **classifying by underlying function and structure rather than surface appearance, while remaining genuinely open to legitimate hybrid or boundary-crossing cases rather than forcing every instance into a pre-existing, mutually exclusive category** — the same transfer skill needed in recognizing that a musical work can genuinely blend genres (jazz-classical fusion) rather than needing to be forced into one label, or that a biological organism can display features spanning traditional taxonomic boundaries in ways that reveal the boundaries themselves are analytical conveniences, not absolute divisions in nature.
