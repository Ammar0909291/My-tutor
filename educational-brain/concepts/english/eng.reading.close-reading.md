## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Perform a single, careful analytical pass on a passage — not multiple re-readings — producing specific word-choice analysis.
2. Identify 2-3 standout details (unusual word choice, repetition/motif, structural emphasis, tone shift) rather than attempting uniform analysis of every word.
3. Categorize standout details by type.
4. Connect 2-3 analyzed details into one coherent interpretive claim about a passage's meaning or effect.
5. Perform a complete, independent close reading of a novel passage: identify standout details, analyze each, and synthesize a coherent interpretive argument.

## Core Understanding

Close reading is defined by **analytical depth, not by reading speed or repetition** — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact: a learner who re-reads a passage three times but can only restate the literal plot afterward has not close-read it, while a learner who reads it once but can point to three specific word choices and explain their contribution to tone, symbolism, or characterization has. The "closeness" refers to analytical attention to specific textual details, not time spent or number of passes. The concept's second, equally load-bearing fact is that **not every detail in a text rewards equal analytical attention** — skilled close readers scan strategically for standout details (unusual or surprising word choices, repeated words or images, structurally emphasized positions like sentence beginnings and endings, sudden shifts in tone or pacing) and focus deep analysis there, rather than attempting exhaustive, uniform coverage of every word, which produces unfocused, shallow analysis that misses the details actually doing meaningful work.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "close reading means reading slowly or reading something multiple times."**
The runnable simulation: re-read a passage repeatedly or slow down significantly, treating time-on-task as the goal, without necessarily performing any deeper analytical work during the extra passes. This is a reasonable default, since "close" plausibly suggests physical proximity or careful, unhurried attention, and slowing down does genuinely feel like a more careful approach than a quick first read.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing a student who reads slowly three times but can only restate plot against a student who reads once but points to three specific word choices with analysis, and being asked which actually performed close reading.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Close reading is measured by analytical depth, not by speed or repetition — a single careful pass with real analysis beats ten passive re-reads."

**Intermediate — "I understand close reading means analytical depth, but I try to analyze every single word equally, which leaves me exhausted and unfocused."**
The simulation: correctly shifts from repetition to analysis, but applies that analytical effort uniformly across every word, missing the load-bearing details in favor of exhaustive but shallow coverage of everything.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing an attempt to close-read every single word against a strategic approach identifying 2-3 standout details, and being asked which produces richer understanding in a reasonable amount of time.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Not every word rewards equal attention — scan first for what stands out (unusual choices, repetitions, emphasized positions, tone shifts), then focus your deep analysis there."

**Advanced — "I scan strategically for standout details, analyze them with genuine depth, and synthesize my analyses into one coherent interpretive claim."**
The simulation: performs a full close reading independently, identifying standout details, analyzing each with specific evidence, and connecting them into a unified interpretive argument. This is the target model, matching TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: the two direct unlocks (`eng.reading.critical-reading`, `eng.literature.narrative-elements`), where this concept's strategic, evidence-based analytical habit becomes the foundation for broader critical evaluation and for identifying specific literary techniques.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the core strategic-analysis discipline. The next steps apply it to evaluating a text's claims critically and to identifying specific narrative techniques."

**Expert — "at full close-reading fluency, standout-detail detection and evidence-based interpretation happen as an integrated part of a single attentive reading, with formal annotation and written synthesis reserved for building a defensible, shareable analysis."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit scan-then-analyze-then-synthesize routine is a scaffold for a skill that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated part of attentive reading itself — named here so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is fluent, continuous close attention, not a permanently maintained formal-annotation exercise.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **"close" plausibly suggests careful, slow, repeated attention rather than a specific analytical method**, so a learner's assumption that close reading means reading slowly or multiple times is a reasonable inference from the term itself, not an arbitrary error — nothing about the phrase "close reading" signals that the actual defining property is analytical depth rather than time investment.

The second mechanism is that **exhaustive coverage feels like the safer, more responsible approach** once a learner has correctly shifted toward analysis — if every word might matter, skipping any of them feels like a risk, so a learner reasonably (if unsustainably) tries to analyze everything rather than making the judgment call about which details actually warrant deep attention.

The third mechanism, specific to this concept's terminal position in the curriculum, is that **without further scaffolded unlocks to reinforce standout-detail identification specifically**, a learner exiting with only partial mastery of the strategic-attention half of this concept (per the Blueprint's own S9 failure-routing, requiring both MP-1 and MP-2 for certification) has no built-in downstream concept designed to catch and repair the gap, even though both cross-linked concepts explicitly assume this skill is already solid.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-CLOSE-READING-MEANS-READING-SLOWLY-OR-RE-READING-MULTIPLE-TIMES
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-CLOSE-READING-MEANS-READING-SLOWLY-OR-RE-READING-MULTIPLE-TIMES — includes the repeated-shallow-read/single-deep-read discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 3 (language contamination), where the everyday sense of "close" (physically near, careful, unhurried) gets carried into a technical reading term that specifically names an analytical method, not a pacing choice.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because slowing down or re-reading isn't itself wrong, only insufficient on its own, the repair must **add active analytical questioning to whatever pace the learner uses, not mandate a specific speed** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this correctly, noting a single careful read can outperform ten passive re-reads.
- **Verification of death**: given a passage, the learner performs a single analytical pass and produces specific, evidence-based word-choice analysis, rather than defaulting to repetition without new analytical content.

### MC-EVERY-DETAIL-IN-A-TEXT-IS-EQUALLY-SIGNIFICANT-AND-MUST-BE-ANALYZED
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-EVERY-DETAIL-IN-A-TEXT-IS-EQUALLY-SIGNIFICANT-AND-MUST-BE-ANALYZED — includes the uniform-coverage/strategic-selection discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable instinct that careful reading shouldn't skip anything, extended into treating every word as equally analysis-worthy, when in fact most words are functional and only some carry special interpretive weight.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **a concrete, checkable category list of what actually stands out** (unusual word choices, repetitions, structural emphasis, tone shifts), since an abstract instruction to "focus on what matters" gives the learner no way to judge which details qualify.
- **Verification of death**: given a passage, the learner identifies 2-3 standout details using the named categories, rather than attempting equal-depth analysis of every word.

## Analogies

**Best — the Museum Spotlight** (Blueprint Component 3): a museum room full of mostly ordinary objects, with a few placed under a special curator's spotlight signaling "look closely here," directly seeds the strategic-attention fix; contrasting a visitor who walks the whole museum slowly without ever looking closely at anything (repetition without depth) against one who walks briskly but stops meaningfully at each spotlighted object directly seeds the depth-not-speed fix.
*Breaking point*: a museum curator has already done the work of deciding what's spotlight-worthy, while a close reader must independently identify which textual details deserve that attention — useful for the strategic-not-uniform intuition, less precise for implying the "spotlight" is externally provided rather than a skill the reader must develop.

**Alternative — panning for gold versus mining for coal.** Panning for gold means scanning a large amount of ordinary sediment quickly to find the rare, valuable flecks worth close examination, rather than treating every grain of sediment as equally worth detailed geological analysis, which is the more exhaustive, undiscriminating approach mining for a uniformly-distributed material like coal would require.
*Breaking point*: gold panning implies the valuable material is rare and randomly scattered, while a skilled author's standout details are deliberately, non-randomly placed by craft — useful for the scan-then-focus intuition, less precise for implying standout details are as rare or accidental as gold flecks in sediment.

**Visual anchor — the Museum Spotlight** as already described above, doubling as both the primary teaching metaphor and the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"Read it again, slower this time, and you'll understand it better."** Directly installs MC-CLOSE-READING-MEANS-READING-SLOWLY-OR-RE-READING-MULTIPLE-TIMES as stated advice, precisely the repetition-as-depth error this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"A thorough reader analyzes every single word in the passage."** Directly installs MC-EVERY-DETAIL-IN-A-TEXT-IS-EQUALLY-SIGNIFICANT-AND-MUST-BE-ANALYZED as a stated standard, exactly the uniform-coverage error this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Museum Spotlight (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Which of these highlighted words do you think the curator would spotlight?" Then point the spotlight prop and analyze each.
2. **The repeated-shallow-read/single-deep-read collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Which student actually did close reading?" Then compare their outputs and resolve.
3. **The uniform-coverage/strategic-selection collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Which approach produces richer understanding in a reasonable time?" Then compare and resolve.
4. **The independent full close reading (learner activity, TA-5).** *Predict first*: "Which details in this new passage do you expect to stand out?" Then perform the full analysis and confirm together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (depth-over-repetition; strategic-over-uniform attention) is directly felt through the learner's own comparison of analytical outputs.

1. **Need**: "If you read a passage five times but can only tell me the plot afterward, did you really close-read it?"
2. **Playground**: the learner attempts both a repeated-shallow read and a single-deep read of two comparable passages freely, before being told which approach is "correct."
3. **Invention**: "Which approach let you point to specific word choices and explain their effect?" The learner compares their own two attempts.
4. **Collision**: present a passage and ask the learner to try analyzing every single word, then ask how sustainable and productive that felt.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — depth defines closeness, and strategic attention beats uniform coverage — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "One careful pass, spotlighting what stands out — not many passive ones covering everything equally."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Annotation-based analysis (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting the first misconception before any standout-detail vocabulary is introduced, since the core shift from passive re-reading to active analytical questioning must be secure before refining which details to focus that questioning on.
- **Identifying standout details (TA-2) comes second**, giving the learner concrete categories (unusual word choice, motif, structural emphasis, tone shift) to search for, building the vocabulary TA-3's strategic practice depends on.
- **Strategic vs. uniform attention (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the second misconception once the learner has the standout-detail categories (TA-2) needed to make the strategic-versus-uniform choice concretely rather than abstractly.
- **Building a close-reading argument (TA-4) and independent close reading (TA-5) come last**, with TA-4 adding the synthesis step (connecting isolated observations into one coherent claim) and TA-5 requiring the learner to integrate annotation (TA-1), strategic selection (TA-2/TA-3), and synthesis (TA-4) on a genuinely novel, unscaffolded passage — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **spoken analytical reasoning with cited textual evidence** — the learner's identified standout details and their stated analysis, plus the synthesized interpretive claim connecting them, since a plausible-sounding interpretation with no cited specific word choices cannot be distinguished from a vague, unsupported impression.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated "why this word, not an obvious alternative?" question applied spontaneously to a specific word choice** — the direct behavioral signature of genuine analytical engagement rather than passive reading.
- **Selective identification of 2-3 details with a stated reason each stands out** (unusual, repeated, structurally emphasized, or tone-shifting), rather than an attempt to comment on every word — the direct target evidence for the second misconception's repair.
- **A repeated re-reading of the same passage with no new observation offered after each pass** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-CLOSE-READING-MEANS-READING-SLOWLY-OR-RE-READING-MULTIPLE-TIMES still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"One careful pass, spotlighting what actually stands out — depth, not repetition, is what makes it close."*

**Mastery certification trigger**: a single analytical pass producing specific word-choice analysis; strategic identification of 2-3 standout details on a novel passage; correct categorization of standout-detail types; a coherent, connecting interpretive claim built from multiple analyzed details; and a full, independent close reading of a novel, unscaffolded passage.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"I read it three times, shouldn't that count as close reading?"** — take this as an accurate report of real effort, not a mistake to be dismissed; respond by redirecting the effort, not discarding it: "That real effort is a great starting point — now let's put it toward analysis instead of repetition. On this next read, stop at one word that surprises you and ask why the author chose it."
- **"I don't know which details matter, so I try to write about everything"** — a genuine, reasonable response to uncertainty about what "counts"; respond by naming the actual categories directly: "You don't need to guess randomly — look specifically for words that feel unusual, words or images that repeat, or anything placed right at the start or end of a sentence. Those are the details worth digging into."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full independent close reading with synthesis, down to **identifying and analyzing just one, maximally obvious standout detail in a short passage, with no synthesis-across-multiple-details demand at all.** This removes the multi-detail integration entirely, isolating the most basic strategic-identification-plus-analysis skill before returning to harder, fuller close readings.
- **Never shrink to "just pick any word and say something about it."** This bypasses the actual strategic-selection judgment this concept teaches, producing analysis of arbitrary rather than genuinely load-bearing details.
