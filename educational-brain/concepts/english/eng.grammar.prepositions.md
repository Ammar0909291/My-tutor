## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify and produce location prepositions (in, on, under, behind,
   between, near) correctly describing spatial relationships.
2. Identify and produce time prepositions (at for specific times, on
   for days/dates, in for months/years/longer periods).
3. Identify other relationship prepositions expressing manner/means
   (by, with), accompaniment, and other connections (of, for, about,
   despite, without).
4. Correctly use common adjective + preposition collocations (good at,
   interested in, afraid of, proud of), recognizing these as
   conventional pairings to be learned directly, not logically derived.
5. Correctly use common verb + preposition collocations (depend on,
   believe in, listen to, look for), extending the collocation-learning
   skill to another word class.

## Core Understanding

Prepositions show the relationship between a noun/pronoun and another
word in the sentence — this includes LOCATION (in, on, under) but also
TIME (at, on, in — used differently for time than for place), manner/
means (by, with), and other relationships (of, for, about, despite,
without). Location is the most vivid, common category and often the
first taught, but it is far from the only one — "at 3pm" shows a time
relationship, not a physical position, using the same preposition
category (at/on/in) that also serves location. A second, equally
load-bearing fact is that many preposition choices, especially after
certain adjectives (good AT, interested IN, afraid OF, proud OF) and
verbs (depend ON, believe IN, listen TO), are CONVENTIONAL/idiomatic
combinations that must be learned individually — they often cannot be
reliably derived from pure logic or direct translation from another
language. English speakers say "good AT math," not "good in math,"
for no derivable logical reason other than that being the conventional
pairing that has taken hold — this is a genuine, bounded memorization
task, not a rule waiting to be reasoned out.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "prepositions show location — where something
  is"**: the arriving, pre-instructional model this concept exists to
  correct — a natural default since location prepositions are the most
  vivid, easily demonstrated, and typically first-taught category,
  giving no built-in signal that time and other relationship
  prepositions are equally central.
- **Intermediate model — "I can identify and produce location and time
  prepositions"**: installed by TA-1's location practice and TA-2's
  time practice, using the Component 3 preposition obstacle course
  anchor (a location round with physical props, then a time round with
  a calendar/clock prop) to make the expansion concrete, resolving
  MC-PREPOSITIONS-ONLY-SHOW-LOCATION. Upgrade trigger: correctly using
  a fresh time preposition without hesitation, recognizing "at/on/in"
  work differently for time than for place.
- **Advanced model — "I can identify other relationship prepositions
  (manner, accompaniment, and more) beyond location and time"**:
  installed by TA-3's practice, completing the three-part expansion
  of the concept's core categories.
- **Expert model — "I recognize that adjective/verb + preposition
  pairings are conventional, not logically derivable, and I learn the
  specific correct pairings directly"**: installed by TA-4's
  adjective-collocation practice and TA-5's verb-collocation practice,
  resolving MC-PREPOSITION-CHOICE-IS-PREDICTABLE-FROM-MEANING. This is
  the concept's genuine mastery bar — accepting that certain
  combinations are bounded, learnable vocabulary items rather than
  rule-derivable, and redirecting effort toward effective memorization
  rather than futile logical derivation.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own Adaptive Flags, for
  S9 learners whose L1 preposition system maps very differently onto
  English (a near-universal, well-documented L2 English challenge —
  "afraid FROM" instead of "afraid OF," "married WITH" instead of
  "married TO"), preposition collocations should be expected as one of
  the single most persistent, high-frequency error sources across all
  proficiency levels — this concept's practice should be understood as
  a starting point, not a quickly-resolved topic, with substantial,
  ongoing, spaced practice needed well beyond this single session.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, both reasonable
extensions of how prepositions are typically first encountered. The
location-only failure exists because location prepositions genuinely
ARE the most vivid, easily demonstrated, and typically first-taught
category (using physical objects and spatial props), so a learner
reasonably extends "prepositions show location" from this consistently
reinforced pattern, with nothing marking the time/other-relationship
exception until directly tested against a sentence like "The meeting
is AT 3pm." The logical-derivation failure exists because most other
grammar patterns a learner has met genuinely ARE logically derivable
from consistent rules (e.g., pluralization, tense formation), so a
learner reasonably extends this expectation to preposition choice too,
with nothing in that broader grammatical experience marking that
certain preposition pairings are conventional rather than rule-based
until a specific mismatch between "logical" and "actual" pairing is
directly tested (like "good AT" rather than the seemingly more logical
"good IN").

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the preposition obstacle course itself is the
  analogy, not a supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): physically
  moving an object to demonstrate location prepositions, then shifting
  to a calendar/clock prop to demonstrate the same preposition category
  (at/on/in) working differently for time, directly embodies the
  location-to-time expansion within one continuous, hands-on
  demonstration. Breaking point: a physical obstacle course is well
  suited to location and works reasonably for time via a calendar prop,
  but doesn't naturally extend to TA-3's more abstract "other
  relationship" prepositions (manner, accompaniment) — this entry
  treats the obstacle-course anchor as the introductory frame for
  location and time specifically, with TA-3 extending beyond the
  anchor's own physical demonstration.
- **Alternative — learning a foreign phrase exactly as a native speaker
  says it, not translating word-by-word**: idiomatic expressions in any
  language must often be learned as fixed units rather than translated
  literally — parallels why "good AT" must be learned as its own
  conventional pairing rather than derived from what "at" logically
  "should" mean. Breaking point: useful for the conventional-pairing
  acceptance specifically, but less directly tied to the location-vs-
  time-vs-other-relationship category expansion the obstacle-course
  anchor covers.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe preposition collocations as
  "exceptions to the rules" or "irregular"**: framing collocations as
  exceptions implies there IS an underlying rule the learner should be
  seeking, reinforcing MC-PREPOSITION-CHOICE-IS-PREDICTABLE-FROM-
  MEANING; the correct framing is that these ARE the actual pattern —
  conventional, memorized pairings — not deviations from some other,
  more "correct" logical system.

- **Physical/kinesthetic (the preposition obstacle course)**: the

- **Home/no-equipment**: any small object and household items (a table,

- **Teacher-demo (time-preposition and collocation contrasts)**: the
  tutor models the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstrations —

- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a

  collocation pairing (TA-4/TA-5), ask the learner to state their own
  guess before the tutor confirms — Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** The
multi-category expansion and the conventional-pairing acceptance are
both genuine, directly felt phenomena well-suited to guided discovery:
**need** (the learner, having secured location prepositions, is asked
whether "at 3pm" shows a physical location); **playground/invention**
(TA-1's location practice with physical props builds initial
confidence with the category); **collision** (the Blueprint's own "at
3pm" probe breaks the location-only assumption; the "good in math"
probe breaks the logical-derivation assumption); **formalization**
(MP-5's own-words statement of both the multi-category principle and
the conventional-pairing principle); **compression** (TA-5's fluent
verb-collocation use without each pairing separately re-derived). The
specific term "collocation" itself is convention-bound terminology
best introduced by direct instruction once the learner has already felt
the underlying conventional-pairing distinction through TA-4.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (location prepositions) is placed first as the concept's most
intuitive, physically demonstrable entry point, per the Blueprint's own
obstacle-course anchor. TA-2 (time prepositions) follows immediately,
directly targeting MC-PREPOSITIONS-ONLY-SHOW-LOCATION — per the
Blueprint's own session architecture, this conflict-evidence probe is
placed right after TA-2, once the learner has secure location practice
to contrast against. TA-3 (other relationship prepositions) extends the
category framework further into manner/means and accompaniment,
completing the concept's core three-part relationship-type expansion.
TA-4 (adjective + preposition collocations) is placed next, directly
targeting MC-PREPOSITION-CHOICE-IS-PREDICTABLE-FROM-MEANING, addressing
the SECOND, independent dimension (conventional pairing vs. logical
derivation) once the learner already has a secure sense of what
prepositions do across all three relationship types. TA-5 (verb +
preposition collocations) is placed last, extending the collocation-
learning skill from TA-4 to a second word class, reinforcing that the
conventional-pairing discipline generalizes beyond adjectives alone.
This entry does not restate the Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session
script (Component 7) — see Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner spontaneously identifies
  a preposition's relationship TYPE (location, time, manner, other)
  when meeting a fresh sentence (rather than defaulting to a location
  assumption) — this is the primary diagnostic signal for whether
  TA-2/TA-3's disciplines have genuinely generalized beyond the
  specific practice items.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause before producing an
  adjective/verb + preposition combination, followed by a correct
  conventional pairing recalled directly (not derived), suggests the
  learner is consciously retrieving a learned pairing rather than
  reasoning it out — a good sign, and the EXPECTED pattern for
  collocations, not a sign of insufficient understanding. Confident but
  logically-derived incorrect pairings (e.g., "good in math") are the
  concerning pattern for MC-PREPOSITION-CHOICE-IS-PREDICTABLE-FROM-
  MEANING.
- **Persistent-error stakes**: real and specifically named in the
  Blueprint's own Adaptive Flags — for S9 learners, preposition
  collocation errors are among the most persistent, universal L2
  English challenges, well-documented to lag behind other grammar
  areas even at advanced proficiency levels; this should be treated as
  an expected, long-term practice area, not a deficiency to quickly
  resolve.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "is there a logical reason, or is this
  just the pairing you have to learn?" is this concept's load-bearing
  sentence for MC-PREPOSITION-CHOICE-IS-PREDICTABLE-FROM-MEANING, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` — asked
  whenever a fresh adjective/verb + preposition combination is
  introduced, making the conventional-pairing acceptance a routinely-
  invited response rather than continued logical searching.

  acceptance has generalized — this should be named and praised
  explicitly, per the Blueprint's own regulation-tail guidance
  (praising learning conventional pairings directly over forcing
  logical derivation).

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: fact/framework combined with procedure — a
relationship-type classification system (location/time/other) applied
to fresh sentences, plus a bounded, direct-memorization vocabulary set
(adjective/verb + preposition collocations) requiring ongoing spaced
practice rather than logical derivation; mastery is demonstrated by
correctly classifying unfamiliar preposition uses (MP-1/MP-2/MP-3) and
correctly recalling (not deriving) collocation pairings (MP-4/MP-5).
Interleaving partners: mix relationship-type classification items
(testing TA-1/TA-2/TA-3) with collocation-recall items (testing TA-4/
TA-5) in ongoing review, and per the Blueprint's own Adaptive Flags,
extend collocation review well beyond this single session given
prepositions' documented status as one of the most persistent,
long-tail error sources in English acquisition.
