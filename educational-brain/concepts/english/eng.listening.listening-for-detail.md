## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify, before listening, exactly what specific piece of information is needed, then attend selectively for it rather than attempting total recall.
2. Recognize common signal phrases that flag an upcoming detail (names, times, numbers, locations).
3. Track multiple independent requested details in one passage, reporting whichever subset was caught rather than treating any single miss as total failure.
4. Use surrounding context to recover or narrow down a missed detail on a repeat listen.
5. Distinguish a precisely requested detail from a similar-sounding distractor detail nearby in the same passage.

## Core Understanding

Listening for detail means deciding **what specific piece of information you need before you listen**, then letting everything else pass by while waiting for that one piece to arrive — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact, and it directly counters the intuitive but exhausting strategy of trying to mentally transcribe every word: a listener who decides in advance to listen only for a time, a name, or a location can let the surrounding speech flow past without effort to retain it, arriving at the answer with far less strain and far higher accuracy than a listener attempting total capture. The concept's second load-bearing fact is that when a task requests **multiple separate details, each is caught or missed independently** — like items on a shopping list, missing one item doesn't erase or invalidate the ones already found; a learner who catches two of three requested details has genuinely succeeded at two-thirds of the task, and should report that success confidently rather than treating the one miss as proof the whole listening attempt failed.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "I need to catch and remember every single word, or I haven't really listened."**
The runnable simulation: attempt total, real-time mental transcription of an entire passage, losing the thread and feeling overwhelmed the moment any word or phrase is missed. This is a reasonable default carried over from earlier listening work (gist listening's own "catch what you can" framing notwithstanding), since detail-listening's specific permission to *ignore* most of the passage is a genuinely counterintuitive instruction the first time it's given.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing a learner told to "catch every word" against one told to "just find out what time the tour starts," and observing which one answers correctly with less stress.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Decide what you're listening for before you listen — then let everything else pass by. You don't need to hold onto all of it."

**Intermediate — "I listen selectively for the specific detail I need, but if a task asks for several details and I miss one, I feel like the whole attempt failed."**
The simulation: correctly applies target-first listening for a single detail, but treats a multi-detail task as all-or-nothing, refusing to report the details successfully caught once one is missed.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — catching 2 of 3 requested details and being asked whether to report the 2 caught or refuse to answer anything at all because one was missed.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Each requested detail is its own separate catch. Missing one doesn't erase what you got right on the others — report what you know."

**Advanced — "I decide my listening target in advance, track multiple requested details independently, use context to recover a miss on a repeat listen, and can pick out the precise requested detail even when a similar-sounding distractor is nearby."**
The simulation: applies target-first listening, treats each detail in a multi-detail task as independently recoverable, and correctly distinguishes a requested detail from a nearby distractor using signal words and context. This is the target model, matching TA-4/TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: the two direct unlocks (`eng.listening.following-instructions`, `eng.listening.note-taking-while-listening`), where this concept's target-first, independent-item tracking extends to sequences of action steps and to sustained note-capture respectively.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the core skill — decide the target, track independently, use context to recover. The next steps apply it to following multi-step instructions and to taking notes across a longer passage."

**Expert — "at full listening fluency, target-detail extraction from fast, natural speech happens largely automatically, with explicit target-setting reserved for high-stakes or unusually dense information."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit, deliberate target-first routine is a scaffold for a skill that, at true listening expertise, operates largely below conscious awareness for ordinary information-dense speech (announcements, directions, briefings) — named here so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is automatic, real-time extraction, not a permanently maintained conscious checklist.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **total-capture listening feels like the "complete" or "thorough" strategy**, so a learner's instinct to try to retain everything is not an arbitrary error but an understandable, if exhausting, response to the fear of missing something important — the failure surfaces specifically because human working memory cannot actually sustain total verbatim retention of extended natural speech, making the strategy self-defeating exactly when the passage is long or information-dense enough to matter.

The second mechanism is that **all-or-nothing thinking about task success is a general, not listening-specific, tendency** — a learner who misses one of several requested details reasonably (if unhelpfully) generalizes "I missed something" into "I failed the task," rather than recognizing that most real-world detail-listening tasks (a schedule, an address with several parts, instructions with several steps) are structured as multiple independent sub-tasks where partial success is genuine, valuable success.

The third mechanism, specific to distractor-detail items (TA-5), is that **two similar-sounding pieces of information genuinely can occur close together in real speech** (an original time and a revised time, in the Blueprint's own flight-departure example), and a learner who hasn't yet built the habit of listening for signal words that distinguish "originally"/"now" or "before"/"after" will reasonably catch the first-heard number without checking whether a later correction superseded it.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-LISTENING-FOR-DETAIL-MEANS-TRYING-TO-CATCH-EVERY-WORD
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-LISTENING-FOR-DETAIL-MEANS-TRYING-TO-CATCH-EVERY-WORD — includes the "catch every word"/"just find the time" discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable instinct that thoroughness means completeness, extended into an unsustainable total-recall strategy for a task that specifically doesn't require it.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because thoroughness itself isn't the wrong instinct, only its total-capture expression, the repair must **redirect the instinct toward selecting the right target rather than abandoning care altogether** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this by reframing "letting the rest pass by" as the deliberate, correct strategy, not a lowering of standards.
- **Verification of death**: given a passage with one clearly requested detail, the learner attends selectively and reports the target detail without attempting to recount unrelated content from the passage.

### MC-IF-YOU-MISS-ONE-DETAIL-THE-WHOLE-LISTENING-TASK-FAILED
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-IF-YOU-MISS-ONE-DETAIL-THE-WHOLE-LISTENING-TASK-FAILED — includes the 2-of-3-caught discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of a general all-or-nothing success standard, extended into a listening context where partial, independent success is genuinely the correct evaluative frame.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct practice reporting partial success out loud**, since simply being told "partial success is fine" is less convincing than the felt experience of confidently stating two correct details while explicitly naming the one that was missed.
- **Verification of death**: given a multi-detail task where one detail is likely missed, the learner reports the details caught confidently, without waiting for permission or apologizing for the miss.

## Analogies

**Best — panning for gold in a river, letting the water and silt flow through your hands while only the gold flakes are kept.** Trying to catch every drop of water (every word) is impossible and misses the point; the skilled panner lets everything but the target (the gold — the requested detail) flow through, catching only what matters.
*Breaking point*: gold panning has a single target type throughout, while detail-listening tasks often require tracking several different target types (a name, then a time, then a location) within one pass — useful for the let-most-of-it-pass intuition, less precise for the multiple-simultaneous-targets complication TA-3 introduces.

**Alternative — a shopping list at a large supermarket.** You don't need to notice or remember every single product on every shelf you pass — you're scanning specifically for the items on your list, and finding three of four items on your list is a successful, useful trip, not a failure just because one item was out of stock or missed.
*Breaking point*: a shopping list is checked at leisure with no time pressure, while detail-listening usually happens in real time with no ability to pause — useful for the independent-item, partial-success intuition, less precise for the real-time-pressure dimension TA-1's target-first strategy specifically addresses.

**Visual/physical anchor — the Shopping List Listener** (Blueprint Component 3): not a metaphor but the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around, using physical flip-cards to make both the target-first strategy and the independent-detail principle concretely visible in one activity.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"A good listener remembers everything they hear."** Directly installs MC-LISTENING-FOR-DETAIL-MEANS-TRYING-TO-CATCH-EVERY-WORD as an implicit standard, precisely the total-capture goal this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"If you can't get all the details, you might as well not answer."** Directly installs MC-IF-YOU-MISS-ONE-DETAIL-THE-WHOLE-LISTENING-TASK-FAILED, exactly the all-or-nothing framing this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Shopping List Listener (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Which of these 3 cards do you think you'll catch?" Then listen and flip each card as it's caught.
2. **The "catch every word" vs. "just find the time" comparison (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Which listener do you think will answer correctly with less stress?" Then compare and resolve.
3. **The 2-of-3-caught scenario (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Should this listener report the 2 they caught, or refuse to answer because they missed one?" Then resolve.
4. **The distractor-detail passage (learner activity, TA-5).** *Predict first*: "This passage mentions two times — which one do you think is the one actually being asked for?" Then listen and confirm using signal words.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (target-first efficiency; independent-detail partial success) is directly experienced through the learner's own listening attempts.

1. **Need**: "If you try to remember every single word of a long announcement, how much do you think you'll actually retain?"
2. **Playground**: the learner listens to a passage with a stated single target, freely noting whether attending only to that target felt easier than trying to catch everything.
3. **Invention**: "Out of these 3 requested details, which did you catch, and does missing one make the others you caught less true?" The learner tests this directly.
4. **Collision**: present a passage with a distractor detail and ask which of two similar-sounding pieces of information is the one actually requested.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — decide the target before listening, and treat each requested detail as independently caught or missed — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Know your target before you listen — and report what you caught, not what you missed."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Target-first listening (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting MC-LISTENING-FOR-DETAIL-MEANS-TRYING-TO-CATCH-EVERY-WORD before any multi-detail complexity is introduced, since the core selective-attention habit must be secure on a single target before it can be extended to tracking several targets at once.
- **Detail-type signal words (TA-2) come second**, giving the learner concrete linguistic cues (names, times, numbers, locations) to listen for, building the vocabulary needed for TA-3's independent-tracking task.
- **Multiple independent details (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting MC-IF-YOU-MISS-ONE-DETAIL-THE-WHOLE-LISTENING-TASK-FAILED once single-target listening (TA-1) and signal-word recognition (TA-2) are both secure, since independent tracking of several details presupposes the learner can already reliably catch one.
- **Context-based recovery (TA-4) and distractor precision (TA-5) come last**, with TA-4 adding a repair strategy for genuine misses and TA-5 requiring the sharpest discrimination (a real detail vs. a similar-sounding decoy) — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's hardest, most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **audio-based and inherently a spoken-listening task** — the tutor presents audio (or read-aloud passages) and the learner's response is spoken identification of specific details, making this concept as directly audio-dependent as `eng.listening.distinguishing-sounds-in-speech`.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Confident reporting of 2 of 3 requested details, with the third named as missed rather than avoided** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-IF-YOU-MISS-ONE-DETAIL-THE-WHOLE-LISTENING-TASK-FAILED's repair; this should be explicitly praised per the Blueprint's own regulation-tail guidance.
- **A stated signal-word cue accompanying a correct detail identification** ("I caught 'Suite 204' because of 'in'") — the direct behavioral signature of genuine signal-word attention rather than a lucky catch.
- **Visible overwhelm or a request to "hear it again from the start" immediately after a single missed word, on a task with a clearly stated single target** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-LISTENING-FOR-DETAIL-MEANS-TRYING-TO-CATCH-EVERY-WORD still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Know what you're listening for before you listen — and report what you caught, even if you missed something else."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Target-first single detail (MP-1) and partial-success reporting (MP-2).** Per the Blueprint's own S9 protocol routing, mastery must NOT be certified unless both pass — a learner who recognizes signal words (MP-3) but panics on a partial miss has not internalized the resilience this concept requires.
- **Signal word recognition (MP-3).** Listen for whether the learner names the specific signal phrase, not just the correct detail — the phrase-level awareness is what generalizes to novel passages.
- **Distractor precision (MP-4).** This concept's most direct test of fine discrimination; a correct final answer with no stated distinguishing reasoning is weaker evidence than one accompanied by the specific words that disambiguated the two candidates.
- **Multi-detail passage with context recovery (MP-5).** For any missed detail, listen for a plausible, context-grounded recovery strategy (not a guess) — this is the synthesis item combining TA-3's independent tracking with TA-4's context-based repair.

**Mastery certification trigger**: correct target-first identification on a novel single-detail item; confident partial-success reporting on a novel multi-detail item; signal-phrase-cited identification across name/time/location types; correct distractor discrimination with stated distinguishing evidence; and a full multi-detail passage response with a plausible recovery strategy for any genuine miss.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"I lost track after the second detail, I don't think I got any of it"** — take this as an honest report of genuine difficulty, not evidence of total failure; respond by checking directly: "Let's see — what's the very first thing you remember hearing, even if it's not one of the three targets? Often you caught more than you think." Then rebuild from there.
- **"I feel like I failed because I missed one"** — a genuine, reasonable frustration given ordinary all-or-nothing task norms; respond by naming the actual standard directly: "Missing one out of three is real, solid partial success — report the two you got confidently, and we'll use context to work on the third."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full multi-detail, distractor-laden passage, down to **a single, clearly stated target detail in a short, unambiguous passage with no distractor at all.** This removes both the multi-tracking and discrimination demands entirely, isolating the most basic target-first skill before returning to harder, multi-detail cases.
- **Never shrink to "just try to remember as much as you can."** This directly licenses MC-LISTENING-FOR-DETAIL-MEANS-TRYING-TO-CATCH-EVERY-WORD's total-capture pattern, undermining the target-first habit this concept specifically builds.
