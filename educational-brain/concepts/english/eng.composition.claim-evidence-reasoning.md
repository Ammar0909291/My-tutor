## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify the claim, evidence, and reasoning components in a given argumentative passage.
2. Recognize when reasoning is missing even though a claim and evidence are both present.
3. Recognize when a reasoning sentence merely restates the claim rather than adding genuine explanatory content.
4. Construct a complete claim-evidence-reasoning unit, including an explicit reasoning sentence stating why the evidence supports the claim.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept. First, **evidence placed next to a claim is not enough** — leaving the connection between them implicit assumes the reader will supply the reasoning themselves, but the same evidence could support several different, even contradictory conclusions; the reasoning sentence is the explicit "middle section" that carries the reader from evidence to claim, and without it a real logical gap remains. Second, **reasoning does not mean simply restating the claim again in different words** — a reasoning sentence must add genuinely new explanatory content (the mechanism or logical step connecting evidence to claim), and if a "reasoning" sentence could be deleted and swapped for the claim itself with no real loss of information, it's an echo, not reasoning.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "putting my evidence right after my claim is enough, and any sentence that sounds like it's explaining counts as reasoning."**
The simulation: write a claim immediately followed by a piece of evidence with no connecting sentence, and when prompted for reasoning, write something that just restates the claim in different words.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a claim-then-evidence pair tested for whether several different conclusions could be drawn from the same evidence, and a genuine reasoning sentence compared against one that merely echoes the claim.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Evidence and claim sitting on opposite riverbanks need a bridge — the reasoning is that bridge deck. A bridge built from the same material as one bank never reaches the other side."

**Intermediate — "I can now add a reasoning sentence and check it's not circular, but my first attempt at a new topic often still comes out circular before I catch it."**
The simulation: correctly identifies missing or circular reasoning in others' writing but produces a first-draft reasoning sentence for a new topic that still echoes the claim before self-correcting.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own protocol-routing observation that this progression (learning to ADD reasoning, then defaulting to circular reasoning at first) is natural, not a regression — the "cover up the claim" test applied as a self-check.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Cover up your claim sentence — could someone reading only your evidence and reasoning guess what the claim probably is? If not, add the missing mechanism."

**Advanced — "I construct a full claim-evidence-reasoning unit from scratch, and diagnose and repair a flawed unit with an unnamed defect."**
The simulation: integrates the missing-bridge detection and the echo-detection into one coherent, self-directed construction and diagnosis skill. Matches TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: `eng.composition.argumentation-basics`'s dependence on constructing one sound CER unit reliably before building complete, multi-claim arguments.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you one sound argument unit. Building complete arguments from several of these comes next."

**Expert — "at fluency, checking that reasoning bridges the gap and adds genuine content happens as an integrated part of ordinary argumentative writing, without a separate deliberate checklist."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit identify-detect-construct routine is a scaffold for an argumentative habit that, at fluency, operates as an integrated part of composition.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **the connection between evidence and claim often feels obvious to the WRITER**, who already holds the full reasoning chain in mind, making the explicit reasoning sentence feel redundant to write even though the reader has no access to that unstated mental chain.

The second mechanism is that **a restated claim genuinely sounds like an answer to "why?"** on a surface level, since it uses assertive, conclusion-shaped language, making the circularity easy to miss without the explicit "does this add new information?" test.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-EVIDENCE-NEXT-TO-A-CLAIM-IS-ENOUGH
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from the evidence-to-claim connection feeling obvious to a writer who already holds the full reasoning chain in mind, masking the reader's lack of access to that unstated chain.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **show that the same evidence could support multiple different conclusions**, since the reasoning gap is far more convincing demonstrated through genuine ambiguity than asserted abstractly.
- **Verification of death**: given a claim-evidence pair with no reasoning, the learner identifies that reasoning is missing and writes the specific sentence needed to bridge the gap.

### MC-B-REASONING-MEANS-JUST-RESTATING-THE-CLAIM-AGAIN
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of "a sentence that follows the evidence and sounds conclusive" being mistaken for genuine reasoning, since a restated claim surface-level resembles an explanatory answer.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **the "cover up the claim" test applied directly**, since circularity is far more convincing demonstrated by testing whether the claim is guessable from the reasoning alone than asserted abstractly.
- **Verification of death**: given a claim, evidence, and a reasoning sentence that merely restates the claim, the learner identifies the circularity and revises it into genuine reasoning.

## Analogies

**Best — the Broken-Bridge Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): two riverbanks labeled "Evidence" and "Claim" with a gap between them, and no way across until a bridge deck labeled "Reasoning" is drawn in — directly seeding the fix for MC-A. Extended: a "bridge" built from the same material as one bank (a restated claim) doesn't actually span the gap, it just extends one bank further without reaching the other side — directly seeding the fix for MC-B.
*Breaking point*: a physical bridge either clearly spans a gap or doesn't, a binary, visually obvious state, while a real reasoning sentence's adequacy is a more graded, judgment-dependent quality (some reasoning is partially adequate) than a bridge simply reaching or not reaching the other bank.

**Alternative — a math proof's missing justification step.** Writing "2x = 10, therefore x = 5" without stating "divide both sides by 2" leaves the SAME kind of implicit gap a reader must fill themselves — and simply writing "x = 5 because x = 5" restates the conclusion without ever supplying the actual justification step.
*Breaking point*: a math proof's missing step has one single, objectively correct justification to supply, while an argumentative reasoning sentence can have multiple, genuinely different valid mechanisms connecting the same evidence to the same claim, a difference in solution-uniqueness the math-proof analogy doesn't fully capture.

**Visual anchor — the Broken-Bridge Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"Just put your evidence right after your claim and the reader will get it."** Directly installs the evidence-is-enough misconception as a stated definition.
- **"Say 'this shows that [restate your claim]' and you've written your reasoning."** Directly installs the restated-claim-is-reasoning misconception as a stated definition.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Broken-Bridge Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "How does something get from the Evidence bank to the Claim bank with no bridge?"
2. **The missing-bridge collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Does this survey result necessarily mean lunch periods should be longer, or could it mean something else?"
3. **The echo-detection collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Does this reasoning sentence tell us anything we didn't already know from the claim alone?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both facts are directly checkable by testing whether alternative conclusions could follow from the same evidence, and by covering up the claim to test reasoning's self-sufficiency.

1. **Need**: "If a survey shows students feel rushed at lunch, does that necessarily mean lunch periods should be longer — or could there be other explanations?"
2. **Playground**: the learner freely writes a claim-then-evidence pair before being told the official reasoning-gap test.
3. **Invention**: "Cover up your claim sentence — could someone guess it from your evidence and reasoning alone?"
4. **Collision**: present a genuine reasoning sentence and a restated-claim "reasoning" sentence side by side, and ask which adds new information.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — evidence needs an explicit reasoning bridge to a claim, and reasoning must add new content, not echo the claim — from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Build the bridge explicitly; don't just extend one bank."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Identifying claim, evidence, and reasoning (TA-1) comes first**, establishing basic component recognition before either misconception is directly confronted.
- **Detecting the missing bridge (TA-2) comes second**, directly confronting the first misconception once component recognition is established.
- **Writing genuine reasoning, not an echo (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the second misconception once the reasoning-gap is understood.
- **Constructing a full CER unit from scratch (TA-4) comes fourth**, integrating both fixes into original production.
- **Evaluating and strengthening weak arguments (TA-5) comes last**, the generative capstone integrating diagnosis and repair across multiple flaw types, including one (evidence-claim mismatch) not directly taught by either misconception — matching this program's established sequencing principle of reserving the most integrative task for the culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated test-application when checking their own reasoning sentence** — whether they apply the "cover up the claim" test or the "this shows that... because..." stem — since a correct-looking CER unit with no stated reasoning-check cannot be distinguished from lucky imitation of an example.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated gap-check** ("could a reader draw a different conclusion from this same evidence without my reasoning sentence?") applied to a new claim-evidence pair — the direct behavioral signature of the missing-bridge fix taking hold.
- **Voluntary application of the "cover up the claim" test** to a self-written reasoning sentence, revising if it fails — direct evidence the echo-detection fix is internalized.
- **Placing evidence immediately after a claim with no connecting sentence, or writing "this shows that [claim restated]" as the entire reasoning** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-A or MC-B still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Build the bridge explicitly — don't just extend one bank."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads:

- **Component identification (MP-1).** Failure indicates basic component identification (TA-1) needs review.
- **Detecting missing reasoning (MP-2).** Failure alone indicates residual MC-A.
- **Detecting circular reasoning (MP-3).** Failure alone indicates residual MC-B.
- **Constructing a full CER unit (MP-4).** Failure with MP-1 through MP-3 passing indicates a production gap distinct from recognition.
- **Full diagnosis and repair (MP-5).** The synthesis item; listen for correct flaw-type identification across missing reasoning, circular reasoning, or evidence-claim mismatch, since MP-5 failure alone (with all others passing), particularly on the mismatch variant not directly taught by either misconception, indicates a diagnosis-under-ambiguity gap requiring one more mixed-flaw worked example.

**Mastery certification trigger**: a correct three-component labeling across five paragraphs; a correctly identified and filled reasoning gap; a correctly identified and revised circular reasoning sentence; a correctly constructed full CER unit passing both tests; a full diagnosis and repair of an unnamed-defect paragraph.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But the evidence obviously supports the claim, why do I need to spell it out?"** — take this as an accurate observation about the connection feeling clear to the writer, not a mistake; respond by adding the reader test: "It's clear to you, that's true — but could a reader draw a DIFFERENT conclusion from this exact evidence? If so, you need the explicit bridge."
- **"I wrote 'this shows that lunch should be longer' — isn't that my reasoning?"** — a genuine, reasonable first attempt at connecting the two; respond by naming the additional layer: "That sentence does follow the evidence, yes — but does it tell us anything NEW, or does it just repeat the claim? Try the cover-up test."
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full diagnosis-and-repair down to **identifying one single missing-reasoning gap in a claim-evidence pair, with circular-reasoning detection and full construction set aside.** Isolates the most basic gap-detection skill before returning to fuller tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just put your evidence right after your claim."** This directly licenses the evidence-is-enough pattern, undermining the explicit-reasoning discipline this concept specifically builds.
