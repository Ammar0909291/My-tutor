## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Hold a full multi-step spoken instruction in mind before beginning to act, rather than acting immediately on the first step heard.
2. Identify sequence markers ("before," "after," "once you have") that reveal the true execution order.
3. Write out the correct execution order of a multi-step instruction, distinct from its mention order.
4. Detect an execution error caused by defaulting to mention order instead of true order.
5. Correctly comprehend and execute (or state) a full novel instruction including at least one reordering marker.

## Core Understanding

A multi-step spoken instruction must be **held complete in mind before any action begins** — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact: acting on the first step the moment it's heard shifts attention away from listening, causing later steps to be forgotten or missed entirely, exactly as dialing a phone number after hearing only the first two digits means acting on incomplete information. The concept's second, equally load-bearing fact is that **the order steps are mentioned in speech does not always match the order they must actually be executed in** — sequence markers like "before," "after," and "once you have" can reorder steps relative to their mention order, so "Before you close the window, turn off the light" requires turning off the light *first*, despite "close the window" being mentioned first. Correctly following spoken instructions therefore requires two separate disciplines working together: complete listening before any action, and active tracking of reordering markers rather than defaulting to mention order as if it were always execution order.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "I start doing the first thing I hear as soon as I hear it, and I assume things happen in the order they're mentioned."**
The runnable simulation: begin executing the first step immediately, and assume any subsequent mentioned steps happen in that same mentioned sequence. This is a reasonable default, since immediate responsiveness often feels like attentive, engaged listening, and mention order does match execution order in the majority of simple, everyday instructions.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — being stopped mid-instruction and asked whether attention was still on listening or had shifted to acting; and being shown "Before you close the window, turn off the light" and asked which action was mentioned first versus which should actually happen first.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Hold the whole instruction in mind before acting — and watch for words like 'before' and 'after' that can flip the actual order."

**Intermediate — "I wait to hear the full instruction before acting, but I still sometimes default to mention order when a reordering word is present."**
The simulation: correctly holds the complete instruction before acting, but doesn't yet reliably catch or apply reordering markers, occasionally executing steps in mention order even when a marker signals otherwise.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — being asked to state the correct execution order for an instruction using "before," and confirming the marker-designated order rather than the mention order.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Mention order and execution order aren't always the same — actively check for 'before,' 'after,' and 'once you have' every time."

**Advanced — "I hold the full instruction in mind, identify reordering markers as they occur, and correctly execute steps in their true order, not their mention order."**
The simulation: comprehends and correctly sequences any novel multi-step instruction, applying both disciplines together under real, unhurried conditions. This is the target model, matching TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: none within the current curriculum — this concept is terminal, with no further KG unlocks.
*Shelf-life warning*: none required; this concept's instruction-following skill is intended to generalize directly to real-world tasks without a further formal teaching step.

**Expert — "at full listening fluency, tracking sequence markers and holding multi-step instructions in mind happens largely automatically, even under real-time pressure and for instructions considerably longer than this concept's own practice length."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit hold-then-reorder routine is a scaffold for a skill that, at true fluency, operates largely below conscious awareness for ordinary-length instructions — named here so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is fluent, automatic sequencing, not a permanently maintained conscious checklist.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **immediate responsiveness genuinely feels like good, attentive listening**, so a learner's instinct to act on the first heard step is not an arbitrary error but a reasonable (if premature) expression of engagement — the cost of this instinct (losing the remaining steps) is invisible to the learner in the moment, since attention has already shifted to the action itself.

The second mechanism is that **mention order matches execution order in the great majority of ordinary, everyday instructions**, making the mention-order default a well-reinforced, usually-successful strategy — the failure surfaces specifically in the minority of instructions deliberately using a reordering marker, which requires active, ongoing attention to notice rather than passive pattern-matching against typical cases.

The third mechanism, specific to this concept's terminal position in the curriculum, is that **without further scaffolded unlocks to reinforce sequence-marker tracking specifically**, a learner exiting with only partial mastery of the reordering half of this concept (per the Blueprint's own S9 failure-routing, the more commonly weaker half) has no built-in downstream concept designed to catch and repair the gap.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-ACTING-IMMEDIATELY-ON-THE-FIRST-INSTRUCTION-HEARD-IS-THE-RIGHT-APPROACH
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the immediate-action/complete-listening discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable instinct that responsive engagement is good listening, extended into acting before the full instruction is even given.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because responsiveness isn't the wrong instinct, only its premature timing, the repair must **redirect the instinct toward holding the complete instruction, not discard responsiveness itself** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this by framing the goal as complete-then-act, not passive-then-act.
- **Verification of death**: given a novel three-step instruction, the learner repeats back all three steps before indicating any action, rather than beginning to act partway through.

### MC-B-INSTRUCTIONS-ARE-ALWAYS-EXECUTED-IN-THE-SAME-ORDER-THEY-ARE-MENTIONED
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-B — includes the "before"-reordered/matching-order discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of a genuinely reliable pattern (mention order usually equals execution order) extended past the cases where a reordering marker explicitly overrides it.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **a concrete, checkable vocabulary of reordering markers** ("before," "after," "once you have") rather than an abstract caution that order "might" differ, since a specific, namable signal is far more actionable than a vague warning.
- **Verification of death**: given a novel instruction using a reordering marker, the learner correctly states the marker-designated execution order, not the mention order.

## Analogies

**Best — the Incomplete-Phone-Number and Movie-Credits-Order Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): dialing a phone number after hearing only the first two digits directly seeds the complete-listening fix; movie credits presented at the end even though the events they represent happened throughout the film directly seeds the mention-order-versus-true-order fix.
*Breaking point*: a phone number's digits have no internal "reordering" logic (they're simply incomplete if cut short), while an instruction's steps genuinely can be reordered by markers, not just cut short — useful for the complete-before-acting intuition specifically, less directly applicable to the reordering fact, which the movie-credits half of the anchor addresses instead.

**Alternative — reading a recipe's ingredient list before starting to cook.** A skilled cook reads the entire recipe first, including any note like "marinate the chicken the night before" — a step that must happen well ahead of the recipe's other, later-mentioned steps — rather than starting with the first listed ingredient and discovering the marinating instruction too late to follow it.
*Breaking point*: a written recipe can be re-read at any time, while a spoken instruction is transient and typically given only once — useful for the read-the-whole-thing-first and reordering-note intuitions, less precise for the real-time, non-repeatable pressure spoken instructions carry that a recipe doesn't.

**Visual anchor — the Incomplete-Phone-Number and Movie-Credits-Order Anchor** as already described above, doubling as both the primary teaching metaphor and the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"The fastest way to follow instructions is to start doing the first thing right away."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated strategy, precisely the premature-action error this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"Just do things in the order you hear them."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated rule, exactly the mention-order-as-execution-order assumption this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Incomplete-Phone-Number and Movie-Credits-Order Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "If you started dialing after just two digits, would you get the right number?" Then extend to instructions and resolve.
2. **The three-step-instruction collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "If you started opening the drawer the instant you heard it, were you still listening to the rest?" Then confirm the missed steps and resolve.
3. **The "before"-reordering collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-B. *Predict first*: "Which action happens first — the one mentioned first, or something else?" Then resolve using the "before" marker.
4. **The full instruction comprehension task (learner activity, TA-4).** *Predict first*: "Do you expect this instruction's execution order to match how it's said?" Then determine and confirm the true order together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (complete-listening necessity; marker-based reordering) is directly checkable through the learner's own attempts at repeating back and sequencing real instructions.

1. **Need**: "If you start acting the moment you hear the first step, can you be sure you'll remember the rest?"
2. **Playground**: the learner listens to several multi-step instructions, freely noting how many steps they retain when acting immediately versus waiting to hear the whole thing.
3. **Invention**: "For this instruction, does the word 'before' change which step happens first?" The learner tests the marker's effect directly.
4. **Collision**: present an instruction using "before" and ask the learner to state the mention order and the true order side by side.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — hold the full instruction before acting, and track reordering markers rather than assuming mention order — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Hear it all first — and watch for 'before,' 'after,' and 'once you have.'"

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Listening to the full instruction before acting (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting MC-A before any marker-tracking content is introduced, since the discipline of holding a complete instruction must be secure before the harder judgment of determining its true internal order can be meaningfully practiced.
- **Recognizing sequence markers (TA-2) comes second**, directly confronting MC-B once the complete-listening habit (TA-1) is in place, introducing the specific vocabulary of reordering words.
- **Determining true execution order (TA-3) comes third**, extending TA-2's marker-recognition into the generative task of writing out a full, correctly-reordered sequence from a multi-step instruction.
- **The full application (TA-4) comes last**, requiring the learner to integrate complete-listening (TA-1) and marker-based reordering (TA-2/TA-3) simultaneously on a genuinely novel instruction — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the mandatory pause-and-repeat-back checkpoint for learners who lose track on longer sequences, the mention-order-then-renumber scaffold, and the transfer parallel with `eng.listening.listening-for-detail`'s own small-but-meaning-changing-word attention).

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **audio-based and inherently a spoken-listening task** — the tutor presents a spoken instruction and the learner's response is a spoken repeat-back or stated execution order, making this concept as directly audio-dependent as its prerequisite, `eng.listening.listening-for-detail`.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A complete, accurate repeat-back of all steps before any indication of acting** — the direct target evidence for MC-A's repair.
- **A stated marker-based reasoning accompanying a correct execution-order statement** ("it says 'before,' so the light goes off first, even though it's mentioned second") — the direct behavioral signature of genuine marker tracking rather than a lucky guess.
- **Confident statement of execution order matching mention order on an instruction that actually contains a reordering marker, with no reference to the marker at all** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-B still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Hear the whole thing first — and watch for the word that tells you the real order."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"I already started doing the first thing, I forgot there was more"** — take this as an honest, common report, not evidence of carelessness; respond by naming the pattern directly: "That's exactly the habit we're working on — the moment you start acting, your attention shifts away from listening. Let's try again, holding off on any action until you've heard everything."
- **"It said 'close the window' first, so I did that first"** — a genuine, reasonable inference from mention order; respond by pointing to the actual marker: "That's the order it was mentioned in — but look at the word right before it: 'before.' That word is telling you something different actually has to happen first."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full four-step instruction with a reordering marker, down to **a two-step instruction with a single, isolated reordering marker and no additional complexity at all.** This removes the multi-step tracking demand almost entirely, isolating the most basic marker-recognition skill before returning to harder, longer instructions.
- **Never shrink to "just do whatever you remember hearing."** This directly licenses both misconceptions at once (partial listening, and unreflective ordering), undermining the complete-listening-plus-marker-tracking discipline this concept specifically builds.

## Memory Hooks

- **Concept type**: sequential, real-time listening skill (complete-instruction retention, marker-based reordering) requiring genuine working-memory discipline under time pressure.
- **Review form**: spaced items always mixing straightforward mention-order-matches-execution-order instructions with reordering-marker instructions in the same review cycle, since presenting only reordering cases would over-alert the learner to expect a trick every time, while presenting only matching cases would never exercise the actual discrimination this concept teaches.
- **Concept-specific deviation**: because this concept is terminal (no further KG unlocks), review should track long-term retention of the marker-tracking half specifically, rather than relying on a downstream concept to naturally re-exercise it, as most non-terminal concepts in this program can.
