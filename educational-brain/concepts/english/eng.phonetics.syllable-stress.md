# Word Stress — `eng.phonetics.syllable-stress`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.phonetics.syllable-stress` (canonical English
  KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / phonetics / "Word Stress" — the
  concept that extends IPA notation into suprasegmental territory
  (properties spanning more than one segment/phoneme), teaching that
  English words carry a stress pattern entirely unmarked by spelling.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.phonetics.ipa-basics` — specifically fluent
  transcription; load-bearing because TA-2 requires the learner to add
  a stress mark directly onto an already-produced IPA transcription,
  which presupposes the transcription itself is already a secure, non-
  effortful skill.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.phonetics.sentence-stress`. Word-level
  stress is the atomic unit sentence-level stress patterns are built
  from — a learner cannot reason about which WORDS receive prominence
  in a sentence without first being able to identify which SYLLABLE
  within any given word carries its stress.
- **Difficulty**: developing · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.75 · **Est. hours**: 2

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify the stressed syllable in a two-syllable word by ear, using
   length, pitch change, and vowel clarity together, not volume alone.
2. Mark primary stress on an IPA transcription using the stress
   symbol.
3. Identify unstressed syllables whose vowel has reduced to schwa,
   recognizing vowel-quality change as a signal of unstressed status.
4. Correctly produce and explain the noun/verb stress-shift pattern for
   words like "record" and "present."
5. Identify primary stress in longer or genuinely novel multisyllabic
   words, including cases where stress shifts as suffixes are added.

## Core Understanding

English word stress is a bundle of at least three co-occurring acoustic
cues — increased syllable LENGTH, a PITCH change, and retained full
VOWEL QUALITY — none of which is, by itself, simple loudness. This
bundling is the single most consequential fact this concept must
install, because volume alone is both an unreliable and an unnatural-
sounding way to mark stress in real speech; a stressed syllable that is
merely shouted, without the accompanying length/pitch/vowel-quality
cues, sounds robotic rather than like ordinary English. A second,
equally load-bearing fact is that English spelling never marks stress
at all — there is no accent mark, no visual cue whatsoever — so stress
placement must be learned by ear or by reference (dictionary,
transcription) rather than read off a word's letters. This absence of
a spelling cue does not mean stress is entirely random, however: a
genuine, bounded, learnable pattern exists for many two-syllable
words that shift stress depending on grammatical role — noun uses
typically stress the first syllable, verb uses the second (REcord the
noun vs. reCORD the verb) — a real foothold within an otherwise largely
unpredictable system.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "stress means saying a syllable louder"**: the
  arriving, pre-instructional (and incorrect) model this concept exists
  to correct — a natural first-guess intuition about "emphasis" that
  works reasonably in some isolated, exaggerated contexts but fails to
  capture how stress actually functions in fluent, natural speech.
- **Intermediate model — "stress is a bundle of length, pitch, and
  vowel clarity together, which I can feel and hear"**: installed by
  the Component 3 stress-clap-and-stretch anchor and TA-1's two-
  syllable identification practice, resolving MC-STRESS-IS-JUST-
  LOUDNESS directly through the physical demonstration of an unnaturally
  all-loud rendition versus a natural one. Upgrade trigger: correctly
  identifying stress in a word produced at normal (not exaggerated)
  volume, relying on length/pitch/vowel-clarity cues rather than a
  loudness spike.
- **Advanced model — "unstressed syllables often reduce to schwa, and
  this reduction is itself a reliable marker of stress location"**:
  installed by TA-3, per the Blueprint's own explicit Adaptive Flag
  naming schwa recognition as "the single most reliable, concrete
  signal students can learn to listen for" — schwa detection becomes
  the learner's primary practical tool for locating stress, more
  tractable than subtle pitch or length differences alone.
- **Expert model — "stress is unwritten but not random — the noun/verb
  shift is a real, bounded pattern, and I can apply stress-identification
  to genuinely novel and longer words"**: installed by TA-4 (the noun/
  verb shift pattern, resolving MC-STRESS-PATTERN-IS-FIXED-BY-SPELLING)
  and TA-5 (extension to longer, novel words, including cases where
  stress shifts as suffixes are added — photograph/photography/
  photographic).
- **Do not upgrade early**: a learner not yet reliably identifying
  stress on straightforward two-syllable words by ear (TA-1) should not
  be given TA-4's noun/verb shift pairs — the shift pattern is a
  refinement layered on top of already-secure basic stress perception,
  not a substitute path to acquiring it.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here. The loudness-only
failure exists because volume genuinely IS a component of how emphasis
works in many everyday, exaggerated contexts (calling someone's name
loudly, for instance), so a learner reasonably reaches for the most
familiar, single-dimension cue available rather than the more subtle,
multi-cue bundle that actually constitutes stress in ordinary fluent
speech — this is an availability/simplicity bias toward the most
obvious candidate cue, not a wrongly-taught rule. The spelling-
expectation failure exists because every other phonics/phonetics
concept in this curriculum up to this point has ultimately been
groundable in SOME visual or spelling-adjacent cue, however imperfect
(letters, digraphs, silent-e patterns) — stress is the first genuinely
"invisible in print" property the learner meets, and the reasonable
prior expectation that "there must be SOME spelling signal, even an
imperfect one" fails completely here, producing either an incorrect
search for a nonexistent spelling rule or, in overcorrection, a belief
that stress is therefore entirely unpatterned.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.phonetics.syllable-stress.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-STRESS-IS-JUST-LOUDNESS (Blueprint Component 1) — Type 2,
perceptual intuition**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: this is a genuine perceptual default
  rather than an overextended rule — loudness is the single most
  immediately available, non-technical dimension of vocal emphasis a
  learner has direct, pre-instructional access to, so it is the natural
  first hypothesis when asked to identify "emphasis" in a word,
  independent of any specific prior teaching that might have suggested
  it.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, on hearing the
  natural (non-shouted) pronunciation of "banana" contrasted with an
  all-loud version, says something like "the second one sounds weird,
  like a robot" (rather than needing this described to them) is showing
  the conflict-evidence probe working exactly as intended — this
  spontaneous "unnatural" judgment is itself strong evidence the
  multi-cue model is beginning to form.

**MC-STRESS-PATTERN-IS-FIXED-BY-SPELLING (Blueprint Component 1) — Type
3, language contamination**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: this is contamination from the learner's
  entire prior literacy experience, where EVERY other phonological
  property met so far (sounds, syllable types, even the general
  reliability of spelling-sound correspondence) has had at least some
  imperfect visual grounding — the reasonable but incorrect expectation
  that stress must too is an intrusion of "spelling generally signals
  pronunciation features" into the one specific domain (word stress)
  where English orthography provides absolutely no cue whatsoever,
  distinct from a within-domain overgeneralized RULE.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, on meeting the
  record/reCORD contrast, responds with genuine surprise ("same
  spelling but totally different?!") rather than a flat acceptance is
  showing the conflict is landing as intended — this specific surprise
  reaction is a stronger, more diagnostic signal than a calm, unfazed
  acceptance of the same fact, since it indicates the prior spelling-
  grounds-everything expectation was genuinely held, not merely
  assumed by the tutor.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the stress-clap-and-stretch anchor itself is the
  analogy, not a supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): clapping
  louder AND holding the syllable longer, physically felt and echoed,
  directly instantiates the length+emphasis bundle this concept
  teaches. Breaking point: clapping is a discrete, percussive action,
  which may understate that pitch change (a continuous, melodic
  property) is also part of the bundle — this entry supplements the
  clap-and-stretch anchor with explicit attention to pitch during TA-1,
  not relying on the clap alone to convey all three cues.
- **Alternative — a spotlight moving across a stage, not a switch
  turning fully on or off**: stress "shines" more brightly on one
  syllable (via length/pitch/vowel-clarity together) without other
  syllables going completely dark (silent) — useful for conveying that
  unstressed syllables are reduced, not absent. Breaking point: a
  spotlight is purely visual/binary in a way that may understate the
  specific, listed acoustic cues (length, pitch, vowel quality) this
  concept requires the learner to actually check.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe stress as "the syllable you say
  loudest"**: this framing directly installs MC-STRESS-IS-JUST-
  LOUDNESS by encoding volume as the definitional criterion rather than
  one component of a bundle. Stress should always be introduced via the
  Blueprint's own bundled framing (length + pitch + vowel clarity),
  never reduced to a single-word "loud" synonym.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/auditory (stress-clap-and-stretch)**: the Blueprint's
  Component 3 anchor, including its deliberate wrong-stress
  demonstration ("taBLE") — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: clapping and stretching syllables requires
  nothing beyond the learner's own hands and voice.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-4's noun/verb pair production
  (saying the same word both ways) is itself a hands-on, generative
  contrastive activity.
- **Teacher-demo (all-loud vs. natural contrast)**: the tutor models
  the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstration — shouting the
  stressed syllable versus producing it naturally with length/pitch —
  making the loudness-alone inadequacy audible and concrete.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming stress
  placement (TA-1) or a noun/verb shift (TA-4), ask the learner to
  predict from feel/pattern before the tutor confirms — Universal
  Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions; the formal
IPA stress-marking convention itself is correctly direct instruction.**
The loudness-inadequacy insight and the noun/verb shift pattern are
both genuine, directly-testable phenomena well-suited to felt
discovery: **need** (the learner, assuming loudness is sufficient, is
asked to compare an all-loud rendition against a natural one; or,
assuming spelling should show stress, is asked to explain the record/
reCORD contrast); **playground/invention** (the clap-and-stretch
technique applied to several words; the noun/verb pair set tried
across TA-4's several examples); **collision** (the unnatural-sounding
all-loud rendition; the identical-spelling-different-stress fact);
**formalization** (MP-5's own-words statement of the multi-cue bundle
and the noun/verb pattern); **compression** (TA-5's fluent stress
identification on longer, novel words without re-deriving either
insight each time). The IPA stress-marking notation itself (TA-2 — where
exactly the stress symbol goes in a transcription) is a pure convention
best introduced by direct instruction, since there is no regularity to
discover in the symbol's form, only its placement rule to apply.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (two-syllable stress identification) must precede TA-2 (stress
marking in IPA) because the learner must first reliably PERCEIVE stress
by ear before attaching a written symbol to it — marking a stress
position the learner cannot yet independently identify would produce
copying rather than genuine notation. TA-3 (schwa recognition) is
placed after TA-2, extending the multi-cue model with its single most
concrete, teachable component (vowel reduction), directly targeting
the loudness-only misconception with a more reliable alternative signal
than pitch or length alone. TA-4 (noun/verb shift) is placed after the
basic identification skill and the schwa marker are both secure,
since recognizing a STRESS SHIFT between two pronunciations of the same
spelling requires the learner to already reliably identify stress in
either pronunciation individually. TA-5 (longer/novel words) is placed
last as the full generalization check, extending the skill to genuinely
unpracticed and structurally more complex material, including cases
where stress itself shifts as a word's morphology changes. This entry
does not restate the Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script
(Component 7) — see Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: this concept is, more than most in this
  domain, dependent on genuine audio evidence — length, pitch, and
  vowel quality are all properties of the learner's actual spoken
  production, not merely a correct eventual answer. Listen specifically
  for whether the learner's OWN production of a stressed syllable
  includes length and pitch change, not merely volume, and whether
  unstressed vowels are genuinely reduced toward schwa rather than kept
  full.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a pause before producing a noun/verb
  stress-shift pair, followed by a correct contrastive production, is a
  good sign — it suggests the pattern is being consciously applied
  rather than guessed; a fast, confident production using the SAME
  stress for both grammatical roles is the concerning pattern.
- **Pronunciation stakes**: real and central, though genuinely dialect-
  sensitive for some specific words (a small number of words vary in
  which syllable carries primary stress across English varieties) — the
  STRUCTURAL skill (identifying that stress exists, locating it via the
  multi-cue bundle, recognizing schwa reduction) is what must be
  correct, judged against the learner's own natural dialect rather than
  a single reference variety.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "listen for length, pitch, and a clear
  vowel — not just loudness" is this concept's load-bearing sentence,
  and per the foundations' own note on the platform's real voice-channel
  limitations, this concept is one of the places where live, attentive
  human/tutor listening for pitch and length matters more than most,
  since these are subtler acoustic properties than simple correctness,
  per `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` and its
  §7 channel-reality note.

## Assessment Signals

- **Diagnostic (golden probe)**: the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence
  probe (Component 1) — comparing an all-loud "banana" rendition
  against a natural one and asking which sounds like real English —
  cleanly separates a learner still relying on loudness alone from one
  beginning to notice the fuller cue bundle.
- **Guided practice → independent practice**: the Blueprint's WE-1 → WE-2
  → WE-3 → MP-1 through MP-5 fading ladder is reused directly
  (Components 5–6) — not re-authored here.
- **Mastery gate set** (`../../assessment/05-mastery-verification.md
  §3`): production (fresh two-syllable stress identification, MP-1);
  notation (MP-2, marking stress in a fresh IPA transcription); schwa
  identification (MP-3); noun/verb shift (MP-4 — a strong diagnostic
  item, since correct production of BOTH grammatical roles for the same
  spelling demonstrates the shift pattern is genuinely internalized,
  not merely one memorized pronunciation); explanation (MP-5, requiring
  articulation of both the multi-cue bundle and the spelling-
  independence/pattern facts together).
- **Interpretation of response pattern**: fast-and-correct stress
  identification on a genuinely novel multisyllabic word, using
  length/pitch/vowel-clarity cues (audibly, not merely volume) confirms
  the multi-cue model is internalized. Fast-and-WRONG identification
  based purely on which syllable was said loudest is the dangerous
  quadrant for MC-STRESS-IS-JUST-LOUDNESS, warranting a return to the
  clap-and-stretch demonstration rather than more identification
  drilling at a single cue. On noun/verb pairs, producing only one
  stress pattern regardless of the sentence's grammatical context is
  the dangerous quadrant for MC-STRESS-PATTERN-IS-FIXED-BY-SPELLING.
- **Transfer items**: near (a fresh two-syllable word's stress
  identified); far (a longer, multi-suffix word set like photograph/
  photography/photographic, testing whether stress-shift awareness
  generalizes beyond the noun/verb case specifically); real-world
  (identifying stress in the learner's own name or a word from a
  conversation, and noticing any noun/verb pairs encountered in
  everyday speech).

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know"** on a stress-identification task shrinks to the
  Component 3 clap-and-stretch anchor using the most familiar word
  available (typically "table," the first taught), with the tutor
  modeling the correct-vs-wrong contrast first.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: an all-loud, unnatural-
  sounding attempt (rather than "I don't know") is more likely, per
  MC-STRESS-IS-JUST-LOUDNESS's mechanism — route directly to that
  misconception's recovery (the natural-vs-shouted contrast) rather
  than treating it as a generic pronunciation gap. On a noun/verb
  question, using the same stress for both roles is more likely than
  hesitation — redirect explicitly to the record/reCORD conflict-
  evidence probe.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who visibly
  slows down and stretches a syllable before answering (rather than
  answering immediately) is showing the correct length-attentive
  behavior even when the eventual identification needs correction —
  this should be named and praised explicitly.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: fact/procedure hybrid — the stress pattern of a
specific, already-taught word is a fact (recalled per word, since
stress placement is genuinely not derivable from spelling and must be
memorized or referenced per item), while the multi-cue LISTENING
procedure (checking length, pitch, and vowel quality together) and the
noun/verb shift PATTERN are both procedures reviewed through continued
application to novel words. Interleaving partners: mix words with
straightforward, single fixed stress against noun/verb shift-pair words
within the same practice set, and periodically revisit schwa
identification alongside stress location, since the two skills are
mutually reinforcing (finding schwa often locates the unstressed
syllable, and vice versa).

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: identifying stress in fresh two-syllable and longer words.
- **Far**: this concept's word-level stress skill is the direct
  atomic-unit foundation for `eng.phonetics.sentence-stress`, where the
  same length/pitch/vowel-clarity cues operate at the level of which
  WORDS (not merely which syllables) receive prominence within a full
  sentence.
- **Real-world**: any multisyllabic word the learner encounters in
  speech, reading aloud, or conversation is immediately describable and
  practicable using this concept's framework; the noun/verb shift
  pattern is directly and frequently useful in ordinary spoken English.
- **Expert-transfer**: eventual fluent, natural-sounding spoken English
  prosody (well beyond this node) depends on stress placement becoming
  fully automatic rather than consciously checked.
