# Vowel Phoneme Classification — `eng.phonetics.vowel-sounds`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.phonetics.vowel-sounds` (canonical English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / phonetics / "Vowel Phoneme
  Classification" — the sibling branch to `eng.phonetics.consonant-
  sounds`, both descending directly from articulation-organs, applying
  the same physical-description discipline to the structurally
  different vowel system.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.phonetics.articulation-organs` —
  specifically the physical articulator vocabulary and the general
  principle that speech sounds are describable by physical tongue
  configuration; load-bearing because this concept's entire opening
  misconception (vowels have no describable place) is the direct
  overreach of a lesson genuinely learned FROM consonants — that
  "place" for consonants means a point of closure or blockage — applied
  incorrectly to a sound class that has no closure at all.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.phonetics.ipa-basics`, jointly with its
  sibling `eng.phonetics.consonant-sounds`. IPA vowel symbols are
  organized by exactly the height/backness/rounding dimensions this
  concept installs, paralleling how IPA consonant symbols are organized
  by place/manner/voicing.
- **Difficulty**: developing · **Bloom**: understand · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.75 · **Est. hours**: 3

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Classify a vowel by tongue height (high, mid, low) using felt tongue
   position.
2. Classify a vowel by tongue backness (front, central, back).
3. Classify a vowel by lip rounding (rounded vs. unrounded/spread).
4. Distinguish a monophthong (steady tongue position throughout) from a
   diphthong (tongue gliding from one position to another) using the
   slow-stretch technique.
5. Produce a complete classification (height + backness + rounding) for
   a monophthong, or a start-point/end-point description for a
   diphthong, without scaffolding.

## Core Understanding

Vowels are just as physically describable as consonants, but along a
genuinely different set of dimensions, because vowels are defined by
the ABSENCE of the closure or narrow constriction that defines
consonant place — there is no single "spot" to touch or point to for a
vowel the way there is for a consonant's place of articulation. Instead,
vowel quality is determined by the overall SHAPE of the oral cavity,
captured by three dimensions: tongue HEIGHT (how close the tongue body
is to the roof of the mouth), tongue BACKNESS (how far forward or back
in the mouth the tongue body sits), and lip ROUNDING (whether the lips
are rounded or spread). A second, independent structural fact this
concept must install is that not every vowel sound is a single, fixed
tongue position held for its whole duration — a MONOPHTHONG is; a
DIPHTHONG is a single vowel sound in which the tongue audibly glides
from a starting position toward a different ending position within one
uninterrupted syllable nucleus, and English has both types in everyday
use (the vowel in "see" is a monophthong; the vowel in "time" is a
diphthong gliding from a low-central start toward a high-front end).

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "vowels are just open-mouth noises with no
  specific tongue position, unlike consonants"**: this is the arriving,
  pre-instructional (and incorrect) model this concept exists to
  correct — carried over directly from the prerequisite concept's own
  correct teaching that consonant PLACE means a point of closure, over-
  applied here to conclude that a sound WITHOUT closure has no place at
  all. This concept does not build this model from scratch; it opens by
  directly challenging it.
- **Intermediate model — "I can feel and classify my own tongue's
  height, backness, and rounding for a given vowel"**: installed by
  TA-1 through TA-3, systematically isolating each dimension using
  contrastive pairs, exactly paralleling the sibling consonant-sounds
  concept's one-dimension-at-a-time structure. Upgrade trigger:
  correctly identifying a felt height or backness difference between
  two vowels without needing the tutor to confirm first.
- **Advanced model — "some vowels are a single held position
  (monophthongs) and some are a glide between two positions
  (diphthongs), and I can tell which by stretching the sound out
  slowly"**: installed by TA-4's slow-stretch technique, resolving
  MC-MONOPHTHONGS-AND-DIPHTHONGS-ARE-THE-SAME. Upgrade trigger: correct
  classification of a genuinely novel word's vowel as monophthong or
  diphthong using the stretch technique, not by recalling a previously
  classified example.
- **Expert model — "I can produce a complete three-dimension
  description for any monophthong, or a start/end description for any
  diphthong, without scaffolding"**: installed by TA-5, the full
  integration step, directly paralleling the consonant-sounds concept's
  own three-dimension integration for its final TA.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own explicit sequencing
  (corner vowels before subtle contrasts), a learner not yet secure
  distinguishing the four extreme "corner" vowels (high-front, low-
  front, high-back, low-back) should not be given closer mid-vowel
  contrasts (e.g., /ɛ/ vs. /æ/) — the corner vowels are deliberately
  easiest to feel distinctly and must anchor the system before finer,
  more continuous distinctions are attempted.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here. The no-place failure
exists precisely because the prerequisite concept correctly taught that
consonant "place" is a locatable point of closure or narrowing — a
learner who has genuinely internalized that lesson reasonably (though
incorrectly) concludes that a sound class defined by the ABSENCE of
closure (vowels) must therefore have no place at all, rather than
recognizing that vowels are described by a different kind of physical
configuration (overall tongue-body position) rather than by no physical
description whatsoever. The monophthong/diphthong conflation failure
exists because vowel duration and internal gliding are genuinely subtle
to an untrained ear — both vowel types simply sound like "one vowel
sound" in ordinary, natural-speed speech, and only the deliberate,
artificial technique of stretching the sound out slowly reveals the
internal movement that distinguishes them; without that specific
technique, there is no naturally-occurring cue that would prompt a
learner to suspect two categories exist at all.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.phonetics.vowel-sounds.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-VOWELS-HAVE-NO-ARTICULATION-PLACE (Blueprint Component 1) — Type 1,
overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — "place means a
  point of closure" is genuinely correct and thoroughly reinforced for
  consonants in the immediately-preceding concept, and the learner
  extends that specific definition of "place" (closure-based) into a
  domain (vowels) where the very absence of closure is the defining
  feature, concluding — reasonably but incorrectly — that no closure
  means no place at all, rather than a different KIND of place
  (overall tongue-body configuration).
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, on first being
  asked to compare tongue position for "ee" and "ah," responds with
  genuine surprise ("oh, my tongue actually IS somewhere different!")
  is showing the conflict-evidence probe working exactly as intended —
  this discovery-flavored surprise is itself the strongest available
  evidence the misconception is resolving, more informative than a
  flat, unsurprised acceptance of the same fact.

**MC-MONOPHTHONGS-AND-DIPHTHONGS-ARE-THE-SAME (Blueprint Component 1) —
Type 2, perceptual intuition**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: this is a genuine perceptual limitation
  rather than an overextended rule — at natural speech speed, the
  internal glide of a diphthong is simply not perceptually salient
  without a specific slowing technique, so the learner's flat
  classification of all vowels as one undifferentiated category is a
  reasonable response to the actual available evidence at normal
  speed, not a wrongly-applied rule from elsewhere.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, mid-stretch,
  spontaneously says "wait, it's changing!" while sustaining a
  diphthong is demonstrating the exact intended conflict-evidence
  moment — this real-time noticing, captured live during the stretch
  technique itself, is stronger evidence of resolution than a later,
  reflective correct answer to a classification question.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the vowel space map itself is the analogy, not a
  supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): the physical vowel
  quadrilateral, with corner vowels physically felt at each extreme, IS
  the concept's primary explanatory device for height and backness
  together. Breaking point: the map's discrete corners may suggest
  vowels occupy only four fixed points, when the space between corners
  is genuinely continuous (this is explicitly why "vowel differences
  feel subtle/subjective," per the Blueprint's own S6 protocol routing)
  — the map should be introduced as a simplification tool for a
  continuous space, not a claim that only four vowel positions exist.
- **Alternative — a dimmer switch, sliding smoothly rather than an
  on/off switch**: vowel height and backness are continuous, gradient
  properties (unlike many consonant contrasts, which are more sharply
  categorical) — reinforces why vowel distinctions can feel harder to
  pin down than consonant ones. Breaking point: should not be over-
  extended to suggest vowel categories are arbitrary or unlearnable;
  the gradient nature does not prevent reliable classification, it only
  explains why finer contrasts require more careful listening.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe vowels as "just how open your mouth
  is"**: mouth-openness alone conflates height with the OVERALL
  impression of a vowel and omits backness and rounding entirely — this
  oversimplification would leave a learner unable to distinguish, for
  instance, two vowels of similar height but different backness (e.g.
  /iː/ vs. /uː/, both high, but front vs. back and unrounded vs.
  rounded). Openness alone is a starting intuition, not the full
  three-dimension system this concept requires.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/kinesthetic (vowel space map, corner-to-corner)**: the
  Blueprint's Component 3 anchor — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: every classification test (height, backness,
  rounding, slow-stretch) requires nothing beyond the learner's own
  mouth and, optionally, a mirror for the rounding check.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-4's monophthong/diphthong sort
  and TA-5's full classification are both hands-on, felt-sensation
  activities requiring the learner to test and report.
- **Teacher-demo (slow-stretch contrast)**: the tutor models stretching
  a monophthong ("see") and a diphthong ("say") side by side at the
  same slowed pace, explicitly narrating that one holds steady while
  the other visibly moves — directly instantiating the Blueprint's own
  conflict-evidence probe.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  height, backness, or rounding judgment (TA-1 through TA-3), ask the
  learner to predict from feel alone before the tutor confirms —
  Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits the vowel-has-a-place insight and
the monophthong/diphthong distinction; direct instruction is correct for
the formal three-dimension vocabulary itself.** The insight that vowels
DO have a physical, felt configuration (countering the arriving no-
place assumption) and the discovery that some vowels glide are both
genuine, directly-testable physical facts well-suited to felt discovery:
**need** (the learner, having just secured consonant place, assumes
vowels have none, and is asked to compare tongue position for "ee" and
"ah" directly); **playground/invention** (the vowel-space corner-to-
corner tour lets the learner feel several distinct positions and notice
a pattern); **collision** (the slow-stretch technique applied to a
diphthong directly breaks the assumption that all vowels are static);
**formalization** (MP-5's full classification, naming height/backness/
rounding explicitly); **compression** (TA-5's fluent, unscaffolded
classification of novel vowels). The formal TERMS themselves (height,
backness, rounding, monophthong, diphthong) are convention-bound
vocabulary best introduced by direct instruction once the underlying
physical facts have already been felt.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (height) must precede TA-2 (backness), which must precede TA-3
(rounding), following the Blueprint's own explicit corner-vowels-first
sequencing — each dimension is isolated one at a time using the most
extreme, easiest-to-feel contrasts before finer distinctions are
attempted, directly paralleling the consonant-sounds concept's own
one-dimension-at-a-time structure. TA-4 (monophthong/diphthong
discrimination) is placed after all three static dimensions are secure,
because the slow-stretch technique itself requires the learner to
already be attentive to tongue position throughout a sound's duration —
a skill built by TA-1 through TA-3's felt-position practice. TA-5 (full
classification) is placed last as the integration step, requiring
height, backness, rounding, AND the monophthong/diphthong distinction
all operating together. This entry does not restate the Blueprint's own
turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see Blueprint References
below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner physically slows down and
  stretches a vowel sound when asked to classify it as monophthong or
  diphthong, versus answering at natural speed from impression alone —
  the stretch behavior itself is the diagnostic instrument, not merely
  its eventual correct answer.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a genuine pause mid-stretch,
  accompanied by a felt "it's moving" realization, is the strongest
  positive sign for the monophthong/diphthong distinction; a fast,
  confident classification with no attempt at stretching is the
  concerning pattern, regardless of whether it happens to be correct.
- **Pronunciation stakes**: real but must be handled carefully — English
  vowel systems vary substantially by dialect (the Blueprint's own S9
  routing explicitly anticipates this), so height/backness/rounding
  judgments should be made against the LEARNER's own natural
  pronunciation, never against a single reference accent; the
  monophthong/diphthong STATUS of a given word's vowel can genuinely
  differ by dialect and should be treated as a legitimate point of
  variation, not an error.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "stretch it out slowly — does your
  tongue stay still, or does it move?" is this concept's load-bearing
  sentence, directly reused from the Blueprint's own diphthong conflict-
  evidence probe, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3`.

## Assessment Signals

- **Diagnostic (golden probe)**: the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence
  probe (Component 1) — comparing tongue position for "ee" and "ah" —
  cleanly separates a learner still assuming vowels have no place from
  one who is beginning to notice a felt difference.
- **Guided practice → independent practice**: the Blueprint's WE-1 → WE-2
  → WE-3 → MP-1 through MP-5 fading ladder is reused directly
  (Components 5–6) — not re-authored here.
- **Mastery gate set** (`../../assessment/05-mastery-verification.md
  §3`): production (fresh height classification, MP-1); backness
  (MP-2); rounding (MP-3); monophthong/diphthong discrimination (MP-4 —
  the concept's single most diagnostic item, since it tests the
  genuinely subtle distinction via the stretch technique on a novel
  word rather than a memorized example); full classification (MP-5).
- **Interpretation of response pattern**: fast-and-correct
  classification of a novel monophthong/diphthong via visible stretching
  confirms the technique is internalized; fast-and-WRONG classification
  with NO stretching attempted is the dangerous quadrant — it signals
  the learner is guessing from impression rather than applying the
  actual diagnostic method, and warrants returning to TA-4's stretch-
  technique modeling rather than more classification drilling at
  natural speed.
- **Transfer items**: near (a fresh vowel's height/backness/rounding);
  far (a vowel from the learner's own L1, if applicable, classified
  using the same three-dimension framework — testing whether the system
  generalizes beyond English); real-world (classifying the vowel sounds
  in the learner's own name or a favorite word).

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know"** on a classification task shrinks to the Component 3
  vowel-space-map corner-to-corner tour, re-run with the tutor modeling
  the physical tongue-travel first, on the most extreme (easiest to
  feel) corner-vowel contrast available.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: a confident "vowels
  don't really have a specific spot" response is more likely than "I
  don't know," per MC-VOWELS-HAVE-NO-ARTICULATION-PLACE's mechanism —
  route directly to that misconception's recovery (the ee/ah tongue-
  position comparison) rather than treating it as a generic phonetics
  gap. On monophthong/diphthong tasks, a fast, unstretched guess is more
  likely than genuine uncertainty — redirect explicitly to the slow-
  stretch technique rather than confirming or denying the guess.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who
  spontaneously slows down and stretches a vowel before answering,
  without being prompted, is showing the correct diagnostic behavior
  has already generalized — this should be named and praised explicitly
  as exactly the target habit.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: concept/procedure hybrid — the height/backness/
rounding classification is a felt-fact system reviewed through
continued application to novel vowels (not simple recall, since the
underlying tongue positions must be re-felt each time), while the
monophthong/diphthong slow-stretch technique is a procedure reviewed
through ongoing application, particularly to dialect-varying or subtle
cases. Interleaving partners: mix corner vowels with closer, more
subtle mid-vowel contrasts within ongoing review (never block practice
by "easy" vs. "hard" vowels), and mix monophthongs with diphthongs
within the same practice set so the learner cannot predict the category
from context or position alone.
