# Gerunds and Infinitives — `eng.grammar.gerunds-and-infinitives`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.grammar.gerunds-and-infinitives` (canonical
  English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / grammar / "Gerunds and
  Infinitives" — the -ing verb form used as a noun (gerund) and the
  "to + base verb" form (infinitive), and the verbs that govern each.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.grammar.present-tenses` — load-bearing
  because gerunds are spelled IDENTICALLY to the -ing form used in
  present continuous verb tense; a learner cannot distinguish a gerund
  from a present-continuous verb form by function (noun role vs. verb
  role) without already having the present continuous form itself
  fully secure as a comparison point.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.grammar.participles-and-participial-
  phrases`. That downstream concept formally extends the same -ing
  (and -ed) verb-form-functioning-as-something-else pattern this
  concept establishes for gerunds (noun role) to participles
  (adjective role) — both require the identical function-over-spelling
  discrimination skill this concept installs first.
- **Difficulty**: proficient · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery threshold**:
  0.75 · **Est. hours**: 2

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify gerunds functioning as subjects and objects, correctly
   distinguishing them from the identically-spelled present-continuous
   -ing verb form.
2. Identify infinitives functioning as subjects, objects, and purpose
   expressions.
3. Produce the correct following form (gerund or infinitive) for common
   gerund-only verbs (enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest) and
   infinitive-only verbs (want, decide, hope, plan, agree).
4. Produce either form correctly for verbs that accept both with little
   meaning difference (like, start, begin, continue).
5. Distinguish the genuine meaning difference carried by gerund vs.
   infinitive after meaning-changing verbs (stop, remember, forget,
   try).

## Core Understanding

A gerund is the -ing form of a verb functioning as a NOUN (subject,
object, or complement) — and this is the concept's single most load-
bearing insight: a gerund is spelled IDENTICALLY to the -ing form used
in present continuous verb tense, but it plays a completely different
grammatical ROLE. In "Swimming is fun," "swimming" is the subject — a
noun's job. In "I am swimming," "am swimming" is the verb phrase
itself. The reliable test is always the word's JOB in that specific
sentence, never its spelling. An infinitive is the "to + base verb"
form, functioning as a subject, object, or expression of purpose ("To
exercise is important"; "I want to exercise"; "I exercise to stay
healthy"). A second, independent, equally load-bearing insight concerns
which form follows a given verb: specific verbs GOVERN whether a gerund
or infinitive follows — this is NOT freely interchangeable and must be
learned per verb, similar to preposition collocations. Some verbs
require ONLY a gerund (enjoy, avoid, finish, mind — "I enjoy swimming,"
never "to swim"); some require ONLY an infinitive (want, decide, hope,
plan — "I want to swim," never "swimming"); some accept EITHER with
little meaning difference (like, start, begin); and a small set of
verbs (stop, remember, forget, try) genuinely CHANGE MEANING depending
on which form follows ("I stopped smoking" = quit the habit; "I
stopped to smoke" = paused another activity in order to smoke).

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "any -ing word is part of the verb tense, and
  gerunds and infinitives are always freely interchangeable after any
  verb"**: the arriving, pre-instructional model this concept exists to
  correct — a natural default because the -ing form is most commonly
  first met in present continuous tense, making its noun-role use feel
  like an unexpected exception, and because both forms genuinely
  describe similar ideas (an activity), making interchangeability a
  reasonable first guess.
- **Intermediate model — "I identify gerunds by checking whether an
  -ing word is doing a noun's job or a verb's job"**: installed by
  TA-1, using the Component 3 noun-job-vs-verb-job sort anchor,
  resolving MC-GERUND-IS-JUST-PRESENT-CONTINUOUS. Upgrade trigger:
  correctly classifying a fresh -ing word by function, not spelling,
  without hesitation.
- **Advanced model — "I identify infinitives and their functions, and I
  produce the correct form for gerund-only and infinitive-only verbs"**:
  installed by TA-2 and TA-3, beginning to resolve
  MC-GERUND-AND-INFINITIVE-ARE-ALWAYS-INTERCHANGEABLE by directly
  demonstrating verbs that accept only one form.
- **Expert model — "I produce either form correctly for either-form
  verbs, and I distinguish the genuine meaning differences carried by
  meaning-changing verbs"**: installed by TA-4 and TA-5, the concept's
  genuine mastery bar — fully resolving
  MC-GERUND-AND-INFINITIVE-ARE-ALWAYS-INTERCHANGEABLE by demonstrating
  the full spectrum from strict governance to genuine free choice to
  meaning-carrying choice.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own Protocol Routing, for
  S9 learners whose L1 has a single, unified way to express these
  constructions or no gerund/infinitive distinction at all, expect this
  concept to be one of the most persistent, well-documented advanced-
  learner error areas — gerund/infinitive verb governance is
  idiosyncratic even WITHIN English's own system and has no reliable
  logical derivation, warranting substantial, ongoing, spaced practice
  specifically on high-frequency governing verbs, not a single-session
  drill.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, and the Blueprint
itself explicitly names both as genuinely persistent, memorization-
dependent challenges even for otherwise fluent speakers. The gerund-is-
just-present-continuous failure exists because the -ing form is most
commonly first encountered in present continuous tense, making that
association the learner's default reference point — nothing marks the
noun-role gerund use as a genuinely different grammatical category
until a learner is directly asked whether "swimming" in "swimming is
fun" is the sentence's main verb or its subject. The gerund-and-
infinitive-always-interchangeable failure exists because both forms
genuinely describe similar underlying ideas (an activity or action),
making interchangeability a reasonable first guess — nothing marks the
specific verb-governance requirement until a learner directly produces
"I enjoy to swim" and is asked whether fluent speakers actually say
this (they don't; "enjoy" strictly governs the gerund).

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.grammar.gerunds-and-infinitives.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-GERUND-IS-JUST-PRESENT-CONTINUOUS (Blueprint Component 1) —
Type 2, perceptual intuition**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 2, not Type 1 — this is a genuine
  perceptual default rather than an overextension of a partially-
  correct grammatical rule: the -ing spelling itself is the most
  salient, first-encountered cue a learner meets (via present
  continuous), and the visual/spelling identity between the two forms
  creates a strong, intuitively-felt association independent of any
  taught rule; the misconception survives because the spelling truly
  IS identical, offering no surface signal that the underlying
  function differs.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when shown a
  fresh -ing word, spontaneously asks "is this doing a noun's job or a
  verb's job here?" before classifying it is showing the function-
  over-spelling test has been genuinely internalized.

**MC-GERUND-AND-INFINITIVE-ARE-ALWAYS-INTERCHANGEABLE (Blueprint
Component 1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — both forms
  genuinely describe similar underlying activity concepts, so treating
  them as freely swappable is a reasonable extension from real,
  partial semantic overlap; the misconception survives because some
  verbs genuinely DO accept either form (like, start, begin),
  reinforcing the interchangeability instinct until a strictly-governed
  verb like "enjoy" directly contradicts it.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when meeting a
  fresh governing verb, spontaneously pauses to recall which form it
  requires (rather than defaulting to either form on autopilot) is
  showing the per-verb-governance discipline has generalized beyond the
  specific TA-3 practice items.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the noun-job-vs-verb-job sort itself is the
  analogy, not a supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): sorting
  -ing sentence cards by asking "could I replace this word with 'it' or
  'that activity'?" (a noun-job test) versus "is this part of what's
  happening right now?" (a verb-job test) directly embodies the
  function-over-spelling discrimination as a concrete, checkable
  sorting task, extended by color-coded verb-governance tags
  (gerund-only, infinitive-only, either) to build the second insight
  visually. Breaking point: the sort demonstrates both core insights
  well but doesn't on its own address the meaning-changing verb
  category (TA-5), which needs its own separate, dedicated contrast
  pairs (stop/remember/forget/try).
- **Alternative — an actor's costume that looks identical on two
  different roles**: the same -ing costume can be worn by an actor
  playing a "noun" role (gerund) or a "verb" role (present continuous)
  — checking WHICH SCENE (sentence context) the actor is in, not the
  costume itself, tells you which role is being played. Breaking point:
  useful for the identical-form-different-function insight, but doesn't
  address the verb-governance memorization task, which the sort
  anchor's own color-coded tags handle directly.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe gerund/infinitive verb-governance
  patterns as "totally random" or "impossible to predict"**: this
  framing risks discouraging the memorization effort by implying no
  learnable structure exists; instead, per the Blueprint's own explicit
  framing, present this as a genuine, BOUNDED memorization task
  (parallel to preposition collocations) covering a manageable list of
  high-frequency verbs, not an infinite or arbitrary system.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/visual (the noun-job vs. verb-job sort)**: the Blueprint's
  Component 3 anchor — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any activity the learner genuinely enjoys or
  avoids supplies real TA-1/TA-3 material for gerund-role and gerund-
  only-verb practice; any genuine goal or plan the learner has supplies
  real TA-2/TA-3 infinitive material.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-5's meaning-changing-verb
  discrimination task (distinguishing "I stopped smoking" from "I
  stopped to smoke") is inherently hands-on, requiring the learner to
  actively construct and compare two genuinely different meanings, not
  merely recognize a stated fact.
- **Teacher-demo (the main-verb test)**: the tutor models the
  Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstration — asking whether
  "swimming" in "swimming is fun" is the sentence's main verb (like in
  "I am swimming") or the subject, making the function-over-spelling
  test directly audible and testable.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  gerund/infinitive identification or production attempt (TA-1 through
  TA-5), ask the learner to state their own attempt before the tutor
  confirms — Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** The
function-over-spelling test and the per-verb-governance discipline are
both genuine, directly testable phenomena well-suited to guided
discovery: **need** (the learner, having secured TA-1's gerund-as-
subject practice, is asked whether "swimming" in "swimming is fun" is
the main verb, discovering their spelling-based instinct feels wrong
once function is checked); **playground/invention** (TA-1's gerund
practice and TA-2's infinitive practice build initial confidence with
both forms independently before the governance complication is
introduced); **collision** (the Blueprint's own "swimming is fun" probe
breaks the gerund-is-present-continuous assumption; the "I enjoy to
swim" probe breaks the always-interchangeable assumption);
**formalization** (MP-5's own-words statement of both the function-
over-spelling test and the per-verb-governance requirement);
**compression** (TA-5's fluent meaning-changing-verb discrimination,
applying both prior disciplines directly without each instance
separately re-derived). The specific terms "gerund" and "infinitive"
are convention-bound grammatical terminology best introduced by direct
instruction once the learner has already felt the underlying
distinctions through the noun-job/verb-job sort and TA-1–2.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (gerunds as subjects and objects) is placed first, directly
targeting MC-GERUND-IS-JUST-PRESENT-CONTINUOUS, per the Blueprint's own
session architecture — establishing the function-over-spelling test
immediately, since this is the concept's most foundational correction.
TA-2 (infinitives as subjects, objects, and purpose) follows, extending
the concept to the second -ing-alternative form and its own range of
functions. TA-3 (gerund-only and infinitive-only verbs) directly
targets MC-GERUND-AND-INFINITIVE-ARE-ALWAYS-INTERCHANGEABLE, addressing
a SECOND, independent dimension (per-verb governance, not form
identification) once both forms are individually secure. TA-4 (verbs
accepting either form) extends the governance spectrum to genuinely
flexible verbs, building on the now-secure strict-governance
distinction from TA-3. TA-5 (meaning-changing verbs) is placed last as
the concept's integration capstone, requiring the learner to
distinguish genuine semantic differences carried by form choice — the
most subtle, highest-stakes governance case. This entry does not
restate the Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7)
— see Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner spontaneously checks an
  -ing word's function (noun vs. verb job) before classifying it, and
  whether the learner spontaneously pauses to recall a fresh verb's
  governed form rather than defaulting to either form on autopilot —
  these are the primary diagnostic signals for whether TA-1 and TA-3's
  disciplines have genuinely generalized beyond the specific practice
  items.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause before producing the
  following form for a fresh governing verb, followed by the correct,
  governance-consistent choice, suggests the learner is consciously
  recalling the specific verb's requirement rather than guessing — a
  good sign, and genuinely expected even for fluent speakers on less-
  common verbs. Instant, confident use of "enjoy to [verb]" or similar
  strictly-ungoverned combinations (without self-correction) is the
  concerning pattern for
  MC-GERUND-AND-INFINITIVE-ARE-ALWAYS-INTERCHANGEABLE.
- **Native-intuition-gap stakes**: real and specifically named in the
  Blueprint's own Protocol Routing — for S9 learners whose L1 has a
  single, unified way to express these constructions, reassure that
  this is one of the most persistent, well-documented advanced-learner
  challenge areas, idiosyncratic even by English's own internal
  standards, warranting sustained, spaced practice well beyond a single
  session.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "is that word doing a noun's job or a
  verb's job right here?" is this concept's load-bearing sentence for
  MC-GERUND-IS-JUST-PRESENT-CONTINUOUS, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` — asked
  whenever a fresh -ing word's classification is required, making the
  function check a routinely-invited response rather than an
  afterthought.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedural/framework — a function-over-spelling
discrimination system (gerund vs. present continuous) combined with a
lexical verb-governance memorization system (gerund-only, infinitive-
only, either, meaning-changing), applied to genuinely novel sentences
and verbs; mastery is demonstrated by correctly classifying and
producing fresh gerund/infinitive forms and correctly recalling fresh
governing verbs' requirements (MP-1 through MP-4) rather than by
recognizing a fixed, previously-taught example list. Interleaving
partners: mix function-classification items (testing TA-1/TA-2) with
per-verb-governance items across all four governance categories
(testing TA-3/TA-4/TA-5) in ongoing review, since this domain benefits
from spaced, distributed practice on individual governing verbs more
than most other grammar concepts in this curriculum, per the
Blueprint's own explicit framing.

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: identifying fresh gerunds and infinitives by function;
  producing the correct governed form for fresh gerund-only/infinitive-
  only/either-form verbs; distinguishing fresh meaning-changing-verb
  pairs.
- **Far**: this concept's identical-spelling-different-function
  discrimination discipline is the direct foundation for
  `eng.grammar.participles-and-participial-phrases`'s identical
  challenge — -ing/-ed forms functioning as ADJECTIVES rather than as
  the NOUNS this concept covers, requiring the same function-over-
  spelling test applied to a new grammatical role.
- **Real-world**: every activity, preference, plan, or goal the learner
  expresses — in speech or writing — depends on correctly selecting
  between gerund and infinitive forms, especially for the high-
  frequency governing verbs that appear constantly in everyday
  communication.
- **Expert-transfer**: fluent, natural-sounding production at any
  advanced level depends on effortless, automatic recall of verb-
  governance patterns without conscious deliberation, since gerund/
  infinitive errors (especially with strictly-governed high-frequency
  verbs like "enjoy" and "want") remain among the most persistent and
  immediately noticeable markers of non-native production, even at
  otherwise advanced proficiency.
