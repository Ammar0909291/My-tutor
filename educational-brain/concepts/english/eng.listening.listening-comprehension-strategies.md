## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Infer meaning and continue listening when encountering an unfamiliar word, rather than freezing.
2. Make a specific prediction about what comes next in spoken discourse.
3. Revise a prediction when new information contradicts it, rather than treating predictions as fixed guesses.
4. Apply inference-and-continue and predict-and-revise together in a real listening passage.
5. Explain why good listening comprehension does not require understanding every single word perfectly.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept. First, **good listening does not require understanding every word perfectly on the first pass** — encountering an unfamiliar word should trigger inference from context and continued listening, not a freeze that causes the listener to lose the thread of everything that follows; a listener who stops to fully resolve one unknown word sacrifices comprehension of the next several sentences spoken while they were stuck. Second, **predicting what comes next in spoken discourse is not random guessing — it is an active, revisable hypothesis based on context**, and a good listener continuously updates that prediction as new information arrives rather than either refusing to predict at all or clinging rigidly to an initial guess once it's contradicted. Both corrections replace a perfectionist, word-by-word listening model with an active, inference-and-prediction-based one — the same shift from passive reception to active construction that this program's reading-domain concepts (predicting-and-confirming, close reading) make for text.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "good listeners understand every word perfectly the first time; predicting what comes next means guessing randomly, or I'm not allowed to predict at all."**
The simulation: freeze on an unfamiliar word, losing track of subsequent content while trying to resolve it, and either avoid prediction entirely or produce a rigid guess never revised.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — being shown that freezing on one unknown word causes loss of the following passage, versus inferring and continuing; and being shown a prediction contradicted by new information, asked whether a good listener revises or clings to the original guess.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Infer and keep listening past an unknown word. Predict actively, and revise when new information arrives."

**Intermediate — "I infer and keep listening now, but I still tend to treat my first prediction as fixed once I've made it."**
The simulation: correctly avoids freezing on unfamiliar words but doesn't yet reliably revise an initial prediction when later content contradicts it.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-3/TA-4's direct practice with a passage that deliberately contradicts an initial reasonable prediction, requiring the learner to notice and revise.
*Shelf-life warning*: "A prediction is a working guess, not a final answer — update it the moment new information contradicts it."

**Advanced — "I infer past unknown words, predict actively, and revise predictions fluidly, applying both together in a real, unscaffolded listening passage."**
The simulation: integrates inference-and-continue with predict-and-revise into one coherent, real-time listening strategy. Matches TA-4's full application.
*Upgrade trigger*: the direct unlock `eng.listening.critical-listening`, which builds on this concept's active-listening foundation to add evaluative judgment about the speaker's claims.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you active, resilient comprehension. The next step adds evaluating what's being said, not just understanding it."

**Expert — "at fluency, inference-and-continue and predict-and-revise happen automatically and unconsciously during real-time listening, without deliberate strategy invocation."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit two-strategy routine is a scaffold for a listening habit that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated, automatic part of real-time comprehension.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **reading habits (where a reader CAN pause indefinitely on an unfamiliar word without losing the text, since it remains on the page) transfer inappropriately to listening**, where the audio stream continues regardless of whether the listener has resolved the unknown word — a strategy that works in one modality actively fails in the other.

The second mechanism is that **"guessing" carries a negative connotation of being unreliable or dishonest**, so learners taught to value accuracy interpret "predicting what comes next" as equivalent to careless guessing, when in fact it is a disciplined, evidence-based hypothesis explicitly meant to be revised — the same misconception pattern this program has identified in the reading domain's predicting-and-confirming concept.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-GOOD-LISTENERS-UNDERSTAND-EVERY-WORD-PERFECTLY-THE-FIRST-TIME
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry — the freeze-versus-infer-and-continue discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 6 (analogy overextension), from reading's tolerance for indefinite pausing on an unfamiliar word being implicitly applied to listening, where the audio stream doesn't wait.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **explicitly name the modality difference** (text waits, audio doesn't), since the strategy that works perfectly well in reading is precisely the one that fails here — an abstract "don't worry about every word" is less effective than naming why the reading habit doesn't transfer.
- **Verification of death**: given a listening passage with an unfamiliar word, the learner infers meaning from context and continues tracking subsequent content, rather than freezing.

### MC-B-PREDICTING-WHAT-COMES-NEXT-MEANS-GUESSING-RANDOMLY-OR-JUST-ASSUMING-YOURE-RIGHT
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — the no-prediction/rigid-prediction-versus-revisable-prediction discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 3 (language contamination), from "guessing" carrying an everyday connotation of carelessness or unreliability, contaminating the technical, disciplined sense of "prediction" as an evidence-based, revisable hypothesis.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **demonstrate a prediction being made, contradicted, and revised in one continuous sequence**, since the fix isn't merely permission to predict but the explicit revision step that distinguishes disciplined prediction from either refusal-to-predict or rigid guessing.
- **Verification of death**: given a passage that reasonably suggests one direction and then contradicts it, the learner revises their prediction rather than clinging to the original guess or refusing to have predicted at all.

## Analogies

**Best — the Hazy-Stargazing-and-Weather-Forecast Anchor** (Blueprint's own anchor, name confirmed): stargazing through haze where individual stars (words) are sometimes obscured but the overall constellation (meaning) remains trackable by inferring the obscured points from the visible pattern, directly seeding the infer-and-continue fix; a weather forecast that predicts rain but is explicitly revised as new radar data arrives, directly seeding the predict-and-revise fix, since forecasters are trusted precisely because they update, not because their first guess was permanently correct.
*Breaking point*: a constellation's true shape is fixed and independently verifiable regardless of haze, while a spoken passage's "true meaning" is genuinely harder to verify independently — useful for the infer-the-gap-from-context intuition, less precise for implying there's always a single verifiable ground truth waiting to confirm the inference.
*Breaking point (forecast)*: a weather forecast is revised based on new instrument data, an external objective source, while a listening prediction is revised based on the same ongoing spoken stream being interpreted — useful for the revision-is-expected-and-good intuition, less precise for the different evidentiary sources involved.

**Alternative — a detective following a case, revising a working theory as clues (some ambiguous) arrive.** A good detective forms an initial theory from available clues, doesn't stop investigating because one piece of evidence is unclear, and revises the theory when a new clue contradicts it — precisely modeling both infer-and-continue and predict-and-revise together.
*Breaking point*: a detective's investigation can pause indefinitely to examine one clue, unlike the real-time, non-pausable nature of live spoken audio — useful for the working-theory-revision intuition, less precise for the strict time-pressure aspect unique to listening.

**Visual anchor — the Hazy-Stargazing-and-Weather-Forecast Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"A good listener catches and understands every single word."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated standard.
- **"Predicting is just guessing — you shouldn't do it if you want to be accurate."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated warning against prediction.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Hazy-Stargazing-and-Weather-Forecast Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint's own script. *Predict first*: "If a few stars are hazy, can you still tell what constellation this is?" Then connect to inferring past an unfamiliar word.
2. **The freeze/infer-and-continue collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "If you stop to figure out one word, what happens to everything said while you're stuck?"
3. **The rigid-prediction/revised-prediction collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-B. *Predict first*: "Does a good listener stick with their first guess no matter what, or update it?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both strategic facts are directly checkable by the learner's own experience listening to a passage with a deliberately obscured word and a deliberately contradicted prediction.

1. **Need**: "If you don't know one word in a sentence you just heard, is the whole sentence lost to you?"
2. **Playground**: the learner practices inferring several unfamiliar words from context in short passages before being told the official strategy name.
3. **Invention**: "Based on what you've heard so far, what do you think comes next? Now that you've heard more — does your guess still hold?"
4. **Collision**: present a passage that contradicts an initial reasonable prediction and ask whether a good listener should stick with the original guess.
5. **Formalization**: name the two strategies — infer and continue past an unfamiliar word; predict actively and revise on new evidence — from the learner's own successful practice.
6. **Compression**: "Infer past the gap; predict, and update when you're wrong."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Prerequisite integration is assumed entering this concept** (note-taking and gist-listening already established), so no separate foundational stage is needed before the two strategies themselves are introduced.
- **Infer-and-continue is established before predict-and-revise** (matching the Blueprint's misconception ordering, MC-A before MC-B), since resilience to unfamiliar words is the more basic strategic requirement that active prediction then builds upon in the same real-time listening stream.
- **Full application in a real passage comes last**, integrating both strategies together — matching this program's established sequencing principle of reserving the most integrative task for the culminating stage.

## Tutor Actions

In recommended order, from `../teaching-actions/`:

1. **Demonstration** (SHOW) — the Hazy-Stargazing-and-Weather-Forecast Anchor and both collision scenarios.
2. **Prediction** (TEST-THINKING) — the predict-and-revise practice is a direct prediction action, core to this concept.
3. **Error Analysis** (TEST-THINKING) — both collisions are guided error analysis against a plausible-but-incorrect assumption (perfectionism, or guess-avoidance).
4. **Worked Example** (SHOW) — a modeled think-aloud of a fluent listener inferring an unfamiliar word and revising a prediction mid-passage.

**Does not fit**: **Game** — the real-time, attention-demanding nature of live listening comprehension is not well served by added competitive pressure, which risks increasing anxiety around the very freeze-response this concept is correcting. **Concept Map** — the skill is a real-time procedural strategy, not a static relationship structure.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept is **inherently a listening/spoken-input skill** — its core evidence is the learner's real-time behavior during and immediately after hearing a passage (what they infer, what they predict, whether they revise), not written analysis of a transcript alone.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Continued tracking of subsequent content after encountering an unfamiliar word**, evidenced by the learner correctly reporting what was said afterward — the direct behavioral signature of the infer-and-continue fix, distinct from a freeze that causes a comprehension gap.
- **An explicitly stated, revisable prediction** ("I think it's going to say X... actually, given what they just said, I think it's Y instead") — direct evidence the predict-and-revise fix is internalized.
- **Silence or a stated "I don't know" refusal when asked what comes next**, rather than any attempt at inference — the direct behavioral signature of MC-B's guess-avoidance still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Keep listening past what you don't know, and update your guess the moment you learn more."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5, per the Blueprint's own item bank). Response-pattern reads:

- **Inferring past an unfamiliar word without freezing, and producing then revising a prediction on contradiction.** Per this program's established dual-pass pattern for concepts with two registered misconceptions, mastery should not be certified unless both the infer-and-continue and predict-and-revise behaviors are independently demonstrated, not just one.
- **Applying both strategies together in a full, real listening passage.** The synthesis item; listen for both behaviors co-occurring naturally within one continuous passage, not only when separately prompted.

**Mastery certification trigger**: successful inference-and-continue past at least one unfamiliar word with accurate tracking of subsequent content; a stated prediction later correctly revised upon contradiction; both strategies demonstrated together in one full passage; a clear explanation of why perfect word-by-word understanding is not required for good listening comprehension.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I missed that word, so I don't know what they meant"** — take this as a genuine, reasonable listening habit carried over from reading; respond by naming the modality difference: "In reading you can go back — in listening, the sound keeps moving. Let's guess from what came around it and keep going."
- **"I don't want to guess wrong"** — a reasonable, accuracy-motivated concern; respond by reframing prediction as expected-to-be-revised: "A prediction isn't a final answer — it's your best guess right now, and updating it when you learn more is exactly what good listeners do."
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full real-passage application down to **inferring a single isolated unfamiliar word from one surrounding sentence, with no prediction-revision task demanded.** Isolates the most basic resilience strategy before returning to fuller listening tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just try to catch every word you can."** This directly re-licenses the perfectionist freeze-risking pattern this concept specifically works to dismantle.
