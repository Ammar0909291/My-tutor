# Direct and Indirect Speech — `eng.grammar.direct-and-indirect-speech`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.grammar.direct-and-indirect-speech` (canonical
  English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / grammar / "Direct and Indirect
  Speech" — reporting another person's exact words (direct speech)
  versus paraphrasing them with appropriate tense and pronoun shifts
  (indirect/reported speech).
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.grammar.tense-consistency` — load-bearing
  because tense backshift (the core mechanism of indirect speech) is
  itself a MOTIVATED tense shift — reflecting a genuine change in
  vantage point (reporting later, from a different perspective) — and a
  learner cannot correctly judge when backshift is required versus
  optional without the motivated-vs-unmotivated tense-shift discipline
  that concept already secured.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.grammar.quotation-marks`. Correctly
  punctuating direct speech with quotation marks depends on first
  reliably distinguishing direct speech (quoted, requiring marks) from
  indirect speech (paraphrased, never quoted) — the categorical
  distinction this concept establishes. **Cross-links** (from KG):
  `eng.writing.narrative-writing` — direct and indirect speech are
  core dialogue-representation tools in narrative writing; this entry
  notes the connection without restating that concept's own content.
- **Difficulty**: proficient · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery threshold**:
  0.75 · **Est. hours**: 2

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Apply the correct tense backshift when converting direct speech to
   indirect speech (present becomes past, past becomes past perfect,
   etc.).
2. Apply the correct pronoun shift and time/place word shift (today→
   that day, here→there, tomorrow→the next day) alongside the tense
   backshift.
3. Combine all three shift types (tense, pronoun, time/place)
   simultaneously when converting a direct-speech sentence to indirect
   speech.
4. Correctly identify when tense backshift can be optionally skipped —
   reporting a still-true general fact or an ongoing, still-current
   situation.
5. Convert direct questions and commands to indirect form, using
   if/whether or the question word with statement word order for
   questions, and "to + base verb" for commands.

## Core Understanding

Converting direct speech to indirect (reported) speech is not simply
removing quotation marks — it requires THREE coordinated changes
applied together, reflecting the fact that reporting happens from a
DIFFERENT time, place, and perspective than the original statement.
TENSE BACKSHIFT moves the verb one step further into the past (present
becomes past, past becomes past perfect), since the reporter is now
speaking from a later vantage point ("I am tired" → "she was tired").
PRONOUN SHIFT adjusts first/second person pronouns to match the
reporting perspective ("I" → "she," "you" → "I/he/she" depending on
context). TIME/PLACE WORD SHIFT adjusts deictic words that depend on
when/where the original statement was made ("today" → "that day,"
"here" → "there," "tomorrow" → "the next day"). A second, independent,
equally load-bearing insight concerns WHEN backshift is required:
backshift is the DEFAULT rule, but it can be OPTIONALLY SKIPPED when
reporting something still true or current at the time of reporting —
general truths, scientific facts, or ongoing situations ("She said the
Earth orbits the Sun," not forced into "orbited"). Backshift is not a
mechanical habit disconnected from meaning — it specifically reflects
whether the reported information's truth or relevance still holds at
the moment of reporting.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "converting to indirect speech just means removing
  the quotation marks (and maybe adjusting 'said'), and backshift
  always applies no matter what"**: the arriving, pre-instructional
  model this concept exists to correct — a natural default because
  removing quotation marks IS the single most visually obvious change
  a learner notices, and because backshift genuinely is the correct
  DEFAULT rule, making its universal application feel reasonable until
  the still-true exception is directly demonstrated.
- **Intermediate model — "I apply tense backshift, and I apply the
  pronoun and time/place shifts alongside it"**: installed by TA-1 and
  TA-2, using the Component 3 reporter's-translation-booth anchor,
  beginning to resolve
  MC-INDIRECT-SPEECH-IS-JUST-REMOVING-QUOTATION-MARKS. Upgrade trigger:
  correctly applying all three shift types together for a fresh
  sentence, not just tense.
- **Advanced model — "I combine all three shifts simultaneously for
  fully correct indirect speech"**: installed by TA-3, the concept's
  base mechanical mastery bar, fully resolving
  MC-INDIRECT-SPEECH-IS-JUST-REMOVING-QUOTATION-MARKS.
- **Expert model — "I recognize when backshift can be skipped for
  still-true facts, and I convert questions and commands to indirect
  form"**: installed by TA-4 and TA-5, the concept's genuine mastery
  bar, resolving
  MC-BACKSHIFT-ALWAYS-REQUIRED-REGARDLESS-OF-TRUTH-STATUS by connecting
  the backshift rule to its underlying communicative purpose rather
  than applying it as an unconditional habit.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own Protocol Routing, for
  S9 learners whose L1 has a different or no backshift convention for
  reported speech, expect this concept's tense-backshift mechanism
  specifically to be a genuinely novel, challenging structural feature
  requiring substantial, dedicated practice — not an assumed intuitive
  transfer.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here. The just-remove-
quotation-marks failure exists because removing the quotation marks IS
the single most visually salient, most obviously necessary change a
learner notices when converting a quoted sentence — nothing marks the
additional, less visually obvious tense/pronoun/time-place shifts as
equally required until a learner is directly asked whether "I" still
makes sense once the sentence is no longer a direct quote from the
original speaker. The backshift-always-required failure exists because
backshift genuinely IS the correct default rule that a learner has just
secured — over-applying it universally is a reasonable extension of
real, mostly-correct partial evidence, with nothing marking the still-
true exception until a learner is directly asked whether a timeless
scientific fact like "the Earth orbits the Sun" genuinely needs to be
forced into past tense simply because it's being reported.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.grammar.direct-and-indirect-speech.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-INDIRECT-SPEECH-IS-JUST-REMOVING-QUOTATION-MARKS (Blueprint
Component 1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — removing
  quotation marks genuinely IS a real, necessary part of the
  conversion, making a partial-rule application (quotation marks only)
  a reasonable, incomplete extension of real evidence, not a wholesale
  error; the misconception survives because the quotation-mark change
  is the most visually obvious step, delaying the moment the pronoun
  and tense mismatches are directly noticed.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when converting a
  fresh direct-speech sentence, spontaneously flips through all three
  mental "checklist" items (tense, pronoun, time/place) rather than
  stopping after removing the quotation marks is showing the full
  three-part discipline has been genuinely internalized.

**MC-BACKSHIFT-ALWAYS-REQUIRED-REGARDLESS-OF-TRUTH-STATUS (Blueprint
Component 1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — backshift
  genuinely IS the correct default rule for the large majority of
  reported statements, so applying it universally is a reasonable
  extension of real, frequently-correct evidence; the misconception
  survives because most everyday reported statements genuinely ARE
  time-bound personal states that do warrant backshift, delaying
  exposure to genuine still-true exceptions.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when reporting a
  fresh timeless fact or still-current situation, spontaneously keeps
  the original present tense rather than mechanically backshifting is
  showing the meaning-connected understanding of backshift has
  generalized beyond the specific TA-4 practice items.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the reporter's translation booth itself is the
  analogy, not a supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): a role-play
  where a "reporter" relays a direct-speech statement while physically
  flipping three checklist cards ("Tense?" "Pronoun?" "Time/Place
  word?"), directly embodies the full three-part conversion as a
  concrete, sequential, consciously-tracked process rather than a
  single quotation-mark-removal action. Breaking point: the translation-
  booth anchor demonstrates the three-shift mechanics well but doesn't
  on its own address the still-true exception (TA-4), which needs its
  own separate, meaning-focused demonstration extending beyond the
  mechanical checklist.
- **Alternative — a letter written yesterday, read aloud today**:
  reading a letter written yesterday aloud requires adjusting "today"
  to "yesterday" and other deictic references to reflect the new
  reading context — parallel to how reported speech must adjust its
  time/place/pronoun anchors to the reporting context, not the original
  speaking context. Breaking point: useful for the shifted-perspective
  insight generally, but doesn't address the specific tense-backshift
  mechanics, which the translation-booth anchor's own TA-1 practice
  handles directly.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe backshift as "an automatic tense
  penalty for using reported speech"**: this framing directly
  reinforces
  MC-BACKSHIFT-ALWAYS-REQUIRED-REGARDLESS-OF-TRUTH-STATUS by casting
  backshift as a mechanical, meaningless formality rather than a
  meaningful signal about whether the reported information remains
  true or relevant; backshift should always be taught as MEANING-
  CARRYING, not as an arbitrary grammatical tax on reporting.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** The full-
three-shift discipline and the meaning-connected backshift exception
are both genuine, directly testable phenomena well-suited to guided
discovery: **need** (the learner, having secured TA-1's tense-backshift
practice, is asked to convert "She said, 'I am tired'" using only
quotation-mark removal, discovering "She said I am tired" feels wrong
once the pronoun mismatch is examined); **playground/invention**
(TA-1's tense-backshift practice and TA-2's pronoun/time-place-shift
practice build initial confidence with each shift type independently
before combining them); **collision** (the Blueprint's own "I am
tired" probe breaks the quotation-marks-only assumption; the "the
Earth orbits the Sun" probe breaks the backshift-always-required
assumption); **formalization** (MP-5's own-words statement of both the
three-shift requirement and the still-true exception); **compression**
(TA-3's fluent simultaneous three-shift conversion, applying all
disciplines directly without each instance separately re-derived). The
specific terms "backshift," "reported speech," and "deictic" (time/
place words) are convention-bound grammatical terminology best
introduced by direct instruction once the learner has already felt the
underlying distinctions through the translation-booth anchor and
TA-1–3.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (tense backshift in indirect speech) is placed first as the
concept's core mechanical entry point, per the Blueprint's own
translation-booth anchor — establishing the tense dimension before the
pronoun and time/place dimensions are added. TA-2 (pronoun and time/
place shifts) follows immediately, directly targeting
MC-INDIRECT-SPEECH-IS-JUST-REMOVING-QUOTATION-MARKS by adding the two
additional, less visually obvious shift types. TA-3 (combining all
three shifts together) is the concept's base mechanical mastery bar,
requiring the learner to apply all three shift types simultaneously,
not just individually. TA-4 (exceptions: still-true facts and ongoing
situations) introduces a SECOND, independent dimension (WHETHER to
backshift, not HOW), directly targeting
MC-BACKSHIFT-ALWAYS-REQUIRED-REGARDLESS-OF-TRUTH-STATUS, once the base
three-shift mechanics from TA-1–3 are secure. TA-5 (reporting questions
and commands) is placed last as the concept's integration capstone,
extending the now-secure statement-conversion mechanics to two
genuinely new sentence-type structures. This entry does not restate the
Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see
Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner spontaneously applies
  pronoun and time/place shifts alongside tense backshift for a fresh
  sentence (rather than stopping after removing quotation marks), and
  whether the learner spontaneously keeps the original tense for a
  fresh timeless-fact or still-current statement rather than
  mechanically backshifting — these are the primary diagnostic signals
  for whether TA-2/TA-3 and TA-4's disciplines have genuinely
  generalized beyond the specific practice items.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause before converting a
  fresh still-true statement, followed by a correct decision to keep
  the original tense, suggests the learner is consciously checking
  meaning rather than mechanically backshifting — a good sign. Instant,
  confident backshifting of a genuinely timeless fact (without
  checking whether it's still true) is the concerning pattern for
  MC-BACKSHIFT-ALWAYS-REQUIRED-REGARDLESS-OF-TRUTH-STATUS.
- **Native-intuition-gap stakes**: real and specifically named in the
  Blueprint's own Protocol Routing — for S9 learners whose L1 doesn't
  require tense-shifting in reported speech, budget substantial,
  dedicated practice specifically for the backshift mechanism itself,
  since this may be a genuinely novel structural feature with no direct
  L1 parallel.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "is this still true right now, or was
  it only true at the moment it was said?" is this concept's load-
  bearing sentence for
  MC-BACKSHIFT-ALWAYS-REQUIRED-REGARDLESS-OF-TRUTH-STATUS, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` — asked
  whenever a fresh potentially-still-true statement is being converted,
  making the truth-status check a routinely-invited response rather
  than an afterthought.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedural/framework — a three-part coordinated
transformation system (tense, pronoun, time/place) with one meaning-
connected exception (still-true facts), extended to questions and

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: converting fresh direct-speech statements to indirect speech
  with all three shifts correctly applied; correctly identifying fresh
  still-true exceptions; converting fresh questions and commands to
  indirect form.
- **Far**: this concept's direct-vs-indirect categorical distinction is
  the direct prerequisite for `eng.grammar.quotation-marks`'s
  punctuation rules, which depend on reliably distinguishing quoted
  (direct, requiring marks) from paraphrased (indirect, never quoted)
  speech; the KG's `cross_links` field connects this concept to
  `eng.writing.narrative-writing`, where direct and indirect speech
  function as core dialogue-representation tools.
- **Real-world**: every retelling of a conversation, interview, or
  overheard statement the learner produces — in speech or writing —
  depends on correctly and fluently converting between direct and
  indirect speech, and correctly signaling whether reported information
  remains current.
- **Expert-transfer**: sophisticated narrative and journalistic writing
  at any advanced level depends on the deliberate, fluent choice
  between direct quotation (preserving exact words for authenticity or
  emphasis) and indirect paraphrase (for concision or flow), a skill
  that presupposes fluent, error-free conversion mechanics as its
  foundation.
