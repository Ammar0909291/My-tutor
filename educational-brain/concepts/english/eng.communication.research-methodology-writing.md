## Learning Objective
The learner will describe a research method with enough specific detail for replication, explicitly justify why the chosen method suits the research question, and transparently acknowledge at least one genuine limitation rather than presenting the method as flawless.

## Core Understanding
Writing research methodology has two skills that a bare procedural list never delivers on its own: JUSTIFICATION and HONEST LIMITATION. A methods description that only lists steps taken ("We surveyed 50 students. We analyzed the results using a chi-square test.") tells a reader WHAT was done but not WHY these specific choices — this sample size, this statistical test, this method rather than a plausible alternative — suit the research question. Separately, a methodology section that presents its method as flawless, with no acknowledged limitations, is LESS credible to a careful reader, not more: acknowledging a real, specific limitation (a small sample, self-report's imprecision, limited generalizability) while still defending the method's overall suitability signals genuine methodological rigor, not weakness.

## Mental Models
- **Recipe-Technique Anchor**: a recipe that lists ingredients and steps but never explains why a particular technique was chosen (why fold, not stir; why bake at this temperature) leaves a fellow cook unable to judge whether the technique suits the dish or adapt it intelligently. A methods section needs to explain not just what was done, but why those choices suit this specific research question.
- **Honest-Mechanic Anchor**: a mechanic who claims a repair has zero possible failure points earns LESS trust than one who honestly notes "this part should hold for years, but it's the one component we can't fully test under all conditions" — the honesty signals expertise, not weakness. Acknowledging a real, specific limitation while still defending the method's overall suitability works the same way.

## Why Students Fail
Learners transitioning from simpler writing tasks default to procedural, "what happened" narration because that's the easiest content to generate — connecting each choice back to the research question with an explicit rationale is a distinct, harder cognitive step often skipped under time pressure. Separately, learners fear that naming a limitation will "weaken" their research, mistaking honesty for self-sabotage, because they haven't yet seen that a careful reader distrusts claimed perfection more than an acknowledged, bounded flaw.

## Misconceptions
- **MC-A (Type 5, instruction-induced from "describe what you did" prompts)**: "Describing a method means just listing the steps taken with no justification." Diagnostic: present a bare procedural description ("We surveyed 50 students about their study habits") against a justified one ("...using self-report because the research question concerns subjective time-allocation decisions best captured directly from participants") and ask which helps a reader understand WHY these choices were right. Repair: recipe-technique anchor — for every significant choice, add an explicit "...because..." clause connecting it to the specific research question.
- **MC-B (Type 1, overgeneralization from "admitting flaws weakens credibility")**: "A good methodology section should present the method as flawless with no acknowledged limitations." Diagnostic: present two methodology sections — one claiming perfection, one transparently naming a specific limitation while defending overall suitability — and ask which seems more credible to a careful reader. Repair: honest-mechanic anchor — after justifying the method, name at least one genuine, specific limitation directly, since acknowledging it while still defending the method's appropriateness signals rigor.

## Analogies
- Recipe-technique anchor (above) for justification-not-mere-listing.
- Honest-mechanic anchor (above) for honest-limitation-as-credibility.
- Anti-analogy: acknowledging a limitation is not the same as retracting the method — the goal is bounded, honest transparency plus continued defense, not self-sabotage that treats the limitation as disqualifying.

## Demonstrations
- Compare a bare method description against one adding explicit justification for the same study, asking which explains WHY the choices fit the question.
- Present a method claiming zero limitations against one honestly naming a specific limitation (self-report recall accuracy) while still defending the method, asking which reads as more rigorous.
- Full worked methodology for "Does listening to music while studying affect vocabulary retention?" — precise detail, explicit justification, and an honest limitation acknowledgment (immediate testing doesn't reflect longer-term retention, though appropriate given the study's specific focus).

## Discovery Questions
- "Why is THIS sample size, THIS method, suited to THIS specific research question, compared to a plausible alternative?"
- "What's one genuine, specific limitation of this method — not a vague, generic 'no method is perfect' hedge?"
- "Does naming this limitation retract your method, or does it honestly bound it while still defending its overall fit?"

## Teaching Sequence
1. Diagnostic: academic-writing-advanced revision-and-situating check.
2. Anchor: recipe-technique and honest-mechanic framing together.
3. TA-1: describe methods with precise, replicable detail (numbers, timeframes, procedures).
4. TA-2: justify method choices with explicit "...because..." clauses (targets MC-A).
5. TA-3: identify genuine, specific limitations (not vague generic caveats).
6. TA-4: acknowledge a limitation while defending overall appropriateness (targets MC-B).
7. TA-5: full methodology section — precise detail, justification, honest limitation.

## Tutor Actions
- SHOW: bare-list vs. justified method description pair; flawless-claim vs. honest-limitation methodology pair.
- DO: add "...because..." clauses after every methodological choice in a provided draft.
- TEST-THINKING: predict a genuine, specific limitation of a given method before checking against the model answer.
- ORGANIZE: a describe→justify→limit→defend checklist as scaffolding for the full synthesis task.

## Voice Teaching Notes
MC-A generally needs resolving before MC-B — a learner who hasn't developed the habit of justifying choices at all often hasn't developed the analytical habit needed to identify genuine limitations either.

## Assessment Signals
- Probe: "Given this bare method description and its research question, write an explicit justification." A response restating the method without a "because" clause signals residual MC-A.
- Probe: "Identify a genuine, specific limitation of this method." A vague answer ("no study is perfect") signals residual MC-B — or at minimum, the analytical specificity TA-3 targets is missing.
- Confident-wrong signature: a methodology section that reads fluently and confidently but claims the method has no limitations at all — flag MC-B regardless of how well-written the rest of the section is.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy
If a learner overcorrects after MC-B into undermining their own method entirely (treating the limitation as disqualifying), clarify that the goal is honest transparency plus continued defense, not self-sabotage.

## Memory Hooks
"Why fold, not stir?" for justification; "the honest mechanic earns more trust" for limitation acknowledgment.
