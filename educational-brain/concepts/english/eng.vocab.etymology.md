# Etymology — `eng.vocab.etymology`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.vocab.etymology` (canonical English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / vocab / "Etymology" — the
  historical origin and semantic change of words over time, including
  borrowing between languages, extending root identification into full
  historical word-journey tracing.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.vocab.roots-and-origins` — load-bearing
  because etymology extends the exact same root-identification skill
  that concept installed across a word's FULL historical timeline and
  cross-language journey; a learner who cannot yet identify a common
  root and its core meaning has no foundation for tracing that root
  through successive languages and centuries of meaning drift.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.vocab.semantic-fields`,
  `eng.linguistics.historical-linguistics-intro`. Grouping words by
  shared semantic territory (semantic fields) is aided by understanding
  how words' meanings have historically shifted and clustered; the
  linguistics concept formalizes this concept's own origin-tracing and
  language-contact ideas using proper historical-linguistic methodology
  and terminology.
- **Difficulty**: advanced · **Bloom**: analyze · **Mastery threshold**:
  0.75 · **Est. hours**: 3

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Trace a word's history from origin to current meaning using a
   straightforward, well-documented etymology.
2. Confirm that a word's CURRENT meaning, not its historical/
   etymological origin, is the meaning that actually matters for
   communication today, correctly explaining dramatic cases of meaning
   drift.
3. Trace a word's multi-language borrowing path across successive
   language stages, rejecting the assumption that every word has one
   simple, single-language origin.
4. Use a word's etymology as a helpful but appropriately cautious
   initial clue to its meaning, confirming or adjusting the guess
   against actual current usage.
5. Independently research and present the etymology of a self-selected
   word, including its origin language(s), any borrowing path, and any
   documented meaning drift.

## Core Understanding

Etymology traces a word's historical origin and how its meaning has
changed over time, including the languages it has passed through — but
this concept's genuine teaching challenge lies in two dimensions where
the history can mislead if treated as authoritative. First, a word's
ORIGINAL meaning is not its "true" or "correct" meaning today — language
changes constantly, and a word's CURRENT meaning, however much it has
drifted from its origin, is what the word actually means now: "nice"
originally meant "ignorant" or "foolish" (from Latin "nescius"), but no
living speaker uses it that way, and insisting on the etymological sense
would make a speaker impossible to understand; etymology is a history
lesson, not a rulebook for current usage. Second, and equally load-
bearing, not every English word traces back cleanly to one single
ancestor language — English is famous for extensive borrowing, taking
in enormous numbers of words from French, Latin, Greek, and dozens of
other languages through trade, conquest, and contact, sometimes with a
single word passing through two or three languages before landing in
English ("restaurant" travels Latin → Old French verb → Modern French
noun → English; "algebra" travels Arabic → Medieval Latin → English) —
assuming a simple, single-language origin for every word oversimplifies
English's genuinely international vocabulary history.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "a word's original historical meaning is its
  'real' or most correct meaning, and modern usage that differs is
  simply wrong"**: the arriving, pre-instructional model this concept
  exists to correct — a natural assumption once a word's fascinating,
  sometimes surprising origin story is learned, since the origin can
  feel more "authentic" or foundational than a meaning that has
  apparently "drifted away" from it.
- **Intermediate model — "I can trace a word's history from a
  straightforward, well-documented origin to its current meaning"**:
  installed by TA-1's simple-word-history-trace practice, using the
  Component 3 Word River Journey anchor to make the origin-to-present
  path visually concrete. Upgrade trigger: correctly tracing a fresh
  word's straightforward etymology from origin to current meaning
  without confusing the stages.
- **Advanced model — "a word's current meaning, however much it has
  drifted, is the real, functioning meaning today — etymology explains
  history, it doesn't override present usage"**: installed by TA-2's
  meaning-drift practice, resolving MC-A-WORDS-ORIGINAL-MEANING-IS-ITS-
  TRUE-OR-CORRECT-MEANING-TODAY through the felt contrast between
  "nice" (ignorant → pleasant) and its modern function. Upgrade
  trigger: correctly stating that a word's drifted current sense, not
  its origin, is the one to use in actual communication, without being
  told to prioritize current usage.
- **Expert model — "many English words have genuinely multi-language
  borrowing paths, not a single simple origin, and I can use etymology
  as a cautious, checked clue rather than an unconditional rule, and
  independently research a word's full history"**: installed by TA-3's
  borrowing-path tracing (resolving MC-EVERY-WORD-CAME-FROM-A-SINGLE-
  CLEAR-LANGUAGE-WITH-NO-BORROWING), TA-4's cautious-clue-use practice,
  and TA-5's original research capstone. Per the Blueprint's own
  explicit mastery gate, certification requires BOTH MP-1 (current-
  meaning confirmation) and MP-2 (multi-language path tracing) to
  pass — a learner who traces simple, straightforward histories (MP-3)
  but insists on etymological "correctness" or oversimplifies borrowing
  paths has not learned the core historical-linguistic judgment this
  concept requires.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own explicit mastery-
  gate rule (Component 7's S9 protocol), do not certify mastery on
  simple-word-history tracing alone (MP-3) — both MP-1 and MP-2 must
  independently pass, since tracing an already-clean, single-path
  etymology is a fundamentally easier task than the judgment (current-
  usage priority, multi-language recognition) this concept is actually
  built to install.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, both reasonable
extensions of the fascination etymology itself naturally generates. The
etymology-as-authority failure exists because a word's origin story
genuinely IS a real, historically accurate fact, and learning it can
feel like uncovering the word's "true" or foundational identity — a
learner reasonably extends this sense of historical authenticity into
an assumption that the origin should govern current usage, with nothing
in the fascinating discovery of an origin story itself marking that
the current, drifted meaning is what actually functions in
communication today. The single-origin-assumption failure exists
because most vocabulary instruction a learner meets prior to this
concept (including the prerequisite roots-and-origins concept itself)
tends to present clean, single-source root derivations, so the learner
reasonably extends "a word has one root, one origin" into "a word has
one single ancestor language," with nothing in that simpler prior
exposure marking the many common cases where a word's actual documented
history involves passing through several distinct languages.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.vocab.etymology.md`, Component 1) documents both misconceptions
this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/
discrimination content — reused by reference below, with birth-type
classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-A-WORDS-ORIGINAL-MEANING-IS-ITS-TRUE-OR-CORRECT-MEANING-TODAY
(Blueprint Component 1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — for many words a
  learner first meets in etymology work, the origin and current meaning
  ARE still closely related or identical, so the learner extends "the
  origin explains the meaning" into "the origin IS the correct meaning"
  as an unconditional rule, with nothing in those consistent cases
  marking the many words (like "nice" or "awful") whose meaning has
  drifted dramatically until directly tested against one.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, upon learning a
  surprising or dramatic etymological origin, spontaneously says
  something like "that's a fascinating history, but obviously the word
  means something totally different now" is showing the etymology-as-
  history-not-authority model has been genuinely internalized, rather
  than needing correction after insisting on the original sense.

**MC-EVERY-WORD-CAME-FROM-A-SINGLE-CLEAR-LANGUAGE-WITH-NO-BORROWING
(Blueprint Component 1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: also a Type 1 signature, distinct
  mechanism — the prerequisite roots-and-origins concept itself
  reasonably presented clean, single-source root derivations as the
  norm (bio = life, tele = far, both traced to one clear source
  language), so the learner extends that simpler pattern into an
  assumption that every word's full origin is similarly single-
  language, with nothing in that prior, correctly-taught simplification
  marking the many documented multi-language borrowing paths until
  directly traced.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when researching a
  word's history, spontaneously checks for intermediate languages
  ("did this pass through another language before reaching English?")
  before assuming a single, direct origin is showing the borrowing-
  and-contact-recognition model has generalized beyond the specific
  TA-3 practice words.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the Word River Journey itself is the analogy, not a
  supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): a winding river with
  labeled tributary points where a word picked up material or meaning
  changes from different language sources, with the river's MOUTH
  (today's current meaning) as what actually matters for everyday use
  while the whole winding path behind it is fascinating but non-
  authoritative history, directly embodies MC-A's fix; a second river
  with THREE tributaries merging from different starting countries
  directly embodies MC-B's fix. Breaking point: a river's mouth is a
  single, final endpoint, which usefully parallels a word's single
  current meaning at any given moment, but rivers don't naturally
  convey that a word's meaning can continue drifting FURTHER after the
  "current" snapshot — this entry treats the river-mouth framing as the
  introductory anchor for "current beats historical," not a claim that
  meaning drift has stopped or ever will.
- **Alternative — a person's childhood story vs. who they are as an
  adult**: knowing someone was shy as a child is a genuinely interesting
  historical fact about them, but it doesn't override who they actually
  are as an adult today — treating the childhood fact as more "real"
  than their current adult personality would be a mistake. Breaking
  point: useful for reinforcing that history explains but doesn't
  override the present, but less vivid and less directly tied to the
  MULTI-LANGUAGE-borrowing insight than the river anchor's tributary
  structure.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe a word's drifted meaning as
  "corrupted" or a "mistake" relative to its original sense**: framing
  meaning drift as degradation or error directly reinforces MC-A by
  implying the original sense remains the "proper" standard being
  deviated from; drift should always be framed as a normal, expected,
  value-neutral historical process — the current meaning is simply the
  word's meaning now, not a fallen or corrupted version of something
  better.

- **Physical/visual (the Word River Journey)**: the Blueprint's

- **Home/no-equipment**: the river diagram can be drawn on any paper or

- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-4's cautious-clue-use practice and

  stated fact.
- **Teacher-demo (meaning-drift and multi-language contrasts)**: the
  tutor models the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstrations —
  "nice" from "ignorant" to "pleasant" for MC-A, and "restaurant"/

  directly and memorably visible.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  current-meaning judgment (TA-2) or a borrowing-path trace (TA-3), ask
  the learner to state their own hypothesis before the tutor confirms —
  Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** Current-
meaning priority and multi-language borrowing are both genuine,
directly felt phenomena well-suited to guided discovery: **need** (the
learner, having secured root identification, is asked what "nice"
actually means today after learning it once meant "ignorant");
**playground/invention** (TA-1's simple word-history traces across
several documented etymologies let the learner notice the origin-to-
present pattern); **collision** (the Blueprint's own "nice" contrast
breaks the etymology-as-authority assumption; the restaurant/khaki/
house comparison breaks the single-origin assumption); **formalization**
(MP-5's original research task, requiring explicit documentation of
origin, borrowing path, and drift); **compression** (TA-4's fluent
cautious-clue use without each check separately re-derived). There is
no convention-bound terminology here requiring separate direct
instruction beyond the general concept of "etymology" and "borrowing"
themselves, best introduced once the learner has already felt the
underlying current-meaning-priority and multi-language patterns through
the anchor and TA-1–2.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (tracing a simple word history) is placed first as the concept's
confirmatory baseline, giving the learner secure practice with clean,
well-documented single-path etymologies before either complication
(drift or borrowing) is introduced. TA-2 (meaning drift over time)
follows, directly targeting MC-A-WORDS-ORIGINAL-MEANING-IS-ITS-TRUE-OR-
CORRECT-MEANING-TODAY, using the discrimination pairs already built
into the misconception register to introduce dramatic drift cases
through directly contrastive examples. TA-3 (borrowing and multi-
language journeys) is placed next, directly targeting MC-EVERY-WORD-
CAME-FROM-A-SINGLE-CLEAR-LANGUAGE-WITH-NO-BORROWING, addressing the
SECOND, independent dimension (origin complexity, not meaning drift)
once the learner already has secure single-path tracing (TA-1) to
contrast against genuinely multi-language paths. TA-4 (using etymology
as a meaning clue, with appropriate caution) reinforces the productive
use of etymology (echoing the prerequisite concept) while explicitly
flagging when NOT to over-rely on it — placed after TA-2/TA-3 so the
learner already understands both reasons for caution (drift, and
multi-language complexity) before applying etymology practically. TA-5
(researching an original word history) is placed last as the concept's
fully independent, generative capstone, requiring the learner to
investigate origin language(s), borrowing path, and meaning drift
together for a self-selected word. This entry does not restate the
Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see
Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner spontaneously prioritizes
  a word's current meaning over a fascinating historical origin when
  discussing actual usage (rather than treating the origin as more
  authoritative) — this is the primary diagnostic signal for whether
  TA-2's current-meaning-priority discipline has genuinely generalized
  beyond the specific practice examples.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause before assuming a
  word's origin is single-language, followed by a spontaneous check for
  intermediate borrowing languages, suggests the learner is consciously
  applying TA-3's multi-language-recognition discipline rather than
  defaulting to a simple story — a good sign. Instant, confident
  claims that a word "just comes from" one language with no
  consideration of borrowing is the concerning pattern for MC-EVERY-
  WORD-CAME-FROM-A-SINGLE-CLEAR-LANGUAGE-WITH-NO-BORROWING.
- **Research-independence stakes**: real and central to TA-5/MP-5 —
  this concept's genuine mastery bar includes independent investigation
  using reference material, not just recognizing pre-taught examples;
  the tutor should ensure the learner has access to and practice using
  genuine etymology references (dictionaries with etymology notes)
  rather than relying solely on tutor-provided facts.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "what does this word actually mean
  TODAY, regardless of where it came from?" is this concept's load-
  bearing sentence for MC-A, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` — asked
  whenever a fresh etymological fact is learned, making current-meaning
  confirmation a routinely-invited response rather than an afterthought.
