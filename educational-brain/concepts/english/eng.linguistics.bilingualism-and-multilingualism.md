## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Explain that bilingualism is the global norm for most of the world's population, not a rare or unusual condition.
2. Evaluate a bilingual individual's overall communicative competence holistically across both languages, rather than requiring identical native-like mastery in each language separately.
3. Explain that a bilingual child's code-mixing reflects a normal, well-documented developmental pattern, not confusion or delay.
4. Analyze a new bilingual scenario, evaluating competence holistically and correctly interpreting any code-mixing observed.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept. First, **"real" bilingualism does not require perfectly equal, native-like fluency in both languages considered SEPARATELY and identically** — most bilingual individuals have different strengths in different languages for different domains (stronger vocabulary for home topics in one language, stronger academic vocabulary in the other), a well-documented, normal pattern (differential/domain-specific competence) that should be evaluated holistically across both languages together, not as a deficiency in either. Second, **a bilingual child mixing elements from both languages within a single utterance (code-mixing) is not evidence of confusion or developmental delay** — code-mixing during bilingual acquisition is a normal, well-documented developmental pattern that typically resolves as the child's ability to separate the two systems for different contexts develops with age and experience.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "a true bilingual must be equally fluent in both languages in every way, and a child mixing languages is confused or behind."**
The simulation: judge a bilingual speaker with domain-specific strengths as "not really fluent" in either language, and worry that a young bilingual child's code-mixing signals a developmental problem.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a bilingual speaker fluent in family topics in one language and academic topics in the other, and documented research showing code-mixing as a common, temporary, non-delay-correlated pattern.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Two specialized toolsets, each mastered for its own domain — that's holistic competence, not a deficiency. And two bicycles with different brakes explain temporary mixing, not confusion."

**Intermediate — "I can now evaluate competence holistically and recognize code-mixing as normal, but I still implicitly rank one language as more 'complete' based on prestige or school use."**
The simulation: correctly avoids requiring identical fluency but still treats the school/academic language as the "real" or primary one.
*Upgrade trigger*: the adaptive-flags challenge to any implicit primary/secondary ranking based on prestige rather than genuine holistic evaluation.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Holistic evaluation means neither language is inherently more 'complete' just because it's used in more prestigious domains."

**Advanced — "I evaluate a new bilingual scenario's competence holistically and correctly interpret any code-mixing observed, all together."**
The simulation: integrates holistic-competence evaluation and non-pathologizing code-mixing interpretation into one coherent scenario analysis. Matches TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: `eng.linguistics.translation-studies-intro`'s use of this understanding of how bilingual minds actually process and relate multiple language systems.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the core bilingual-competence toolkit. Translation between languages builds directly on this next."

**Expert — "at fluency, evaluating bilingual competence holistically and reading code-mixing correctly happens as an integrated part of ordinary reasoning about multilingual individuals, without a separate deliberate checklist."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit evaluate-interpret routine is a scaffold for a professional and personal judgment that, at fluency, operates as an integrated habit.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **monolingual educational and testing norms implicitly use a single-language, native-speaker standard as the default measure of "real" fluency**, making a bilingual's uneven, domain-distributed competence look like two incomplete monolingual competencies rather than one complete bilingual one.

The second mechanism is that **code-mixing is visually and audibly salient as "not one language,"** making it easy to interpret as a sign that neither language is being learned properly, rather than recognizing it as a normal, temporary feature of the acquisition process itself.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-A-TRUE-BILINGUAL-MUST-HAVE-EQUAL-NATIVE-LIKE-FLUENCY-IN-BOTH-LANGUAGES-SEPARATELY
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 5 (instruction-induced), from monolingual testing and educational norms implicitly using a single-language native-speaker standard as the default measure of fluency.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **map out which domains a bilingual speaker uses each language for**, since the holistic, domain-distributed reality is far more convincing demonstrated through a concrete domain map than asserted abstractly.
- **Verification of death**: given a bilingual speaker's domain-specific strengths across two languages, the learner evaluates their overall competence holistically rather than requiring identical mastery in each language separately.

### MC-B-A-BILINGUAL-CHILD-MIXING-TWO-LANGUAGES-IN-ONE-SENTENCE-IS-CONFUSED-OR-DELAYED
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from code-mixing being visually and audibly salient as "not one language," making it intuitively look like confusion rather than a normal developmental feature.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **citing documented research showing code-mixing as common, systematic, and temporary**, since the non-pathologizing framing is far more convincing demonstrated through evidence than asserted abstractly.
- **Verification of death**: given three examples of bilingual children's language use, the learner distinguishes normal, temporary code-mixing from a genuine, broader language difficulty unrelated to bilingualism.

## Analogies

**Best — the Two-Toolset-and-Two-Bicycles Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): a person with two specialized tool sets, each mastered for its own domain rather than identically duplicated — directly seeding the fix for MC-A. A child learning to ride two bicycles with different brake systems, temporarily mixing them up before mastering the distinction — directly seeding the fix for MC-B.
*Breaking point*: tool sets and bicycles are physically separable, discrete systems, while a bilingual mind's two language systems are cognitively integrated and interact in more complex, less cleanly separable ways than two distinct physical toolkits.

**Alternative — a professional athlete skilled in two different sports.** An athlete who plays both soccer and basketball at a high level doesn't need identical skill profiles in each — different sports reward different specific strengths, and overall athletic competence is evaluated across both, not by requiring an identical skill set in each sport separately.
*Breaking point*: sports skills are typically evaluated independently for each sport in competition, while bilingual competence is more genuinely integrated and situational (a single conversation can call on both languages), a difference the separate-sports analogy doesn't fully capture.

**Visual anchor — the Two-Toolset-and-Two-Bicycles Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"A real bilingual speaks both languages exactly the same way a native speaker would."** Directly installs the equal-native-fluency misconception as a stated definition.
- **"If a child mixes languages, something's wrong with their language development."** Directly installs the code-mixing-as-delay misconception as a stated definition.

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both facts are directly checkable by mapping domain-specific language use and by comparing documented developmental patterns against a genuine delay.

1. **Need**: "If someone speaks their heritage language fluently at home but prefers their school language for academic topics, are they 'less bilingual' than someone who is identical in both?"
2. **Playground**: the learner freely maps which domains a hypothetical bilingual speaker might use each language for before being told the official holistic-evaluation test.
3. **Invention**: "Why might a bilingual child temporarily mix words from both languages in one sentence?"
4. **Collision**: present a young bilingual child's code-mixing example alongside documented research on its typical, temporary resolution, and ask whether it indicates confusion.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — bilingual competence should be evaluated holistically across domains, and code-mixing is a normal, temporary developmental pattern — from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Evaluate holistically across domains; code-mixing is normal, not confusion."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Bilingualism as the global norm (TA-1) comes first**, establishing the basic reframing before either misconception is directly confronted.
- **Holistic, domain-specific competence (TA-2) comes second**, directly confronting the first misconception once bilingualism is established as common and normal.
- **Code-mixing as normal bilingual development (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the second misconception once holistic evaluation is established as the norm.
- **Full application to a new bilingual scenario (TA-4) comes last**, the generative capstone integrating both misconception fixes — matching this program's established sequencing principle of reserving the most integrative task for the culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for evaluating competence holistically, and for interpreting code-mixing as normal or as warranting further attention**, since a correct-looking judgment with no stated reasoning cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated domain-mapping approach** ("this speaker is strong in family topics in one language and academic topics in the other — that's still full bilingual competence") applied to a new scenario — the direct behavioral signature of the holistic-evaluation fix taking hold.
- **Voluntary non-pathologizing interpretation of code-mixing**, distinguishing it from a genuine, broader difficulty — direct evidence the normal-development fix is internalized.
- **Judging a bilingual speaker as "not really fluent" due to domain-specific strengths, or recommending language restriction to "avoid confusion"** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-A or MC-B still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Evaluate holistically across domains — and code-mixing is normal, not confusion."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But they're better in English than in their home language, so isn't English their 'real' language?"** — take this as an accurate observation about relative strength in one domain, not a mistake; respond by adding the holistic test: "They may be stronger there for academic topics, that's true — but are they weaker overall, or just distributed differently across domains? Evaluate both languages together."
- **"My cousin mixes languages when they talk, is something wrong?"** — a genuine, reasonable concern given how salient code-mixing sounds; respond by naming the additional layer: "That sounds unusual to hear, yes — but is this a well-documented, temporary bilingual pattern, or does it show a broader difficulty affecting both languages equally?"
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full bilingual scenario analysis down to **evaluating one single domain-specific competence profile holistically, with code-mixing interpretation set aside.** Isolates the most basic holistic-evaluation skill before returning to fuller tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just test them separately in each language and average the scores."** This directly licenses the equal-fluency-requirement pattern, undermining the holistic-evaluation discipline this concept specifically builds.
