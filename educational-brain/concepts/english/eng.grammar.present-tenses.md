# Present Tenses — `eng.grammar.present-tenses`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.grammar.present-tenses` (canonical English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / grammar / "Present Tenses" — the
  simple, continuous, perfect, and perfect continuous present-tense
  forms and the distinct temporal/aspectual meaning each one carries.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.grammar.verbs` — load-bearing because tense is
  a property realized ON the verb (via auxiliary + inflection); a
  learner who cannot yet reliably identify the verb in a sentence has no
  stable element to apply tense forms to.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.grammar.past-tenses`,
  `eng.grammar.future-tenses`, `eng.grammar.modals`,
  `eng.grammar.gerunds-and-infinitives`,
  `eng.grammar.subject-verb-agreement`. This concept installs the
  simple/continuous/perfect/perfect-continuous four-form tense
  architecture that the past and future tense concepts directly mirror;
  the perfect-continuous form's have/has-been + -ing structure and the
  present continuous's am/is/are + -ing structure are the first
  auxiliary-plus-nonfinite-verb combinations the learner meets, directly
  preparing `eng.grammar.gerunds-and-infinitives`'s -ing/to-infinitive
  distinctions and `eng.grammar.modals`'s auxiliary-plus-base-verb
  pattern; the third-person singular "-s" agreement in TA-1 is the
  concept's first genuine encounter with subject-verb agreement,
  directly preparing the dedicated agreement concept.
- **Difficulty**: developing · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery threshold**:
  0.75 · **Est. hours**: 3

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Produce and identify simple present forms (including third-person
   singular "-s") for habits, routines, and general truths.
2. Produce and identify present continuous forms (am/is/are + -ing) for
   actions in progress at the moment of speaking.
3. Produce and identify present perfect forms (have/has + past
   participle) for unspecified-time experiences and present-relevant
   past actions.
4. Discriminate present perfect from simple past using the specific-
   time-marker test.
5. Produce and identify present perfect continuous forms (have/has been
   + -ing) for ongoing-duration actions that began in the past and
   continue now.

## Core Understanding

"Present tense" does not mean "happening right now" — it is an umbrella
label covering four distinct forms, each answering a different question
about how an action relates to time. Simple present ("she works")
answers "is this a habit, routine, or general truth?" — it is
specifically NOT the form for describing an action in progress this
instant. Present continuous ("she is working") answers "is this
happening at this exact moment?" Present perfect ("she has worked")
answers "is this a past action or experience whose time is unspecified,
or whose effect matters right now?" — critically, it is barred from
combining with a specific past-time marker ("in 2019," "yesterday"),
which is what separates it cleanly from simple past. Present perfect
continuous ("she has been working") answers "is this an action that
started in the past and is STILL ongoing, with the duration itself being
the point?" All four forms are genuinely "present" in the sense that
each one is anchored to, or relevant to, the moment of speaking — they
differ in HOW they connect to that moment, not in whether they do.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "present tense means it's happening right now, so
  I use the same form regardless of whether I mean a habit or an
  in-progress action"**: the arriving, pre-instructional model this
  concept exists to correct — a natural default because "present" is
  taken at its most literal, everyday meaning, and because many world
  languages use a single present form for both habitual and in-progress
  meaning, giving no internal signal that English splits this into two
  forms.
- **Intermediate model — "I distinguish habits/general truths (simple
  present) from actions happening right now (present continuous)"**:
  installed by TA-1 and TA-2, using the Component 3 timeline anchor's
  dots-vs-point distinction, resolving
  MC-SIMPLE-PRESENT-MEANS-HAPPENING-RIGHT-NOW. Upgrade trigger:
  correctly choosing continuous over simple present for a fresh
  in-progress scenario without prompting.
- **Advanced model — "I can produce present perfect for experiences and
  present-relevant past actions, and I recognize it connects a past
  event to now"**: installed by TA-3, extending the timeline anchor with
  the past-to-now arrow shape.
- **Expert model — "I discriminate present perfect from simple past
  using the specific-time-marker test, and I produce present perfect
  continuous for ongoing-duration actions"**: installed by TA-4
  (resolving MC-PRESENT-PERFECT-IS-JUST-PAST-TENSE) and TA-5, the
  concept's genuine mastery bar — checking for a stated or implied
  specific time marker as a concrete, applicable rule rather than
  relying on an abstract felt sense of "connection to the present."
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own Protocol Routing, for
  S9 learners whose L1 lacks a present-perfect-equivalent distinction
  (many languages merge it with simple past) or lacks grammatical
  continuous-aspect marking, expect TA-2's continuous forms and
  especially TA-3/TA-4's present-perfect-vs-past distinction to require
  substantial, ongoing dedicated practice — these are well-documented,
  persistent transfer challenges across proficiency levels, not minor
  details.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here. The
happening-right-now failure exists because "present tense" is, quite
reasonably, taken at its literal meaning by a learner meeting English
tense terminology for the first time, and because many world languages
use one present form to cover both habitual and in-progress meaning —
so a learner reasonably extends "present tense = now" until directly
shown that English specifically reserves a SEPARATE form (continuous)
for in-progress meaning, with the plain "present" form instead reserved
for habits and general truths. The present-perfect-as-past failure
exists because both present perfect and simple past genuinely describe
something that happened before the moment of speaking, making them feel
functionally interchangeable at first contact — nothing marks the
present perfect's specific "connects to now, and therefore cannot
combine with a specific past-time marker" restriction until a learner
directly tries (and fails) to attach a specific date to it.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.grammar.present-tenses.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full
trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content — reused by
reference below, with birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-SIMPLE-PRESENT-MEANS-HAPPENING-RIGHT-NOW (Blueprint Component 1) —
Type 1, overgeneralization (with a Type 3 language-contamination
component for L1s lacking the habitual/in-progress split)**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: primarily Type 1 — "present tense" is a
  reasonable, literal extension of the word "present" itself, and the
  learner's early exposure to the term gives no signal that English
  requires a separate continuous form for in-progress meaning. For
  learners whose L1 uses one present form for both meanings, the
  misconception also carries a Type 3 (language contamination)
  component — the L1's own grammatical structure is being directly
  transferred, not merely guessed.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when asked to
  describe an action happening at this exact moment, spontaneously
  self-corrects from a simple-present attempt to "wait — I AM doing it"
  is showing the habitual-vs-in-progress split has been genuinely
  internalized.

**MC-PRESENT-PERFECT-IS-JUST-PAST-TENSE (Blueprint Component 1) — Type
1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: exact Type 1 signature — both present
  perfect and simple past genuinely describe something that happened
  before now, so a learner reasonably treats them as interchangeable
  until directly shown the specific-time-marker restriction that
  separates them; the misconception survives on strong partial evidence
  (both forms genuinely work for many "before now" statements) rather
  than a wholesale category error.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, when given a
  sentence with a specific time marker like "yesterday" or "in 2019,"
  spontaneously reaches for simple past without being told the marker
  is the deciding factor is showing the specific-time-marker test has
  generalized beyond the specific TA-4 practice pairs.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the tense timeline itself is the analogy, not a
  supplement to it** (Blueprint Component 3): drawing a "NOW" point and
  representing simple present as repeating dots (a pattern spread across
  the whole line, not tied to one moment), present continuous as a
  single highlighted point exactly at "NOW," and present perfect as an
  arrow starting in the past and pointing TO "now," directly embodies
  each form's distinct temporal shape rather than treating the four
  forms as an arbitrary list of endings to memorize. Breaking point: the
  timeline's three shapes (dots/point/arrow) do not on their own
  distinguish present perfect from simple past — both could plausibly be
  drawn as "something before now" — so the timeline anchor must be
  paired with the separate, explicit specific-time-marker test (TA-4)
  for that particular discrimination.
- **Alternative — a photograph vs. a security-camera recording vs. a
  scar**: a photograph (simple present) is a repeatable, non-time-bound
  representation of a general truth or pattern; a security-camera
  recording playing live (present continuous) shows an action unfolding
  right now; a scar (present perfect) is visible evidence, right now, of
  something that happened at an unspecified past time. Breaking point:
  the scar image communicates present-relevance well but does not by
  itself convey the "no specific time marker allowed" restriction,
  which still needs the explicit TA-4 test.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT describe present perfect as "a slightly more
  formal or old-fashioned way to say simple past"**: this framing is a
  common but incorrect simplification that directly reinforces
  MC-PRESENT-PERFECT-IS-JUST-PAST-TENSE by implying the two forms are
  stylistic variants of the same meaning rather than genuinely different
  meanings (unspecified-time/present-relevance vs. specific completed
  past time); present perfect and simple past are not interchangeable
  register choices and should never be presented as such.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/visual (the tense timeline)**: the Blueprint's Component 3
  anchor — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any daily routine supplies real TA-1 simple-
  present material; anything the learner is doing at the moment of the
  lesson (reading, sitting, listening) supplies real TA-2 present-
  continuous material; any genuine unspecified-time personal experience
  (places visited, foods tried) supplies real TA-3 present-perfect
  material.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-4's discrimination task (choosing
  present perfect vs. simple past based on whether a specific time
  marker is present) is inherently hands-on judgment practice, not mere
  recognition of a stated fact.
- **Teacher-demo (the marker-attachment test)**: the tutor models the
  Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstration — attempting to add
  "in 2019" to "I have visited Paris" and showing why it breaks,
  contrasted against "I visited Paris in 2019," which works — making the
  specific-time-marker restriction directly audible and testable rather
  than an abstract rule to memorize.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a tense-
  form identification or production attempt (TA-1 through TA-5), ask the
  learner to state their own attempt before the tutor confirms —
  Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** The
habitual-vs-in-progress split and the specific-time-marker test are both
genuine, directly testable phenomena well-suited to guided discovery:
**need** (the learner, having secured simple present with TA-1, is
asked to describe what they are doing at this exact moment, discovering
the simple-present form feels wrong); **playground/invention** (TA-1's
habit/routine practice and TA-2's in-progress practice build initial
confidence with both forms independently before their contrast is
named); **collision** (the Blueprint's own "I eat dinner" while eating
right now probe breaks the happening-right-now assumption; the "I have
visited Paris in 2019" marker-attachment probe breaks the present-
perfect-as-past assumption); **formalization** (MP-5's own-words
statement of the three-way distinction between simple present, present
continuous, and present perfect); **compression** (TA-5's fluent
production of present perfect continuous, treating it as a natural
extension of the now-secure perfect-aspect concept rather than a fifth
unrelated form to separately memorize). The specific terms "simple
present," "present continuous," "present perfect," and "present perfect
continuous" are convention-bound grammatical terminology best introduced
by direct instruction once the learner has already felt the underlying
temporal distinctions through the timeline anchor and TA-1–3.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (simple present for habits and general truths) is placed first as
the concept's most stable, least contested entry point, per the
Blueprint's own session architecture — establishing the habitual/general-
truth form, including third-person "-s" agreement, before any contrast
is introduced. TA-2 (present continuous for in-progress actions) follows
immediately, directly targeting
MC-SIMPLE-PRESENT-MEANS-HAPPENING-RIGHT-NOW — per the Blueprint's own
session architecture, this conflict-evidence probe is placed right after
TA-2, once the learner has secure simple-present practice to contrast
against. TA-3 (present perfect for experiences and present relevance)
extends the concept into the past-to-now connection, building on the
now-secure present-tense forms. TA-4 (present perfect vs. simple past
discrimination) is placed next, directly targeting
MC-PRESENT-PERFECT-IS-JUST-PAST-TENSE, addressing this SECOND,
independent dimension (a discrimination against an entirely different
tense, not another present form) once present perfect itself is secure.
TA-5 (present perfect continuous for ongoing duration) is placed last as
the concept's integration capstone, extending the perfect aspect the
learner already has with the continuous aspect they already have from
TA-2, converging both prior distinctions into the most structurally
complex of the four forms. This entry does not restate the Blueprint's
own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see Blueprint References
below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner spontaneously reaches for
  present continuous, not simple present, when describing an action
  happening at the moment of speaking, and whether the learner
  spontaneously checks for a specific time marker before choosing
  between present perfect and simple past — these are the primary
  diagnostic signals for whether TA-2 and TA-4's disciplines have
  genuinely generalized beyond the specific practice items.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause before choosing between
  present perfect and simple past, followed by a correct, marker-
  consistent choice, suggests the learner is consciously applying the
  time-marker test rather than guessing — a good sign. Instant,
  confident use of present perfect immediately followed by a specific
  time marker (without self-correction) is the concerning pattern for
  MC-PRESENT-PERFECT-IS-JUST-PAST-TENSE.
- **Native-intuition-gap stakes**: real and specifically named in the
  Blueprint's own Protocol Routing — for S9 learners whose L1 lacks a
  present-perfect-equivalent distinction or continuous-aspect marking,
  reassure that this is a well-documented, persistent challenge across
  proficiency levels, not a sign of weak general English ability, and
  budget substantial ongoing practice specifically for these two forms.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "can you add a specific date or time to
  that sentence?" is this concept's load-bearing sentence for
  MC-PRESENT-PERFECT-IS-JUST-PAST-TENSE, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` — asked
  whenever a fresh present-perfect-vs-past discrimination item is
  introduced, making the marker test a routinely-invited response
  rather than an afterthought.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedural/framework — a four-form tense-and-aspect
system (simple/continuous/perfect/perfect-continuous) applied to
genuinely novel contexts and time frames; mastery is demonstrated by
correctly producing and discriminating among the forms for fresh
scenarios (MP-1 through MP-4) rather than by recognizing a fixed,
previously-taught example list. Interleaving partners: mix production
items (testing TA-1/TA-2/TA-3/TA-5) with discrimination items (testing
TA-4) in ongoing review, so the learner continues exercising both the
generative and the judgment dimensions of the tense system.

  deliberation, since misuse of present perfect (especially attaching a
  specific time marker) remains a noticeable marker of non-native
  production even at otherwise advanced proficiency.
