## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Reject appearance-based credibility judgment, choosing a plain but well-substantiated source over a polished but unsubstantiated one.
2. Evaluate individual claims within one source rather than assigning one blanket credibility label to the whole source.
3. Apply a four-question framework (authorship, evidence, currency, purpose/bias) systematically.
4. Identify the need for and describe a cross-check against an independent source.
5. Produce a comparative credibility assessment of two novel sources on the same topic.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept, extending `eng.reading.critical-reading`'s single-citation evaluation to whole sources. First, **credibility is substance-based, not appearance-based** — professional design, an official-sounding name, and polished formatting are easy to fake and expensive to produce even with false content, so genuine credibility must be checked through authorship (qualifications), evidence (specific, cited support), and track record (established accuracy over time), never through visual polish alone. Second, **credibility is not a binary, all-or-nothing label applied to an entire source** — it varies by degree and by specific claim, since even a generally reliable source can get one claim wrong or under-sourced outside its area of expertise, and even a generally biased source can accurately report simple, verifiable facts; evaluation must happen claim-by-claim, not as a blanket judgment. Both corrections replace a simple heuristic (looks official; is either trustworthy or not) with the genuinely evaluative discipline `eng.reading.critical-reading` already established, now scaled up to whole sources.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "a source is credible if it looks professional or official; a source is either completely reliable or completely unreliable."**
The simulation: judge credibility by visual polish, and stamp one fixed reliability label on an entire source regardless of which specific claim is being evaluated.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a slick, anonymous, unsourced site versus a plain, credentialed, cited one, asked which looks credible versus which actually is; and a reputable source's one poorly-sourced claim outside its expertise alongside a biased source's accurate sports score, asked whether "trustworthy"/"untrustworthy" are the only two categories.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Check substance (authorship, evidence, track record), not appearance. Evaluate claim-by-claim, not source-by-source as one blanket label."

**Intermediate — "I check substance now, but I still tend to trust or distrust an entire source uniformly once I've formed an impression of it."**
The simulation: correctly resists appearance-based judgment but hasn't yet reliably applied claim-specific evaluation within a single source.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-3's direct practice with one source making claims of varying quality, requiring individual evaluation of each.
*Shelf-life warning*: "A source's general reputation is useful context, but it doesn't automatically transfer to every individual claim it makes."

**Advanced — "I apply the four-question framework, evaluate claims individually, cross-check important claims, and produce a comparative credibility assessment on novel sources."**
The simulation: integrates substance-checking, claim-specific evaluation, and cross-checking into one coherent comparative judgment. Matches TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: the two cross-linked concepts — reading across genres and media literacy — the latter applying this same degree-based, substance-over-surface evaluation to a wider range of media formats.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the framework for written sources. Media literacy applies the same degree-based evaluation across images, video, and other formats."

**Expert — "at fluency, substance-checking and claim-specific evaluation happen as an integrated part of ordinary information consumption."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit four-question checklist is a scaffold for a habit that, at true fluency, is inseparable from everyday reading and research.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **visual and presentational cues are the fastest, most immediately available signal**, while checking authorship, evidence, and track record requires deliberate effort most casual reading doesn't invest — the same mechanism identified for citation-presence trust in `eng.reading.critical-reading`, now operating at the whole-source level.

The second mechanism is that **binary trust categories ("reliable source" / "fake news") are common in everyday media discourse**, reinforcing an all-or-nothing framework that is cognitively simpler than tracking credibility as a continuous, claim-specific variable.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-SOURCE-IS-CREDIBLE-IF-IT-LOOKS-PROFESSIONAL-OR-OFFICIAL
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry — the polished-anonymous/plain-credentialed discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from visual polish functioning as a fast, surface authority signal, the same mechanism identified for citation presence in the prerequisite concept, now scaled to whole-source design.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct side-by-side comparison of a polished-but-unsubstantiated source against a plain-but-substantiated one**, since the surface signal is powerful enough to require an equally vivid counter-demonstration.
- **Verification of death**: given such a pair, the learner correctly judges the plain source as more credible using substance criteria.

### MC-A-SOURCE-IS-EITHER-COMPLETELY-RELIABLE-OR-COMPLETELY-UNRELIABLE
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — the reputable-source's-weak-claim/biased-source's-accurate-fact discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable practice of forming a general reputation judgment about a source, extended into treating that general judgment as uniformly applicable to every individual claim the source makes.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair supplies the **dial-not-switch reframing**, since the fix requires replacing a binary category with a continuous, claim-specific one, not merely softening the binary judgment.
- **Verification of death**: given one source making claims of varying quality, the learner evaluates each claim individually rather than assigning one blanket label.

## Analogies

**Best — the Credibility Dial, Not a Switch** (Blueprint Component 3): a turnable dial (not an on/off switch) set per specific claim based on evidence, directly seeding the binary-label fix; a separate "surface polish meter" pointing in a different direction from the credibility dial for the same source, directly seeding the appearance-based fix.
*Breaking point*: a physical dial has one objectively readable position, while a source's credibility is often a matter of degree open to reasonable disagreement — useful for the continuous-not-binary intuition, less precise for implying credibility is as precisely measurable as a dial reading.

**Alternative — a witness's testimony in different areas of expertise.** An expert witness might be highly credible testifying within their specialty and far less credible testifying about an unrelated field — the same witness, different credibility depending on the specific claim.
*Breaking point*: courtroom expertise boundaries are relatively clear-cut, while a media source's "area of expertise" for a given claim is often less clearly bounded.

**Visual anchor — the Credibility Dial, Not a Switch**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"If it looks professional, you can trust it."** Directly installs the appearance-based misconception as a stated rule.
- **"Once you know a source is biased/unreliable, ignore everything it says."** Directly installs the binary-label misconception as a stated rule.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Credibility Dial, Not a Switch (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Will this source's dial be high or low — and does the surface-polish meter agree?" Then test several sources.
2. **The polished-anonymous/plain-credentialed collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "Which looks more credible? Which actually is?"
3. **The reputable-weak-claim/biased-accurate-fact collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Are 'trustworthy' and 'untrustworthy' the only two categories here?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both evaluative facts are directly checkable by comparing real sources against the four-question framework.

1. **Need**: "If a website looks official and polished, does that tell you anything about whether its claims are actually true?"
2. **Playground**: the learner turns the credibility dial for several source examples freely before being told the official four-question framework.
3. **Invention**: "Can you find one claim in this source that's more credible than another claim in the SAME source?"
4. **Collision**: present a reputable source's one weak claim alongside a biased source's accurate fact; ask whether "trustworthy"/"untrustworthy" are the only two categories.
5. **Formalization**: name the four-question framework and the dial-not-switch principle from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Check substance, not surface — and judge claim by claim, not source by source."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Substance over surface checks (TA-1) comes first**, directly confronting the appearance-based misconception before the systematic framework is introduced.
- **The four-question framework (TA-2) comes second**, installing the systematic evaluative procedure once the surface-vs-substance distinction is secure.
- **Claim-specific, degree-based evaluation (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the binary-labeling misconception.
- **Cross-checking (TA-4) and full comparative evaluation (TA-5) come last**, extending the framework to verification practice and integrating everything into a generative capstone — matching this program's established sequencing principle.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for a source or claim-level credibility judgment**, since a correct-looking judgment with no articulated substance-check or claim-specific reasoning cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated substance check** ("who wrote this, and are they qualified? what's the actual evidence?") applied to a new source, independent of its visual design — direct evidence the appearance-based fix has taken hold.
- **Different credibility judgments for two different claims from the SAME source** — the direct behavioral signature of the binary-labeling fix.
- **A credibility verdict based solely on how "official" or professional a source looks**, with no substance check — the direct behavioral signature of MC-A-SOURCE-IS-CREDIBLE-IF-IT-LOOKS-PROFESSIONAL-OR-OFFICIAL still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Check substance, not surface — and judge each claim on its own, not the whole source as one blanket label."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads:

- **Rejecting appearance-based judgment (MP-1) and evaluating claims individually (MP-2).** Per the Blueprint's S9 routing, mastery must NOT be certified unless both pass — a learner who applies the four-question framework correctly (MP-3) but still judges by appearance or uses blanket labels has not learned the core evaluative discipline.
- **Applying the four-question framework (MP-3).** Confirms the systematic procedure is internalized.
- **Cross-checking (MP-4).** Confirms recognition of when independent verification is needed.
- **Full comparative evaluation (MP-5).** The synthesis item; listen for genuinely different treatment of the two sources' distinct strengths and weaknesses, not a templated identical assessment applied twice.

**Mastery certification trigger**: correct substance-based preference for a plain, well-substantiated source; correct individual evaluation of claims within one source; systematic four-question application to a new source; correct identification of a needed cross-check; a full comparative credibility assessment of two novel sources.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But this website looks so professional, it must be legit"** — take this as a reasonable inference from a genuinely powerful, fast surface signal; respond with the substance check: "Polish is cheap to fake. Who's the author? What evidence do they cite? Let's check those instead."
- **"This source is biased, so nothing it says can be trusted"** — a reasonable-sounding overcorrection; respond by narrowing the judgment: "Even a biased source can get simple facts right. Let's check THIS specific claim, not the source's whole reputation."
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full comparative evaluation down to **applying the substance check to a single, clearly contrasting pair (one polished/unsubstantiated, one plain/substantiated), with no framework or cross-check task demanded.** Isolates the most basic distinction before returning to fuller evaluation.
- **Never shrink to "just check if it looks trustworthy."** This directly re-licenses the appearance-based misconception this concept specifically works to dismantle.
