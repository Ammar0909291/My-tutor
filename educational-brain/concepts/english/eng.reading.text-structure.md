# Text Structure — `eng.reading.text-structure`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.reading.text-structure` (canonical English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / reading / "Text Structure" —
  recognizing organizational patterns in text, including narrative
  sequence, cause-effect, compare-contrast, problem-solution, and
  description.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.reading.main-idea-and-details` — load-bearing
  because text-structure vocabulary is only meaningful once the learner
  can already notice how details relate to each other and to a main
  idea, per the Blueprint's own Protocol Routing (S0) — the five
  structural patterns are simply names for detail-relationship types
  the learner must already be able to informally describe before
  formal structure vocabulary adds value.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): `eng.reading.genre-recognition`,
  `eng.reading.compare-and-contrast-texts`. Per the Blueprint's own
  Component 8 Adaptive Flags, genre conventions often correlate with
  characteristic structural patterns (persuasive texts frequently use
  problem-solution), giving a direct bridge from this concept to genre
  recognition, while text comparison specifically extends this
  concept's compare-contrast pattern to comparing entire texts against
  each other.
- **Difficulty**: proficient · **Bloom**: analyze · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.75 · **Est. hours**: 3

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify a passage's organizational pattern (sequence, cause-effect,
   compare-contrast, problem-solution, or description) based on the
   logical relationship between ideas, independent of paragraph count
   or formatting.
2. Recognize the characteristic signal words associated with each
   structural pattern and use them as supporting evidence for a
   pattern identification.
3. Distinguish closely related patterns — sequence from cause-effect,
   and compare-contrast from description — using precise structural
   criteria rather than surface similarity.
4. Identify multiple distinct structural patterns within different
   sections of a single, longer passage, rather than forcing one
   overall label onto the whole text.
5. Construct an original passage that deliberately follows an assigned
   structural pattern, then self-verify it against signal-word and
   pattern criteria.

## Core Understanding

Text structure is not a matter of formatting — paragraph breaks are
about where a writer chose to start a new visual block on the page,
while text structure is about how the IDEAS underneath are logically
connected. Five recurring patterns capture the ways ideas can relate:
SEQUENCE (time order, no causal claim), CAUSE-EFFECT (one thing
specifically produces the next), COMPARE-CONTRAST (two or more subjects
set against each other), PROBLEM-SOLUTION (a difficulty and its fix),
and DESCRIPTION (features or qualities of one subject, no chain of
events). A single unbroken paragraph — even a single sentence — can
follow one of these patterns just as clearly as a multi-paragraph
essay; formatting and structure are independent dimensions. A second,
equally load-bearing insight concerns the SCOPE at which a pattern
applies: short, simple passages often use one pattern consistently
throughout, but longer or more complex texts commonly COMBINE multiple
patterns, with different sections doing different organizational jobs
— a passage that explains why a lake became polluted (cause-effect) and
then describes the cleanup solutions being tried (problem-solution) is
not malformed or ambiguous; it is legitimately using two patterns in
two sections. Forcing a single overall label onto such a passage
misrepresents its actual organization; the accurate approach is to
check each section separately and name each section's pattern rather
than defaulting to one label for the whole.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "text structure is about where the paragraph
  breaks are, and a whole passage has exactly one structure"**: the
  arriving, pre-instructional model this concept exists to correct —
  two related defaults (formatting-as-structure, and single-pattern-
  only) that both substitute a surface shortcut for the actual logical-
  relationship analysis this concept requires, per the Blueprint's own
  Protocol Routing (S1).
- **Intermediate model — "I identify a passage's pattern by the logical
  relationship between its ideas, regardless of paragraph count, using
  characteristic signal words as evidence"**: installed by TA-1
  (single-paragraph pattern identification) and TA-2 (signal words per
  pattern) using the Five Blueprint Shapes anchor, resolving MC-TEXT-
  STRUCTURE-IS-JUST-ABOUT-PARAGRAPH-BREAKS. Upgrade trigger: correctly
  identifying a cause-effect pattern in a single, unbroken paragraph
  without hesitating over the absence of paragraph breaks.
- **Advanced model — "I precisely distinguish closely related patterns
  — sequence from cause-effect, compare-contrast from description —
  using structural criteria, not surface similarity"**: installed by
  TA-3 (distinguishing similar patterns), deepening the pattern-
  identification judgment beyond the five-pattern basics.
- **Advanced-continued model — "I check each section of a longer
  passage separately rather than forcing one overall structural
  label"**: installed by TA-4 (multi-pattern passages) using the
  anchor's multi-room-house extension, resolving MC-A-TEXT-CAN-ONLY-
  HAVE-ONE-STRUCTURE-PATTERN.
- **Expert model — "I construct an original passage deliberately
  following an assigned structural pattern, and I self-verify it
  against signal-word and pattern criteria"**: installed by TA-5
  (constructing a passage to a specified structure), the concept's
  genuine mastery bar — moving from recognizing given patterns to
  independently generating text that embodies one.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own Protocol Routing
  (S9), mastery must NOT be certified unless MP-1 (single-paragraph
  identification) and MP-2 (multi-pattern identification) both pass — a
  learner who identifies single-pattern passages correctly (MP-3) but
  cannot handle a multi-pattern passage (MP-2) has not yet learned the
  full flexibility this concept requires for longer, real-world texts.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, and the Blueprint
itself explicitly frames both as ways of substituting a surface
shortcut for the actual logical-relationship analysis this concept
requires. The paragraph-breaks-are-structure failure (MC-TEXT-
STRUCTURE-IS-JUST-ABOUT-PARAGRAPH-BREAKS) exists because paragraph
breaks ARE a genuinely visible, easy-to-notice feature of a text,
making them an attractive but ultimately superficial proxy for the
much less visible, more effortful task of analyzing how ideas are
logically connected; nothing marks the gap between formatting and
structure until the learner is shown a single unbroken paragraph that
clearly follows a real structural pattern. The single-pattern-only
failure (MC-A-TEXT-CAN-ONLY-HAVE-ONE-STRUCTURE-PATTERN) exists because
early, simple practice passages ARE typically constructed to use one
pattern consistently — a learner who has only encountered such
passages reasonably concludes that "the structure" is a single,
whole-passage property; the assumption breaks down specifically on
longer or more complex texts, which the learner has not yet had reason
to classify section-by-section.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.reading.text-structure.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-TEXT-STRUCTURE-IS-JUST-ABOUT-PARAGRAPH-BREAKS (Blueprint
Component 1) — Type 2, perceptual intuition**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 2, not Type 1 — this is a genuine
  surface-visibility default rather than an overextension of a
  previously-correct rule: paragraph breaks are a directly perceptible,
  visual feature of any text, while logical relationships between ideas
  require active analytical reading to notice at all; in the absence of
  a prior correct rule to over-extend, a learner reaching for the most
  visually salient feature (formatting) is a perceptual, not logical,
  shortcut.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, given a single
  unbroken paragraph, spontaneously identifies its logical pattern
  ("this is cause-effect — the rain caused the river to rise, which
  caused the flood") without hesitating over the lack of paragraph
  breaks is showing the formatting-vs-structure distinction has been
  internalized.

**MC-A-TEXT-CAN-ONLY-HAVE-ONE-STRUCTURE-PATTERN (Blueprint Component
1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 1 — the single-pattern rule
  genuinely IS correct for short, simple passages, which constitute a
  learner's typical early practice material, so extending "a passage
  has one structure" to all texts is a reasonable extension of real,
  partial evidence; the overgeneralization surfaces specifically on
  longer or more complex texts, which combine patterns across sections
  in a way the learner's early, single-pattern practice never modeled.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, given a two-
  section passage, spontaneously states "the first part uses cause-
  effect and the second part uses problem-solution" rather than forcing
  one overall label is showing the multi-pattern-recognition insight
  has generalized past the single-pattern default.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the Five Blueprint Shapes and Multi-Room-House
  combined anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): five distinct shape cards
  (an arrow chain for sequence, a domino chain for cause-effect, a
  side-by-side scale for compare-contrast, a broken-then-fixed gear for
  problem-solution, a snapshot/camera for description) make the five
  patterns concretely distinguishable by their underlying logical
  shape rather than by paragraph count — directly seeding MC-TEXT-
  STRUCTURE-IS-JUST-ABOUT-PARAGRAPH-BREAKS's fix. The multi-room-house
  extension — one floor plan containing rooms of different shapes —
  directly seeds MC-A-TEXT-CAN-ONLY-HAVE-ONE-STRUCTURE-PATTERN's fix:
  one passage can legitimately contain sections of different structural
  shapes, just as one house can contain rooms of different shapes.
  Breaking point: the anchor demonstrates both core insights clearly at
  the conceptual level, but TA-2's signal-word vocabulary and TA-3's
  close-pair discrimination (sequence vs. cause-effect, compare-
  contrast vs. description) need their own dedicated, example-rich
  practice beyond the shape-matching anchor alone.
- **Alternative — a recipe vs. a floor plan vs. a Venn diagram**: a
  sequence passage reads like a recipe's numbered steps; a compare-
  contrast passage reads like a Venn diagram's two overlapping circles;
  a description passage reads like a single photograph's caption.
  Breaking point: useful for building quick pattern-recognition
  intuition, but doesn't on its own address the multi-pattern insight
  (a single document rarely mixes a recipe with a Venn diagram), which
  needs the house anchor's own explicit multi-section framing.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT teach text structure by asking "how many
  paragraphs does this have, and what's in each one?"**: this framing
  directly reinforces MC-TEXT-STRUCTURE-IS-JUST-ABOUT-PARAGRAPH-BREAKS
  by anchoring the analysis to paragraph count from the start; instead,
  per the Blueprint's own replacement text, the correct approach is to
  ignore paragraph breaks entirely at first and ask directly what
  logical relationship connects the ideas — time order, causation,
  comparison, problem-and-fix, or feature description.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/visual (the Five Blueprint Shapes and Multi-Room-House)**:
  the Blueprint's Component 3 anchor — canonical, referenced not
  restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any short news article, recipe, or set of
  instructions the learner has access to supplies genuine TA-1/TA-2
  material — identify its structural pattern and underline any
  characteristic signal words present; no special materials needed.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-5's generative capstone
  (writing an original passage following an assigned structural
  pattern, then self-checking it against signal-word and shape-card
  criteria) is inherently hands-on, requiring the learner to produce
  and verify text embodying a pattern rather than merely recognize one.
- **Teacher-demo (the removed-signal-word test)**: the tutor models
  the Blueprint's own WE-2 demonstration directly — comparing "She woke
  up, brushed her teeth, and ate breakfast" (sequence) with "She
  overslept, so she skipped breakfast and rushed out the door"
  (cause-effect), and asking whether removing the causal signal word
  ("so") would change the passage's actual claim, making the sequence-
  vs-cause-effect distinction directly testable.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  pattern-identification, signal-word, close-pair-discrimination,
  multi-pattern, or construction attempt (TA-1 through TA-5), ask the
  learner to state their own attempt before the tutor confirms —
  Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** Both the
formatting-vs-structure insight and the multi-pattern-recognition
insight are genuinely felt, directly testable phenomena well-suited to
guided discovery: **need** (the learner is given a single, unbroken
paragraph clearly following a cause-effect pattern and asked to
identify its structure, discovering — when they initially answer "there
isn't one, it's just one paragraph" — that the logical chain of
causation is present regardless of formatting); **playground/invention**
(TA-1's single-paragraph pattern-matching and TA-2's signal-word
practice build initial confidence with the five patterns before the
multi-pattern complication is introduced); **collision** (the
Blueprint's own two-paragraph lake-pollution passage — cause-effect in
paragraph 1, problem-solution in paragraph 2 — directly breaks the
single-pattern assumption when the learner is asked to name "the"
structure of the whole passage and struggles to fit both paragraphs
under one label); **formalization** (the logical-relationship-not-
formatting framing for MC-TEXT-STRUCTURE-IS-JUST-ABOUT-PARAGRAPH-
BREAKS, and the check-each-section-separately framing for MC-A-TEXT-
CAN-ONLY-HAVE-ONE-STRUCTURE-PATTERN); **compression** (TA-5's fluent
original-passage construction to a specified pattern, applied without
each pattern separately re-derived from scratch).

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (single-paragraph pattern identification) is placed first,
directly targeting MC-TEXT-STRUCTURE-IS-JUST-ABOUT-PARAGRAPH-BREAKS,
per the Blueprint's own session architecture — using single-paragraph
examples deliberately, one per pattern, to prove pattern identification
works independent of paragraph count from the very first activity.
TA-2 (signal words per pattern) supplies concrete, checkable textual
evidence for each pattern, giving the learner a second, corroborating
signal beyond the logical-relationship judgment alone. TA-3
(distinguishing similar patterns) sharpens the judgment on the two
closest-confused pairs (sequence vs. cause-effect, compare-contrast vs.
description) once the five basic patterns are individually secure.
TA-4 (multi-pattern passages) directly targets MC-A-TEXT-CAN-ONLY-
HAVE-ONE-STRUCTURE-PATTERN, introduced only once single-pattern
identification and close-pair discrimination are both reliable —
applying the same per-pattern judgment section-by-section rather than
to a whole passage at once. TA-5 (constructing a passage to a specified
structure) is placed last as the concept's genuine generative capstone,
requiring the learner to produce, not merely recognize, text embodying
an assigned pattern, then self-verify it against the same signal-word
and shape criteria built throughout the sequence. This entry does not
restate the Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7)
— see Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner spontaneously names a
  structural pattern's logical relationship (not its paragraph count)
  when asked to identify structure, and whether the learner
  spontaneously checks a longer passage section-by-section rather than
  assigning one overall label immediately — these are the primary
  diagnostic signals for whether TA-1 and TA-4's disciplines have
  genuinely generalized.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause to mentally trace the
  logical relationship between ideas (rather than counting paragraphs)

  structure rather than defaulting to formatting — a good sign. Instant

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedural/framework — a logical-relationship
classification system (five structural patterns, each with
characteristic signal words) applied section-by-section to genuinely
novel passages, including generative construction; mastery is
demonstrated by correctly identifying patterns in fresh, previously-
