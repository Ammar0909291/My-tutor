## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify and name all three prosodic components (stress, rhythm, intonation) co-occurring in a given utterance, never intonation alone.
2. Identify the grammatical/structural function a given utterance's prosody signals (question type, list phrasing, contrastive emphasis).
3. Identify the attitudinal or emotional function a given utterance's prosody conveys, independent of or layered on top of its grammatical function.
4. Detect sarcasm or irony from prosodic delivery, correctly identifying an intended meaning that conflicts with the utterance's literal words.
5. Produce a full prosodic analysis of an extended, natural utterance, integrating stress pattern, rhythmic structure, intonation contour, grammatical function, and attitudinal/emotional colouring.

## Core Understanding

Prosody is the **combined, integrated system of stress, rhythm, and intonation working together** — not a synonym for any one of the three, and specifically not a synonym for intonation alone, which is the single most common narrowing this concept must correct. Each component contributes independently and simultaneously to an utterance's overall effect: stress marks which words or syllables carry prominence; rhythm governs the timing pattern across the utterance; intonation traces the pitch contour. The concept's second major structural fact is that prosody performs **two genuinely distinct functions simultaneously**, and analysing only one is a systematic under-analysis: a **grammatical/structural function**, signalling things like question type, list phrasing, or which information is new versus already-known (largely the content this program's prior phonetics concepts have already covered); and an **attitudinal/emotional function**, independently conveying sarcasm, boredom, genuine interest, doubt, excitement, or warmth — often through exaggerated pitch range, unusual stress placement, or distinctive rhythm that goes beyond anything the grammatical signalling alone would require. These two functions can align or conflict: prosody's single most striking demonstration of its own communicative power is **sarcasm**, where the attitudinal layer directly *overrides* the literal meaning of the words themselves ("That's just great," delivered with flat, exaggerated, mocking prosody, means the opposite of what the words literally state) — a case where correctly understanding an utterance requires attending to prosody as the primary, decisive signal, not a secondary confirmation of what the words already say.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "prosody is basically the same thing as intonation — the rise and fall of pitch."**
The runnable simulation: analyse an utterance's pitch contour and treat that analysis as a complete prosodic description. This is not tutor-installed — it is a reasonable consequence of intonation being taught first, and being the most visually traceable, most concretely anchored of the three components (via the pitch-line drawing technique already established at `eng.phonetics.intonation-patterns`), making it the natural default when a learner is asked to "describe the prosody" without yet appreciating that two further, equally real components exist alongside it.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — correctly describing an utterance's rising-then-falling pitch, then being asked what else is happening with timing and stress, and realising those haven't been considered at all.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Pitch is one-third of the picture. Stress and rhythm are doing real work too, and real communicative effects usually depend on all three together."

**Intermediate — "I can name all three components together, and I analyse prosody for its grammatical function — what structure is being signalled — treating that as the complete analysis."**
The simulation: correctly identify stress, rhythm, and intonation as co-occurring features, and correctly extract grammatical information from them (question type, list phrasing), while treating this grammatical analysis as prosody's whole communicative job. This model has resolved the first misconception and not yet met the second.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — correctly identifying an utterance as grammatically a statement, then being asked whether the speaker sounds genuinely interested or bored, and realising the prosody is carrying information the grammatical analysis alone missed entirely.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Grammar is one job prosody does. It's also carrying the speaker's attitude and feeling, often at the same time — and that layer is just as real, even though it's more interpretive."

**Advanced — "prosody is the integrated stress-rhythm-intonation system, performing both a grammatical/structural function and an independent attitudinal/emotional function, with sarcasm as the clearest case where the attitudinal layer overrides literal word meaning entirely."**
The simulation: given any utterance, the learner names all three components, extracts both functions, and — critically — can detect and correctly interpret sarcasm or irony where the prosodic and literal meanings conflict. This is the target model, matching TA-4 and TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: `eng.listening.active-listening` and `eng.linguistics.what-is-linguistics` (the two direct unlocks), where this concept's integrated analytical skill becomes one tool among several for genuinely comprehending real, extended discourse, or is situated within the broader theoretical study of language.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gets you a full, precise analysis of one utterance's prosody. The next steps use that skill as part of a bigger toolkit — for real conversations, or for studying language itself."

**Expert — "prosody's attitudinal/emotional signalling conventions are substantially culture- and language-specific, and reading them accurately across a language boundary is a genuine cross-cultural pragmatics skill, not a universal, automatically-transferable perceptual ability."**
Named here to mark the arc; the Blueprint's own S9 protocol routing directly anticipates this expert-level fact, treating cross-cultural difficulty with sarcasm/irony detection specifically as a legitimate pragmatics challenge requiring explicit, patient modelling, not as an intonation-skill deficiency to be remediated with more pitch-contour drilling.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is a **direct, structurally predictable consequence of this program's own prior teaching sequence**: intonation was taught first among the three components, was given the most concrete, most visually-anchored teaching instrument (the pitch-line drawing), and was the component most heavily emphasised across three full prior concepts (`eng.phonetics.syllable-stress`, `eng.phonetics.sentence-stress`, `eng.phonetics.intonation-patterns` itself named "intonation patterns"). A learner arriving at this concept with intonation as their most available, most practised analytical lens has excellent reason to reach for it first and to mistake a complete pitch analysis for a complete prosodic one — this is not a conceptual confusion so much as an entirely predictable recency-and-practice effect.

The second mechanism is that **grammatical/structural prosodic functions are genuinely more rule-based and therefore easier to teach, assess, and feel confident about than attitudinal/emotional functions**, which are comparatively interpretive, context-dependent, and resistant to a clean right-or-wrong answer — a learner (and, honestly, an instructional sequence) naturally gravitates toward the more tractable half of a two-part system, and the Blueprint's own S6 protocol routing correctly validates this as a genuine, reasonable difficulty rather than a sign the learner is failing at something that should be straightforward.

The third mechanism, specific to sarcasm and irony detection, is that **the specific prosodic and cultural conventions signalling non-literal meaning are not universal**, and a learner whose L1 or cultural background signals attitude and stance through substantially different means (different prosodic conventions, or a greater reliance on explicit lexical markers rather than delivery) is not experiencing a deficiency in perceptual skill so much as a genuine, well-documented instance of cross-cultural pragmatic mismatch — exactly the case the Blueprint's own S9 routing anticipates and treats as requiring patient, explicit modelling rather than remediation.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-PROSODY-IS-JUST-INTONATION
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-PROSODY-IS-JUST-INTONATION — includes the intonation-alone-versus-full-analysis and identical-intonation-different-stress-and-rhythm discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 5 (instruction-induced), and specifically induced by this program's own teaching sequence rather than by any external prior instruction — a novel and worth-flagging variant of the instruction-induced pattern, since the "instruction" responsible is this curriculum's own deliberate, three-concept emphasis on intonation immediately preceding this concept.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because the source of the narrowing is this program's own prior teaching (not a learner misunderstanding or an external bad habit), the repair is straightforward and low-friction — **explicitly name stress and rhythm as co-equal, independently-contributing components** from the very first example (per the Blueprint's own TA-1, which requires all three to be named, not intonation alone), rather than assuming the learner needs to unlearn anything more than an entirely reasonable emphasis-driven default.
- **Verification of death**: given a novel utterance, the learner's first analytical move is to name all three components before offering any interpretation of meaning or function — the *unprompted inclusion of stress and rhythm*, not merely correct answers when specifically asked about them, is the actual target evidence, since a learner who only produces stress-and-rhythm analysis when directly prompted for it has not yet made the three-component default automatic.

### MC-PROSODY-ONLY-CONVEYS-GRAMMAR
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-PROSODY-ONLY-CONVEYS-GRAMMAR — includes the sarcastic-versus-sincere and bored-versus-engaged discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely more tractable, more rule-based grammatical half of prosody's dual function onto the whole of what prosody does — the learner has correctly and thoroughly learned prosody's grammatical signalling (largely inherited from three prior concepts) and has not yet been shown, as a matter of explicit content, that an equally real second function exists alongside it.
- **Teaching consequence**: because the grammatical-function knowledge being over-extended is itself entirely correct, the repair requires **adding a genuinely new, co-equal analytical dimension** rather than correcting anything — the Blueprint's own same-sentence-many-feelings anchor is the right instrument precisely because it holds grammatical structure and literal words constant while varying only attitude/emotion, isolating the new dimension as cleanly as the intonation concept's own minimal-pair demonstration isolated pitch as meaning-bearing.
- **Verification of death**: given a novel utterance whose grammatical structure and attitudinal colouring both carry genuine, non-obvious information, the learner correctly reports both — not merely the grammatical function alone, and not merely a vague, unanalysed sense that "something else is going on" — with the attitudinal analysis stated in terms of the specific prosodic features (pitch range, stress placement, rhythm) that signal it, mirroring the specificity already required of the grammatical half.

## Analogies

**Best — a musical performance's melody, together with its dynamics (loud/soft) and tempo (fast/slow), all contributing to the piece's overall effect.** A piece of music isn't just its melody (the tune, roughly analogous to intonation's pitch contour) — the same melody played loudly and quickly conveys something entirely different from the same melody played softly and slowly (dynamics and tempo, roughly analogous to stress and rhythm). Prosody is the full musical performance of a sentence, not just its tune.
*Breaking point*: musical dynamics and tempo are continuously variable and often notated explicitly; stress and rhythm in speech are somewhat more discrete and, crucially, largely unconscious and unnotated — useful for the *three co-equal dimensions* intuition, not for implying speech prosody is composed as deliberately as music.

**Alternative — an actor's full performance versus only their script's punctuation.** A script's punctuation (periods, question marks) tells an actor roughly the grammatical shape of a line — but the actual performance (how they deliver it, with what feeling) is where the emotional truth of the scene lives, often independent of or even in tension with what the punctuation alone would suggest. Grammatical prosodic function is like the punctuation; attitudinal/emotional function is the actor's actual performance choice.
*Breaking point*: punctuation is a discrete, written convention; grammatical prosody is itself spoken and continuous — useful for the *two distinct layers, one more rule-bound than the other* framing, not for implying grammatical prosody is as simple or fixed as punctuation.

**Story analogy — the same sentence, "I can't believe you did that," said five different ways (Blueprint Component 3's own anchor), functioning like five different actors reading identical lines in five different scenes.** The words never change; the scene — and therefore the meaning the audience receives — changes completely with each delivery. This directly maps to the anchor's own design, reinforcing rather than replacing it.
*Breaking point*: none serious; this is a restatement of the concept's own designed core instrument rather than a novel analogy.

**Visual analogy — the same-sentence-many-feelings exercise** (Blueprint Component 3): one sentence, five deliveries, each conveying a different attitude through varied stress, rhythm, and pitch together. Not a metaphor but the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organised around, and it directly isolates the attitudinal/emotional dimension by holding words and grammar maximally constant.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"Prosody is just a fancier word for intonation."** Directly installs MC-PROSODY-IS-JUST-INTONATION by name, presenting the misconception's own content as if it were a simplifying definition.
- **"Prosody tells you the grammar; tone of voice tells you the feeling — they're separate things."** Splits what this concept establishes as one integrated system (stress, rhythm, and intonation together) into two unrelated systems, when in fact the same three acoustic components carry both functions simultaneously, often through the identical physical signal analysed for different purposes.
- **"You can just feel sarcasm, there's no need to analyse it."** True that sarcasm is often perceived immediately and intuitively by a fluent speaker, but this framing forecloses exactly the analytical skill TA-4 and TA-5 require — naming which specific stress, rhythm, and pitch features signal the sarcasm, not merely recognising that it's present.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The same-sentence-many-feelings exercise (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there, using one sentence delivered five different ways. *Predict first*, before each delivery: "What emotion do you think I'm about to convey, just from how I'm about to say this — before I say it?" (Reversed slightly from a pure post-hoc prediction, since the anchor's design already elicits attention to the *change*; the tutor should also ask, after each delivery, what specifically changed.)
2. **The stress-and-rhythm collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "If I ask you to fully describe this sentence's prosody, is naming the pitch pattern enough?" Then walk through naming stress and rhythm as well, confirming they add genuinely new information.
3. **The bored-versus-engaged collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "This sentence is grammatically just a statement. Do you think the speaker's feelings about it could still come through?" Then compare a flat, disengaged delivery against a lively, engaged one.
4. **The sarcasm-detection task (learner activity, TA-4).** *Predict first*: "If someone says 'Oh, that's just perfect' in a flat, slow, exaggerated way, do you think they mean it literally?" Then confirm the sarcastic reading and name the specific prosodic cues that signalled it.

## Discovery Questions

This concept splits, following the pattern established at several advanced phonetics concepts in this program: **the three-component definition of prosody is best delivered directly** (a matter of terminology and scope, not something to discover), but **the existence and independence of the attitudinal/emotional function is genuinely well suited to guided discovery**, since the evidence (identical words conveying different feelings) is directly available to the learner through their own varied production.

1. **Need**: "If the words and the grammar of a sentence never change, can how someone feels about it still come through?"
2. **Playground**: the learner says the same sentence several different ways freely, aiming for different feelings, without being told in advance what specifically to vary.
3. **Invention**: "What did you actually change each time to make it sound like a different feeling?" The learner identifies, from their own production, that stress placement, timing, and pitch range all shifted, often together.
4. **Collision**: present a case (sarcasm) where the attitudinal signal doesn't merely add colour to the literal meaning but actively contradicts it — confronting the learner with a case where ignoring prosody produces a genuinely wrong interpretation, not merely an incomplete one.
5. **Formalization**: name the two functions — grammatical/structural and attitudinal/emotional — as co-equal, independently real jobs prosody performs, often simultaneously.
6. **Compression**: "Ask what structure this signals, AND what feeling it signals — never stop at either alone."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **All three components are named together from the very first task (TA-1), before any functional analysis is attempted** — establishing the complete, three-part descriptive vocabulary before asking what that description *means* separates the perceptual/descriptive skill from the interpretive one, exactly the same skill-separation principle established at several concepts throughout this program.
- **Grammatical function (TA-2) is reviewed and explicitly named as one of two functions before attitudinal function (TA-3) is introduced** — this ordering matters specifically because grammatical function draws on genuinely prior knowledge (three earlier concepts' worth), and explicitly framing it as "one of two things prosody does" (rather than simply revisiting it as familiar review) is what pre-empts, rather than merely follows, the second misconception.
- **Sarcasm/irony detection (TA-4) is deliberately sequenced after the general attitudinal-function insight (TA-3) is established, as its own dedicated, higher-stakes application** — the Blueprint's own Component 8 flag names this as "the highest-value, most advanced application," and reserving it for its own stage, after the general insight is secure, allows the learner to appreciate sarcasm as a specific, powerful instance of a principle they already hold, rather than meeting the override-literal-meaning phenomenon cold.
- **Full integrated analysis of extended speech (TA-5) comes last**, requiring the learner to simultaneously name all three components and extract both functions from genuinely extended, natural material — matching the general principle established throughout this program that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the always-analyse-all-three-together requirement, the dual-function assessment mandate, the sarcasm-detection emphasis, and cross-cultural pragmatic-variation acknowledgment for S9 learners).

## Tutor Actions

In recommended order, from `../teaching-actions/`:

1. **Demonstration** (SHOW) — the same-sentence-many-feelings exercise, ideally learner-performed (producing the varied deliveries themselves) as well as tutor-modelled, since production practice reinforces the analytical skill this concept ultimately requires.
2. **Concept Map** (ORGANIZE) — a running two-column analysis (grammatical function / attitudinal function) of every utterance examined, consolidating the concept's dual-function structure into one compact, continuously-built artefact.
3. **Error Analysis** (TEST-THINKING) — the sarcasm-detection task is fundamentally an error-analysis-style exercise: identifying where a literal reading of the words would produce a wrong interpretation, and correcting it using prosodic evidence.
4. **Prediction** (TEST-THINKING) — predicting an emotional delivery before hearing it, and predicting whether a given utterance's literal and prosodic meanings will align or conflict, both directly exercising the target skills.
5. **Matching** (DO) — utterance to full three-component description, and utterance to correctly-identified grammatical-plus-attitudinal function pair, exercising the concept's complete analytical scope in a single task type.

**Does not fit**: **Worked Example** in the derivation sense — there is no multi-step calculation, only an integrated perceptual-and-analytical skill better served by Demonstration and Error Analysis. **Game** — this concept's genuinely interpretive, context-dependent attitudinal half (per the Blueprint's own S6 validation) is a poor fit for speed-pressured gamification, which would likely reward superficial pattern-matching over the more nuanced judgement this concept requires.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **audio and fully prosodic** — the simultaneous coordination of stress, rhythm, and intonation across an utterance — making it, alongside `eng.phonetics.syllable-stress`, `eng.phonetics.sentence-stress`, and `eng.phonetics.intonation-patterns`, among the concepts in this program most severely affected by the runtime's plain-text STT capture gap (`../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §7`): this concept's entire subject matter is precisely the acoustic detail a transcription-only channel discards, and its most advanced application (sarcasm detection) is *specifically* the case where the discarded acoustic signal, not the preserved textual content, carries the decisive meaning.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A complete, unprompted three-component description offered for a novel utterance, without needing stress and rhythm to be specifically requested.** The target state for the first misconception's repair, and it should be distinguished from a prompted, checklist-style response (which may reflect compliance rather than genuinely internalised habit) by occasionally asking only "describe this utterance's prosody" with no further scaffolding.
- **Accurate grammatical-function analysis paired with a flat, unexamined, or entirely absent attitudinal-function analysis**, even when the utterance clearly carries a detectable emotional colouring. The direct signature of MC-PROSODY-ONLY-CONVEYS-GRAMMAR, and it should be met by directly asking what the speaker seems to feel, not merely what the sentence structurally is.
- **Correct sarcastic-versus-sincere discrimination, with the learner able to name the specific prosodic features (exaggerated pitch range, unusual stress, slowed rhythm) responsible**, rather than a correct intuitive judgement with no analytical basis offered. Both the correct judgement and the analytical justification matter for full mastery, mirroring the specificity requirement already established for the grammatical half.
- **Hesitation, uncertainty, or a stated "I'm not sure how to describe this" specifically on subtler attitudinal judgements (doubt, mild boredom, restrained enthusiasm), contrasted with confident, immediate judgements on more extreme, obvious cases (clear excitement, clear sarcasm).** This is expected, per the Blueprint's own S6 validation of attitudinal prosody as genuinely more interpretive, and should be met with patience and continued modelling on subtler cases, not treated as a failure relative to the clearer cases already mastered.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Name all three — stress, rhythm, pitch — and then ask both what structure this signals and what the speaker actually feels."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Three components together (MP-1).** *All three named without prompting for each individually* → strong evidence the default has generalised beyond the drilled examples. *Named only when each is separately, specifically requested* → weaker evidence; the descriptive habit may still be checklist-dependent rather than automatic.
- **Grammatical function (MP-2).** Largely a review and integration check of prior-concept knowledge; carries limited new diagnostic weight for this concept specifically, beyond confirming the foundation this concept builds on remains solid.
- **Attitudinal function (MP-3).** This is the item type that most directly discriminates genuine mastery of the second misconception's repair — *correct identification of two distinct attitudes from the same words, with the differentiating prosodic features named* → the full target evidence.
- **Sarcasm/irony detection (MP-4).** *Correct identification of the ironic meaning, with the conflicting literal-versus-prosodic evidence both explicitly named* → the strongest possible evidence this concept's most advanced application has been mastered. *Correct identification with no explanation of the conflict* → weaker evidence; the learner may be relying on general life experience with sarcasm rather than the specific analytical skill this concept teaches.
- **Explanation (MP-5).** Listen for whether the explanation names both the three-component composition of prosody *and* its dual function — a response covering only one has an incomplete model even if prior items were answered correctly by other means.

**Mastery certification trigger**: unprompted, complete three-component description of a novel utterance; correct grammatical-function identification for a novel example; correct identification of two distinct attitudes conveyed by identical words, with the differentiating prosodic features named; correct sarcasm/irony detection on a novel example, with the literal-versus-prosodic conflict explicitly articulated; and an explanation naming both prosody's three-component composition and its dual grammatical-plus-attitudinal function. The explicit-articulation requirements on the attitudinal and sarcasm items are essential — per this program's established practice, a correct judgement without stated analytical reasoning is weaker, more ambiguous evidence than one accompanied by a specific, feature-level justification.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"I already know how to describe intonation, isn't that the same thing?"** — take this as an accurate report of genuine, well-earned prior skill from three earlier concepts, not a misunderstanding; respond by building on rather than contradicting it: "Your intonation skills are solid, and you'll keep using them — prosody is that skill plus two more layers, stress and rhythm, working alongside it." Framing this as addition, not correction, protects the learner's confidence in real, prior achievement.
- **"I can't tell if this is sarcastic or not, it just sounds weird"** — a genuine, reasonable report of a subtler, harder case, particularly likely for an S9 learner whose L1 or cultural background signals irony differently; respond with explicit modelling and patience rather than pushing for a quick judgement: "That's a genuinely tricky one — let's break down specifically what's happening with the pitch, the stress, and the timing, piece by piece, rather than trying to just feel it."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from full integrated analysis or sarcasm detection, down to **the same-sentence-many-feelings exercise reduced to just two, maximally contrasting deliveries** (e.g., clearly excited versus clearly bored), asking only "which one sounds more excited?" This removes the three-component naming and the fine-grained attitudinal analysis entirely, isolating the most basic perceptual contrast before returning to the harder, more integrated tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just go with your gut feeling."** For the grammatical half, this bypasses the analytical skill entirely; for the attitudinal half, while intuition is a genuine and valuable starting point (per the Blueprint's own S6 note), gut feeling alone cannot produce the feature-level justification this concept's actual mastery bar requires.
