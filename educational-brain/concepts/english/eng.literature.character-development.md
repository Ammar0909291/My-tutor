## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Infer a character trait from show-not-tell evidence (action, dialogue, thought, others' reactions) rather than expecting a direct statement.
2. Identify partial, specific character growth as genuine growth, not "no growth."
3. Infer a trait using multiple converging evidence types.
4. Trace character growth across a story with textual evidence at beginning and end.
5. Analyze a novel excerpt, inferring two traits from show-not-tell evidence and identifying any specific area of growth.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept. First, **character traits are far more often SHOWN through action, dialogue, thought, and others' reactions than directly TOLD by the author** — a character who rushes into a burning building without any narrator label reveals bravery through action alone, and showing traits this way is generally considered stronger writing than direct statement; a reader must expect to infer traits from behavior far more often than to find them stated outright. Second, **character growth is very often partial and specific, not a complete personality overhaul** — a character can learn patience specifically with a younger sibling while remaining just as impatient in traffic or at work, and this localized, specific growth is just as legitimate and often more realistic than an all-or-nothing transformation; growth should be assessed by looking for the SPECIFIC area of change, not judged as absent simply because it isn't total. Both corrections replace an expectation of directness and completeness with the inferential, nuanced analysis literary characterization actually requires.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "character traits are directly stated by the author; a character either changes completely or doesn't change at all."**
The simulation: search for explicit trait-labeling sentences and give up or guess when none exists, and judge character growth as binary — either total transformation or no change.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a character rushing into a burning building with no narrator label, asked what trait this reveals; and a character who grows in patience specifically with a sibling while remaining impatient elsewhere, asked whether this counts as growth.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Infer traits from action, dialogue, thought, and reactions — most aren't stated directly. Growth is often partial, in one specific area."

**Intermediate — "I can infer traits from action now, but I still tend to dismiss a character's growth as 'not real' if it isn't total across their whole personality."**
The simulation: correctly infers traits from show-not-tell evidence but hasn't yet accepted partial, localized growth as genuine.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-3's direct comparison using the discrimination pairs, presenting a character who grows in one specific relationship while remaining unchanged elsewhere.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Look for the SPECIFIC area of growth, not total transformation. Partial growth is still genuine growth."

**Advanced — "I infer traits from multiple converging evidence types, trace specific growth across a story, and analyze a novel excerpt integrating both skills."**
The simulation: integrates multi-evidence trait inference and growth-tracing into one coherent character analysis. Matches TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: the direct unlock `eng.literature.theme-and-message`, which extends this concept's evidence-based inference to identifying a story's larger message, often built from a character's specific growth.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the character-level analysis. Theme builds the story's larger message from exactly this kind of specific growth."

**Expert — "at fluency, show-not-tell trait inference and partial-growth recognition happen as an integrated part of ordinary literary reading."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit evidence-gathering and specific-area-of-growth routine is a scaffold for a reading habit that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated part of literary comprehension.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **directly-stated information is easier to locate and process than inferred information**, so a learner's default search strategy (scan for an explicit label) is a reasonable, low-effort approach that fails specifically when skilled writing deliberately avoids direct statement in favor of the stronger show-not-tell technique.

The second mechanism is that **narrative and media conventions frequently emphasize dramatic, complete transformation arcs** (a villain fully redeemed, a coward becoming fully brave), reinforcing a binary change-or-no-change expectation that doesn't match the more common reality of partial, localized growth in realistic characterization.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-CHARACTERS-TRAITS-ARE-DIRECTLY-STATED-BY-THE-AUTHOR
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry — the burning-building-action/direct-label discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), from directly-stated information being more perceptually accessible than inferred information, mistaken for the primary or only valid method of characterization.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **have the learner directly practice inferring from show-only passages**, since the fix requires developing the inferential skill itself, not merely being told that inference is sometimes necessary.
- **Verification of death**: given a passage revealing a trait only through action/dialogue/thought, the learner correctly infers the trait and cites the specific evidence.

### MC-A-CHARACTER-EITHER-CHANGES-COMPLETELY-OR-NOT-AT-ALL
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — the sibling-patience/traffic-impatience discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely common, dramatic total-transformation arc encountered in many stories, extended into treating it as the only valid form of character growth.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair supplies the **partial-repair-shop demonstration** (repairing only one car part), since the fix requires a concrete model of legitimate partial improvement, not merely an abstract claim that growth can be partial.
- **Verification of death**: given a character who grows in one specific area while remaining unchanged elsewhere, the learner correctly identifies this as genuine growth, not "no growth."

## Analogies

**Best — the Evidence Detective Board and the Partial Repair Shop** (Blueprint Component 3): pinning evidence cards (action, dialogue, thought, reaction) around a central trait-verdict card, reaching the conclusion through inference, directly seeds the show-not-tell fix; repairing only one part of a five-part car model while leaving the others unchanged directly seeds the partial-growth fix.
*Breaking point*: a detective's evidence board typically converges on one definitive, provable conclusion, while literary trait inference often remains a matter of reasonable interpretation with more than one defensible reading — useful for the multiple-evidence-converging intuition, less precise for implying character analysis always has one single correct verdict.
*Breaking point (repair shop)*: a car's parts are functionally independent in a way personality traits often aren't, since a character's patience with a sibling might subtly relate to their patience elsewhere — useful for the partial-improvement-is-real intuition, less precise for implying complete independence between areas of growth.

**Alternative — a student who becomes an excellent writer but remains a weak public speaker.** Genuine, significant growth in one skill area doesn't require, or even typically produce, equal growth in an unrelated area.
*Breaking point*: academic skill domains are more clearly delineated than the more fluid, interconnected nature of personality traits within a single character.

**Visual anchor — the Evidence Detective Board and the Partial Repair Shop**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"If the author doesn't tell you a character's trait directly, you can't really know it."** Directly installs the direct-statement-only misconception as a stated limitation.
- **"A character either has a complete character arc or they don't develop at all."** Directly installs the binary-transformation misconception as a stated rule.

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both analytical facts are directly checkable by testing candidate passages and growth scenarios against the evidence-based inference standard.

1. **Need**: "If a character does something brave but the author never says 'she was brave,' do you know anything about her personality?"
2. **Playground**: the learner infers traits from several show-only passages freely before being told the official evidence-type categories.
3. **Invention**: "Can you find a character who grows in just ONE specific way but stays the same in others?"
4. **Collision**: present a character who grows in one relationship while remaining unchanged elsewhere; ask whether this counts as growth.
5. **Formalization**: name the two analytical facts — traits are usually shown, not told; growth is often partial and specific — from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Infer from evidence, not labels — and look for growth in a specific area, not total change."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Show vs. tell trait sorting (TA-1) comes first**, directly confronting the direct-statement misconception before multi-evidence inference is introduced.
- **Inferring traits from multiple evidence types (TA-2) comes second**, deepening the inferential skill established in TA-1 across all four evidence types.
- **Partial vs. total character growth (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the binary-transformation misconception once trait inference is secure.
- **Tracing growth across a story (TA-4) and full novel-excerpt analysis (TA-5) come last**, integrating trait inference and growth-tracing into one coherent generative capstone — matching this program's established sequencing principle.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for a trait inference or a growth judgment**, since a correct-looking trait label or growth assessment with no cited evidence cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated inference chain from specific action/dialogue/thought/reaction evidence to a trait conclusion** — the direct behavioral signature of the show-not-tell fix taking hold.
- **Identification of growth in one specific relationship or behavior, explicitly noting the character remains unchanged elsewhere** — direct evidence the partial-growth fix is internalized.
- **A flat "this character doesn't really grow" verdict for a character with clear, localized growth**, because the change isn't total — the direct behavioral signature of MC-A-CHARACTER-EITHER-CHANGES-COMPLETELY-OR-NOT-AT-ALL still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Infer from what a character does and says — and look for growth in one specific place, not everywhere at once."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But the author never says what this character is like"** — take this as a reasonable expectation from less demanding reading experiences; respond by redirecting to evidence: "That's true — so let's look at what they DO and SAY instead. What does this action tell you?"
- **"This character doesn't really change, they're still impatient with everyone else"** — a reasonable-sounding strict standard; respond by narrowing the claim: "You're right that they're still impatient elsewhere — but did they change with just this one person? That specific change still counts as growth."
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full novel-excerpt analysis down to **inferring a single trait from one clearly show-only passage, with no growth-tracing task demanded.** Isolates the most basic inferential skill before returning to fuller analysis.
- **Never shrink to "just tell me what the author says about this character."** This directly licenses the direct-statement-only pattern, undermining the inferential discipline this concept specifically builds.
