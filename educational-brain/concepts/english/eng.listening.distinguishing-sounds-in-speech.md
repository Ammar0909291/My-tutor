## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Distinguish similar-sounding words by attending to their actual phonetic difference, rather than guessing from sentence context alone.
2. Recognize that a phonetic contrast clearly audible in careful, isolated pronunciation can blur or reduce in fast, connected natural speech.
3. Identify the correct word in a sentence with genuinely ambiguous context, using only the phonetic evidence available.
4. Describe specifically what became harder to hear when a minimal pair moves from isolated to connected-speech form.
5. Apply trained phonetic attention to a new, unaided passage of natural connected speech containing minimal-pair contrasts.

## Core Understanding

This concept extends two already-secure skills — minimal-pair discrimination in careful speech, and sustained attentive listening — into the genuinely harder, more realistic task of **perceiving fine phonetic contrasts within fast, connected, natural speech**, where two separate complications arise that did not exist in either prerequisite alone. The first complication is that **sentence context, while often helpful, cannot always resolve which of two similar-sounding words was said** — some sentences are constructed so that either word fits equally well, and a listener who has only ever relied on context to disambiguate minimal pairs has no fallback the moment context stops helping; the actual, reliable fallback is the same phonetic-contrast-attending skill trained in the prerequisite, now applied under pressure. The second complication is that **the same phonetic contrast clearly audible in careful, isolated pronunciation can genuinely blur, reduce, or nearly merge under the faster pace and connected articulation of natural speech** — "would have" sounding close to "would of" in casual speech is a concrete instance — meaning that strong isolated-word discrimination does not automatically transfer to strong connected-speech discrimination; the two are related but genuinely separate skills, and the second must be built through dedicated practice specifically on connected, natural-pace material, not assumed to follow automatically from the first.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "if context makes sense, that's the word that was said."**
The runnable simulation: use sentence meaning as the primary or sole test for resolving a minimal-pair ambiguity, without independently checking the actual sound. This works reliably whenever context happens to be unambiguous, which is most of the time in ordinary conversation, making the strategy feel sufficient until it isn't.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a genuinely ambiguous sentence ("I saw the ship/sheep in the distance" in a foggy-harbor context) where context provides no disambiguation at all.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Context is a helpful clue most of the time, but it isn't a fallback you can always count on — train your ear to hear the actual sound difference, so you have something to rely on when context doesn't settle it."

**Intermediate — "I can hear the contrast clearly when words are said carefully, so I'll be fine hearing it in normal conversation too."**
The simulation: correctly attends to phonetic contrast rather than relying on context alone, but assumes the isolated-word skill transfers automatically and completely to fast, connected speech, without recognizing that connected speech genuinely reduces and blurs some contrasts that are clear in careful pronunciation.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — hearing the same minimal pair first spoken carefully (clearly distinguishable) and then in fast connected speech (noticeably blurred), and being asked to name what changed.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Hearing a contrast clearly in careful speech is real progress, but it doesn't guarantee you'll hear it the same way at full natural speed — that takes its own separate practice."

**Advanced — "I attend to actual phonetic evidence rather than context alone, and I know connected speech reduces contrasts in specific, describable ways that isolated speech doesn't — so I practice specifically on connected material."**
The simulation: applies trained phonetic attention as the primary tool (not context), and treats connected-speech performance as a genuinely separate, dedicated skill requiring its own practice beyond isolated-word success. This is the target model, matching TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: none within the current curriculum — this concept is terminal, with no further KG unlocks.
*Shelf-life warning*: none required; this concept's skill is intended to generalize directly to ordinary, real-world listening without a further formal teaching step.

**Expert — "at full listening fluency, phonetic discrimination in connected speech becomes as automatic and unconscious as it is in isolated speech, with conscious attention reserved only for genuinely unusual or highly ambiguous cases."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit, deliberate attention-training routine is a scaffold for a skill that, at true listening fluency, operates largely below conscious awareness — named so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is unconscious automaticity, not a permanently maintained conscious procedure.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **context usually does work**, so a learner's habit of relying on it is not an arbitrary error but a well-reinforced strategy that succeeds the great majority of the time in ordinary conversation — the failure surfaces specifically and only in the minority of cases where context is genuinely ambiguous, meaning the strategy's weakness is rarely visible until directly probed.

The second mechanism is that **isolated-word discrimination and connected-speech discrimination are genuinely different perceptual tasks**, even though they share an underlying phonetic-attention skill — connected speech's faster pace, co-articulation with neighboring sounds, and common reductions (contractions, blended consonants) introduce acoustic changes that simply are not present in careful, isolated pronunciation, so a learner's confident isolated-word performance provides real but incomplete evidence about their connected-speech performance.

The third mechanism, specific to this concept's terminal position in the curriculum, is that **without further scaffolded unlocks to fall back on, a learner who exits this concept with only partial mastery has no built-in downstream concept designed to catch and repair the gap** — making the mastery-probe stage's diagnostic precision (per Component 8's adaptive flags) especially important here relative to concepts with further downstream reinforcement.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-YOU-CAN-ALWAYS-TELL-SIMILAR-SOUNDING-WORDS-APART-JUST-BY-USING-SENTENCE-CONTEXT
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the ambiguous-versus-disambiguating foggy-harbor discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely effective and well-reinforced strategy of using context to resolve ambiguity, extended past the cases where context actually succeeds into an assumed universal fallback.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because context-reliance is not a wrong strategy in general, only an incomplete one, the repair must **add phonetic attention as a supplement, not discard context entirely** — the Blueprint's own bridge text frames this correctly as "context often helps," never as "context is unreliable."
- **Verification of death**: given a novel sentence with genuinely ambiguous context around a minimal pair, the learner identifies the correct word from the audio alone and can name the specific phonetic feature that gave it away, rather than guessing or claiming the sentence "must" mean one thing or the other.

### MC-B-IF-YOU-CAN-DISTINGUISH-A-MINIMAL-PAIR-IN-CAREFUL-ISOLATED-SPEECH-YOULL-AUTOMATICALLY-DISTINGUISH-IT-IN-FAST-CONNECTED-SPEECH
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-B — includes the isolated-versus-connected-speech discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the reasonable assumption that a mastered skill transfers automatically to a harder version of the same task, without recognizing that connected speech introduces genuinely new acoustic complications absent from isolated pronunciation.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct exposure to the same content in both forms back-to-back** (isolated, then connected), since an abstract claim that "connected speech is harder" is far less convincing than personally experiencing the same contrast blur in real time.
- **Verification of death**: given a minimal pair the learner distinguishes easily in isolation, they explicitly seek out or expect additional, connected-speech-specific practice rather than assuming isolated success already guarantees connected-speech success.

## Analogies

**Best — recognizing a friend's voice on a bad phone connection versus a clear one.** On a clear line (isolated speech), the voice is unmistakable; on a bad connection (fast connected speech), the same voice can become harder to distinguish from a similar one, not because the voice changed, but because the transmission conditions did — training your ear to catch subtler cues becomes necessary specifically under the harder condition.
*Breaking point*: a bad phone connection is a passive degradation outside the listener's control, while connected-speech reduction is a normal, expected feature of natural language the listener must learn to work with, not a malfunction — useful for the harder-conditions intuition, not for implying connected speech is a defect to be fixed.

**Alternative — picking out a single instrument's note within a slow solo versus within a fast, full-band performance.** Identifying a specific note played slowly and alone (isolated minimal pair) is one skill; picking that same note out amid a fast, busy arrangement (connected speech) is a related but genuinely harder, separate skill requiring its own practice.
*Breaking point*: musical arrangements are composed deliberately with all instruments audible in principle, while some connected-speech reductions genuinely eliminate or merge acoustic information that was present in the isolated form — useful for the separate-skill intuition, less precise for implying all information remains technically recoverable.

**Visual/auditory anchor — the Map-Reading-vs-Asking-for-Directions and Isolated-Note-vs-Fast-Music anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): not a metaphor but the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching uses, directly seeding both misconceptions' fixes in a single combined anchor.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"If the sentence makes sense one way, that's definitely the word."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated rule, precisely the context-as-sufficient-fallback error this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"Once you can hear it, you can always hear it."** Directly installs MC-B by implying discrimination skill is a single, context-independent capacity rather than one that must be separately built for connected speech.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The ambiguous foggy-harbor sentence (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-A. *Predict first*: "Can you tell which word was said just from the sentence's meaning here?" Then confirm context provides no disambiguation, requiring phonetic evidence.
2. **The isolated-versus-connected minimal pair (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for MC-B. *Predict first*: "Do you think this pair will sound as easy to tell apart in fast, natural speech as it did just now, spoken carefully?" Then play both forms and confirm the contrast blurs.
3. **The targeted connected-speech practice set (learner activity, TA-3).** *Predict first*: "How many of these five sentences do you think you'll get right at full natural speed?" Then attempt and confirm together.
4. **The full unaided passage (learner activity, TA-4).** *Predict first*: "Do you feel ready to try a new passage with no support?" Then attempt and confirm.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (context insufficiency; connected-speech contrast reduction) is directly, personally audible through the learner's own listening attempts.

1. **Need**: "If two sentences could both make sense with either of two similar-sounding words, how would you actually decide which one was said?"
2. **Playground**: the learner listens to several sentences with embedded minimal pairs, some with clear context, some genuinely ambiguous, freely, before being told which is which.
3. **Invention**: "Which of those sentences could you resolve just from meaning, and which needed you to actually listen closely to the sound?" The learner sorts their own attempts.
4. **Collision**: present the same minimal pair first isolated (easily heard) then embedded in fast connected speech — did the learner's confidence change?
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — context isn't always enough, and connected speech reduces contrasts that are clear in isolation — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Don't just trust the sentence — listen for the actual sound, and expect it to be harder to catch at full speed."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Attending to phonetic contrast over context (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting MC-A before the connected-speech complication is introduced, since the connected-speech skill (TA-2/TA-3) presupposes the learner is already attending to actual sound evidence rather than defaulting to context.
- **Recognizing contrast reduction in connected speech (TA-2) comes second**, building the awareness that connected speech behaves differently before asking the learner to perform the harder discrimination task itself (TA-3) — awareness of the complication precedes the skill for handling it.
- **Targeted connected-speech practice (TA-3) and full unaided application (TA-4) come last**, with TA-3 building the specific skill through dedicated practice and TA-4 requiring the learner to apply phonetic-attention-over-context (TA-1) and connected-speech-specific listening (TA-2/TA-3) simultaneously to genuinely novel material — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **audio-based and inherently a spoken-listening task** — the tutor presents audio (or read-aloud connected speech) and the learner's response is itself spoken identification, making this among the most directly audio-dependent concepts in this program, distinct from concepts whose evidence is primarily the learner's honest self-report.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated phonetic feature accompanying a correct identification** ("I could tell because the vowel sounded longer") — the direct behavioral signature of genuine phonetic attention rather than a lucky guess, and the specific target evidence for MP-1.
- **Immediate, confident identification based on sentence meaning alone, with no reference to the actual sound, on a genuinely ambiguous item** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-A still active.
- **A visible difficulty gap between isolated-form and connected-form performance on the same underlying contrast, paired with genuine surprise or frustration** — expected, diagnostic evidence of MC-B surfacing naturally, not a sign of a deeper problem.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Listen for the actual sound first — don't let the sentence's meaning decide for you."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Ambiguous-context identification (MP-1).** *Correct identification with a stated phonetic feature* → full target evidence; *correct identification with no stated feature* → cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.
- **Contrast-reduction description (MP-2).** Listen for a specific, concrete description (a particular sound blending or shortening) rather than a vague "it was just harder."
- **Embedded connected-speech identification (MP-3).** This concept's most direct connected-speech skill test; track accuracy across all five items, not just an aggregate score, since a pattern of errors on specific sound types is more diagnostically useful than a single percentage.
- **Transfer explanation (MP-4).** Listen for whether the explanation names the genuine mechanism (connected speech alters/reduces the acoustic signal) rather than a vague "it's just different."
- **Full connected passage (MP-5).** *Correct identification across an unaided, multi-contrast passage* → the full synthesis target; a failure here with MP-1 through MP-4 passing indicates a specific integration gap under combined task demands, per Component 8's adaptive flags.

**Mastery certification trigger**: correct identification with stated phonetic-feature reasoning on a genuinely ambiguous-context item; a specific, concrete description of what changes in connected speech; correct identification across the full five-item embedded-connected-speech set; a transfer explanation naming the genuine acoustic-reduction mechanism; and correct performance on a novel, unaided connected-speech passage.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But the sentence obviously means [X], so it has to be that word"** — take this as an accurate statement about what context suggests, not a mistake to be dismissed; respond by adding rather than contradicting: "You're right that the sentence makes good sense that way — but this particular sentence could also make sense with the other word. Let's listen again for the actual sound, just to be sure."
- **"I could hear it fine before, why can't I hear it now?"** — a genuine, frustrating experience when isolated success meets connected-speech difficulty; respond by naming the mechanism directly, not merely reassuring: "Your ear for the sound itself is working — connected speech genuinely changes how that sound comes out, faster and blended with what's around it. That's a real, separate skill, and it's exactly what we're building now."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full unaided connected passage, down to **a single minimal pair presented first in isolation, then in the exact same two-word phrase spoken slightly slower than natural speed.** This removes both the multi-contrast complexity and the full natural-speed pressure, isolating the core skill before returning to harder, faster material.
- **Never shrink to "just pick whichever word makes the sentence make sense."** This directly licenses MC-A's context-as-sufficient-fallback pattern, undermining this concept's specific purpose.

## Memory Hooks

- **Concept type**: perceptual discrimination skill under two specific difficulty conditions (ambiguous context, connected-speech reduction), requiring genuine auditory training rather than declarative knowledge.
- **Review form**: spaced items always mixing isolated-form confidence-checks with connected-speech items on the same underlying contrasts, since this concept's core lesson is precisely the gap between the two forms — reviewing only one form would understate the skill actually being maintained.
- **Concept-specific deviation**: because this concept is terminal (no further KG unlocks), review should track long-term retention of the connected-speech-specific skill directly, rather than relying on a downstream concept to naturally re-exercise it, as most non-terminal concepts in this program can.
