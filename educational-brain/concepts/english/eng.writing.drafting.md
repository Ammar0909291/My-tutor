## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Mark (not fix) minor uncertainties during drafting and keep writing rather than stopping to resolve them immediately.
2. Apply a push-through strategy (placeholder, skip-ahead) when stuck on a specific section, rather than halting the entire drafting process.
3. Sort writing concerns into drafting's content-focus versus editing's correctness-focus.
4. Complete a full original draft from an outline, using flow-preservation and push-through strategies throughout.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept. First, **drafting's primary job is capturing content and ideas, not achieving polished correctness as you go** — stopping to fix every small error (spelling, word choice) disrupts the flow of thought and often means losing track of the larger idea being developed; error-correction properly belongs to the later editing stage, and during drafting the right move is to mark an uncertain spot and keep writing. Second, **getting stuck on one specific section does not mean the entire drafting process must stop** — skilled writers use push-through strategies (a placeholder note, skipping ahead to an easier section and returning later, writing a rough approximate version to refine during revision) that keep momentum going on the rest of the piece, rather than letting one stuck spot halt everything.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "you have to fix every error as you notice it, and if you get stuck you have to stop until you know exactly what to write."**
The simulation: stop mid-sentence repeatedly to fix minor issues, losing momentum, and halt the entire drafting session when hitting one difficult section.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a writer who stops constantly versus one who marks uncertain spots and keeps moving, and a driver who stops the car completely at a road closure versus one who follows a detour sign and keeps going.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Mark it and keep moving — that's a detour sign, not a stop sign. Minor issues wait for the pit stop."

**Intermediate — "I can now mark uncertainties and use push-through strategies, but I still hesitate to skip ahead when a specific section feels especially important."**
The simulation: applies flow-preservation on minor issues but still stalls on a stuck section if it feels central to the piece.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-2's direct placeholder-and-skip-ahead practice, given a genuinely difficult, important section to push through rather than resolve immediately.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Important or not, a placeholder note keeps you moving — you can always come back once the rest of the draft clarifies what this section needs."

**Advanced — "I complete a full original draft from my own outline, using flow-preservation and push-through strategies throughout, without stopping the overall process."**
The simulation: integrates flow-preservation and push-through strategies into one sustained, complete drafting session. Matches TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: `eng.writing.revising-for-content`'s resolution of the marked uncertain spots and placeholder notes left behind during drafting.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you a complete draft with marked spots to revisit. Content revision is where those marked spots finally get resolved."

**Expert — "at fluency, preserving flow and pushing through stuck points happens as an integrated part of ordinary drafting, without a separate deliberate strategy check."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit mark-and-push-through routine is a scaffold for a drafting habit that, at fluency, operates as an integrated part of composition.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **school writing is frequently submitted and graded as a single pass**, reinforcing an expectation that correctness must be achieved simultaneously with content generation, rather than modeling drafting and editing as genuinely separate stages with separate jobs.

The second mechanism is that **getting stuck feels like a signal to stop and "figure it out properly"** before continuing, a natural but counterproductive instinct that treats momentum as secondary to certainty, when in fact continuing past a stuck point (with a placeholder) often provides the context needed to resolve it later more easily.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-YOU-MUST-STOP-AND-FIX-EVERY-ERROR-DURING-DRAFTING
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 5 (instruction-induced), from single-pass graded writing tasks reinforcing the expectation that correctness and content generation must happen simultaneously.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **have the learner practice marking, not fixing, a real uncertainty while drafting**, since the flow-preservation benefit is far more convincing demonstrated through direct comparison than asserted abstractly.
- **Verification of death**: given a drafting scenario with a minor uncertainty, the learner marks it and continues rather than stopping to resolve it immediately.

### MC-IF-YOU-GET-STUCK-YOU-SHOULD-STOP-DRAFTING-UNTIL-YOU-KNOW-EXACTLY-WHAT-TO-WRITE
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of "resolve uncertainty before continuing" as a generally reasonable instinct, overextended to justify halting an entire drafting session rather than using a workaround.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **a concrete push-through strategy demonstration (placeholder note)**, since the momentum-preserving benefit is far more convincing demonstrated through a real stuck-point scenario than asserted abstractly.
- **Verification of death**: given a scenario where the learner is stuck on a specific section, they apply a placeholder or skip-ahead strategy rather than stopping the entire drafting process.

## Analogies

**Best — the Road Trip with Detour Signs, Not a Stop Sign** (Blueprint Component 3): a driver who follows a detour sign and keeps driving toward the destination rather than stopping the car completely at road construction — directly seeding the fix for MC-IF-YOU-GET-STUCK-YOU-SHOULD-STOP-DRAFTING-UNTIL-YOU-KNOW-EXACTLY-WHAT-TO-WRITE. A "keep moving" dashboard light versus a "pit stop for repairs" light, showing minor issues can be marked and addressed later without stopping the whole trip — directly seeding the fix for MC-YOU-MUST-STOP-AND-FIX-EVERY-ERROR-DURING-DRAFTING.
*Breaking point*: a road trip's detour is externally imposed and clearly signposted, while a writer's decision to push through a stuck section is a more internal, self-directed judgment call without an external "detour sign" telling them exactly when to switch strategies.

**Alternative — a sculptor roughing out a full figure before refining any one detail.** A sculptor blocks out the entire form first, roughly, rather than perfecting one hand before any of the rest of the body exists — refining details too early risks investing precision in a part that might need to change once the whole form is visible.
*Breaking point*: a sculpture's rough block-out is a physically continuous single object, while a written draft's "stuck" sections are discrete, separable parts that can genuinely be skipped and returned to independently, a flexibility a single sculptural mass doesn't have.

**Visual anchor — the Road Trip with Detour Signs, Not a Stop Sign**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"Fix every mistake the moment you notice it while you're writing."** Directly installs the simultaneous-correctness misconception as a stated definition.
- **"If you don't know exactly what to write next, stop until you figure it out."** Directly installs the full-stop misconception as a stated definition.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Road Trip with Detour Signs, Not a Stop Sign (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Should a driver stop completely at road construction, or follow the detour and keep going?"
2. **The flow-preservation collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Which approach produces more actual content — stopping constantly to fix small things, or marking and moving on?"
3. **The push-through collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Does writing a placeholder and continuing keep the drafting process moving better than stopping entirely?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both facts are directly checkable by comparing drafting approaches for actual content produced and momentum preserved.

1. **Need**: "If you stop every few words to fix a small issue, what happens to your train of thought?"
2. **Playground**: the learner freely drafts a short passage, marking uncertain spots, before being told the official flow-preservation test.
3. **Invention**: "If you get stuck on one specific section, what could you do besides just waiting there until it's resolved?"
4. **Collision**: present a writer stopping entirely at a difficult transition versus one using a placeholder note, and ask which keeps the overall process moving.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — drafting defers error-correction to editing, and getting stuck calls for a push-through strategy, not a full stop — from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Mark it and keep moving; when stuck, detour, don't stop."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Flow-preservation practice (TA-1) comes first**, directly confronting the first misconception before push-through strategies are introduced.
- **Placeholder and skip-ahead strategies (TA-2) comes second**, directly confronting the second misconception once basic flow-preservation is established.
- **Drafting from an outline (TA-3) comes third**, applying both strategies together on a sustained drafting task.
- **Distinguishing draft-stage from editing-stage concerns (TA-4) comes fourth**, formalizing the content/correctness boundary underlying both misconception fixes.
- **Completing a full draft from an original outline (TA-5) comes last**, the generative capstone integrating both misconception fixes into a complete, original piece of writing — matching this program's established sequencing principle of reserving the most integrative task for the culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's actual drafting behavior when a minor uncertainty or a stuck section arises**, since a completed draft alone cannot reveal whether the process behind it preserved flow or stalled repeatedly.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Voluntary marking of an uncertain word choice** ("not sure if this is the right word") followed by continued writing, without stopping to resolve it — the direct behavioral signature of the flow-preservation fix taking hold.
- **Voluntary use of a placeholder note or skip-ahead move** when stuck, rather than halting — direct evidence the push-through fix is internalized.
- **Repeated pauses to perfect minor word choices, or a complete stop at a difficult section with no forward movement on the rest of the draft** — the direct behavioral signature of either misconception still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Mark it and keep moving — when you're stuck, detour, don't stop."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I noticed the mistake, shouldn't I just fix it now?"** — take this as an accurate observation about noticing an error, not a mistake; respond by adding the deferral test: "You caught it, that's great — but does fixing it right now help you keep writing, or does it break your flow? Mark it and keep going."
- **"I don't know how to write this part, so I have to stop"** — a genuine, reasonable feeling when facing a hard section; respond by naming the additional layer: "You don't know yet, that's true — but could you write a placeholder note and come back once the rest of the draft makes it clearer?"
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full original draft down to **marking one single uncertain word choice in a short passage and continuing, with push-through strategies and the full draft set aside.** Isolates the most basic flow-preservation move before returning to fuller tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just stop and get it perfect before moving on."** This directly licenses the simultaneous-correctness pattern, undermining the flow-preservation discipline this concept specifically builds.
