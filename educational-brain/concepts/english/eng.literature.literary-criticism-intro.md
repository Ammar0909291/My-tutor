## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Describe at least three major critical approaches (historical/biographical, formalist, reader-response) and the distinct kind of question each asks about a text.
2. Apply at least two different critical lenses to the same short text and produce genuinely different, lens-appropriate interpretations.
3. Explain that no single critical approach is the one "correct" or "complete" way to read a text.
4. Apply at least two critical lenses to a new text, producing genuinely distinct interpretations and articulating what different question each lens answered.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept. First, **literary criticism is not a search for the single, objectively "correct" hidden meaning of a text that different approaches are all racing to find** — formalist, historical, and reader-response critics are not competing for the same right answer; each approach asks a genuinely different KIND OF QUESTION about the same text, and multiple readings can be simultaneously valid because they're not actually answering the same question. Second, **reader-response criticism is not "any personal reaction counts as a valid interpretation, no textual support needed"** — it is a rigorous approach examining how a reader's engagement co-creates meaning, still grounded in specific, describable features of the text; an unsupported "I liked/didn't like it" is not yet a reader-response interpretation, only the raw material one could start from, and a genuine interpretation must connect the reaction back to a specific textual feature.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "one of these critical approaches must be the correct one, and my gut reaction to a story is automatically a valid interpretation."**
The simulation: get confused or frustrated when two readings of the same text reach different conclusions, treating the divergence as a problem, and offer "I didn't like it" as a complete reader-response reading.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a historical reading and a formalist reading of the same poem, both legitimate but answering different questions, and a grounded reader-response reading compared against an ungrounded personal opinion.
*Shelf-life warning*: "A doctor, a nutritionist, and a trainer aren't competing for one right answer about the same person. And a sommelier's tasting notes are grounded in specific features, not just 'I don't like it.'"

**Intermediate — "I can now name what question each lens asks and ground a reaction in a textual feature, but I still privately feel one lens is 'more correct' when I have strong personal opinions about a text."**
The simulation: correctly distinguishes lenses in neutral exercises but reverts to seeking one "real answer" when genuinely invested in a text's meaning.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-4's direct complementary-lenses comparison, explicitly naming what different question each of the three produced readings answered.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Even when you care a lot about what a text 'really' means, ask what SPECIFIC question each lens is answering — the plurality principle applies here too."

**Advanced — "I apply at least two critical lenses to a new text, produce genuinely distinct interpretations, and explain what different question each lens answered."**
The simulation: integrates lens-plurality and evidence-grounded reader-response into one coherent, multi-lens analysis. Matches TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: `eng.literature.comparative-literature-intro`'s requirement to be explicit about which critical lens is doing the comparing across cultures or traditions.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the major single-text lenses. Comparing across works and traditions requires being explicit about which lens you're using, next."

**Expert — "at fluency, applying multiple critical lenses and grounding reader-response in textual evidence happens as an integrated part of ordinary literary evaluation, without a separate deliberate checklist."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit multi-lens-comparative routine is a scaffold for a reading habit that, at fluency, operates as an integrated part of literary evaluation.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **prior schooling often frames literary analysis as finding "what the author meant" or "the right answer" for grading purposes**, reinforcing a single-correct-interpretation model well before a learner encounters multiple, genuinely different critical traditions that don't compete for that one answer.

The second mechanism is that **reader-response criticism's very name ("reader's response") sounds like it's granting license for any personal reaction**, an easy but incorrect inference from the name alone, since the actual method still requires the same textual grounding any other criticism does — just directed at explaining the reader's own reaction rather than the text's independent meaning.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-THERE-IS-ONE-SINGLE-CORRECT-INTERPRETATION-OF-A-TEXT-THAT-CRITICISM-UNCOVERS
*(Blueprint Component 1, first entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 5 (instruction-induced), from prior schooling's grading-driven "what the author meant" framing, reinforcing a single-correct-answer model before multiple, non-competing critical traditions are encountered.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **have the learner name the SPECIFIC QUESTION each of two differing readings is actually answering**, since the non-competing plurality is far more convincing demonstrated by making the different questions explicit than asserted abstractly.
- **Verification of death**: given two different critical readings of the same text, the learner explains why both can be simultaneously valid rather than one being "more correct" than the other.

### MC-B-ANY-PERSONAL-REACTION-TO-A-TEXT-COUNTS-AS-A-VALID-READER-RESPONSE-INTERPRETATION
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 3 (language contamination), from "reader-response criticism"'s name itself sounding like it grants license for any personal reaction, an easy but incorrect inference from the label.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **a structural requirement (the "I felt/thought this because the text specifically ___" sentence stem)** forcing the connection to a textual feature, since an ungrounded reaction is easy to offer unless the grounding step is made structurally unavoidable.
- **Verification of death**: given a personal reaction to a text with no textual grounding, the learner revises it into a genuine reader-response interpretation by connecting the reaction to a specific textual feature.

## Analogies

**Best — the Doctor-Nutritionist-Trainer Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): three professionals examining the same person, each asking a different legitimate question, none "more correct" than the others — directly seeding the fix for MC-A. Extended with the sommelier framing: personal and experiential, but grounded in specific, describable features — directly seeding the fix for MC-B.
*Breaking point*: a doctor, nutritionist, and trainer's assessments are ultimately verifiable against objective physical outcomes, while different critical readings of a text don't converge on any single verifiable "ground truth" the way health assessments arguably do — the analogy's clean resolvability slightly overstates literary criticism's own certainty.

**Alternative — three different maps of the same city.** A subway map, a street map, and a topographical map all represent the same city but highlight completely different features for different purposes — none is "the one correct map," and each serves a specific kind of question (how do I get somewhere by train, by foot, or understand the terrain).
*Breaking point*: maps aim for objective, verifiable accuracy about a physical city, while literary interpretation involves genuinely more contestable, meaning-construction work that a purely representational map doesn't require.

**Visual anchor — the Doctor-Nutritionist-Trainer Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"One of these readings has to be the right one."** Directly installs the single-correct-interpretation misconception as a stated definition.
- **"Reader-response means whatever you personally feel about the story is a valid interpretation."** Directly installs the ungrounded-reaction misconception as a stated definition.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Doctor-Nutritionist-Trainer Anchor (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "Is the doctor's assessment more 'correct' than the nutritionist's, or are they just asking different questions?"
2. **The competing-versus-complementary-lenses collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Are these two readings racing to find the one correct answer, or asking two different questions?"
3. **The grounded-versus-ungrounded-reaction collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Is 'I didn't like this story, it was boring' doing the analytical work reader-response criticism requires?"

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both facts are directly checkable by naming the specific question each reading answers and by testing whether a reaction connects to a textual feature.

1. **Need**: "If two respected critics read the same poem and reach different conclusions, does one of them have to be wrong?"
2. **Playground**: the learner freely produces a historical reading and a formalist reading of the same text before being told the official complementary-lenses test.
3. **Invention**: "Can you say why you reacted a certain way to a story's ending by pointing to something SPECIFIC the author did?"
4. **Collision**: present "I didn't like this story" alongside a reaction grounded in a specific textual feature, and ask which is doing real analytical work.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — different critical lenses ask different, non-competing questions, and reader-response requires textual grounding — from the learner's own testing.
6. **Compression**: "Name the question each lens asks; ground your reaction in a specific feature."

## Teaching Sequence

- **Historical/biographical criticism (TA-1) comes first**, introducing the first concrete lens before the misconceptions are directly confronted.
- **Formalist criticism (TA-2) comes second**, introducing a genuinely different lens on the same text, setting up the contrast needed for MC-A.
- **Reader-response criticism (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the second misconception as the third lens is introduced.
- **Multiple lenses as complementary, not competing (TA-4) comes fourth**, directly confronting the first misconception by comparing all three produced readings.
- **Full application: multi-lens analysis of a new text (TA-5) comes last**, the generative capstone integrating both misconception fixes — matching this program's established sequencing principle of reserving the most integrative task for the culminating stage.

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for why two differing readings can both be valid, and their explicit connection of a personal reaction to a specific textual feature**, since a correct-looking multi-lens analysis with no stated reasoning cannot be distinguished from superficial pattern-matching.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated different-question framing** ("this reading is answering what the structure does, not what the author's life explains") applied to two new readings — the direct behavioral signature of the complementary-lenses fix taking hold.
- **Voluntary grounding of a personal reaction in a specific textual feature**, without needing the sentence-stem prompt — direct evidence the evidence-grounded reader-response fix is internalized.
- **Insisting one reading is "the real meaning" and the other wrong, or offering an ungrounded personal reaction as a complete interpretation** — the direct behavioral signature of MC-A or MC-B still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Name the question each lens asks — and ground your reaction in a specific feature."*

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads:

- **Producing a formalist reading (MP-2).** Failure alone indicates the formalist-mechanics step (TA-2) needs review, distinct from either misconception.
- **Grounding a reader-response interpretation (MP-3).** Failure alone indicates residual MC-B.
- **Explaining complementary lenses (MP-4).** Failure alone indicates residual MC-A.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But which reading is actually right about what the poem means?"** — take this as an accurate, reasonable desire for a clear answer, not a mistake; respond by adding the different-question test: "That's a natural question, yes — but is the historical reading answering the SAME question as the formalist one, or a different one entirely?"
- **"I just said I didn't like the ending — isn't that a reader-response reading?"** — a genuine, reasonable starting point given the approach's name; respond by naming the additional layer: "That's a real reaction, yes — but reader-response criticism asks you to go further: what SPECIFIC thing in the text produced that feeling?"
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full multi-lens analysis down to **naming the specific question one single critical approach asks, with production and comparison set aside.** Isolates the most basic lens-identification skill before returning to fuller tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just say what you think the story means."** This directly licenses the single-correct-interpretation pattern, undermining the multi-lens discipline this concept specifically builds.
