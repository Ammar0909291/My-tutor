# Skimming and Scanning — `eng.reading.skimming-and-scanning`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.reading.skimming-and-scanning` (canonical
  English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / reading / "Skimming and Scanning"
  — rapidly surveying a text for its general idea (skimming) or
  locating specific information (scanning).
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.reading.reading-fluency` — load-bearing
  because skimming and scanning are both accelerated, purposeful
  READING techniques (structural sampling, targeted visual search),
  not substitutes for reading ability — a learner who has not yet
  built automatic word recognition and appropriate-rate reading has no
  fluent baseline to accelerate away from, and would simply be
  guessing rather than genuinely skimming or scanning.
- **Unlocks** (from KG): none — this is a terminal node in the current
  curriculum sequence, per the Blueprint's own explicit framing.
- **Difficulty**: developing · **Bloom**: apply · **Mastery threshold**:
  0.75 · **Est. hours**: 1

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Select the correct rapid-reading technique for a given purpose —
   skimming for a text's general idea, scanning for one specific piece
   of information — rather than confusing the two or applying the
   wrong one.
2. Execute a skim by sampling structural cues (headings, topic
   sentences, first/last sentences of paragraphs) without reading every
   word.
3. Execute a scan by searching for a specific word, number, or name
   while deliberately ignoring surrounding text.
4. Recognize when a task genuinely requires full, careful reading
   rather than skimming or scanning, rather than treating rapid
   techniques as adequate for every reading task.

## Core Understanding

Skimming and scanning are two DISTINCT, deliberately NON-complete
rapid-reading techniques, each matched to a specific purpose — they are
not interchangeable names for the same shortcut. SKIMMING samples a
text's structure (headings, topic sentences, first/last lines) to grasp
its GENERAL idea quickly, without reading every word. SCANNING hunts
for ONE SPECIFIC target — a name, a date, a term — while deliberately
ignoring everything else in the text that isn't that target. Choosing
between them depends entirely on the reader's actual purpose: a
general-idea question calls for skimming; a locate-one-fact question
calls for scanning; applying the wrong one wastes time and produces the
wrong kind of result (scanning word-by-word for a general sense misses
the big picture; skimming broadly when hunting for one specific fact
wastes time reading irrelevant structure). A second, equally load-
bearing insight concerns the LIMITS of both techniques: neither gives
the same depth of understanding as full, careful reading. Both are
useful for previewing or locating — they are misapplied when they
replace careful reading for tasks that genuinely require deep
comprehension, such as understanding an author's nuanced, multi-step
argument or following how several ideas connect. Recognizing WHEN a
task needs full careful reading, rather than defaulting to a rapid
technique for everything, is as much a part of this concept's mastery
bar as correctly executing skim or scan mechanics themselves.

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "skimming and scanning are basically the same
  thing — reading fast"**: the arriving, pre-instructional model this
  concept exists to correct for MC-A — a natural default because both
  techniques share the surface property of NOT reading every word,
  masking their genuinely different targets (general idea vs. one
  specific fact).
- **Intermediate model — "I match the technique to my actual purpose —
  skim for general idea, scan for one specific fact — and I know how to
  execute each one mechanically"**: installed by TA-1 (choosing skim
  vs. scan), TA-2 (executing a skim), and TA-3 (executing a scan) using
  the Telescope-vs-Magnifying-Glass half of the anchor, resolving
  MC-A-SKIMMING-AND-SCANNING-ARE-THE-SAME-TECHNIQUE-WITH-DIFFERENT-
  NAMES.
- **Advanced model — "I recognize when a task needs full, careful
  reading instead of a rapid technique"**: installed by TA-4 using the
  Building-Outline-from-Across-the-Street half of the anchor, resolving
  MC-B-SKIMMING-OR-SCANNING-GIVES-THE-SAME-DEPTH-OF-UNDERSTANDING-AS-
  CAREFUL-READING.
- **Expert model — "across a mixed set of tasks with varying depth
  requirements, I select and correctly execute skim, scan, or full
  careful reading for each one"**: installed through the worked-
  example/mastery-probe integration (WE-3, MP-5), the concept's genuine
  mastery bar — a learner who matches technique correctly in isolation
  but defaults to one technique across a mixed task set (per Component
  8's first Adaptive Flag) has not yet reached this level.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own Component 8
  Adaptive Flags, correct isolated technique-matching (MP-1) does not
  guarantee correct application under mixed-task conditions (MP-5) — do
  not certify mastery on isolated matching alone if mixed-task
  performance still shows a default-to-one-technique pattern.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, each with its own
distinct root. The techniques-are-interchangeable failure (MC-A) exists
because skimming and scanning share the same surface signature — both
involve NOT reading every word, both feel like "going fast" — masking
that they target genuinely different outcomes (a holistic gist vs. one
isolated fact); a learner reasoning from surface similarity alone has
no built-in signal that the underlying purpose, not just the "not
reading everything" behavior, is what determines which technique
applies. The rapid-technique-equals-careful-reading failure (MC-B)
exists because skimming and scanning genuinely DO work well for their
intended purposes (general idea, specific fact) — successfully using
them for appropriate tasks reasonably builds confidence in the
technique, and nothing marks the technique's actual boundary until the
learner attempts to apply it to a task requiring nuanced, multi-step, or
connective understanding and discovers the rapid technique's rough
shape doesn't supply the needed depth.

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.reading.skimming-and-scanning.md`, Component 1) documents both
misconceptions this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/
replacement/discrimination content — reused by reference below, with
birth-type classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-A-SKIMMING-AND-SCANNING-ARE-THE-SAME-TECHNIQUE-WITH-DIFFERENT-
NAMES (Blueprint Component 1) — Type 2, perceptual intuition**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 2, not Type 1 — this is a genuine
  surface-similarity default rather than an overextension of a
  previously-correct rule: both techniques share the same visible
  behavior (not reading every word, moving quickly through text), and
  in the absence of any prior rule distinguishing them, a learner
  reaching for "these look/feel the same" is a perceptual, not
  logical, shortcut, similar in kind to how surface features drive
  Type 2 misconceptions generally.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, given a fresh
  general-idea task, spontaneously states "this needs skimming, not
  scanning, because I need the gist, not one fact" before beginning is
  showing the purpose-based (not surface-based) distinction has been
  internalized.

**MC-B-SKIMMING-OR-SCANNING-GIVES-THE-SAME-DEPTH-OF-UNDERSTANDING-AS-
CAREFUL-READING (Blueprint Component 1) — Type 1, overgeneralization**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: Type 1 — skimming and scanning genuinely
  ARE sufficient and effective for their intended purposes (general
  idea, specific fact), so a learner who has successfully used them
  reasonably extends that success to imply broader sufficiency; the
  overgeneralization surfaces specifically on tasks requiring nuance,
  multi-step reasoning, or connections between ideas — exactly the
  category of task the rapid techniques were never designed to serve.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, faced with a task
  requiring explanation of how several reasons connect, spontaneously
  states "I'll need to actually read this carefully, not just skim it"
  before attempting the task is showing the technique-boundary
  awareness has generalized past confident but shallow rapid-technique
  reliance.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — the Telescope-vs-Magnifying-Glass and Building-
  Outline-from-Across-the-Street combined anchor** (Blueprint Component
  3): a telescope reveals the big picture from far away (skimming for
  general idea), while a magnifying glass zooms in on one tiny specific
  detail (scanning for a specific fact) — directly seeding MC-A's fix,
  that these are different tools for different purposes, not
  interchangeable techniques. Glancing at a building's outline from
  across the street reveals a rough shape, not its actual interior
  layout — directly seeding MC-B's fix, that skimming/scanning give a
  rough shape, not the detailed understanding careful reading provides.
  Breaking point: both halves of the anchor build the conceptual
  distinction clearly, but TA-2 and TA-3's actual mechanical execution
  (which structural cues to sample, how to search while ignoring
  surrounding text) needs its own dedicated, hands-on practice beyond
  the anchor's framing alone.
- **Alternative — a headline scan vs. a Ctrl+F search**: skimming is
  like reading only the headlines and subheadings of a newspaper to get
  the day's gist; scanning is like using a search function (Ctrl+F) to
  find one specific word in a long document, ignoring everything else.
  Breaking point: useful and immediately familiar for digitally-fluent
  learners, but doesn't on its own address the careful-reading-boundary
  insight (MC-B), which needs the building-outline framing.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT frame skimming and scanning as "the fast way
  to read anything, so you don't have to read carefully"**: this
  framing directly reinforces MC-B by presenting rapid techniques as a
  general substitute for careful reading rather than purpose-specific
  tools; instead, per the Blueprint's own Adaptive Flags, every rapid-
  reading task should begin with an explicit purpose check — general
  idea, one specific fact, or genuine full understanding — before a
  technique is selected.

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/visual (the Telescope-vs-Magnifying-Glass and Building-
  Outline anchor)**: the Blueprint's Component 3 anchor — canonical,
  referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any news article, recipe, or webpage the
  learner has access to supplies genuine TA-2/TA-3 material — skim it
  for the general topic using only headings and first sentences, then
  scan it for one specific number or name; no special materials needed.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: WE-3/MP-5's mixed-task practice
  (a passage with tasks of varying depth) is inherently hands-on,
  requiring the learner to select AND correctly execute the matching
  technique — skim, scan, or full careful reading — for each task in
  turn, rather than applying one technique uniformly.
- **Teacher-demo (the technique-purpose contrast)**: the tutor models
  the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence demonstration directly — asking
  "what is this article generally about?" (requiring skimming) beside
  "what year did this event happen?" (requiring scanning) on the same
  article, making the purpose-driven technique choice directly visible.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  technique-selection, skim-execution, scan-execution, or careful-
  reading-recognition attempt (TA-1 through TA-4), ask the learner to
  state their own attempt before the tutor confirms — Universal
  Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**A brief, argued discovery step suits both misconceptions.** Both the
purpose-driven technique-selection insight and the rapid-technique-
boundary insight are genuinely felt, directly testable phenomena
well-suited to guided discovery: **need** (the learner is given a
general-idea question and a specific-fact question on the same
article and asked whether the same reading approach would efficiently
answer both, discovering the two questions call for genuinely different
reading behaviors); **playground/invention** (TA-1's technique-matching
practice and TA-2/TA-3's mechanical execution build initial comfort
with both techniques before the careful-reading-boundary complication
is introduced); **collision** (the Blueprint's own "explain the three
reasons the author gives and how they connect" task, attempted via a
skim alone, directly breaks the rapid-technique-sufficiency assumption
when the connections are missed); **formalization** (the different-
tools-different-purposes framing for MC-A, and the rough-shape-vs-
detailed-understanding framing for MC-B); **compression** (WE-3/MP-5's
fluent technique selection across a mixed task set, applied without
each purpose separately re-derived).

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (choosing skim vs. scan for the task) is placed first, directly
targeting MC-A, per the Blueprint's own session architecture —
establishing the purpose-driven selection judgment before either
technique's mechanics are practiced in depth, so the learner never
practices skimming or scanning divorced from the purpose that justifies
choosing it. TA-2 (executing a skim) and TA-3 (executing a scan) follow,
building the actual mechanical skill for each technique once the
learner already knows WHEN each applies. TA-4 (recognizing when careful
reading is required) is placed last, directly targeting MC-B, per the
Blueprint's own session architecture — deliberately sequenced after the
learner has built genuine confidence and competence in both rapid
techniques (TA-1 through TA-3), since the careful-reading-boundary
insight is most effectively taught as a limit on techniques the learner
already trusts and can execute, not as an abstract caution introduced
before any real skill exists to bound. This entry does not restate the
Blueprint's own turn-by-turn session script (Component 7) — see
Blueprint References below.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner states their intended
  technique AND purpose before beginning a rapid-reading task ("I'll
  skim this for the general idea" vs. "I'll scan for the date"), and
  whether the learner spontaneously flags a task as requiring full
  careful reading rather than defaulting to skim or scan — these are
  the primary diagnostic signals for whether TA-1 and TA-4's
  disciplines have genuinely generalized.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause before beginning a
  reading task to silently identify the purpose, followed by a
  correctly-matched technique, suggests the learner is consciously
  checking purpose rather than defaulting to a habitual technique — a
  good sign. Instant, uniform application of the same technique
  (usually scanning) regardless of task type is the concerning pattern
  for MC-A, per Component 8's first Adaptive Flag.
- **Native-intuition-gap stakes**: not specifically flagged for L1-
  transfer in this Blueprint — both misconceptions here stem from
  genuine surface-similarity and success-generalization patterns
  rather than L1-specific structural transfer, so no S9-specific
  routing beyond standard practice depth is called for; the Blueprint's
  own S9 note in the prerequisite concept (reading fluency) about
  automatic word recognition transferring readily to rapid scanning
  (Component 8's fourth Adaptive Flag) may be worth drawing explicitly
  for learners with strong prerequisite fluency.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "what's my actual purpose here — the
  general idea, one specific fact, or full understanding?" is this
  concept's load-bearing sentence for both misconceptions, per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3` — asked
  before any rapid-reading task begins, making the purpose check a
  routinely-invited response rather than an afterthought.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know"** on a technique-selection or execution task shrinks
  to the Component 3 Telescope-vs-Magnifying-Glass half of the anchor
  using the simplest, most contrastive task pair available (a clear
  general-idea question beside a clear specific-fact question), with
  the tutor modeling the purpose check before the learner attempts a
  fresh selection, skim, scan, or careful-reading-recognition task
  independently.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: for MC-A, applying the
  wrong technique confidently (scanning for gist, or skimming for a
  fact) is more likely than "I don't know" — route directly to the
  Blueprint's own conflict-evidence probe rather than treating it as a
  generic technique gap. For MC-B, confidently answering a nuanced
  question from a skim alone (missing the connections) is more likely
  than recognizing the limitation — route directly to TA-4's building-
  outline framing.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who
  spontaneously switches from a rapid technique to full careful reading
  mid-task upon recognizing a question requires connections ("wait,
  this needs me to actually read it properly") before being told is
  showing the technique-boundary discipline has generalized — this
  should be named and praised explicitly, consistent with the
  Blueprint's own Component 8 Adaptive Flags emphasis on requiring an
  explicit "does this need connections or reasoning?" check.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: procedural/framework — a purpose-driven technique-
selection judgment (skim, scan, or full careful reading) plus two
distinct mechanical skills (structural sampling, targeted search),
applied to genuinely novel reading tasks; mastery is demonstrated by
correctly selecting AND executing the matching technique across a
fresh, previously-unseen mixed-depth task set (MP-5) rather than by
recognizing a previously-practiced task type. Interleaving partners:
mix general-idea, specific-fact, and full-comprehension tasks together
in ongoing review sets (rather than blocking practice by technique), so
the learner continues exercising the purpose-check judgment, converging
toward fluent mixed-task selection only once each individual technique
is independently secure.

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: correctly matching and executing skim, scan, or full
  careful reading across fresh mixed-depth reading tasks.
- **Far**: none within the current KG — this is a terminal node with no
  further `unlocks`, per both the KG and the Blueprint's explicit
  framing of it as having "no further downstream unlocks in the
  current curriculum."
- **Real-world**: efficiently previewing articles or reports before
  deciding whether to read them fully; quickly locating specific
  information in reference material, documentation, or long
  correspondence; correctly recognizing when a contract, technical
  manual, or complex argument requires full, careful reading rather
  than a rapid pass.
- **Expert-transfer**: professional reading efficiency at any advanced
  level depends on fluently triaging reading purpose before committing
  reading time — an expert reader who correctly skims for orientation,
  scans for specific data, and switches to full careful reading only
  when genuinely needed manages large volumes of text far more
  effectively than one who reads everything at uniform depth or,
  conversely, rapid-reads everything regardless of the task's actual
  demands.
