## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Map a play's plot progression (rising action, climax, falling action) onto its act structure.
2. Explain what effect placing a real intermission at a given tension level creates for a live audience.
3. Assign specific structural functions (context-establishing, escalating, tension-releasing, climactic) to individual scenes, with textual justification.
4. Explain why a play's act break serves a different function than a novel's chapter break.
5. Fully analyze a new play's dramatic structure: plot progression, an act break's live-performance function, and multiple scenes' specific functions.

## Core Understanding

This concept teaches how `eng.literature.plot-structure`'s general five-stage progression is organized within `eng.literature.drama-basics`' act-and-scene toolkit, specifically shaped by live performance conventions. Two corrections define it. First, **acts and scenes are not arbitrary divisions like a novel's chapters** — act breaks in particular are often deliberately placed at moments of heightened tension specifically because they correspond to a real, SHARED pause in the audience's live experience (an intermission), a collective, simultaneous pause a novel's individually-paced chapter break doesn't replicate. Second, **dramatic tension does not build and resolve identically across every scene** — different scenes serve different specific structural functions (some establish character or context, some escalate conflict, some provide brief release before building again, some deliver the climactic confrontation), and analyzing a scene's contribution requires identifying its particular function, not applying one generic "tension-building" label indiscriminately.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "acts and scenes are just arbitrary divisions like chapters in a novel; every scene builds tension in the same generic way."**
The simulation: treat act/scene boundaries as interchangeable with chapter breaks, and label any scene's contribution as generic "tension building" with no further differentiation.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a play's act break at maximum tension right before an intermission versus a novel's individually-paced chapter break, asked whether they create the same kind of pause; and an early character-establishing scene versus a later climactic scene, asked whether both serve the same structural function.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Act breaks exploit a real, shared audience pause a chapter break doesn't have. Different scenes serve different, specific jobs."

**Intermediate — "I understand act breaks are special now, but I still tend to apply one generic 'building tension' label to most scenes rather than naming their specific function."**
The simulation: correctly analyzes act-break placement but hasn't yet reliably differentiated individual scenes' specific structural roles.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-3's direct practice assigning specific function labels (context-establishing, escalating, tension-releasing, climactic) to four scenes across a play.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Don't just say 'building tension' — name the SPECIFIC job this particular scene is doing."

**Advanced — "I map plot progression onto act structure, explain an act break's live-performance function, assign specific scene functions with evidence, and analyze a new play's full dramatic structure."**
The simulation: integrates plot-mapping, live-performance-function analysis, and differentiated scene-function assignment into one coherent structural analysis. Matches TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: none within this concept — confirmed terminal in the current curriculum sequence.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the full dramatic-structure analysis toolkit for any play."

**Expert — "at fluency, live-performance-aware act-break analysis and differentiated scene-function identification happen as an integrated part of ordinary dramatic reading and viewing."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit map-explain-differentiate routine is a scaffold for a reading habit that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated part of dramatic literacy.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **most students' primary exposure to structural "breaks" comes from reading prose, where chapter divisions are a familiar, individually-paced convention**, so this prose-reading habit transfers by analogy onto drama's act structure, obscuring the genuinely different, collective-experience function act breaks specifically exploit.

The second mechanism is that **"building tension" is a genuinely accurate description of a play's OVERALL trajectory**, so it's a reasonable default label to apply to any individual scene within that trajectory — the differentiation required (context-establishing versus escalating versus releasing versus climactic) demands a more granular, scene-by-scene analytical step most casual reading or viewing skips.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-ACTS-AND-SCENES-ARE-JUST-ARBITRARY-DIVISIONS-LIKE-CHAPTERS-IN-A-NOVEL-WITH-NO-SPECIAL-FUNCTION
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the act-break-at-intermission/chapter-break discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 6 (analogy overextension), from prose's chapter-break convention being extended by analogy onto drama's act structure, where the live, shared-audience-pause function is structurally absent from the prose analogue.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair must **explicitly name the collective-versus-individual pause distinction**, since the fix requires the learner to recognize a functional difference the surface similarity (both are "breaks") obscures.
- **Verification of death**: given an act's ending tension level, the learner explains what effect placing a real intermission at that specific point creates for a live audience.

### MC-B-DRAMATIC-TENSION-BUILDS-AND-RESOLVES-IN-EXACTLY-THE-SAME-WAY-ACROSS-EVERY-SCENE-WITH-NO-VARIATION-IN-FUNCTION
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — includes the context-establishing/climactic-confrontation discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely accurate overall-trajectory description ("the play builds tension"), extended into applying that single label uniformly to every individual scene rather than differentiating their specific functions.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct comparison of scenes with visibly different tension levels and content**, since the fix requires developing a granular, scene-by-scene analytical habit, not merely being told scenes differ.
- **Verification of death**: given three scenes from across a play's structure, the learner assigns each a specific structural function with textual justification.

## Analogies

**Best — the Concert-Intermission-vs-Playlist-Pause and Concert-Setlist-Variation Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): a concert's shared, simultaneous intermission versus an individual listener's self-timed playlist pause directly seeds the act-break fix; a concert setlist deliberately varying song intensity and function (mood-establishing, energy-building, breather, climactic finale) directly seeds the scene-differentiation fix.
*Breaking point*: a concert's setlist ordering is entirely under one performer's/curator's control with clear sequential intent, while a play's scene sequence, though also authorially controlled, unfolds within a longer narrative arc with more embedded plot logic than a setlist's purely experiential pacing — useful for the varied-function intuition, less precise for the narrative-versus-purely-experiential distinction.

**Alternative — a sports game's structure (quarters/halves with a real halftime) versus a recorded highlight reel with arbitrary cut points.** A live game's halftime is a real, shared pause the entire stadium experiences together, timed by the game's actual rules, unlike a highlight reel's edited cuts, which any individual viewer could pause anywhere.
*Breaking point*: a sports halftime's timing is fixed by external game rules, not by a deliberate tension-building authorial choice, making it a somewhat different mechanism from a playwright's intentional act-break placement.

**Visual anchor — the Concert-Intermission-vs-Playlist-Pause and Concert-Setlist-Variation Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"Acts and scenes are basically just chapters for plays."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated equivalence.
- **"Every scene in a play is building toward the climax the same way."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated claim.

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both structural facts are directly checkable by comparing act-break placement against tension level and by examining multiple scenes for distinct functions.

1. **Need**: "If a play's whole audience pauses together at intermission, is that the same experience as you individually deciding to put a book down?"
2. **Playground**: the learner examines several scenes from a play's outline freely, guessing their function, before being told the official four-category system.
3. **Invention**: "What effect would placing a real intermission right at this act's most tense moment create for the audience?"
4. **Collision**: present an early context-establishing scene and a late climactic scene; ask whether both serve the same function.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — act breaks exploit a live, shared pause; scenes serve differentiated, not uniform, functions — from the learner's own analysis.
6. **Compression**: "Act breaks share a real pause with the whole audience; scenes each do their own specific job."

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's stated reasoning for an act break's live-performance effect or a scene's specific function**, since a correct-looking analysis with no stated shared-pause or function-specific reasoning cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **A stated shared-experience check** ("the whole audience pauses together right here, unlike a reader who chooses their own stopping point") applied to a new act break — the direct behavioral signature of the live-performance fix taking hold.
- **A specific, named function assigned to a scene** ("this scene is establishing the characters' relationship, not building tension yet") rather than a generic "tension" label — direct evidence the scene-differentiation fix is internalized.
- **An act break analyzed with no reference to the live audience, or every scene labeled identically as "building tension"** — the direct behavioral signature of either misconception still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Act breaks share a real pause with the whole audience; every scene has its own specific job to do."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"Isn't an act break just like a chapter ending in a book?"** — take this as a reasonable extension of prose-reading experience; respond by naming the collective-pause distinction: "A chapter break, you choose when to stop. An act break, the WHOLE audience pauses together at that exact moment — that's a real, shared experience a book doesn't have."
- **"Isn't every scene just building up to the ending?"** — a reasonable-sounding overall-trajectory description; respond by asking for the specific job: "Sure, overall — but what specific job is THIS scene doing right now? Is it introducing someone, escalating a conflict, or something else?"
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full new-play analysis down to **assigning a specific function label to a single, clearly-differentiated scene, with no plot-mapping or act-break task demanded.** Isolates the most basic scene-differentiation skill before returning to fuller analysis.
- **Never shrink to "just say if this scene is tense or not."** This directly re-licenses the generic-tension-labeling pattern this concept specifically works to dismantle.
