## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Identify three specific authorial choices (selection, framing, interpretation) shaping a memoir or essay passage.
2. Match four unlabeled passages to memoir, biography, journalism, or the essay with purpose-based reasoning.
3. Identify two conventions specific to a given passage's subgenre.
4. Explain what makes a genuine nonfiction passage carry authorial voice, as distinguished from a raw factual list.
5. Fully analyze a new nonfiction passage: identify authorial voice, determine its subgenre, and name its subgenre-specific conventions.

## Core Understanding

Two corrections define this concept. First, **"nonfiction" does not mean purely objective, neutral fact-reporting with no author voice or interpretation** — forms like memoir and the personal essay involve genuine authorial perspective, deliberate selection of which real events to include, and interpretive framing of their meaning; "nonfiction" describes the subject matter's factual basis, not the absence of a shaping perspective. Second, **all nonfiction subgenres are not basically the same thing** — memoir (personal, first-person reflection on one's own life), biography (a researched account of someone else's life), journalism (timely reporting of current events), and the essay (exploring or arguing an idea) each serve genuinely distinct purposes with distinct conventions; confusing them leads to misapplying one form's conventions to another, like expecting a biography to read like a diary.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "nonfiction means purely objective, neutral fact-reporting with no author voice; memoir, biography, journalism, and the essay are all basically the same thing."**
The simulation: expect nonfiction to sound flat and impersonal, with no interpretive shaping, and treat any real-event-based writing as one undifferentiated category.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — a memoir passage with clear personal reflection and selective emphasis, asked whether it has genuine voice or is neutral fact; and four opening lines from four subgenres, asked whether all four share the same purpose.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Nonfiction carries genuine authorial voice and interpretation. Each subgenre has its own distinct purpose."

**Intermediate — "I recognize authorial voice in memoir now, but I still tend to lump biography, journalism, and the essay together as similar."**
The simulation: correctly identifies personal shaping in reflective nonfiction but hasn't yet reliably distinguished the four subgenres' distinct purposes.
*Upgrade trigger*: TA-2's direct matching practice, sorting four unlabeled passages into their correct subgenre with purpose-based reasoning.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Ask: is this the author's own life, someone else's researched life, a timely event, or an idea being explored? Each is a different subgenre."

**Advanced — "I identify authorial voice and shaping choices, correctly distinguish the four subgenres, name subgenre-specific conventions, and analyze a new passage integrating all of this."**
The simulation: integrates authorial-voice recognition, subgenre-distinction, and convention-identification into one coherent nonfiction analysis. Matches TA-4.
*Upgrade trigger*: none within this concept — confirmed terminal in the current curriculum sequence.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the full framework for reading nonfiction prose across its major forms."

**Expert — "at fluency, authorial-voice recognition and subgenre-distinction happen as an integrated part of ordinary nonfiction reading."**
Named to mark the arc; the explicit identify-determine-name routine is a scaffold for a reading habit that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated part of literary comprehension.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant mechanism is that **"nonfiction" is defined by contrast with "fiction" (made-up events)**, and this factual-basis definition is easily but incorrectly extended to imply the ABSENCE of any interpretive shaping, when in fact real events can still be selected, framed, and interpreted by an author with genuine voice.

The second mechanism is that **all four subgenres share the surface feature of dealing in real events**, making that shared feature perceptually salient enough to overshadow their genuinely distinct purposes and conventions, which require closer attention to the author's specific relationship to the subject (own life, someone else's life, current events, or an idea) to distinguish.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-A-NONFICTION-MEANS-PURELY-OBJECTIVE-NEUTRAL-FACT-REPORTING-WITH-NO-AUTHOR-VOICE-OR-INTERPRETATION
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-A — includes the raw-facts/memoir-passage discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of nonfiction's genuine factual-basis definition, extended into assuming factual basis implies the absence of any authorial shaping, when the two are actually independent.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct identification of specific authorial choices within a genuinely nonfiction text**, since the fix requires demonstrating shaping IS present despite factual grounding, not merely asserting nonfiction can have voice.
- **Verification of death**: given a memoir or essay passage, the learner identifies three specific authorial choices shaping the account.

### MC-B-ALL-NONFICTION-SUBGENRES-ESSAY-MEMOIR-BIOGRAPHY-JOURNALISM-ARE-BASICALLY-THE-SAME-THING
*(Blueprint Component 1, second entry — includes the four-subgenre-opening-lines discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely shared surface feature (dealing in real events), extended into assuming shared purpose and conventions, when each subgenre actually serves a distinct function.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct side-by-side comparison of all four subgenres' opening lines**, since the fix requires the learner to see the purpose-difference made concrete across genuinely comparable examples, not an abstract list of definitions.
- **Verification of death**: given four short unlabeled passages, the learner matches each to memoir, biography, journalism, or the essay with purpose-based reasoning.

## Analogies

**Best — the Photographer's-Choices and Diary-vs-Biography-vs-News-Article-vs-Op-Ed Anchor** (Blueprint Component 3): a photograph that still involves deliberate choices of angle and framing despite depicting something real directly seeds the authorial-voice fix; a diary, a biography, a news article, and a persuasive op-ed, each serving a genuinely distinct purpose despite all being "about real things," directly seeds the subgenre-distinction fix.
*Breaking point*: a photograph's "choices" (angle, framing, cropping) are visually, immediately apparent even to a casual viewer, while a prose passage's authorial choices (selection, interpretation) require closer, more deliberate textual attention to notice — useful for the choices-shape-reality-even-when-factual intuition, less precise for implying nonfiction's shaping is as immediately visible as a photograph's framing.

**Alternative — a museum's curated exhibit versus a warehouse's unsorted inventory.** A curator selecting, arranging, and labeling real historical artifacts (memoir/essay) makes deliberate interpretive choices about what story the artifacts tell, unlike an unsorted warehouse inventory (a hypothetical purely neutral fact-list) with no organizing perspective.
*Breaking point*: a museum curator's choices are usually made explicit through wall text and exhibit design, while a memoir's or essay's authorial choices are embedded in the prose itself, requiring the reader's own analytical effort to surface.

**Visual anchor — the Photographer's-Choices and Diary-vs-Biography-vs-News-Article-vs-Op-Ed Anchor**, as the primary teaching metaphor and working instrument.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"If it's nonfiction, it's just reporting the facts with no personal viewpoint."** Directly installs MC-A as a stated definition.
- **"Memoir, biography, journalism, and essays are all just nonfiction — same category, same rules."** Directly installs MC-B as a stated equivalence.

## Discovery Questions

Well suited to **guided discovery**, since both facts are directly checkable by identifying authorial choices in a nonfiction passage and by comparing subgenre purposes side by side.

1. **Need**: "If a memoir is about real events, does that mean the author added nothing of their own to how it's told?"
2. **Playground**: the learner reads several nonfiction excerpts freely, guessing which subgenre each belongs to, before being told the official four-subgenre framework.
3. **Invention**: "Can you find three specific choices this author made about what to include or how to frame it?"
4. **Collision**: present four subgenre opening lines; ask whether they all share the same purpose.
5. **Formalization**: name the two facts — nonfiction carries genuine authorial voice; the four subgenres each serve a distinct purpose — from the learner's own comparisons.
6. **Compression**: "Real events, but a real voice too — and each nonfiction form has its own job."

## Voice Teaching Notes

Core evidence is **the learner's cited specific authorial choices and their purpose-based reasoning for a subgenre determination**, since a correct-sounding subgenre label with no cited purpose-based reasoning, or a "voiceless" judgment with no examined authorial choice, cannot be distinguished from a lucky guess.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Specific authorial choices cited** ("the author chose to emphasize this one meal, and frames it retrospectively as meaningful") rather than a bare "this has voice" claim — the direct behavioral signature of the authorial-voice fix taking hold.
- **A subgenre determination justified by purpose** ("this is biography because it's researching someone else's life in the third person") rather than a guess — direct evidence the subgenre-distinction fix is internalized.
- **A nonfiction passage with clear personal reflection dismissed as "just facts," or a biography analyzed using memoir conventions** — the direct behavioral signature of either misconception still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Real events, but always a real voice — and each nonfiction form has its own specific job."*

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"This is nonfiction, so it should just be facts, right?"** — take this as a reasonable inference from the fiction/nonfiction contrast; respond by pointing to specific choices: "It's factually grounded, yes — but look at what the author chose to include and how they framed it. That's a real voice."
- **"A biography and a memoir are both just someone's life story, aren't they?"** — a reasonable-sounding surface similarity; respond by naming the perspective difference: "Whose life is it, and who's telling it? A memoir is your OWN life, in your own voice. A biography is researching SOMEONE ELSE's life."
- **Smaller question to shrink to**: from a full new-passage analysis down to **identifying a single authorial choice in one memoir passage, with no subgenre-matching or convention-identification task demanded.** Isolates the most basic authorial-voice-recognition skill before returning to fuller analysis.
- **Never shrink to "just say if this is fiction or nonfiction."** This licenses a coarse binary judgment without the authorial-voice or subgenre-distinction discipline this concept specifically builds.
