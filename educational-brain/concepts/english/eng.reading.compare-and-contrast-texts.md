## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Write explicit, point-by-point comparison statements connecting two texts on shared dimensions, rather than producing two separate summaries side by side.
2. Select comparison dimensions (theme, structure, tone, purpose, character choices, setting) appropriate to the specific pair of texts being compared.
3. Investigate structural, tonal, and purpose-based differences between two texts independently, even when the texts share a topic.
4. Complete a graphic organizer tracking similarities and differences across multiple dimensions.
5. Produce a complete written comparative analysis of two novel texts.

## Core Understanding

Comparing two texts means **explicitly connecting them on shared dimensions, not placing two separate summaries side by side** — this is the concept's first load-bearing fact: a genuine comparison identifies specific points of contact (theme, structure, tone, character choices) and states how the two texts relate on each one, using language like "both... but" or "unlike Text A, Text B..." — two independent summaries, however accurate each is individually, tell a reader nothing about how the texts relate to each other, which is the entire point of comparison. The concept's second, equally load-bearing fact is that **sharing a topic does not mean two texts are mostly similar** — the same event or theme can be handled in completely different structures, tones, purposes, and perspectives, and a genuine comparison investigates differences just as rigorously as similarities, independently checking each dimension rather than assuming shared topic guarantees shared treatment. A neutral news report and a dramatic survivor's account of the identical historical event can differ substantially in structure, tone, and purpose despite sharing every fact about what happened.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "comparing two texts means writing a summary of the first one, then a summary of the second one."**
The runnable simulation: produce two separate, accurate summaries placed back-to-back, with no sentences directly linking or contrasting the two texts. This is a reasonable default, since summarizing each text individually is a genuinely necessary first step and, on its own, feels like it has "covered" both texts.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing a sequential-summary "comparison" against a genuine point-by-point comparison, and asking which actually helps a reader understand how the two texts relate.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Two summaries side by side aren't a comparison — pick shared dimensions and state explicitly how the two texts are similar or different on each one."

**Intermediate — "I write explicit point-by-point comparisons, but if two texts share a topic, I assume they'll mostly be similar and don't check as carefully for differences."**
The simulation: correctly connects the two texts on shared dimensions using explicit comparison language, but under-investigates genuine differences when the texts happen to share a topic, sometimes overlooking real structural or tonal contrasts.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — comparing a neutral news report and a dramatic survivor's account of the same event, and being asked whether "same topic" means these two texts are mostly the same.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Sharing a topic tells you nothing about structure, tone, or purpose — check each dimension independently, even when the topic is identical."

**Advanced — "I write explicit, dimension-based comparisons, select dimensions appropriate to the specific text pair, and independently investigate similarities AND differences even under a shared topic."**
The simulation: produces a full comparative analysis using an organizer, selecting relevant dimensions for the specific pair and rigorously checking each one rather than assuming similarity from shared topic. This is the target model, matching TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: the direct unlock `eng.reading.reading-across-genres`, where this concept's dimension-based comparison framework extends to comparing texts of entirely different forms.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gives you the comparison framework for any two texts. The next step applies it specifically when the texts belong to entirely different genres, adding genre-convention differences as new dimensions."

**Expert — "at full comparative-reading fluency, identifying meaningful comparison dimensions and independently checking similarity/difference on each happens as an integrated part of reading multiple texts on a topic, with formal organizers reserved for building a defensible written analysis."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit dimension-dial routine is a scaffold for a judgment that, at true fluency, operates as an integrated background process while reading — named here so the tutor understands the routine's eventual goal is fluent, continuous comparative reading, not a permanently maintained formal-organizer exercise.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **summarizing each text individually genuinely is the correct first step**, and a learner who has just spent significant effort producing two accurate summaries reasonably feels the task is largely complete — nothing about having summarized each text separately signals that a further, distinct relational step (explicit connection) is still required.

The second mechanism is that **shared topic is a genuinely strong, real signal of some similarity**, so a learner's assumption that shared topic predicts broader similarity is not an arbitrary error but a reasonable (if incomplete) inference from real correlational evidence — texts on the same topic often do share some content — the assumption breaks down specifically for dimensions (structure, tone, purpose, perspective) that are genuinely independent of topic and can vary widely even when the topic is identical.

The third mechanism, specific to this concept's doubled cognitive demand (per the Blueprint's own S0 protocol routing), is that **comparison requires applying structure-identification and summarizing simultaneously to two texts at once**, so a learner whose grip on either prerequisite skill is only moderately secure will find that weakness compounds quickly under the combined demand, producing errors that look like comparison-specific failures but actually trace back to a shakier prerequisite.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-COMPARING-TWO-TEXTS-MEANS-SUMMARIZING-BOTH-ONE-AFTER-ANOTHER
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-COMPARING-TWO-TEXTS-MEANS-SUMMARIZING-BOTH-ONE-AFTER-ANOTHER — includes the sequential-summary/point-by-point discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of the genuinely correct first step (summarize each text) extended into treating that step as sufficient, when a further, distinct relational step is actually required.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because summarizing each text isn't a wrong step, only an incomplete one, the repair must **add explicit connection-making on top of, not instead of, individual summarization** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this by framing individual summarizing as "just the first step," not as a mistake.
- **Verification of death**: given two texts, the learner writes explicit comparison statements connecting them on specific dimensions, rather than two adjacent, unconnected summaries.

### MC-TWO-TEXTS-ON-THE-SAME-TOPIC-MUST-BE-MOSTLY-THE-SAME
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-TWO-TEXTS-ON-THE-SAME-TOPIC-MUST-BE-MOSTLY-THE-SAME — includes the neutral-news-report/survivor-account discrimination pair.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of a genuinely real correlational pattern (same-topic texts often do share some content) extended past the dimensions (structure, tone, purpose, perspective) that are actually independent of topic.
- **Teaching consequence**: the repair requires **direct, concrete exposure to a genuine same-topic-different-treatment pair**, since an abstract claim that "topic doesn't determine everything" is less convincing than personally investigating a case where the difference is stark and undeniable.
- **Verification of death**: given two same-topic texts with genuinely different structure or tone, the learner independently investigates and correctly identifies the specific differences, rather than assuming similarity from the shared topic.

## Analogies

**Best — the Twin-Lens Viewer** (Blueprint Component 3): two side-by-side lenses, each trained on one text, with shared "dimension dials" (Theme, Structure, Tone, Purpose) turned to produce explicit comparison statements on each dimension directly seeds the point-by-point fix; demonstrating that the Structure and Tone dials can show different settings even when a separate "Topic" filter shows the same topic for both texts directly seeds the same-topic-doesn't-force-similarity fix.
*Breaking point*: a viewer's lenses are fixed, simultaneous, and symmetric, while real comparative reading often proceeds somewhat sequentially (read text A, then text B, then compare) rather than truly side-by-side — useful for the shared-dimension-checking intuition, less precise for the actual temporal reading process.

**Alternative — two chefs' recipes for the same dish.** Both recipes share the topic (the dish), but one chef's version might be elaborate and slow-cooked while another's is quick and simplified — comparing the recipes means checking specific dimensions (ingredients, technique, time) independently, not assuming they're nearly identical just because they're "the same dish."
*Breaking point*: recipes have an objectively checkable end result (the finished dish) that can validate which differences actually matter, while two texts' "success" at their differing purposes is a much less objectively measurable outcome — useful for the same-topic-different-treatment intuition, less precise for implying comparative reading has as clean an evaluative endpoint as tasting the finished dish.

**Visual anchor — the Twin-Lens Viewer** as already described above, doubling as both the primary teaching metaphor and the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organized around.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"To compare two texts, summarize each one and put them together."** Directly installs MC-COMPARING-TWO-TEXTS-MEANS-SUMMARIZING-BOTH-ONE-AFTER-ANOTHER as a stated procedure, precisely the sequential-summary error this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"If two texts are about the same thing, they're basically saying the same thing."** Directly installs MC-TWO-TEXTS-ON-THE-SAME-TOPIC-MUST-BE-MOSTLY-THE-SAME as a stated assumption, exactly the same-topic-implies-similarity error this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The Twin-Lens Viewer (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there. *Predict first*: "If these two texts share a topic, do you expect their Structure and Tone dials to match too?" Then turn the dials and confirm.
2. **The sequential-summary/point-by-point collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "Does this back-to-back summary actually show how the texts relate?" Then compare against a genuine comparison and resolve.
3. **The neutral-report/survivor-account collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "Since these are about the same disaster, do you expect them to read similarly?" Then investigate tone and purpose and resolve.
4. **The full comparative analysis (learner activity, TA-5).** *Predict first*: "Which dimensions do you expect to differ most between these two novel texts?" Then analyze fully and confirm together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (connection-over-sequential-summary; independent-dimension investigation) is directly checkable through the learner's own comparison attempts on real text pairs.

1. **Need**: "If you write a summary of each of two texts, have you actually compared them?"
2. **Playground**: the learner freely writes comparison statements on a self-chosen dimension for a text pair, before being told which dimensions are typically most useful.
3. **Invention**: "For two texts on the same topic, which dimensions do you expect to differ, and which to match?" The learner predicts before checking.
4. **Collision**: present the neutral-report/survivor-account pair and ask whether same topic guarantees similar treatment.
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — comparison requires explicit connection, and shared topic doesn't guarantee shared treatment — from the learner's own evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Connect the texts on shared dimensions — and check for differences even when the topic matches."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **Dimension-based comparison practice (TA-1) is established first**, directly confronting the first misconception before any dimension-selection or shared-topic complications are introduced, since the core skill of writing an explicit, connecting comparison statement must be secure before refining which dimensions to choose or how to handle shared topics.
- **Choosing relevant dimensions (TA-2) comes second**, adding the judgment of *which* dimensions matter for a specific text pair, once the mechanical skill of writing a comparison statement (TA-1) on an assigned dimension is already secure.
- **Investigating differences under a shared topic (TA-3) comes third**, directly confronting the second misconception only once the learner already has a secure comparison-writing skill (TA-1/TA-2) to apply rigorously to the harder, same-topic case.
- **Building a comparison organizer (TA-4) and writing a full comparative analysis (TA-5) come last**, with TA-4 adding a systematic tracking tool and TA-5 requiring the learner to integrate dimension-selection, explicit connection, and independent same-topic investigation on genuinely novel texts — matching this program's established sequencing principle that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the mandatory MP-1/MP-2 dual-pass gate before mastery certification, the TA-5-required prerequisite for the downstream unlock, and the explicit "twin-lens viewer" framing reuse when introducing `eng.reading.reading-across-genres`).

## Tutor Actions

In recommended order, from `../teaching-actions/`:

1. **Demonstration** (SHOW) — the Twin-Lens Viewer and both collision scenarios.
2. **Error Analysis** (TEST-THINKING) — both collisions function as guided error analysis against the learner's own plausible-but-incomplete or incorrect approach (sequential summarizing, or same-topic assumption).
3. **Concept Map** (ORGANIZE) — the comparison organizer (TA-4) is naturally a concept-map artifact, tracking similarities and differences systematically across dimensions.
4. **Matching** (DO) — selecting relevant comparison dimensions for a specific text pair (TA-2) is fundamentally a matching/relevance-judgment action.

**Does not fit**: **Game** — the genuine analytical care this concept requires (checking dimensions independently rather than assuming) could be undermined by speed-pressured competition. **Role-Play** — no natural social/dialogic dimension to a textual-comparison-analysis skill.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is **spoken or written comparative reasoning with explicit connecting language** — the learner's stated comparison statements, using "both... but" or "unlike... " constructions, since two separately-accurate descriptions with no connecting language cannot be distinguished from sequential summarizing even when each individual description is correct.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Spontaneous use of explicit comparison language** ("both texts share this theme, but they differ in...") **when discussing two texts, without being prompted to use a specific sentence template** — the direct behavioral signature of the first misconception's repair.
- **A stated, deliberate check of a dimension (tone, structure) on a same-topic text pair, rather than an assumption of similarity** ("even though it's the same event, let me check if the tone is actually the same") — the direct target evidence for the second misconception's repair.
- **Two accurate, complete, but entirely separate descriptions of two texts, offered one after the other with no linking statement** — the direct behavioral signature of the first misconception still active.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Connect the two texts explicitly on shared dimensions — and never assume same topic means same treatment."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Genuine point-by-point comparison (MP-1) and investigating differences under shared topic (MP-2).** Per the Blueprint's own S9 protocol routing, mastery must NOT be certified unless both pass — a learner who selects dimensions well (MP-3) but still produces sequential summaries or assumes topic-based similarity has not yet learned the genuine relational skill this concept requires.
- **Selecting relevant comparison dimensions (MP-3).** Listen for justification specific to the actual text pair (why these dimensions matter for *these* texts), not a generic, memorized dimension list applied without regard to the specific pair.
- **Completing a comparison organizer (MP-4).** Watch for entries that genuinely differ between the two texts on at least some dimensions — an organizer where every cell reads nearly identically for both texts may indicate insufficient independent investigation, even if the organizer is technically filled out.
- **Independent full comparative analysis (MP-5).** This concept's synthesis item; a strong performance here with weak MP-1/MP-2 scores suggests scripted-practice success without full internalization.

**Mastery certification trigger**: explicit, connected comparison statements on a novel text pair; correctly identified genuine differences under a shared novel topic; justified, pair-specific dimension selection; a completed organizer showing genuine independent investigation per dimension; and a full, independent comparative analysis of two novel texts.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I already summarized both, isn't that comparing them?"** — take this as an accurate report of real, necessary work, not a mistake to be dismissed; respond by adding rather than contradicting: "Both summaries are solid and necessary — now let's take the next step and connect them. Pick one thing both texts do, and write a sentence that says how they're similar or different on that specific thing."
- **"They're about the exact same event, so obviously they're pretty similar"** — a genuine, reasonable inference from real correlational evidence; respond by naming the actual test directly: "Same event, yes — but let's actually check the tone and structure separately, rather than assuming. Sometimes they match, sometimes they really don't."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from a full comparative analysis with an organizer, down to **a single dimension (just tone, for instance) compared across two short, clearly different texts, with no shared-topic complication at all.** This removes the multi-dimension and same-topic-investigation demands entirely, isolating the most basic connecting-comparison skill before returning to harder, fuller cases.
- **Never shrink to "just say they're similar because they're about the same thing."** This directly licenses the same-topic-implies-similarity error, undermining the independent-investigation habit this concept specifically builds.
