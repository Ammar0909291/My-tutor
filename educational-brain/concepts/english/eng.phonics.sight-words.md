# High-Frequency Sight Words — `eng.phonics.sight-words`

## Identity

- **Concept ID**: `eng.phonics.sight-words` (canonical English KG)
- **Curriculum location**: English / phonics / "High-Frequency Sight
  Words" — the concept that shifts fluency emphasis from decoding
  accuracy to reading SPEED, teaching that most common words are best
  handled by instant recognition rather than sound-by-sound decoding,
  even when they are fully decodable.
- **Prerequisites**: `eng.phonics.letter-sound-correspondence` —
  specifically the general symbol-to-sound mapping principle; load-
  bearing because this concept's core insight (many sight words ARE
  decodable, just taught for speed) only makes sense to a learner who
  already has a working decoding toolkit to apply as a first attempt,
  distinguishing genuinely regular sight words from the smaller
  genuinely irregular set.
- **Unlocks / cross-links** (from KG): no `unlocks` entries in the
  phonics domain from this concept specifically, but the KG cross-links
  this concept to `eng.vocab.word-recognition` — this concept is the
  phonics-side automaticity foundation that broader vocabulary-level
  word recognition builds on, a boundary the Blueprint's own Adaptive
  Flags name explicitly (do not duplicate vocabulary-acquisition
  content here).
- **Difficulty**: foundational · **Bloom**: remember · **Mastery
  threshold**: 0.80 · **Est. hours**: 2

## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Attempt to decode any newly-encountered high-frequency word before
   assuming it must be memorized, successfully sounding out
   phonetically regular sight words (and, big, can, get).
2. Recognize a small set of genuinely irregular high-frequency words
   (said, was, of, the, they, one) as requiring direct memorization,
   correctly identifying which specific letter(s) behave unexpectedly
   in each.
3. Recognize a sight word quickly from a brief visual exposure, while
   still processing every letter in order rather than guessing from
   overall shape or length.
4. Discriminate commonly confused "twin" sight-word pairs (was/saw,
   there/three, what/that) by full letter-order checking.
5. Read connected text containing a mix of sight words and decodable
   words fluently, applying instant recognition and sounding-out
   appropriately to each.

## Core Understanding

"Sight word" is a category defined by TEACHING PURPOSE (high frequency,
worth building to instant, effortless recognition) — it is not a
category defined by decodability. This distinction is the single most
consequential fact this concept must install, because the two
properties are independent: most high-frequency sight words (and, big,
can, get, him, not) are fully phonetically regular and can be sounded
out using ordinary letter-sound correspondences; only a genuinely small
subset (said, was, of, the, they, one) have spelling-sound
correspondences irregular enough to require rote memorization
independent of decoding. A learner who conflates "sight word" with
"unreadable by sounding out" loses the ability to apply decoding to the
large majority of the category, while a learner who tries to force
every sight word through regular decoding will fail specifically on the
smaller irregular subset. The correct strategy is therefore always
"attempt to decode first; fall back to memorization only for the
specific words where decoding genuinely fails."

## Mental Models

- **Beginner model — "sight words are common words I'm learning to
  recognize fast"**: installed by the Component 3 flash-and-check
  anchor, establishing speed and automaticity as the category's defining
  feature from the outset, without yet distinguishing regular from
  irregular members.
- **Intermediate model — "most sight words are actually decodable — I
  should try sounding them out first"**: installed by TA-1, which
  presents ONLY phonetically regular high-frequency words initially,
  deliberately building the "these are readable" insight before any
  irregular exceptions are introduced. Upgrade trigger: successful,
  independent decoding of a regular sight word the learner has not
  specifically memorized.
- **Advanced model — "a small, identifiable subset of sight words are
  genuinely irregular and must be memorized, with the specific
  unexpected letter(s) flagged"**: installed by TA-2, which introduces
  the irregular subset only AFTER the regular-decodability insight is
  secure, and explicitly names which letter in each irregular word
  behaves unexpectedly (e.g., "said"'s "ai" not making its expected
  sound) rather than treating the whole word as an undifferentiated
  blob. Upgrade trigger: correct identification of the SPECIFIC
  irregular element in a newly-introduced irregular word, not merely
  successful rote recall of the whole word.
- **Expert model — "fast recognition still requires full letter-order
  processing, not shape-guessing"**: installed by TA-3/TA-4, resolving
  MC-MEMORIZING-THE-WORD-SHAPE-IS-ENOUGH — speed and completeness are
  not in tension; a fluent reader processes every letter, just very
  quickly, and this model is what prevents twin-word confusions
  (was/saw) that shape-based guessing would otherwise produce.
- **Do not upgrade early**: per the Blueprint's own explicit sequencing,
  a learner not yet secure that MOST sight words are decodable (TA-1)
  should not be given the irregular subset (TA-2) — introducing
  irregularity before the regular-decodability insight is established
  risks the learner concluding the entire category is arbitrary,
  undermining the very insight TA-1 exists to build.

## Why Students Fail

Two independent failure mechanisms operate here, matching the
Blueprint's two misconceptions. The refuse-to-decode failure exists
because the label "sight word" is genuinely ambiguous in ordinary usage
— it is easy for a learner (or an under-specified prior instruction) to
interpret "taught for sight recognition" as "cannot be sounded out,"
a plausible but incorrect inference from the name itself, rather than
from the words' actual phonetic properties. The shape-guessing failure
exists because genuine reading fluency DOES eventually involve very
fast, holistic word recognition — a learner who has begun to develop
real fluency is not wrong that fast processing is the goal, but has
prematurely substituted an incomplete cue (shape, length, first letter)
for the complete letter-by-letter processing that underlies genuine
fluent recognition, producing systematic errors specifically on words
that share those incomplete cues (was/saw share all three letters and
similar length; there/three share substantial visual overlap).

## Misconceptions

The existing Blueprint (`docs/curriculum/blueprints/
eng.phonics.sight-words.md`, Component 1) documents both misconceptions
this concept produces, with full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/
discrimination content — reused by reference below, with birth-type
classification added
(`../../misconceptions/01-birth-taxonomy.md`).

**MC-SIGHT-WORDS-CANNOT-BE-SOUNDED-OUT-AT-ALL (Blueprint Component 1) —
Type 3, language contamination**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: this is contamination from the CATEGORY
  LABEL itself rather than an overgeneralized phonics rule — the
  learner has correctly heard "sight word" described as a word to be
  recognized on sight, and reasonably (if incorrectly) infers that this
  teaching-method label implies a property of the word (unreadability),
  when in fact the label describes only the teaching GOAL for that word,
  independent of its actual decodability.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who, on being shown that
  "and" decodes successfully, immediately generalizes ("oh, so I should
  always try sounding it out first") is showing rapid uptake of the
  correct model — a stronger and faster resolution than a learner who
  needs the conflict-evidence probe repeated on several different
  regular sight words before accepting the pattern.

**MC-MEMORIZING-THE-WORD-SHAPE-IS-ENOUGH (Blueprint Component 1) — Type
2, perceptual intuition**
- *Why (birth-type addition)*: this is a genuine perceptual shortcut
  rather than a wrongly-generalized rule — fast, holistic word
  recognition IS the eventual goal of fluent reading, and a learner
  reaching toward that goal prematurely, before full letter-processing
  is automatic enough to remain complete even at speed, is not applying
  an incorrect belief but taking a real perceptual shortcut under
  genuine time pressure, distinguishing this from Type 1's rule-
  overgeneralization mechanism.
- *Full trigger/probe/bridge/replacement/discrimination content*: see
  Blueprint Component 1, verbatim — not restated here.
- *Characteristic phrase (addition)*: a learner who confuses "was" and
  "saw" but, when asked to slow down and check letter-by-letter,
  immediately self-corrects is showing the shortcut is a speed-pressure
  artifact rather than a genuine gap in letter knowledge — this
  distinction should guide whether recovery targets speed regulation
  (slow down, then speed back up) or a more fundamental letter-order
  processing gap.

## Analogies

- **Best analogy — a familiar face recognized instantly, but still by
  its actual features, not a silhouette**: fluent sight-word reading is
  like recognizing a close friend's face in a crowd instantly — fast,
  but still genuinely processing their actual features, not merely
  their height and hair color (which many people could share). Breaking
  point: face recognition is holistic and parallel in a way letter
  processing during reading is understood to be more sequential — the
  analogy targets the COMPLETENESS of processing (all features/letters
  checked), not the exact cognitive mechanism.
- **Alternative — a fast typist who still hits every key, just quickly**:
  reinforces that speed and completeness are compatible, not competing
  — no key (letter) is skipped even though the overall action is fast.
  Breaking point: typing is a production task; sight-word reading is
  recognition — the analogy should not be extended to suggest the
  learner is generating the word rather than recognizing it.
- **ANTI-ANALOGY — do NOT frame sight words as words to be "recognized
  like a picture" or "memorized as a shape"**: this framing directly
  installs MC-MEMORIZING-THE-WORD-SHAPE-IS-ENOUGH by explicitly
  endorsing shape-based processing as the intended strategy. If a
  learner proposes this framing themselves ("so I just remember what
  it looks like?"), correct it immediately: "not the overall shape —
  every single letter, in order, just very fast."

## Demonstrations

- **Physical/auditory-visual (word-card flash-and-check)**: the
  Blueprint's Component 3 anchor, including its explicit twin-word demo
  (was/saw shown in sequence) — canonical, referenced not restated.
- **Home/no-equipment**: any word written on paper or a whiteboard,
  briefly shown and covered, substitutes for a flash card.
- **Interactive/learner-activity**: TA-4's twin-word discrimination task
  is itself a hands-on, side-by-side comparison activity.
- **Teacher-demo (regular vs. irregular contrast)**: the tutor models
  decoding a regular sight word ("big") and then reading an irregular
  one ("of") back to back, explicitly naming which behaved as expected
  from sounding-out and which required a flagged, memorized adjustment
  — making the regular/irregular distinction audible and concrete.
- **Prediction to elicit FIRST, every time**: before confirming a
  flashed word (TA-3), ask the learner to commit to their read before
  the card is shown again for the letter-check — Universal Principle 3.

## Discovery Questions

**Direct instruction wins here — argued, not defaulted to.** There is
no regularity for a learner to discover in WHICH specific words are
irregular (that is a closed, arbitrary list to be shown), nor in the
motor/perceptual skill of fast-but-complete letter processing (that is
a fluency skill to be demonstrated and rehearsed, not induced from
examples) — both halves of this concept are convention/skill content
best served by direct instruction, matching the reasoning already
established for this domain's other pure-convention and pure-procedure
concepts (base consonant sounds, blending/segmenting). The one place a
brief discovery-flavored moment is genuinely earned is TA-1's own
implicit design: presenting several regular sight words in sequence
lets the learner notice for themselves, across repeated successful
decodes, that "sight word" does not mean "undecodable" — but this is a
single inductive noticing, not a full need/playground/invention/
collision/formalization/compression arc, and should not be
over-engineered into one.

## Teaching Sequence

TA-1 (regular sight word decoding) must precede TA-2 (irregular sight
word introduction) because the regular-decodability insight — the
single most important corrective this concept delivers — is only
credible if built from a clean run of successful decodes; introducing
irregular words first, or alongside regular ones, would let the learner
form the (wrong) impression that sight words are unpredictable from the
outset. TA-3 (flash recognition with letter-order check) is placed
after both, because it deliberately mixes regular and irregular words
under speed pressure, which requires both categories to already be
independently secure. TA-4 (twin-word discrimination) is placed after
TA-3 specifically because the shape-guessing shortcut this task
targets tends to emerge or worsen under the speed pressure TA-3
introduces — presenting twin-word contrasts before any speed pressure
exists would not reliably surface the error TA-4 is designed to catch.
TA-5 (connected-text reading) is placed last as the full integration
check. This entry does not restate the Blueprint's own turn-by-turn
session script (Component 7) — see Blueprint References below.

**What does NOT fit, and why**: Error Analysis

deliberately confused was/saw misread can then be analyzed against an
explicit rule (check every letter in order) the learner already holds.

## Voice Teaching Notes

- **What to listen for**: whether the learner attempts a decoding pass
  BEFORE declaring a word unreadable or guessing from shape — this
  attempt-first behavior is itself diagnostic of whether
  MC-SIGHT-WORDS-CANNOT-BE-SOUNDED-OUT-AT-ALL is still active,
  independent of whether the eventual answer is correct.
- **Characteristic hesitations**: a brief pause on a twin-word pair
  (was/saw, there/three) followed by a correct read is a good sign — it
  suggests the letter-order check is being actively, if effortfully,
  applied; a fast, confident WRONG read of a twin word (no hesitation)
  suggests shape-guessing has fully displaced letter processing.
- **Pronunciation stakes**: real for the irregular subset specifically
  (the irregular sound in "said," "was," "of," "the" is a fixed fact
  of the word, not a dialect variant, so a wrong forced-regular
  pronunciation should be corrected), while genuinely dialect-consistent
  variation in vowel quality elsewhere should not be marked wrong.
- **The load-bearing sentence**: "try sounding it out first" is this
  concept's load-bearing sentence for the regular-decodability half;
  "check every letter, in order" is its counterpart for the twin-word/
  shape-guessing half, both deserving genuine space per
  `../../foundations/03-voice-first-learning-model.md §3`.

## Assessment Signals

- **Diagnostic (golden probe)**: the Blueprint's own conflict-evidence
  probe (Component 1) — attempting to sound out "and" after a claimed
  refusal to try any sight word — cleanly separates a learner still
  carrying the sight-word-means-unreadable belief from one who has
  already updated it.
- **Guided practice → independent practice**: the Blueprint's WE-1 → WE-2
  → WE-3 → MP-1 through MP-5 fading ladder is reused directly
  (Components 5–6) — not re-authored here.
- **Mastery gate set** (`../../assessment/05-mastery-verification.md
  §3`): production (fresh regular sight word decoded, MP-1);
  irregular-identification (a novel irregular word with the specific
  unexpected element named, MP-2 — the strongest evidence the learner
  distinguishes regular from irregular rather than treating the whole
  category uniformly); speed (a briefly-flashed word correctly
  identified, MP-3); discrimination (a novel twin-word pair correctly
  distinguished by letter order, MP-4); explanation (MP-5).
- **Interpretation of response pattern**: fast-and-correct on a
  flashed regular sight word confirms genuine automaticity; fast-and-
  WRONG on a twin-word pair (confusing was/saw at speed) is the
  dangerous quadrant — it signals shape-guessing has become the
  dominant strategy rather than complete-but-fast letter processing,
  and warrants returning to TA-4's explicit contrast work rather than
  more speed drilling. Slow-and-correct on an irregular word is an
  acceptable intermediate state (deliberate recall of a memorized
  exception), distinct from a problem.
- **Transfer items**: near (a fresh regular sight word; a fresh twin-
  word pair); far (a sight word not heavily rehearsed during teaching,
  testing generalization of both the decode-first strategy and the
  full letter-order habit); real-world (any sight word — regular or
  irregular — encountered in text the learner is already reading).

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Concept-specific application of `../../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`:
- **"I don't know"** on a sight-word reading task shrinks to attempting
  a decode of the most familiar, most heavily-rehearsed REGULAR sight
  word first (rebuilding the "try decoding first" habit), rather than
  presenting a fresh or irregular word cold.
- **Likeliest utterance/behavior at this node**: an immediate "I can't
  sound that out" refusal (without attempting a decode) is more likely
  than a genuine attempt-then-fail, per
  MC-SIGHT-WORDS-CANNOT-BE-SOUNDED-OUT-AT-ALL's mechanism — when this
  refusal pattern appears, route directly to that misconception's
  recovery (re-run the conflict-evidence probe on a known-regular word)
  rather than treating it as a generic reading difficulty.
- **Non-verbal signal specific to this node**: a learner who, on a
  twin-word pair, visibly runs a finger under the word letter-by-letter
  before answering is showing the correct full-processing behavior even
  on items where the final answer is uncertain — this checking behavior
  should be praised explicitly, separate from correcting the outcome.

## Memory Hooks

**Concept type**: mixed — regular sight words are, functionally, no
different from any other decodable word once the decode-first strategy
is secure (reviewed as part of ordinary decoding fluency practice, not
a separate memory system); the irregular subset is a small, closed fact
set requiring standard spaced fact-review, since these words cannot be
re-derived and must be retained by rote. Interleaving partners: mix
regular and irregular sight words within the same flash-recognition
practice set (TA-3) so the learner cannot predict "regular" or
"irregular" from context or position alone, and continue mixing
twin-word pairs into ongoing review specifically because shape-based
confusions are prone to resurfacing under time pressure even after
initial correction.

## Transfer Connections

- **Near**: recognizing and decoding fresh, unpracticed regular sight
  words; discriminating fresh twin-word pairs.
- **Far**: this concept's core decode-first strategy transfers directly
  to all subsequent reading-fluency work, where the vast majority of
  high-frequency words a learner meets will be regular and decodable —
  the strategy, once internalized here, does not need re-teaching.
- **Real-world**: sight words are, by definition, the most frequently
  encountered words in any text the learner reads — every reading
  experience outside the lesson is immediate practice.
- **Expert-transfer**: fluent, automatic word recognition during silent
  reading, where the regular/irregular distinction becomes entirely
  unconscious, well past this node. Cross-links to
  `eng.vocab.word-recognition`, per the KG's own cross-link field — this
  concept is the phonics-side automaticity foundation that broader
  vocabulary-level word recognition builds on, a boundary this entry
  respects by not duplicating vocabulary-acquisition content.
