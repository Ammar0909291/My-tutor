## Learning Objective

The learner can:
1. Decode an unfamiliar-looking printed word, then explicitly check whether the resulting pronunciation is already known from spoken vocabulary.
2. Demonstrate instant, effortless recognition of high-frequency printed words, connecting print directly to meaning without conscious decoding.
3. Decode a printed word with unusual or irregular-looking spelling before judging its familiarity, rather than assuming an unfamiliar appearance means an unknown word.
4. Distinguish a genuine vocabulary gap (a word remaining unknown even after correct decoding) from a recognition gap (a word instantly known once correctly decoded).
5. Read a short passage, correctly flagging any word decoded accurately but not recognised in meaning, as a genuine vocabulary gap for future attention.

## Core Understanding

Word recognition is the **step that happens after decoding, not decoding itself**: decoding correctly produces a word's pronunciation from its printed form, while recognition is the further, genuinely separate act of connecting that pronunciation to a **meaning already known from spoken vocabulary**. These are two distinct cognitive achievements that can succeed or fail independently of one another — a learner can decode a word perfectly, arriving at exactly the correct pronunciation, and still have no idea what it means, if that specific word (however competently sounded out) is not yet part of their spoken vocabulary at all; conversely, and this is the concept's second, equally load-bearing structural fact, a word whose **printed form looks unfamiliar or unusual** may nonetheless be a word the learner already knows perfectly well from speech — the print simply hasn't yet been connected to that existing spoken knowledge, meaning decoding it correctly can produce a genuine, felt "oh, I know this word!" moment of recognition rather than revealing an actual gap. This distinction matters practically because it separates two genuinely different problems requiring two genuinely different solutions: a **recognition gap** (the word is known in speech, decoding just needs to happen to unlock it) resolves the moment decoding succeeds, while a **true vocabulary gap** (the word is genuinely unknown, in any form) requires actual vocabulary-building instruction that decoding alone cannot supply.

## Mental Models

**Beginner — "if I can sound out a word correctly, I know that word."**
The runnable simulation: treat successful decoding as the complete, final step in dealing with an unfamiliar printed word, with no further check for meaning required. This is not tutor-installed — it is the direct, entirely reasonable residue of this program's entire preceding phonics strand, which has correctly and necessarily treated decoding accuracy as the central goal, since prior to this concept, decoding genuinely was the primary bottleneck being addressed.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — decoding a word perfectly, then being asked what it actually means, and discovering the answer is genuinely unknown.
*Shelf-life warning at replacement*: "Sounding it out correctly gets you to the word's pronunciation. Whether you actually know that word is a separate, further question."

**Intermediate — "I understand decoding and knowing a word are separate, and I assume any printed word that looks unfamiliar to me must be a word I genuinely don't know."**
The simulation: correctly separate decoding from recognition, while defaulting to judging a word's overall familiarity from its printed appearance alone, before ever attempting to decode it. This model has correctly resolved the first misconception and produces a new, related error: giving up on an unfamiliar-*looking* word without ever discovering it might be perfectly familiar once actually sounded out.
*Upgrade trigger*: the Blueprint's own conflict evidence — declaring a word unknown from its unfamiliar printed appearance, then decoding it together and discovering it's a word already used comfortably in speech.
*Shelf-life warning*: "Looking unfamiliar in print doesn't mean the word itself is unfamiliar. Decode it first — you might already know it the moment you hear it said."

**Advanced — "decoding and recognition are genuinely separable steps; instant recognition is the target for high-frequency words; unfamiliar-looking print should always be decoded before its familiarity is judged; and any word decoded correctly but still unknown in meaning is a genuine, valuable vocabulary gap to note, not a decoding failure."**
The simulation: given any printed word, familiar-looking or not, the learner decodes first, checks for recognition, and — for words that remain unknown even after correct decoding — correctly identifies this as useful diagnostic information about where future vocabulary work should focus, rather than as a personal failing. This is the target model, matching TA-4 and TA-5.
*Upgrade trigger*: `eng.vocab.context-clues`, `eng.vocab.synonyms-antonyms`, `eng.vocab.word-families`, and `eng.grammar.word-classes-overview` (the four direct unlocks), where this concept's decode-then-recognise-then-flag-gaps routine becomes the entry point into a much broader body of dedicated vocabulary-building and grammatical-category work.
*Shelf-life warning*: "This gets you the diagnostic — knowing exactly which words are genuine gaps. The next steps are the actual toolkit for closing those gaps once you've found them."

**Expert — "word recognition, at full fluency, becomes near-instantaneous whole-word retrieval (the 'orthographic lexicon') for the vast majority of a skilled reader's vocabulary, such that the decode-then-check routine this concept explicitly teaches is itself a scaffold intended to become largely unconscious and automatic over time, exactly as decoding fluency itself was built to become automatic at the previous concept."**
Named here to mark the arc; this concept's explicit, step-by-step decode-then-recognise routine is a deliberate, temporary teaching scaffold for a process that, at true reading expertise, largely collapses into instant, unconscious word retrieval — named here so the tutor understands the routine's own eventual obsolescence is the actual long-term goal, not a permanent procedure to be maintained indefinitely.

## Why Students Fail

The dominant failure mechanism is that **this program's entire preceding phonics strand has correctly and necessarily emphasised decoding as the central, difficult skill to master**, and a learner who has just spent many prior concepts working hard specifically to decode correctly has excellent, well-earned reason to treat successful decoding as the complete resolution of an encounter with an unfamiliar word — nothing in that prior instruction has needed to distinguish decoding from meaning-access, because until decoding fluency itself was secure, meaning-access was never the binding constraint.

The second mechanism, once decoding and recognition are distinguished, concerns **the specific, well-documented gap between spoken and written vocabulary exposure**: many words a learner already knows and uses comfortably in speech are, for entirely ordinary reasons (lower print frequency, irregular or unusual spelling, simply not yet encountered often in text), genuinely unfamiliar-*looking* in their printed form — a learner has no direct, immediate way to know, from appearance alone, whether an unfamiliar-looking word is a true vocabulary gap or merely a familiar spoken word wearing an unfamiliar printed disguise, and judging from appearance alone will systematically misclassify some genuinely known words as unknown ones.

The third mechanism, specific to and predictable for learners with a still-developing spoken English vocabulary, is that **the proportion of true vocabulary gaps to mere recognition gaps is genuinely, structurally different for this population** — an English learner will predictably encounter a higher rate of words that remain unknown even after correct decoding, not because their decoding or recognition skill is deficient, but because their underlying spoken vocabulary is, entirely normally, still growing, and the Blueprint's own S9 protocol routing correctly frames this as an expected reflection of vocabulary size rather than a skill deficiency to be remediated.

## Misconceptions

The Blueprint's **Component 1 — Misconception Register** owns the trigger signals, conflict evidence, bridge/replacement text, and discrimination pairs for both entries below. Reused by reference; birth-type classification and teaching consequence added here.

### MC-DECODING-EQUALS-RECOGNIZING-MEANING
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-DECODING-EQUALS-RECOGNIZING-MEANING — includes the cat/technical-term and dog-with-recognition/dog-without-recognition discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 1 (overgeneralization), of this program's entire preceding phonics-strand emphasis on decoding accuracy as the primary, sufficient goal, extended past its actual scope now that decoding is no longer, itself, the binding bottleneck.
- **Teaching consequence of the birth type**: because the over-extended prior emphasis (decoding accuracy matters enormously) remains completely correct and foundational, the repair must **add a genuinely new, further check without diminishing decoding's continued importance** — the Blueprint's own bridge text does this precisely, framing decoding and recognition as "two different steps" rather than recasting decoding as having been the wrong focus all along, which would be both inaccurate and demoralising given how much genuine, hard-won skill decoding fluency represents.
- **Verification of death**: given a genuinely novel word (unfamiliar in both print and, potentially, in spoken vocabulary), the learner's response to successful decoding is an explicit further check ("do I know this word?") rather than treating the decoded pronunciation itself as sufficient evidence of understanding — the presence of the *further check*, not merely correct decoding, is the target evidence.

### MC-UNFAMILIAR-PRINTED-WORD-MEANS-UNKNOWN-WORD
*(Blueprint Component 1, MC-UNFAMILIAR-PRINTED-WORD-MEANS-UNKNOWN-WORD — includes the though-recognised-after-decoding and give-up-before-decoding-versus-decode-first discrimination pairs.)*
- **Birth type**: Type 2 (perceptual intuition), reinforced by a genuinely real correlation — unfamiliar-looking print frequently *does* correspond to a genuinely unknown word, so the learner's evidence (this printed form looks strange, and strange-looking words are often unknown) is not unreasonable; the specific overreach is treating that correlation as a certainty sufficient to skip decoding altogether.
- **Teaching consequence**: because the underlying correlation is real (unfamiliar print often does mean genuine unfamiliarity), the repair cannot rely on simply asserting the correlation is false — it requires **direct, concrete counterexamples** where an unfamiliar-looking word turns out to be already known, exactly as the Blueprint's own decode-then-recognise-check anchor supplies, personalised to words the specific learner already uses in speech, making the counterexample maximally vivid and credible.
- **Verification of death**: given a novel word with a genuinely unusual or irregular printed appearance, the learner attempts to decode it fully *before* judging its familiarity, rather than declaring it unknown from appearance alone — the decode-first ordering, not merely an eventual correct recognition judgement, is the target evidence, since a learner could in principle correctly guess a word's familiarity from context without ever having corrected the underlying decode-first habit.

## Analogies

**Best — recognising a friend's voice on the phone, even through poor connection quality that initially makes their voice sound strange or unclear.** The strange quality of the initial audio (unfamiliar print) doesn't mean you don't know the person (the word) — once your ear adjusts and you actually process the sound clearly (decode it fully), recognition often clicks in immediately. Give the connection (the decoding) a real chance before concluding you don't know who's calling.
*Breaking point*: a garbled phone connection is a passive degradation the listener has no control over; decoding is an active skill the reader performs — useful for the *don't judge from initial unclear impression alone* intuition, not for implying the reader is a passive recipient the way a listener on a bad call is.
- **Best-for-second-half — a familiar song played in an unfamiliar cover version or a different key, initially sounding unrecognisable until a specific phrase suddenly clicks and you realise "wait, I know this song!"** The unfamiliar arrangement (unfamiliar spelling) initially obscures recognition, but the underlying song (the word's actual sound, once decoded) is exactly the same, and recognition can click in suddenly and completely once the right cue (the decoded pronunciation) arrives.
*Breaking point*: a song cover retains melodic and lyrical content throughout; an undecoded printed word supplies essentially no auditory cue at all until decoding actually happens — useful for the "click" moment of sudden recognition, not for implying partial cues are available before decoding.

**Alternative — a locked box that you already own the key to, but haven't yet realised the key fits.** Decoding is trying the key (sounding out the letters); recognition is the moment the lock actually turns and you realise you already had access to what's inside (the meaning) all along.
*Breaking point*: a lock-and-key relationship is a one-time, static fact; word recognition happens continuously and is built through repeated experience, gradually shifting from effortful decode-then-check toward instant recognition — useful for the single-encounter "aha" moment, less precise for the gradual-automaticity half of this concept.

**Story analogy — meeting someone at a costume party who looks completely different in their disguise, until they speak and you immediately recognise their voice.** The unfamiliar visual disguise (unusual spelling) doesn't change who the person actually is (the word's actual identity); hearing them speak (decoding to the actual sound) is what triggers real recognition, regardless of how unfamiliar the visual disguise looked.
*Breaking point*: none serious; a close, vivid mapping to the print-versus-sound distinction this concept's second misconception directly addresses.

**Visual analogy — the decode-then-recognize check** (Blueprint Component 3): decoding a personally-relevant, unfamiliar-looking-but-actually-known word step by step, capturing the "aha, I DO know this!" moment directly. Not a metaphor but the actual working instrument this concept's core teaching is organised around, and its personalisation (choosing a word specific to the individual learner's own speech) is distinctive among this program's anchors for being tailored per-learner rather than using a fixed, universal example set.

### ANTI-ANALOGIES (do not use)

- **"If you can read the word out loud, you understand it."** Directly installs MC-DECODING-EQUALS-RECOGNIZING-MEANING as a stated rule, precisely the equation this concept's first misconception consists of.
- **"You either know a word or you don't — there's no in-between."** Erases the concept's actual, central distinction between a recognition gap (closable instantly via decoding) and a true vocabulary gap (requiring further instruction), collapsing two genuinely different situations into one undifferentiated category.
- **"If a word looks hard, it probably is a word you don't know."** Directly installs MC-UNFAMILIAR-PRINTED-WORD-MEANS-UNKNOWN-WORD as encouraging, reasonable-sounding advice, reinforcing the exact judge-from-appearance-alone shortcut this concept's second misconception's repair must counter.

## Demonstrations

Prediction first in every case.

1. **The decode-then-recognize check (learner activity).** Blueprint Component 3 — full script there, using a personally-relevant word. *Predict first*: "Just looking at this word, do you think you already know it?" Then decode it together and check for the "aha" moment.
2. **The technical-term collision (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the first misconception. *Predict first*: "If you can sound this word out perfectly, does that mean you automatically know what it means?" Then decode a genuinely unfamiliar term and confirm the meaning is still unknown despite correct decoding.
3. **The though-recognition reveal (teacher-led, learner-resolved).** Blueprint's own conflict evidence for the second misconception. *Predict first*: "This word looks unusual written down — do you think you already know it once it's said aloud?" Then decode "though" and let recognition click in.
4. **The true-gap-versus-recognition-gap sorting task (learner activity, TA-4).** *Predict first*: "Out of these decoded words, which do you think you'll actually recognise, and which might still be genuinely new to you?" Then sort and confirm together.

## Discovery Questions

This concept is well suited to **guided discovery for both structural facts**, since the evidence for each (decoding success not guaranteeing meaning; unfamiliar print not guaranteeing genuine unfamiliarity) is directly, personally available to the learner through their own decode-then-check attempts.

1. **Need**: "If you can sound out a word perfectly, does that automatically mean you know what it means?"
2. **Playground**: the learner decodes a mixed set of words — some clearly familiar, some genuinely unfamiliar, some unfamiliar-looking-but-actually-known — freely, without being told in advance which category each belongs to.
3. **Invention**: "For each word you just decoded, did you already know it, or not?" The learner sorts their own attempts, discovering firsthand that decoding success and recognition success don't always co-occur.
4. **Collision**: present a word the learner initially judged unknown from its printed appearance, before decoding — did decoding change that judgement?
5. **Formalization**: name the two structural facts — decoding and recognition are separable, and unfamiliar print doesn't guarantee genuine unfamiliarity — from the learner's own sorted evidence.
6. **Compression**: "Decode first, then check if you already know it — never judge from the printed shape alone."

## Teaching Sequence

The pedagogical logic behind this arc:

- **The decode-then-check-for-meaning routine (TA-1) is established first, on a mixed set of already-familiar-looking and less-familiar-looking-but-known words**, directly confronting the first misconception before the second is even introduced — this ordering matters because the second misconception's repair (decode before judging familiarity) presupposes the learner already has the decode-then-check habit from TA-1 to apply.
- **Instant recognition of high-frequency words (TA-2) is introduced as a contrast case**, showing the learner what the *eventual*, automatic endpoint of this process looks like (per the Expert mental model's own framing) before the harder, more effortful cases (unfamiliar-looking-but-known words, TA-3) are introduced — this gives the learner a felt sense of the target state they're working toward.
- **Unfamiliar-looking-but-known words (TA-3) directly confront the second misconception, and this is deliberately sequenced after the decode-then-check routine (TA-1) is secure, not before** — attempting to correct "don't judge from appearance alone" before the learner has any established decode-then-check habit to fall back on would leave them with a correction but no concrete alternative procedure to follow.
- **True vocabulary gaps versus recognition gaps (TA-4) and connected-text application (TA-5) come last**, with TA-4 requiring the learner to hold both structural facts simultaneously (some decoded words are known, some genuinely aren't, and telling the difference matters) and TA-5 applying the full, integrated skill to genuine, extended reading — matching the general sequencing principle established throughout this program that a concept's most integrative task is reserved for its culminating stage.

Turn-by-turn scripting, protocol routing, and adaptive flags: Blueprint **Component 7 — Session Architecture** and **Component 8 — Adaptive Flags** (in particular the decoding-and-recognition-are-separable-skills principle, the decode-first-judge-second ordering, the vocabulary-gaps-as-valuable-data framing, and the higher-expected-true-gap-rate for English learners).

## Tutor Actions

In recommended order, from `../teaching-actions/`:

1. **Demonstration** (SHOW) — the decode-then-recognize check and the technical-term and though-recognition collisions, all benefiting from being personalised to the specific learner's own known and unknown vocabulary wherever possible.
2. **Error Analysis** (TEST-THINKING) — both collisions function as guided error analysis against the learner's own plausible-but-incorrect assumption (that decoding is sufficient, or that unfamiliar print means unknown), made concrete through direct, personally-relevant evidence.
3. **Matching** (DO) — the true-gap-versus-recognition-gap sorting task (TA-4) is fundamentally a matching/classification action, and it is this concept's most direct test of the core distinction it teaches.
4. **Prediction** (TEST-THINKING) — predicting whether a word will be recognised before decoding it, and predicting whether a decoded word's meaning will be known before checking, both directly exercising the target discrimination and surfacing the learner's untested prior before it is confirmed or disconfirmed.
5. **Concept Map** (ORGANIZE) — a simple three-category sort (instantly recognised / recognition gap, now closed / true vocabulary gap) built from a mixed word set encountered during TA-5's connected-text reading, giving a compact, practically useful artefact distinguishing the three possible outcomes this concept's core task can produce.

**Does not fit**: **Worked Example** in the derivation sense — there is no multi-step procedure to model beyond decode-then-check, better practised through Demonstration and Matching. **Game** — this concept's genuine, sometimes emotionally sensitive content (discovering a vocabulary gap, per the Blueprint's own S6 routing) is a poor fit for competitive gamification, which could turn a valuable diagnostic discovery into a source of comparative embarrassment.

## Voice Teaching Notes

This concept's core evidence is a mix of **audio** (the decoded pronunciation, and the learner's spoken confirmation or denial of recognition) and **the learner's own honest self-report** (whether they genuinely knew a word before decoding it, and whether they genuinely recognise it after) — a self-report-dependent evidence profile distinctive among this program's concepts, since the tutor generally cannot independently verify whether a given word actually was, or wasn't, previously known to this specific learner.

What the ideal tutor perceives:

- **Confident, immediate decoding followed by an equally confident but incorrect or fabricated claim of recognition** (stating a plausible-sounding but wrong meaning, or vaguely claiming familiarity without being able to state a meaning or example). The direct behavioural signature of a learner who has not yet internalised the honesty this concept's second misconception's repair and its own s6_path explicitly call for — gently redirect toward the concept's stated value that genuine gaps are useful data, not failures, to reduce any incentive to falsely claim recognition.
- **A genuine pause or "let me think" specifically after decoding, before answering whether the word is recognised** — positive evidence the learner is genuinely checking rather than reflexively answering either "yes" or "no" without real reflection.
- **Confident refusal to decode an unfamiliar-looking word, offered before any attempt is made.** The direct behavioural signature of MC-UNFAMILIAR-PRINTED-WORD-MEANS-UNKNOWN-WORD, distinguishable from a genuine decoding difficulty by the fact that no decoding attempt has actually been made at all.
- **An honest, unembarrassed report of a genuine vocabulary gap** ("I can say it, but I don't know what it means"). The target behaviour for TA-4/TA-5, and it should be explicitly praised as valuable, accurate self-assessment, exactly per the Blueprint's own "gaps discovered here are valuable data, not failures" framing.

**Load-bearing sentence, delivered slowly**: *"Decode it first — then honestly ask yourself whether you already know it, or whether this is a genuinely new word to learn."*

## Assessment Signals

Item bank: Blueprint **Component 6 — Mastery Probe Set** (MP-1…MP-5). Response-pattern reads, which the Blueprint does not own:

- **Decode-then-check (MP-1).** *Correct decoding, followed by a correct, specific statement of meaning or use* → the full target evidence; *correct decoding alone with a vague or absent meaning statement* → the recognition half has not been genuinely verified, and should not be assumed present merely because decoding succeeded.
- **Instant recognition (MP-2).** *Immediate, effortless recognition with no visible sounding-out* → the target automaticity state; carries limited additional diagnostic weight for this concept's core distinction, since it primarily confirms the already-established decoding-fluency prerequisite remains solid.
- **Unfamiliar-looking-but-known word (MP-3).** This is this concept's most diagnostic single item for the second misconception — *the learner attempts decoding before offering any familiarity judgement* → the target evidence, and this ordering (decode first, judge second) should be actively observed, not merely inferred from a correct eventual outcome.
- **True vocabulary gap (MP-4).** *Correct decoding, paired with an honest report of not knowing the meaning* → the strongest, most valuable evidence this concept can produce, and it should be explicitly praised rather than treated as a lesser outcome than a recognition-gap item — a false claim of knowing an unknown word would be a significantly worse response than an honest "I don't know," even though the latter might feel less immediately successful to the learner.
- **Explanation (MP-5).** Listen for whether the explanation names both required elements — that decoding produces pronunciation while recognition connects that pronunciation to already-known meaning, and that the two can succeed or fail independently — a response naming only one has an incomplete model even if prior items were answered correctly by other means.

**Mastery certification trigger**: correct decoding of a novel word followed by a specific, correct statement of its meaning (for a recognition-gap case); instant, effortless recognition of a novel high-frequency word; decode-first ordering (not appearance-based pre-judgement) on a novel unfamiliar-looking word; an honest, correctly-identified true vocabulary gap on a genuinely unknown word, with no false claim of knowing it; and an explanation naming both the decoding/recognition separability and the recognition-gap/vocabulary-gap distinction. The honesty requirement on the vocabulary-gap item is essential and unusual among this program's certification criteria — this concept specifically values and requires an accurate "I don't know" over a confident-sounding but false claim of recognition, which is a different, additional standard beyond mere correctness on the other items.

## Tutor Recovery Strategy

Generic engine: `../foundations/01-recovery-engine.md`. Concept-specific notes only:

- **"But I read it right, so I should know it"** — take this as an accurate, well-earned statement about a genuine, hard-won skill (decoding), not a mistake to be dismissed; respond by adding rather than contradicting: "You really did read it right — that part is solid. Knowing what it means is one more, separate step, and it's completely normal for that step to sometimes come up empty, especially for a brand-new word."
- **"I feel embarrassed that I didn't know that word"** — a genuine, common emotional response to discovering a vocabulary gap, particularly likely per the Blueprint's own S6 routing; respond by reframing the discovery's value directly, not merely offering generic reassurance: "Finding that gap right now, while we're specifically looking for it, is actually the useful part — now we know exactly what to work on next, instead of it staying hidden."
- **The smaller question to shrink to**: from the full true-gap-versus-recognition-gap sort, down to **decoding a single, maximally familiar word and confirming instant recognition, with no unfamiliar or gap-testing material involved at all.** This removes the harder discrimination and honesty demands entirely, isolating the most basic, most successful version of the decode-then-recognise sequence before returning to the harder, gap-revealing tasks.
- **Never shrink to "just say you know it if the pronunciation sounds okay."** This directly licenses the false-recognition-claim pattern this concept's honesty standard specifically exists to prevent, and would corrupt this concept's diagnostic value entirely.
