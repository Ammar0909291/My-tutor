## 1. Concept Spine

A coordinate (dative) covalent bond forms when one atom (a Lewis base) donates BOTH electrons of a shared pair to another atom or ion (a Lewis acid) that has an empty orbital — but once formed, this bond is experimentally IDENTICAL to any other covalent bond of the same type (in NH₄⁺, all four N–H bonds have identical length, strength, and every other measurable property; the "coordinate" origin is purely a historical labelling/bookkeeping convention, not a physical difference); Lewis acids ACCEPT electron pairs (having a vacancy), while Lewis bases DONATE them — the Brønsted/Arrhenius "acid = H⁺ donor" model is actually a SUBSET of Lewis acid behavior (H⁺ itself is a Lewis acid, accepting a lone pair), not a contradiction, and species with no exchangeable proton at all (BF₃, AlCl₃, metal cations) can still be genuine Lewis acids; and coordinate bonds specifically form when a Lewis base donates a lone pair to CREATE A NEW BOND (forming an adduct or complex) — this is distinct from acid-base neutralization reactions like HCl+NaOH→NaCl+H₂O, where ions exchange without forming any new discrete coordinate bond.

## 2. Four-Stage CPA+ Mental Model

**Concrete**: Comparing all four N–H bonds in NH₄⁺ experimentally — discovering the "coordinate" fourth bond is indistinguishable in length and strength from the other three "ordinary" bonds.

**Representational**: A curved-arrow diagram showing NH₃'s lone pair donating both electrons to H⁺'s empty orbital, forming the fourth N–H bond in NH₄⁺.

**Abstract**: The general principle that "coordinate" describes only the electron-SOURCE during bond FORMATION (one atom contributing both electrons instead of one each) — not any lasting physical distinction in the resulting bond; the broader Lewis acid-base framework (electron-pair acceptor/donor) as encompassing, not contradicting, the narrower Brønsted/Arrhenius proton-based framework.

**Transfer**: Given an unfamiliar Lewis acid-base adduct-forming reaction, correctly identifying which species donates the electron pair (Lewis base) and which accepts it (Lewis acid), correctly recognizing the resulting coordinate bond as physically identical to ordinary covalent bonds, and correctly distinguishing genuine coordinate-bond formation from simple ion-exchange neutralization reactions.

## 3. Why Beginners Fail

Students assume a coordinate bond, formed by only one atom's electron contribution, must be inherently weaker than an "ordinary" covalent bond formed by two-atom contribution, missing that the resulting bond is experimentally identical in every measurable respect once formed — the labeling difference reflects only formation history, not lasting physical distinction; they equate "Lewis acid" with "electron donor" (perhaps by loose association with the Brønsted acid concept), missing that Lewis acids specifically ACCEPT electron pairs (having a vacancy to fill), while Lewis bases are the donors; and they assume any reaction involving lone pairs automatically forms a coordinate bond, missing that coordinate bonds specifically require a NEW bond to form (an adduct/complex), which genuinely distinguishes them from simple ionic neutralization reactions where no new discrete bond forms at all.

## 4. Misconception Library

### MC-1: Coordinate bonds are weaker than ordinary covalent bonds because only one atom supplied the electrons
- **Probe**: "In NH₄⁺, which N–H bond is the coordinate bond and is it weaker than the other three?"
- **Characteristic phrase**: "the arrow bond is weaker."
- **Trigger (Type 5, instruction-induced)**: The distinctive curved-arrow notation used to depict coordinate bond formation (showing one-directional electron donation) suggests to students that the resulting bond must somehow be structurally different or weaker than "normal" two-atom-contributed bonds.
- **Conflict evidence [P28]**: All four N–H bonds in NH₄⁺ are experimentally identical in length, strength, and every other measurable property — there is no detectable physical distinction between the "coordinate" fourth bond and the other three "ordinary" bonds; the coordinate-bond LABEL describes only the historical origin (which atom contributed both electrons during FORMATION), a bookkeeping distinction that has no lasting effect on the final bond's properties.
- **Bridge [P30]**: Once a covalent bond forms — regardless of whether one atom contributed both electrons or each atom contributed one — the resulting shared electron pair behaves identically; the electron-source distinction is a useful mechanistic/historical label for understanding HOW the bond formed, not a claim about how the finished bond BEHAVES.
- **Replacement [P31]**: Coordinate bonds are physically identical to ordinary covalent bonds once formed — "coordinate" describes only the electron-source history during formation, never a lasting physical difference in bond strength, length, or any other property.
- **Discrimination pairs [P33]**: The three "ordinary" N–H bonds in NH₄⁺ (formed with one electron from each atom) vs. the "coordinate" fourth N–H bond (formed with both electrons from N) — indistinguishable in every measured property.
- **S6 repair path**: Present the explicit experimental bond-length/strength data for all four NH₄⁺ N–H bonds, showing they're identical, directly disproving any weakness claim.

### MC-2: Lewis acids are substances that donate electrons
- **Probe**: "Is BF₃ a Lewis acid or a Lewis base?"
- **Characteristic phrase**: "acids give electrons."
- **Trigger (Type 3, language contamination)**: Students may loosely associate "acid" with some kind of active, giving role (perhaps drawing a vague analogy to how acids donate protons in the Brønsted model), incorrectly carrying this donation framing over to the Lewis definition.
- **Conflict evidence [P28]**: Lewis acids specifically ACCEPT electron pairs (having an empty orbital/vacancy to fill), while Lewis bases DONATE them — BF₃ is a classic Lewis ACID (boron has an empty p-orbital, genuinely accepting an electron pair from a Lewis base like NH₃ to form the BF₃·NH₃ adduct); the Brønsted/Arrhenius acid model (H⁺ donor) is actually a SUBSET of Lewis acid behavior, since the H⁺ ion itself accepts a lone pair from whatever base it bonds to — but many genuine Lewis acids (BF₃, AlCl₃, metal cations) have no exchangeable proton at all, showing the Lewis framework is broader, not contradictory.
- **Bridge [P30]**: "Acid" across different theories consistently describes the species that ends up bonded to (or accepting something from) the base — in the Lewis framework specifically, that "something" is an electron pair, making Lewis acids acceptors, exactly analogous to how Brønsted acids accept a base's willingness to take their proton (though the acid itself donates the proton in that specific framework).
- **Replacement [P31]**: Lewis acids ACCEPT electron pairs (having a vacancy); Lewis bases DONATE electron pairs — this framework includes but is broader than the Brønsted/Arrhenius proton-based model, encompassing species like BF₃ with no exchangeable proton at all.
- **Discrimination pairs [P33]**: BF₃ (Lewis acid, accepts NH₃'s lone pair, no exchangeable proton) vs. NH₃ (Lewis base, donates its lone pair) — the correct acid/base role assignment.
- **S6 repair path**: Have the student identify BF₃'s empty p-orbital explicitly, connecting the vacancy directly to its electron-accepting (Lewis acid) role.

### MC-3: Every reaction involving lone pairs forms coordinate bonds
- **Probe**: "Is the reaction HCl + NaOH → NaCl + H₂O a coordinate bond formation?"
- **Trigger (Type 1, overgeneralization)**: Students correctly learn coordinate bonds involve lone-pair donation and overextend this into assuming any reaction where lone pairs are conceptually "involved" (including simple ionic neutralization) must count as coordinate bond formation.
- **Conflict evidence [P28]**: Coordinate bonds form specifically when a Lewis base donates a lone pair to a Lewis acid to CREATE A NEW, discrete BOND — forming an adduct or complex (like NH₃+BF₃→NH₃·BF₃, or NH₃+H⁺→NH₄⁺); the HCl+NaOH neutralization reaction instead involves simple ION EXCHANGE (H⁺ and OH⁻ combining to form H₂O, while Na⁺ and Cl⁻ remain as separate spectator ions in solution) — while a new O–H bond genuinely does form when H⁺ combines with OH⁻'s lone pair (which IS technically a coordinate bond formation event), the overall reaction as commonly taught is framed as ionic neutralization, not primarily as coordinate-bond-forming adduct chemistry in the sense the concept usually targets.
- **Bridge [P30]**: Not every process where lone pairs and cations happen to be present should be immediately classified as "coordinate bond formation" in the adduct/complex sense — the concept specifically targets cases where a NEW, identifiable bond forms between two previously separate species to create a distinct new chemical entity (an adduct or complex).
- **Replacement [P31]**: Coordinate bond formation specifically describes lone-pair donation creating a new bond within an adduct or complex — distinguish this from simple ionic exchange/neutralization reactions, even when lone pairs are conceptually present in the mechanism.
- **Discrimination pairs [P33]**: NH₃+BF₃→NH₃·BF₃ (genuine coordinate bond formation, new adduct created) vs. HCl+NaOH→NaCl+H₂O (ionic neutralization, not the adduct-forming coordinate-bond scenario the concept targets).
- **S6 repair path**: Have the student identify whether a genuinely NEW compound/adduct is formed by the lone-pair donation, distinguishing this from simple ion recombination.

## 5. Explanation Library

**Primary explanation**: A coordinate (dative) covalent bond forms when one atom or ion (acting as a Lewis base) donates BOTH electrons of a shared pair to another atom or ion with an empty orbital (acting as a Lewis acid) — but this formation-history distinction has no lasting effect on the resulting bond, which is experimentally identical to any ordinary covalent bond of the same type once formed.

**Secondary explanation (Lewis acid-base and scope framing)**: Lewis acids accept electron pairs (having a vacancy); Lewis bases donate them — this framework is broader than, and consistent with, the Brønsted/Arrhenius proton-based acid-base model, since it includes species like BF₃ that have no exchangeable proton at all. Coordinate bond formation specifically describes creating a genuinely new bond within an adduct or complex, distinct from simple ionic exchange/neutralization reactions where no such new discrete bond forms.

## 6. Analogy Library

- **Primary analogy**: A handshake where one person offers both hands (donating both electrons) while the other person has empty hands ready to receive (an empty orbital) — once the handshake (bond) is formed, an outside observer watching the finished handshake couldn't tell whether both hands came from one person or one from each; the grip itself (the bond) is identical either way.
- **Breaking point**: The handshake analogy conveys the formation-vs-final-state distinction well but doesn't naturally capture the Lewis acid/base terminology or the coordinate-bond-vs-neutralization distinction — those need the explicit electron-pair-acceptor/donor and new-bond-formation arguments.
- **Anti-analogy**: Do NOT say "the coordinate bond is the weaker, arrow-drawn one" — this directly reinforces MC-1.

## 7. Demonstration Library

- **Demonstration 1 (NH₄⁺ bond-property comparison)**: Present the experimental bond-length and bond-energy data for all four N–H bonds in NH₄⁺ side by side, showing they're indistinguishable.
- **Demonstration 2 (BF₃·NH₃ adduct formation)**: Walk through the explicit curved-arrow mechanism for BF₃+NH₃→BF₃·NH₃, identifying the Lewis acid (BF₃, empty orbital) and Lewis base (NH₃, lone pair donor) roles explicitly.

## 8. Discovery Lesson

**Opening**: "In NH₄⁺, one N–H bond formed differently from the other three — nitrogen donated both electrons for that one. Do you think that bond is weaker as a result?"

**Exploration**: Students examine the experimental bond-property data for all four N–H bonds, discovering no measurable difference exists.

**Synthesis**: Guide toward: "coordinate" describes only how a bond formed (electron source), not any lasting property difference in the finished bond.

**Closure**: "Is BF₃ donating electrons, or accepting them, when it reacts with NH₃?" (Directly resolves MC-2.)

## 9. Teaching Actions (session_cap = 4)

- **TA-1 (SHOW)**: Present the explicit NH₄⁺ bond-property comparison, showing all four bonds are identical.
- **TA-2 (TELL)**: State the Lewis acid=acceptor/Lewis base=donor distinction explicitly, immediately followed by the BF₃ empty-orbital example.
- **TA-3 (DO)**: Student identifies the Lewis acid and Lewis base roles in a new adduct-forming reaction.
- **TA-4 (TEST-THINKING)**: Present MC-3's HCl+NaOH probe and ask the student to distinguish this from genuine coordinate-bond-forming adduct chemistry.

## 10. Voice Teaching

Whenever a coordinate bond is discussed, state explicitly "once formed, this bond is identical to any other covalent bond of its type" to preempt the weakness misconception directly. Whenever "Lewis acid" is introduced, immediately pair it with "acceptor, has a vacancy" to preempt the donor confusion.

## 11. Assessment

**Mastery gate**: Student can (a) correctly state that a coordinate bond is physically identical to an ordinary covalent bond once formed, (b) correctly identify Lewis acids as electron-pair acceptors and Lewis bases as donors, (c) correctly distinguish genuine coordinate-bond-forming adduct chemistry from simple ionic neutralization.

- **FA-1**: "In NH₄⁺, which N–H bond is the coordinate bond and is it weaker than the other three?" — targets MC-1.
- **FA-2**: "Is BF₃ a Lewis acid or a Lewis base?" — targets MC-2.
- **FA-3**: "Is the reaction HCl+NaOH→NaCl+H₂O a coordinate bond formation?" — targets MC-3.

**Confidence calibration**: Predict high-confidence-wrong answers on FA-2 among students loosely associating "acid" with an active/donating role from prior Brønsted-theory instruction.

**Delayed retrieval**: Re-probe MC-2's Lewis acid/base distinction before `chem.coord.werner` requires fluent understanding of coordinate bonding for complex-ion formation.

## 12. Recovery Notes

- **S3 (stuck)**: For the bond-weakness confusion, present the explicit NH₄⁺ bond data directly rather than reasoning abstractly about electron-contribution symmetry.
- **S4 (frustrated)**: Normalize — the curved-arrow notation genuinely does visually suggest a directional, potentially unequal bond, making this a reasonable, common misreading of the notation.
- **S6 (collision)**: Use the BF₃ empty-orbital identification for MC-2; use the new-bond-formation criterion for MC-3.
- **S9 (post-repair check)**: Ask the student to explain, unprompted, why "coordinate" describes formation history, not a lasting bond property.
