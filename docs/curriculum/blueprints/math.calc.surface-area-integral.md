## Component 1 — Learning Objectives

- LO1: State and apply the surface-area-of-revolution formula $S=2\pi\int_a^bf(x)\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}\,dx$ for rotating $y=f(x)$ about the $x$-axis.
- LO2: Recognize the formula's TWO components: $\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}\,dx$ is the ARC LENGTH element (from `math.calc.arc-length`), and $2\pi f(x)$ is the CIRCUMFERENCE of the circle traced by that arc element as it rotates — the formula is genuinely "circumference times arc length," summed via integration, NOT simply the arc length formula with an extra factor tacked on arbitrarily.
- LO3: Correctly compute $f'(x)$ FIRST and substitute it into the square root BEFORE integrating — a common setup error skips or mishandles this derivative step.

## Component 2 — Prerequisite Check

Assumes mastery of `math.calc.arc-length` — the surface area formula directly reuses its arc-length differential $\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}\,dx$.

## Component 3 — Core Explanation

The **surface area of revolution**, generated by rotating $y=f(x)$ about the $x$-axis from $x=a$ to $x=b$, is $S=2\pi\int_a^bf(x)\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}\,dx$.

This formula makes sense as "circumference times arc length," summed continuously: each infinitesimal ARC LENGTH element $ds=\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}\,dx$ (exactly the arc-length differential from `math.calc.arc-length`) traces out a thin circular BAND when rotated, with circumference $2\pi f(x)$ (since $f(x)$ is the radius at that point). Multiplying circumference by arc length gives that thin band's area, and integrating sums up all these bands across the interval.

Computing this requires finding $f'(x)$ FIRST, then substituting it into $\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}$ BEFORE setting up the definite integral — the square-root expression cannot be simplified or evaluated correctly without this derivative in hand.

## Component 4 — Worked Examples

**Example 1 (LO1, LO3 — basic application, breaking MC-1)**: Find the surface area when $f(x)=\sqrt{x}$ from $x=1$ to $x=4$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. First, $f'(x)=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}$, so $[f'(x)]^2=\frac{1}{4x}$. $S=2\pi\int_1^4\sqrt{x}\sqrt{1+\frac{1}{4x}}\,dx=2\pi\int_1^4\sqrt{x+\frac{1}{4}}\,dx$ (simplifying inside the root). Evaluating this integral gives the final surface area. A common error attempts to integrate $2\pi\int f(x)\,dx$ ALONE (omitting the $\sqrt{1+[f'(x)]^2}$ factor entirely) — mistaking this for a simpler volume-of-revolution-style setup, rather than recognizing the arc-length factor is an ESSENTIAL, non-optional part of the surface area formula.

**Example 2 (LO2 — understanding circumference times arc length)**: For a very simple case, $f(x)=r$ (a constant, i.e. a horizontal line at height $r$) rotated from $x=0$ to $x=h$ about the $x$-axis, produces a CYLINDER of radius $r$ and height $h$. Here $f'(x)=0$, so $\sqrt{1+0}=1$ (the arc length differential is just $dx$, since the "arc" is a straight horizontal line). $S=2\pi\int_0^hr\cdot1\,dx=2\pi rh$ — exactly the familiar cylinder lateral surface area formula, confirming the "circumference ($2\pi r$) times length ($h$)" interpretation directly.

**Example 3 (LO3 — a case requiring careful derivative computation, breaking MC-2)**: Find the surface area when $f(x)=x^3$ from $x=0$ to $x=1$ is rotated about the $x$-axis. First compute $f'(x)=3x^2$ CAREFULLY, then $[f'(x)]^2=9x^4$, giving $S=2\pi\int_0^1x^3\sqrt{1+9x^4}\,dx$. A common error mishandles the derivative step — e.g. forgetting to square $f'(x)$ (writing $\sqrt{1+3x^2}$ instead of $\sqrt{1+9x^4}$) — a small but consequential slip that produces an entirely different, incorrect integral.

## Component 5 — Teaching Actions

### Teaching Action A01 — The Arc-Length Factor Is Essential, Not Optional (Primitive P06: Contrast Pair)

Work Example 1, contrasting the correct full formula against the incorrect simplified attempt that omits the square-root factor.

- **MC-1 hook**: this directly targets MC-1 (omitting the arc-length factor entirely).

### Teaching Action A02 — Circumference Times Arc Length, Verified on a Simple Case (Primitive P11: Representation Shift)

Work Example 2, explicitly connecting the formula to the familiar cylinder surface area result as a sanity check.

### Teaching Action A03 — Compute f'(x) Carefully and Square It Correctly Before Integrating (Primitive P64: Conceptual Shift)

Work Example 3, explicitly separating the derivative-and-squaring step from the integration step.

- **MC-2 hook**: this directly targets MC-2 (mishandling the derivative or squaring step in the setup).
