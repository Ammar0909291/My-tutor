## 1. Concept Spine

Ground-level ozone (O₃) is NEVER a direct, primary combustion product — cars emit NO₂ (a genuine primary pollutant), which UV light splits into NO+O•, and O•+O₂→O₃, making ozone a SECONDARY pollutant formed through atmospheric photochemistry, never directly emitted from any exhaust; CO₂ does NOT cause acid rain — pure rainwater is already mildly acidic (pH≈5.6) from dissolved CO₂ forming weak carbonic acid, but genuine "acid rain" (pH 4 or below) is caused specifically by SO₂ and NOₓ forming the much STRONGER acids H₂SO₄ and HNO₃ — CO₂'s role, while real, produces only the mild baseline acidity, never the severe acidification anthropogenic acid rain represents; and CO does NOT poison by physically DISPLACING oxygen from breathed air (a bulk-gas-crowding mechanism) — even at trace concentrations (200ppm, far too dilute to physically displace anything), CO is lethal because it binds HEMOGLOBIN at the same site as O₂ with ~200× greater AFFINITY, forming carboxyhemoglobin (COHb) and outcompeting O₂ for binding sites — the mechanism is genuinely BIOCHEMICAL (competitive binding), never physical displacement.

## 2. Four-Stage CPA+ Mental Model

**Concrete**: Tracing the explicit photochemical pathway from car-exhaust NO₂ (primary pollutant) through UV-driven splitting to O• and finally O₃ formation (secondary pollutant), making the "ozone is never directly emitted" distinction concrete.

**Representational**: A pH-scale diagram placing pure CO₂-saturated rainwater (pH≈5.6) alongside genuine acid rain from SO₂/NOₓ-derived H₂SO₄/HNO₃ (pH≤4), visually contrasting the magnitude difference.

**Abstract**: The general principle that a pollutant's observed atmospheric presence doesn't imply direct emission — secondary pollutants form through subsequent atmospheric chemistry from primary emissions; the general principle that "acidifying" gases can differ enormously in acid strength, with only the strongest producing genuine "acid rain" severity; the general principle that a toxic gas's lethality mechanism must be assessed at the actual molecular/biochemical level, never assumed from a bulk-physical intuition.

**Transfer**: Given an unfamiliar air-pollution scenario, correctly distinguishing primary (directly emitted) from secondary (atmospherically formed) pollutants; given an unfamiliar acidic-precipitation scenario, correctly attributing severe acidification to SO₂/NOₓ-derived strong acids, never CO₂ alone; given an unfamiliar toxic-gas exposure, correctly assessing the actual biochemical (not physical-displacement) mechanism of toxicity.

## 3. Why Beginners Fail

Students, hearing the phrase "ozone pollution" and separately knowing CFCs deplete STRATOSPHERIC ozone, conflate ground-level (tropospheric) ozone with the stratospheric ozone layer, and further assume ground-level ozone must be a DIRECT combustion product (like other exhaust components), missing that ozone is never emitted directly from any source — it forms SECONDARILY through a specific atmospheric photochemical sequence starting from primary NO₂ emissions; students, knowing CO₂ contributes to "acidification" in contexts like ocean acidification, extend this same acidifying role to atmospheric rain formation and assume CO₂ is a (or the) cause of acid rain, missing that CO₂'s acidifying effect (forming weak carbonic acid) is genuinely mild, producing only the natural baseline rainwater pH of ~5.6 — the severe acidification that defines "acid rain" (pH 4 or below) requires the much STRONGER acids (H₂SO₄, HNO₃) formed from anthropogenic SO₂ and NOₓ emissions specifically; and students, applying an everyday physical intuition that a "heavy" or "dense" toxic gas might simply crowd out and displace breathable oxygen from the air (a bulk-volume-displacement mechanism), apply this same reasoning to CO poisoning, missing that CO is lethal at trace concentrations (200ppm, far too dilute for any meaningful physical displacement of room air) specifically because of a targeted BIOCHEMICAL mechanism — CO binds hemoglobin's oxygen-binding site with dramatically higher affinity than O₂ itself, outcompeting oxygen at the molecular level for delivery throughout the body.

## 4. Misconception Library

### MC-1: O₃ is a primary pollutant
- **Probe**: "Is ground-level ozone emitted directly from cars? How does it form in photochemical smog?"
- **Characteristic phrase**: "Cars emit ozone as part of exhaust."
- **Trigger (Type 1, overgeneralization)**: "Ozone pollution" is a commonly-heard phrase; students know CFCs destroy ozone but confuse stratospheric ozone depletion with tropospheric ozone formation.
- **Conflict evidence [P28]**: O₃ is never a direct combustion product. Cars emit NO₂ (primary). UV light splits NO₂→NO+O•; O•+O₂→O₃ (secondary). O₃ is a product of atmospheric chemistry, not of combustion.
- **Bridge [P30]**: Combustion processes (like a car engine) directly produce a specific, limited set of chemical species as immediate byproducts of the fuel-burning reaction itself — ozone is NOT among these direct products, since its formation requires a SEPARATE, subsequent atmospheric photochemical reaction sequence (UV-driven splitting of a genuinely-emitted primary pollutant, followed by recombination with atmospheric O₂) that occurs entirely AFTER and SEPARATE FROM the combustion event itself, making ozone a downstream, secondary consequence rather than a direct emission.
- **Replacement [P31]**: Ground-level ozone is always a secondary pollutant, formed through atmospheric photochemistry from primary NO₂ emissions — never a direct combustion/exhaust product.
- **Discrimination pairs [P33]**: NO₂ (primary, directly emitted from combustion) vs. O₃ (secondary, formed only through subsequent UV-driven atmospheric chemistry) — genuinely different pollutant categories.
- **S6 repair path**: Present the explicit photochemical pathway from NO₂ to O₃, tracing the specific atmospheric steps required.

### MC-2: CO₂ causes acid rain
- **Probe**: "Pure rainwater has pH ≈ 5.6 due to CO₂. Why is acid rain defined as below 5.6, not below 7? What gases, not CO₂, are responsible for pH dropping to 4 or below?"
- **Characteristic phrase**: "Acid rain is caused by all greenhouse gases."
- **Trigger (Type 3, language contamination)**: Students know CO₂ "acidifies" (e.g., ocean acidification) and confuse the mechanism of acid rain.
- **Conflict evidence [P28]**: CO₂ produces only mildly acidic rain (pH 5.6). Anthropogenic acid rain (pH 4 or below) comes from SO₂ and NOₓ, which form H₂SO₄ and HNO₃ — far stronger acids than H₂CO₃.
- **Bridge [P30]**: "Acidifying" is not a binary, all-or-nothing property — different acidifying gases produce genuinely different MAGNITUDES of acidity depending on the strength of the acid they form upon dissolving in water, and CO₂'s specific product (carbonic acid, H₂CO₃) is a genuinely WEAK acid producing only mild acidification, categorically different in severity from the STRONG acids (H₂SO₄, HNO₃) formed by SO₂/NOₓ, which are specifically responsible for the severe acidification that defines anthropogenic "acid rain."
- **Replacement [P31]**: CO₂ produces only mild baseline rainwater acidity (pH~5.6, via weak carbonic acid) — genuine "acid rain" (pH≤4) is caused specifically by SO₂ and NOₓ forming strong acids (H₂SO₄, HNO₃), never attribute severe acidification to CO₂.
- **Discrimination pairs [P33]**: CO₂-derived carbonic acid (weak, mild acidification, natural baseline pH~5.6) vs. SO₂/NOₓ-derived sulfuric/nitric acid (strong, severe acidification, pH≤4, defines "acid rain").
- **S6 repair path**: Present the explicit pH-scale comparison, isolating the magnitude difference between weak-acid CO₂ acidification and strong-acid SO₂/NOₓ acidification.

### MC-3: CO suffocates by displacing O₂ from air
- **Probe**: "CO is toxic at concentrations of 200 ppm (0.02%). At that level, it is not displacing room air. Why is it still lethal?"
- **Characteristic phrase**: "CO takes up the space in the air that oxygen would fill."
- **Trigger (Type 2, perceptual intuition)**: CO seems like a "heavy gas that fills the room and forces out oxygen" — a physical displacement intuition.
- **Conflict evidence [P28]**: CO binds to haemoglobin at the same site as O₂ with 200× greater affinity, forming COHb. Even trace CO outcompetes O₂ for haemoglobin sites→less O₂ delivered to cells→hypoxia. The mechanism is biochemical, not physical.
- **Bridge [P30]**: A gas's toxicity mechanism at trace, dilute concentrations (like 200ppm, a mere 0.02% of the air) cannot plausibly be explained by simple bulk physical crowding/displacement, since such a tiny concentration leaves the vast majority of the air's composition (including O₂) essentially unchanged — the actual toxicity mechanism must instead operate at a highly SPECIFIC, targeted molecular level, and CO's dramatically higher binding affinity for hemoglobin's O₂-binding site means it effectively "wins" the competition for those specific binding sites even when present in only trace amounts, severely reducing the blood's actual oxygen-carrying capacity despite ambient O₂ levels remaining essentially normal.
- **Replacement [P31]**: CO poisons via competitive, high-affinity binding to hemoglobin's O₂ site (a biochemical mechanism), never via bulk physical displacement of oxygen from the air — this explains its lethality even at trace concentrations.
- **Discrimination pairs [P33]**: Trace CO (200ppm, negligible physical effect on ambient O₂ levels, yet still lethal via biochemical hemoglobin competition) vs. a hypothetical bulk-displacement mechanism (would require far higher concentrations to meaningfully reduce ambient O₂, contradicted by CO's actual trace-level lethality).
- **S6 repair path**: Present the explicit hemoglobin-binding-affinity comparison (CO vs. O₂), deriving trace-level lethality from the biochemical competition mechanism.

## 5. Explanation Library

**Primary explanation**: Ground-level ozone is always a secondary pollutant, formed through a specific atmospheric photochemical sequence (UV-driven splitting of primary NO₂, followed by O•+O₂ recombination) — never a direct combustion/exhaust product. Acid rain's severe acidification (pH≤4) is caused specifically by the strong acids H₂SO₄ and HNO₃, formed from anthropogenic SO₂ and NOₓ emissions — CO₂'s weak carbonic acid produces only the mild, natural baseline rainwater acidity (pH~5.6), a categorically different magnitude.

**Secondary explanation (CO's biochemical toxicity mechanism)**: CO's lethality at trace concentrations arises from a targeted biochemical mechanism — competitive binding to hemoglobin's O₂-binding site with dramatically higher affinity than O₂ itself — never from bulk physical displacement of oxygen from ambient air, which trace CO concentrations could not plausibly achieve.

## 6. Analogy Library

- **Primary analogy**: A relay race where the "baton" (NO₂, a primary emission) gets passed through several transformative hand-offs (UV splitting, O₂ recombination) before finally becoming a different runner entirely (O₃) — the final runner (ozone) was never directly on the starting line (never a direct exhaust emission).
- **Breaking point**: The relay-race analogy conveys the primary-to-secondary-pollutant transformation concept well but doesn't naturally capture the weak-vs-strong-acid magnitude distinction for acid rain (MC-2) or the biochemical-competition mechanism for CO toxicity (MC-3) — those need the explicit pH-scale comparison and the hemoglobin-binding-affinity comparison.
- **Anti-analogy**: Do NOT say "any acidic gas in the atmosphere causes acid rain" — this directly reinforces MC-2 by ignoring the crucial magnitude/strength distinction between weak and strong acids.

## 7. Demonstration Library

- **Demonstration 2 (pH-scale comparison for CO₂-derived vs. SO₂/NOₓ-derived acidity)**: Present the explicit pH comparison, isolating the magnitude difference between weak and strong acids.
- **Demonstration 3 (hemoglobin-binding-affinity comparison for CO vs. O₂)**: Present the explicit affinity comparison, deriving trace-level CO lethality from the biochemical competition mechanism.

## 8. Discovery Lesson

**Opening**: "Do cars emit ozone directly as part of their exhaust?"

**Exploration**: Students trace the explicit photochemical pathway from NO₂ to O₃, discovering ozone is always a secondary, atmospherically-formed pollutant.

**Synthesis**: Guide toward: a pollutant's presence in the atmosphere doesn't imply direct emission — secondary pollutants form through subsequent atmospheric chemistry.

**Closure**: "Is CO toxic at 200 ppm because it physically displaces oxygen from the room's air?" (Directly resolves MC-3.)

## 9. Teaching Actions (session_cap = 4)

- **TA-1 (SHOW)**: Present the explicit photochemical pathway diagram from NO₂ to O₃.
- **TA-2 (TELL)**: State the weak-vs-strong-acid distinction for acid rain explicitly, anchored to the pH-scale comparison.
- **TA-3 (DO)**: Student predicts the mechanism (physical vs. biochemical) for an unfamiliar toxic-gas exposure scenario.
- **TA-4 (TEST-THINKING)**: Present the CO-at-200ppm probe and ask the student to justify why trace concentrations are still lethal.

## 10. Voice Teaching

Whenever a pollutant is classified, narrate "check whether it's directly emitted (primary) or atmospherically formed (secondary) — never assume from presence alone." Whenever acid rain is discussed, state "check acid strength — CO₂ is weak, SO₂/NOₓ are strong" as the standing reminder.

## 11. Assessment

**Mastery gate**: Student can (a) correctly classify ozone as a secondary, not primary, pollutant, (b) correctly attribute severe acid rain to SO₂/NOₓ-derived strong acids, not CO₂, (c) correctly explain CO toxicity via biochemical hemoglobin competition, not physical displacement.

- **FA-1**: "Is ground-level ozone emitted directly from cars? How does it form in photochemical smog?" — targets MC-1.
- **FA-2**: "Pure rainwater has pH ≈ 5.6 due to CO₂. Why is acid rain defined as below 5.6, not below 7?" — targets MC-2.
- **FA-3**: "CO is toxic at concentrations of 200 ppm. At that level, it is not displacing room air. Why is it still lethal?" — targets MC-3.

**Confidence calibration**: Predict high-confidence-wrong answers on MC-1 among students who conflate tropospheric ozone formation with stratospheric ozone depletion.

**Delayed retrieval**: Re-probe MC-1's primary-vs-secondary pollutant distinction and MC-3's biochemical toxicity mechanism as foundational knowledge for subsequent environmental and public-health chemistry applications.

## 12. Recovery Notes

- **S3 (stuck)**: For the ozone-as-primary-pollutant confusion, have the student explicitly trace the photochemical pathway before classifying it as primary or secondary.
- **S4 (frustrated)**: Normalize — conflating tropospheric ozone formation with stratospheric ozone depletion is genuinely common on first exposure, since both involve "ozone" in the news.
- **S6 (collision)**: Use the explicit pH-scale comparison for MC-2; use the hemoglobin-binding-affinity comparison for MC-3.
- **S9 (post-repair check)**: Ask the student to explain, unprompted, why CO is lethal at trace concentrations despite not meaningfully displacing ambient oxygen.
