## Component 1 — Learning Objective
The student defines an algebraic number field K=ℚ(α) as a finite extension of ℚ of degree [K:ℚ]=n; identifies the ring of integers O_K as the subring of algebraic integers in K; states that O_K is a Dedekind domain (Noetherian, integrally closed, every nonzero prime ideal is maximal); explains why O_K need not be a UFD and how ideal factorisation restores unique factorisation (every nonzero ideal factors uniquely into prime ideals); defines the ideal class group Cl(K)=I(O_K)/P(O_K) and its class number h_K; states Minkowski's bound; and explains the connection between the class number and Fermat's Last Theorem for regular primes.

## Component 2 — CPA Entry Stage
**P — Pictorial** (two factorisations of 6 in ℤ[√−5]: 6=2×3=(1+√−5)(1−√−5); draw a "factorisation tree" with two branches showing each factorisation leads to four different irreducibles; contrast with ℤ where 6=2×3 is the unique tree; motivates why the ring of integers of ℚ(√−5) needs ideal-level analysis to recover uniqueness)

## Component 3 — Misconception Register

| ID | Label | Pattern | Birth type |
|---|---|---|---|
| MC-1 | ALL-RINGS-OF-INTEGERS-ARE-UFDS | Student assumes O_K is always a UFD, extending ℤ's unique factorisation; unaware that ℤ[√−5] (the ring of integers of ℚ(√−5)) fails UFD and this is the whole motivation for ideal theory | Type 5 — instruction-induced (ℤ is the canonical example of a number ring and it IS a UFD; students taught Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic as a universal truth apply it to all rings of integers without knowing the class number condition) |
| MC-2 | IDEAL-CLASS-GROUP-MEASURES-HOW-MANY-IDEALS-THERE-ARE | Student thinks h_K = |Cl(K)| counts total ideals or prime ideals of O_K; doesn't understand it measures failure of principal-ideal generation (i.e. failure of UFD property) | Type 3 — language contamination ("class" and "group" suggest a measure or collection of ideals per se; the actual meaning — equivalence classes of fractional ideals under principal-ideal equivalence — is abstract) |
| MC-3 | ALGEBRAIC-INTEGERS-IN-K-ARE-JUST-INTEGER-LINEAR-COMBINATIONS-OF-BASIS | Student believes O_K = ℤ[α] always; doesn't know that O_K can be strictly larger than ℤ[α] for a generating element α | Type 5 — instruction-induced (examples like ℚ(√2) where O_K=ℤ[√2] and ℚ(√−1) where O_K=ℤ[i] confirm ℤ[α]=O_K; when the minimal polynomial has discriminant not equal to the field discriminant (e.g. ℚ(√5) where O_K=ℤ[(1+√5)/2]≠ℤ[√5]), students are caught off guard) |

## Component 5 — Teaching Action Sequence

### A01 — P11 REPRESENTATION SHIFT (open)
**Four lenses on algebraic number theory:**

| Lens | Content |
|---|---|
| Historical motivation | Kummer's failed proof of Fermat's Last Theorem (1847) assumed ℤ[ζ_p] was a UFD; his "ideal numbers" repair: introduce ideal prime factors to restore unique factorisation at the ideal level |
| UFD failure example | ℤ[√−5]: 6=2×3=(1+√−5)(1−√−5), two distinct factorisations; 2,3,1±√−5 are all irreducible but not prime; class number h=2 measures this failure |
| Ideal factorisation | In O_K=ℤ[√−5]: (2)=(2,1+√−5)(2,1−√−5); (3)=(3,1+√−5)(3,1−√−5); (1+√−5)=(2,1+√−5)(3,1+√−5); (6)=(2,1+√−5)²(2,1−√−5)(3,1+√−5)(3,1−√−5) — unique factorisation restored at the ideal level |
| Class number | h(ℚ(√−5))=2: 2 ideal classes, one principal (generated by single element), one non-principal. h=1 iff O_K is a UFD. Imaginary quadratic fields ℚ(√−D) have h=1 for D=1,2,3,7,11,19,43,67,163 only. |

**Key definitions:**
- **Algebraic number field:** K=ℚ(α) for some algebraic number α; [K:ℚ]=degree of minimal polynomial of α over ℚ
- **Ring of integers:** O_K = {x∈K : x satisfies a monic polynomial with integer coefficients} = algebraic integers in K
- **Dedekind domain:** integral domain where every nonzero ideal factors uniquely into prime ideals (O_K is always a Dedekind domain by the fundamental theorem of algebraic number theory)
- **Ideal class group:** Cl(K) = (fractional ideals of O_K)/(principal fractional ideals); h_K=|Cl(K)|; h_K=1 ↔ O_K is a UFD
- **Minkowski's bound:** every ideal class contains an ideal of norm ≤ M_K = (n!/nⁿ)(4/π)^(r₂)√|disc(K)| where r₂ = number of complex embeddings; only finitely many prime ideals lie below M_K, making h_K computable

**P49 checkpoint:**
- CORRECT → "K=ℚ(α); O_K = algebraic integers in K; O_K is Dedekind domain; ideal factorisation is unique even when element factorisation fails; h_K = |Cl(K)|; h_K=1 ↔ UFD." → A02
- PARTIAL (doesn't understand ideal factorisation) → "An ideal I of O_K is a subgroup of O_K closed under multiplication by O_K. A prime ideal P is one where ab∈P implies a∈P or b∈P. In a Dedekind domain, every ideal (not just elements) factors uniquely into prime ideals. Example: in ℤ[√−5], the element 6 doesn't have unique factorisation, but the ideal (6) does: it equals a specific product of prime ideals, regardless of whether you start from 2×3 or (1+√−5)(1−√−5). The uniqueness lives at the ideal level." → TB-R01 → A02
- INCORRECT → TB-R01 → A02
- NO_RESPONSE → "In ℤ, show that 6=2×3 is unique (up to ordering and units). In ℤ[√−5], compute (1+√−5)(1−√−5)=1+5=6. Are 1+√−5 and 2 related in ℤ[√−5]? Is 2 a unit in ℤ[√−5]?" → TB-R01 → A02

### A02 — P04 PATTERN INDUCTION
**Structure of rings of integers:**

**Quadratic fields ℚ(√D) (D square-free):**
- D≡2,3(mod 4): O_K=ℤ[√D] (basis {1,√D})
- D≡1(mod 4): O_K=ℤ[(1+√D)/2] (basis {1,(1+√D)/2})
- Example: ℚ(√5), D=5≡1(mod 4): O_K=ℤ[φ] where φ=(1+√5)/2 is the golden ratio; φ satisfies φ²−φ−1=0 (monic, integer coefficients). Note ℤ[√5]⊊O_K.

**Key invariants:**
- Discriminant disc(K): for ℚ(√D): disc=4D if D≡2,3(mod 4); disc=D if D≡1(mod 4)
- Unit group O_K×: by Dirichlet's Unit Theorem, O_K×≅ℤʳ×μ where r=r₁+r₂−1 (r₁ real, r₂ complex places) and μ is the finite group of roots of unity. For imaginary quadratic fields: only r=0 (finite unit groups). For real quadratic: r=1 (infinite cyclic, generated by the fundamental unit — connects to Pell's equation!).

**Pell-algebraic integers connection:** the fundamental solution (x₁,y₁) to x²−Dy²=1 is EXACTLY the fundamental unit ε=x₁+y₁√D of ℤ[√D] (when D≡2,3 mod 4). The unit group O_K×={±εⁿ : n∈ℤ}, which gives all Pell solutions.

**Class numbers of imaginary quadratic fields:**
- h=1: D=−1,−2,−3,−7,−11,−19,−43,−67,−163 (Stark-Heegner theorem)
- h=2: D=−5,−6,−10,−13,−15,−22,−35,−37,…
- h grows (logarithmically) as |D|→∞

**P49 checkpoint:**
- CORRECT → "O_K depends on D mod 4. Discriminant distinguishes basis choice. Units: Dirichlet's theorem; real quadratic fields have Pell-generated infinite unit groups; imaginary quadratic fields have finite unit groups. h_K=1 for only 9 imaginary quadratic fields." → A03
- PARTIAL (doesn't connect Pell to units) → "In ℤ[√2]: the equation x²−2y²=1 asks for elements of norm 1 in ℤ[√2]. The fundamental solution (3,2) gives the fundamental unit u=3+2√2. Every unit of ℤ[√2] is ±uⁿ for n∈ℤ — exactly the infinite family of Pell solutions. Algebraic number theory thus contains and generalises Pell's equation." → TB-R02 → A03
- INCORRECT → TB-R02 → A03
- NO_RESPONSE → "In ℤ[√−1]=ℤ[i]: what are the units? List them. Is the factorisation 5=(2+i)(2−i) unique up to units? What is h(ℚ(i))?" → TB-R02 → A03

### A03 — P41 MISCONCEPTION DETECTOR + P64 MC-1 gate
**UFD-failure gate:**

**Gate question (MC-1):** "A student claims that ℤ[√−5] is a UFD because every integer has a unique prime factorisation. Identify the error."

ℤ is a UFD; ℤ[√−5] is NOT. The student has confused the ring ℤ (integers) with the ring ℤ[√−5] (algebraic integers in ℚ(√−5)). In ℤ[√−5], the element 6 has TWO distinct factorisations: 6=2×3=(1+√−5)(1−√−5). One can verify that 2, 3, 1+√−5, 1−√−5 are all irreducible (non-unit, non-factorable further) in ℤ[√−5], yet 2 does not divide 1+√−5 in this ring (there is no element α∈ℤ[√−5] with 2α=1+√−5). So UFD fails. The class number h(ℚ(√−5))=2≠1 confirms this.

**P49 checkpoint:**
- CORRECT → "ℤ[√−5] fails UFD: 6=2×3=(1+√−5)(1−√−5). This failure is measured by h_K=2. Ideal factorisation restores uniqueness." → Gate (P91)
- PARTIAL → TB-R03 → Gate
- INCORRECT → TB-R03 → Gate
- NO_RESPONSE → "Verify: (1+√−5)(1−√−5)=1+5=6. Is this the same factorisation as 2×3=6? Are 1+√−5 and 2 related by a unit in ℤ[√−5]? (Units of ℤ[√−5] are just ±1; is 2/(1+√−5)=(1−√−5)/3 a unit?)" → TB-R03 → Gate

## Component 6 — Protocol B Repair Chains

**TB-R01 (MC-1 ALL-RINGS-OF-INTEGERS-ARE-UFDS):**
Step 1 — "ℤ is a UFD — this is the Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic. But ℤ[√−5] is a different ring. It contains all a+b√−5 with a,b∈ℤ. In this ring, 2 and 1+√−5 are both irreducible: 2=(a+b√−5)(c+d√−5) → take norms: 4=(a²+5b²)(c²+5d²). The only factorisation of 4 as a product of integers from the norm form is 4=1×4=4×1 or 4=2×2. 2=(a²+5b²) has no integer solution. So 2 is irreducible in ℤ[√−5]. Similarly for 3 and 1±√−5." Step 2 — "Yet 6=2×3=(1+√−5)(1−√−5) are two distinct factorisations into irreducibles. In a UFD, irreducible → prime. But 2 is NOT prime in ℤ[√−5]: 2|(1+√−5)(1−√−5) but 2∤1+√−5 and 2∤1−√−5." Step 3 — "Kummer's great discovery: introduce 'ideal prime factors' p₁,p₂,q₁,q₂ such that (2)=p₁p₂, (3)=q₁q₂, (1+√−5)=p₁q₁, (1−√−5)=p₂q₂. Then (6)=p₁p₂q₁q₂ — unique at the ideal level."

**TB-R02 (MC-2 IDEAL-CLASS-GROUP-MEASURES-HOW-MANY-IDEALS-THERE-ARE):**
Step 1 — "The ideal class group does not count ideals. It groups fractional ideals into equivalence classes, where two ideals I and J are equivalent if αI=βJ for some α,β∈O_K (they differ by a principal factor). The class group Cl(K) is the quotient of all fractional ideals by this equivalence." Step 2 — "h_K=|Cl(K)| counts equivalence classes. h_K=1 means every ideal is equivalent to the principal ideal (1)=O_K — so every ideal IS principal — which is exactly the UFD condition." Step 3 — "h_K=2 for ℤ[√−5]: two classes. The non-principal ideal P=(2,1+√−5) is not generated by a single element, but P² IS principal: P²=(2) (the ideal generated by 2). This is the algebraic-number-theory version of the factorisation structure."

**TB-R03 (MC-3 ALGEBRAIC-INTEGERS-IN-K-ARE-JUST-INTEGER-LINEAR-COMBINATIONS-OF-BASIS):**
Step 1 — "For K=ℚ(√5): the naive guess is O_K=ℤ[√5]={a+b√5: a,b∈ℤ}. But consider α=(1+√5)/2 (the golden ratio). Its minimal polynomial is x²−x−1=0 — monic with integer coefficients. So α IS an algebraic integer, but α∉ℤ[√5] (since 1/2 is not an integer)." Step 2 — "So O_K⊋ℤ[√5]. The correct ring is O_K=ℤ[(1+√5)/2]={a+b(1+√5)/2: a,b∈ℤ}. The general rule: for ℚ(√D), if D≡1(mod 4), the ring of integers includes half-integers." Step 3 — "How to detect this: compute disc(ℤ[α]) and compare with disc(K). If disc(ℤ[α])=f²×disc(K) for some f>1, then O_K is strictly larger than ℤ[α]. For ℚ(√5): disc(ℤ[√5])=4×5=20; disc(K)=5. f=2, confirming O_K≠ℤ[√5]."

**P76 — Transfer Probe (Cross-link mode: math.abst.galois-theory):**
(a) For the cyclotomic field K=ℚ(ζ_p) (p prime): the Galois group Gal(K/ℚ)≅(ℤ/pℤ)×. Why is Gal(K/ℚ) abelian? (b) Kummer's approach: if p is an odd prime and p∤h(ℚ(ζ_p)) (p is a 'regular prime'), then Fermat's Last Theorem holds for exponent p (Kummer's theorem). The primes p<100 that are irregular (p|h(ℚ(ζ_p))) are: 37, 59, 67. Explain why h(ℚ(ζ_37))>1 would concern Kummer. (c) Chebotarev Density Theorem: the density of primes that split completely in a degree-n Galois extension is 1/n. For ℚ(√−5)/ℚ (degree 2): half of all primes split completely, a quarter each ramify or remain inert. Verify: 7 is inert (7≡3 mod 4... actually in ℚ(√−5) splitting is determined by Legendre symbol (−5/p)). Compute for p=7: (−5/7)=(−1/7)(5/7)=(−1)^3×(7/5)(−1)^... use quadratic reciprocity.

**P55 — Reflect & Consolidate:** "Cyclotomic fields: Gal(ℚ(ζ_p)/ℚ)≅(ℤ/pℤ)× abelian — this is why abelian extensions of ℚ are all contained in cyclotomic fields (Kronecker-Weber). Class field theory: complete description of abelian extensions in terms of class groups (generalises quadratic reciprocity). Chebotarev: governs prime splitting by Galois group structure."
