## Component 1 — Learning Objectives

- LO1: Instantly state the cardinality (count) of a small collection (1-4 objects/dots) WITHOUT counting each element one by one.
- LO2: Distinguish subitizing (instant perceptual recognition) from counting (sequential enumeration), recognizing which strategy is actually being used.
- LO3: Recognize common small-quantity arrangements (e.g. dice patterns, dominoes) by their spatial configuration, not just their count.

## Component 2 — Prerequisite Check

Assumes mastery of `math.arith.counting` (the sequential one-to-one enumeration process subitizing bypasses for small quantities).

## Component 3 — Core Explanation

**Subitizing** is the ability to instantly perceive "how many" for a SMALL set (typically ≤4 items) without going through the sequential counting process (1, 2, 3...). This is a genuinely different cognitive mechanism from counting — it is immediate, parallel perception, not sequential enumeration. Beyond 4-5 items, humans generally cannot subitize and must revert to counting or grouping strategies (e.g. seeing 6 as "3 and 3").

Subitizing is foundational because it gives young learners (and, situationally, everyone) a fast, reliable anchor for the smallest cardinalities, on which counting, addition, and place-value understanding are later built.

## Component 4 — Worked Examples

**Example 1 (LO1 — instant recognition)**: Shown $\bullet\bullet\bullet$ (three dots) briefly, a learner states "three" immediately, without pointing at or verbally counting each dot in sequence.

**Example 2 (LO2 — subitizing vs. counting, breaking MC-1)**: Shown $\bullet\bullet\bullet\bullet$ (four dots) in a standard dice-five-minus-one arrangement, most learners subitize instantly. Shown 7 scattered dots, the SAME learner typically cannot subitize and must count sequentially (1,2,3,...,7) or group ("4 and 3 makes 7") — the boundary around 4-5 items is a genuine perceptual limit, not a matter of "trying harder" or "practicing more" to subitize larger sets directly.

**Example 3 (LO3 — configuration recognition)**: A standard die face showing 5 pips (four corners plus center) is recognized as "5" instantly by its FAMILIAR SPATIAL PATTERN, not by re-subitizing five scattered dots from scratch — pattern familiarity (conceptual subitizing) extends the perceptual mechanism (perceptual subitizing) to slightly larger, well-known arrangements.

## Component 5 — Teaching Actions

### Teaching Action A01 — Instant Recognition of 1-4 (Primitive P64: Conceptual Shift)

Flash small dot arrangements (1 to 4 dots, varied layouts) briefly and have the learner state the count immediately, without pointing or verbal counting. Reinforce: "say it right away — don't count them one by one."

- **MC-1 hook**: flash a 7-dot scattered arrangement briefly and ask for the immediate count (revealing MC-1: expecting subitizing to extend indefinitely to larger sets, rather than recognizing the genuine ≤4-5 perceptual limit).

### Teaching Action A02 — Subitizing vs. Counting, and the Perceptual Limit (Primitive P06: Contrast Pair)

Contrast Example 1's instant 3-dot recognition against a slower, visibly sequential count of 7 scattered dots, naming the difference explicitly: "small sets — see it instantly. Larger sets — count, or break into smaller subitizable chunks (like 4 and 3)." Introduce Example 3's die-pattern recognition as a bridge — a LEARNED pattern extending recognition slightly beyond the raw perceptual limit.

### Teaching Action A03 — Mastery Gate (Primitive P91)

P91 = P77 → P55 → P76 → P55 → P75 → P55 → P74 → P55 → P78. MAMR = ⌈0.90×5⌉ = 5/5.

- **P77 (4-problem set)**:
  1. State the count for each of four flashed dot arrangements (1, 2, 3, 4 dots, varied layouts) instantly.
  2. Identify a standard die face (e.g. showing 6 pips) by its pattern without individually counting each pip.
  3. Explain, in one sentence, why a 9-dot scattered arrangement cannot typically be subitized the way a 3-dot arrangement can.
  4. Given a 6-dot arrangement grouped visually as "3 and 3," state the total using the grouping strategy rather than counting all six individually.
- **P76 (Transfer Probe, mode = independence)**: "A parent notices their child instantly says 'four!' when shown four scattered buttons, but pauses and counts one-by-one when shown eight scattered buttons. (a) Explain, using this lesson's ideas, why this difference in behavior is expected and not a sign the child is 'bad at counting.' (b) Suggest, based on Example 3's die-pattern idea, one way the parent could help the child recognize groups of 6-8 faster without necessarily subitizing them in one raw perceptual glance."
- **P55 (Score)** after each stage.
- **P75 (Mastery Assessment against MAMR = 5/5)**.
- **P74 (Routing Decision)**: MAMR met → P78 (Completion). MAMR not met → route to the specific missed misconception's repair action before re-attempting P77.

## Component 6 — Misconception Registry

| ID | Label | Description | Severity |
|---|---|---|---|
| MC-1 | SUBITIZING-ASSUMED-UNLIMITED | Expecting instant perceptual recognition to extend to arbitrarily large sets rather than recognizing the genuine ≤4-5 item perceptual limit | Moderate |
| MC-2 | SUBITIZING-CONFLATED-WITH-FAST-COUNTING | Treating a very quick sequential count as "subitizing," rather than recognizing subitizing as a qualitatively different, non-sequential perceptual process | Minor |

### Protocol B — Repair Actions

- **B01 (targets MC-1)**: P27 ("Subitizing Assumed Unlimited") → P41 (detect: flash a 7+ dot scattered arrangement and check whether the learner expects to answer instantly or shifts to counting) → P64 (conceptual shift: contrast the instant 3-dot response against the visibly sequential larger count, naming the perceptual boundary explicitly).
- **B02 (targets MC-2)**: P27 ("Subitizing Conflated with Fast Counting") → P41 (detect: ask the learner to describe HOW they got a count of 3 vs. a count of 7 — check for a description of visible sequential counting even for the small case) → P64 (re-demonstrate the small-set flash, emphasizing there is no separate step of pointing/counting at all — the count is perceived as a whole).

## Component 8 — Teaching Notes

- estimated_hours = 2 and bloom = remember reflect subitizing's nature as a perceptual/recognition skill rather than a procedure to be derived — practice strengthens speed and pattern range, not conceptual understanding per se.
- MC-1 was ranked the more significant of the two misconceptions because it can lead a learner (or a well-meaning adult) to treat a NORMAL perceptual limit as a deficiency, potentially causing unnecessary anxiety around larger quantities that are properly handled by counting or grouping instead.
- The die-pattern example (Example 3) was deliberately included to preview "conceptual subitizing" (pattern-based recognition of familiar larger arrangements) as a distinct, learnable extension — without overclaiming it as raw perceptual subitizing, keeping LO1's ≤4 scope honest.
