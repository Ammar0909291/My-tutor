**Linear Equation in One Variable**  

In this lesson we’ll see how an equation like \(ax + b = c\) (with \(a \neq 0\)) works, and by the end you’ll be able to solve it for the unknown \(x\).

Imagine a kitchen balance scale. On the left pan you place a bag of oranges, and on the right pan you put a known weight \(c\) plus a small extra weight \(b\). The scale balances when the total weight on each side is the same.  

Now picture that you don’t know how many oranges are in the bag, but you do know each orange weighs the same amount, say \(a\) units. The balance tells you:

\[
\text{(number of oranges)} \times a \;+\; b \;=\; c
\]

In plain words, a **linear equation in one variable** is a statement that two expressions are equal, where the unknown (the variable) appears only to the first power and is multiplied by a constant, then possibly shifted by another constant.

The key words are:
- **variable** – the unknown quantity we’re looking for (here \(x\))
- **coefficient** – the number that multiplies the variable (here \(a\))
- **constant term** – a number added or subtracted (here \(b\) or \(c\))

When everything is written in the standard form, it looks like:

\[
a x + b = c
\]

where \(a\) is not zero, so the equation has exactly one solution.

When a variable is multiplied by a number, solve the equation by dividing both sides by that number. This reverses the multiplication and isolates the variable. For any equation of the form ax = b, the solution is x = b / a.

Let’s solve a specific linear equation together:

**Equation:** \(4x + 7 = 31\) (“four x plus seven equals thirty‑one”).

1. **First step – keep the balance:** 
 To get the term with \(x\) alone, we remove the constant \(7\) from the left side. 
 We do the same to the right side: subtract \(7\) from both sides. 

 \[
 4x + 7 - 7 = 31 - 7
 \]

 This simplifies to 

 \[
 4x = 24
 \]

2. **Second step – isolate the variable:** 
 The coefficient of \(x\) is \(4\). We divide both sides by \(4\) to keep the equation balanced.

 \[
 \frac{4x}{4} = \frac{24}{4}
 \]

 Giving 

 \[
 x = 6
 \]

3. **Check the solution:** 
 Put \(x = 6\) back into the original equation:

 \[
 4(6) + 7 = 24 + 7 = 31
 \]

 Both sides are equal, so the solution is correct.

**Question:** Why do we subtract the constant \(7\) before we divide by the coefficient \(4\) instead of dividing first?

[tutor provider=groq fig=none card=none flags=-]
Think of the equation like two bank accounts that must always have the same total amount of money. 
Suppose one account shows **2 × x + 5 dollars** and the other shows **19 dollars**. The “= ” sign means the balances are equal right now. 

If you decide to withdraw 5 dollars from both accounts, the equality stays true: 

- Left side: 2 × x + 5 – 5 = 2 × x 
- Right side: 19 – 5 = 14 

Now the accounts read **2 × x = 14**. To make the left side just “x”, you divide both accounts by 2 (the same operation on both sides): 

- Left side: (2 × x) ÷ 2 = x 
- Right side: 14 ÷ 2 = 7 

Now the balances are **x = 7**, so the unknown amount x must be 7 dollars. The key idea is that whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other, just like making the same deposit or withdrawal in both accounts keeps them equal.

A linear equation like 3x + 5 = 20 is a balance scale that is currently level: whatever is on the left weighs exactly the same as whatever is on the right. Solving means finding what x must weigh — and the one rule is that anything you do to one pan, you must do identically to the other, or the scale tips and the equation stops being true. Take 5 off the left? Take 5 off the right too: 3x = 15. Split the left pan into 3 equal parts? Split the right the same way: x = 5. Check by putting 5 back in the original: 3(5) + 5 = 20. It balances — that substitution check is how YOU know the answer is right without asking anyone.

Now that the constant term is gone, the next move is to isolate the variable itself. Since the term 5x means “5 times x,” we undo the multiplication by doing the opposite operation on both sides: divide each side by 5. This gives x = 15 ÷ 5, which simplifies to x = 3.
