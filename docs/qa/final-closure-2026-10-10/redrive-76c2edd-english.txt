Let’s start our lesson on **Verbs**. A verb is a word that tells what someone or something does, or what state it is in. By the end of this lesson you’ll be able to spot main verbs and helping (auxiliary) verbs in sentences and use them correctly.

Imagine a bright red **ball** on a playground.  
A child picks it up, runs, and **throws** the ball far across the field.  
Picture the moment the ball flies through the air, its path showing the action.

In plain words, the words **run**, **throw**, **is**, and **feel** are verbs because they describe an action (running, throwing) or a state of being (is, feel).  

The word we call this part of speech is **verb**.  
When a verb shows an action, we call it an **action verb**; when it shows a state of being, it’s a **state verb**.

Now let’s look at verbs that link a subject to a description or condition. Consider the sentence **“She is happy.”** The word **is** connects *she* to the feeling *happy*. There’s no movement, but **is** is still doing the work of a verb: it tells us *what* the subject is like right now. In the same way, sentences like **“The sky became dark.”** or **“He seems tired.”** use **became** and **seems** to link the subject to a state. These are called **linking (or state) verbs** because they link the subject to a description rather than showing an action. So, a linking or state verb is a verb that expresses a condition, quality, or identity of the subject, even though nothing is physically moving. Whenever you see a sentence, look for words that tell you *what* something **is**, **becomes**, **seems**, **feels**, or **appears**—those are linking/state verbs. Feel free to try a sentence of your own, and we’ll see the linking verb together.

The auxiliary verbs 'has' and 'been' combine with 'reviewed' to form the complete verb phrase. In English, auxiliaries are part of the verb and cannot be ignored. So the full verb is 'has been reviewed'.

Auxiliary (helping) verbs show tense, aspect, or voice and always work together with the main verb. In the present perfect passive, 'has' marks present perfect, 'been' marks passive voice, and 'reviewed' is the past participle. Recognizing all three as a single verb phrase helps you identify the correct structure in similar sentences.

[tutor provider=groq fig=none card=none flags=-]
Think of a sentence like a simple recipe.

- The **helping verb** is the stove that provides the heat. It tells us *when* and *how* the cooking happens (past, present, ability, etc.). 
- The **main verb** is the food you’re cooking. It’s the actual action or state that the stove works on.

In the sentence **“She is running,”** *is* is the stove (helping verb) and *running* is the food (main verb). Together they create the complete “dish” – the full verb phrase that tells us she is currently doing the action of running.

Now you can see that the helping verb isn’t extra; it’s essential, just like the stove is needed to cook the food.
