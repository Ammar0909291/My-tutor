Now look at the same idea in two different ways. First, the square‑root symbol gives a single, non‑negative answer: √36 = 6. If we instead ask “what numbers y satisfy y² = 36?” we must list both possibilities, y = 6 **and** y = ‑6, because squaring either gives 36. The √ symbol is a function, so it chooses only the non‑negative root. Next, consider roots of negative numbers. An even‑index root, like √(‑9), has no real result because no real number squared becomes negative. But an odd‑index root works: the cube root of ‑8 is ‑2, since (‑2)³ = ‑8. This shows the parity rule: even roots need a non‑negative radicand and return the non‑negative root; odd roots accept any real radicand and keep the sign.

Imagine a square that has an area of 20 square units (for example, 20 cm²). 
The length of each side is the number that, when multiplied by itself, gives 20. In other words, we are looking for the number y such that y² = 20. That number is the square root of 20, written √20.

We know the perfect squares 4² = 16 and 5² = 25, so the side length must lie between 4 and 5. Because the square‑root function is the inverse of squaring, we can estimate √20 by seeing how far 20 is between 16 and 25. A quick calculation gives √20 ≈ 4.47 (you can check: 4.47 × 4.47 ≈ 20). 

On the graph you see, the curve passes through the point (20, √20). You can notice that this point sits right between the points (16, 4) and (25, 5) on the curve, confirming our estimate.

You have answered every new practice question I have on Square Roots in this lesson; a question you have already seen comes back once more a little later. Here is the idea once more.

Taking a square root undoes squaring: √49 = 7 because 7² = 49. If squaring asks "how many counters in this square?", the root asks "how long is the side?".

Both 7 and −7 square to 49, so the equation x² = 49 has two solutions. The SYMBOL √ is defined to give only the non-negative one, which is why √49 is 7 and not ±7 — the plus-or-minus in "x = ±7" is written in deliberately, because the symbol alone will not supply it.

Most roots are not whole numbers, and trapping them between two squares is the honest way to handle it: 49 < 60 < 64, so √60 is between 7 and 8, and nearer 8 because 60 is nearer 64. Answering "about 7.7" is a real answer, not a failure to find the exact one.
